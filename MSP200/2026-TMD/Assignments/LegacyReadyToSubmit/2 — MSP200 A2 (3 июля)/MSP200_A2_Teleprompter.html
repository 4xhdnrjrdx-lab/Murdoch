Intro (~0:55)

Hi, I'm Faiaz Dzhafarov, student number 35553299, and this is my Professional Learning Reflection for MSP200.

I'm doing a Bachelor of Business in Marketing and Management, aiming for roles like Market Research Analyst, Digital Marketing Trainee, and eventually Marketing Supervisor.

To build towards those I did three short edX courses, each aimed at a skill I wanted to grow: Intro to AI for Digital Marketing for digital literacy, Digital Marketing Analytics for critical and creative thinking, and Business Communications for communication.

I chose the first two on purpose, because in Assessment 1 I'd rated those skills lowest. I'll go through each course using the 5R framework, then pull them together at the end.

Course 1: Intro to AI for Digital Marketing → Digital Literacy (~2:20)

Reporting. First, Intro to AI for Digital Marketing, run by Galileo University and Davidson College. It covered the basics of AI in marketing, machine learning, predictive analytics and natural language processing, then generative AI, its risks, and how to write good prompts. I picked it for my digital literacy.

Responding. Honestly, a lot of the technical side went over my head at first, especially telling machine learning, neural networks and data mining apart. The prompt section changed that, though. I'd assumed these tools just "got" what you meant, but a lazy prompt gives a lazy answer. The risks part left me uneasy too: the bias, the deepfakes, the way AI can be confidently wrong.

Relating. This matters to me directly. Market research runs on this now, sentiment analysis from natural language processing, predicting behaviour from past data. Being comfortable with AI isn't a nice extra, it's expected for the roles I want.

Reasoning. Three things really shifted. First, building a proper prompt instead of firing off a question, setting the role, context and format, which changes the output completely. Second, understanding how you steer that output, things like temperature and the few-shot and chain-of-thought tricks. Third, getting more sceptical, checking anything AI gives me for accuracy and bias before I trust it.

Reconstructing. From here I want to keep a small library of prompts that work for me, make fact-checking a habit, and use this on a real research task once I finish the course.

Course 2: Digital Marketing Analytics → Critical & Creative Thinking (~2:20)

Reporting. Second, Digital Marketing Analytics from the University of Maryland. I'll be straight: I finished the free modules, which is as far as the free track goes, since the later machine-learning and big-data parts were behind payment. I took it to push my critical and creative thinking.

Responding. What threw me was reading a paid-search report in the course, for an allergy-free jewellery store. At first the click-through and cost-per-click figures were just a wall of numbers. It took me a while to notice one keyword, "Gold Earrings," had zero impressions because the bid was too low. What surprised me was that "more sales" isn't always the goal, one case tested getting reviews, not purchases, which changed how I think about success.

Relating. For a digital marketing trainee this is the job. Numbers alone tell you little, you have to read them, form a theory, and suggest a fix. That's what an employer actually pays for.

Reasoning. Three things stuck. First, breaking a paid-search problem into causes instead of guessing, is it the bid, the copy, or an unpopular keyword. Second, the creative side, proposing a testable idea rather than just describing the problem. Third, the theory, once I understood the null hypothesis and statistical significance I stopped trusting small bumps in the data and started asking whether a difference was even real.

Reconstructing. My next step is to reach the locked machine-learning material another way, and run a small test of my own so this becomes a habit, not just theory.

Course 3: Business Communications: The Basics → Communication (~2:20)

Reporting. Last, Business Communications from Doane University. It covered the communication model, listening and feedback, communication styles, and emotional intelligence. I took it for my communication skill, which I'll lean on heading towards a supervisor role.

Responding. A couple of things landed. One was that a listener keeps only about half of what they hear straight away, and a quarter two days later. Going through the barriers to listening, I recognised myself. On a group report this trimester, for another unit, I caught myself half-listening while a teammate talked through our recommendations, because in my head I was still on my own section, and I missed part of what she said. Uncomfortable to admit, but useful.

Relating. Communication is what turns analysis into something people act on. As a supervisor I'd spend most of my time presenting, giving feedback and managing people. The course's point that emotional intelligence matters most around management level is exactly where I'm headed.

Reasoning. Three concrete shifts. First, the communication styles, director versus supporter, taught me to read who I'm talking to and adjust. Second, my feedback got sharper once I swapped vague "blur words" for a specific point. Third, on the emotional side, self-awareness and self-regulation, catching a reaction as it happens and pausing before I respond, is something I now do on purpose.

Reconstructing. My plan is simple and I can start now: open feedback with a specific point, and ask for feedback after my own presentations so I can tell whether I'm improving.

Conclusion (~0:55)

Pulling the three together, they aren't really separate skills, they feed into each other. Digital literacy gives me the tools, analytics turns data into meaning, and communication gets it across to people who act on it.

That's the loop a marketer works through, and it fits the roles I'm aiming for, from analyst to supervisor.

In Assessment 1 I'd rated my digital and analytical skills as things I could only do with help. After these courses I'm clearly more confident and can take them on myself, with room still to grow.

From here I'll finish the locked analytics content, keep practising prompts and small tests, and use the feedback habits in every group I work in.

It ties into our automation-and-AI theme, where the smart move is stacking digital and people skills on what I already know. Thanks for watching.
