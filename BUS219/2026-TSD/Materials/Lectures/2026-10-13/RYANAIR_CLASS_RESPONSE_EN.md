# BUS219 · Ryanair cost leadership

Prepared on 13 October 2026 for the request at 03:55–04:42 in the new recording 6 from Downloads. The teacher allowed 15 minutes for research and asked how Ryanair achieves cost leadership, with a comparison to Southwest Airlines. This is a prepared response; delivery in class is not confirmed.

## Short answer to read aloud

Ryanair achieves cost leadership through several connected choices.

It mainly uses Boeing 737 aircraft, which simplifies training and maintenance. It negotiates competitive airport fees and uses direct routes, avoiding the costs of transferring passengers and baggage.

Direct online sales and check-in reduce distribution and handling costs. Optional services, such as baggage and seat selection, generate extra revenue while keeping the basic fare low.

Southwest uses similar principles, including a Boeing 737 fleet and efficient aircraft use. However, their models are not identical.

The main lesson is that low prices need a low-cost operating system behind them.

The factual basis for the Ryanair paragraph is its [FY2026 Form 20-F, printed pages 27–29](https://investor.ryanair.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/20F-Statement-2026.pdf). The Southwest comparison is supported by its [2025 Form 10-K, Cost Structure](https://investors.southwest.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000092380-26-000004/luv-20251231.htm). The concluding lesson is our course analysis.

## Useful follow-up answer

**How is this similar to Southwest?**

Both use fleet simplicity and operational efficiency. The comparison concerns operating principles; it does not mean identical airports, fees or service packages. Southwest's current annual report also describes assigned seating and bag fees for most fare products, so a blanket claim that Southwest has no baggage fees would be outdated. [Southwest 2025 Form 10-K, Fare Structure](https://investors.southwest.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000092380-26-000004/luv-20251231.htm).

Ryanair is a classroom example of cost leadership. Its whole group should not be described as exclusively Boeing: the report also lists Airbus aircraft in Lauda. The speech therefore says “mainly”. [Ryanair Form 20-F, Aircraft](https://investor.ryanair.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/20F-Statement-2026.pdf).
