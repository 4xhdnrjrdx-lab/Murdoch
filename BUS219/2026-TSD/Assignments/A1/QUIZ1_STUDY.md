## Globalisation and international institutions

Globalisation reduces barriers between countries and increases their economic links. It includes trade, investment, information, technology and cultural connections. It can increase choice, competition and growth, while creating adjustment costs for workers and industries.

| Term | Main meaning |
|---|---|
| Decoupling | Emerging economies depend more on their own sources of growth |
| Multipolar world | Both developed and emerging economies drive growth |
| IMF | Monetary stability, exchange rates and balance-of-payments support |
| World Bank | Development, poverty reduction and longer-term investment |
| WTO | Trade rules, negotiations and settlement of trade disputes |
| Market economy | Private ownership and market allocation |
| Command economy | Government directs production and allocation |
| Mixed economy | Market activity and government involvement coexist |

Institutional quality includes property rights, transparency, accountability, competition and control of corruption. Globalisation does not mean that every person or industry benefits equally.

## Trade theories and trade policy

**Absolute advantage** means producing a good with fewer resources. **Comparative advantage** means producing it at a lower opportunity cost. Trade can benefit a country even when another country has an absolute advantage in both goods.

Example invented for practice: Country A can make 10 cars or 20 computers with the same resources. Country B can make 6 cars or 18 computers. A gives up 2 computers per car; B gives up 3. A has comparative advantage in cars. B gives up one third of a car per computer; A gives up one half. B has comparative advantage in computers.

| Theory or policy | Recognition cue |
|---|---|
| Mercantilism | Accumulate national wealth through exports and a trade surplus |
| Heckscher–Ohlin | Export goods that use the country's abundant factors intensively |
| Porter's diamond | Factor conditions; demand conditions; supporting industries; firm strategy, structure and rivalry |
| Specific tariff | Fixed tax per unit imported |
| Ad valorem tariff | Tax calculated as a percentage of import value |
| Import quota | Limit on the quantity imported |
| Voluntary export restraint | Exporting country agrees to restrict exports |
| Domestic content requirement | A required share must be sourced or produced locally |
| Countertrade | Exchange linked to a commitment to buy goods in return |
| Embargo | Prohibition or severe restriction on trade |

Trade surplus: exports exceed imports. Trade deficit: imports exceed exports. FDI involves investment in foreign business assets and control; exporting by itself is not FDI. Tariffs and quotas can protect domestic producers but raise costs and invite retaliation.

## Regional integration and exchange rates

Each stage below adds to the preceding stage.

| Stage | What it adds |
|---|---|
| Free trade area | Members remove trade barriers between themselves; keep separate external tariffs |
| Customs union | Members use a common external tariff |
| Common market | Capital and labour can move between members |
| Economic union | Members coordinate economic policies and institutions |
| Political union | Members integrate political authority |

A common market therefore includes a common external tariff. A free trade area does not require one. Integration can increase market size, scale economies and investment, but can reduce policy independence and expose protected industries to competition.

The **current account** records trade in goods and services, income and transfers. The **financial account** records transactions in financial assets, including direct investment. A trade deficit is not the same as a deficit in every balance-of-payments component.

**Appreciation:** a currency becomes more valuable relative to another. **Depreciation:** it becomes less valuable. All else equal, a stronger domestic currency makes imports cheaper in domestic currency and exports more expensive for foreign buyers. A weaker currency tends to do the reverse. Read the quotation carefully: a fall from 8 to 6 units of domestic currency per dollar means the domestic currency has strengthened.

**Fixed exchange rate:** a currency is pegged. **Floating rate:** market forces determine the rate. **Managed float:** market pricing with intervention. **Dollarisation:** use of a foreign currency alongside or instead of a domestic one.

**Law of one price:** an identical good should have the same price after conversion under the model's assumptions. **PPP:** comparison of purchasing power across countries using a basket of goods. Transport costs, barriers and non-traded goods limit PPP. **Arbitrage:** buying in a cheaper market and selling in a dearer one.

## Culture

Culture is learned. Language, religion, values, customs, material culture, education, aesthetics and social institutions affect business. Backward translation checks whether a translated message retains its meaning.

| Hofstede dimension | Main contrast |
|---|---|
| Power distance | Acceptance of unequal power |
| Uncertainty avoidance | Need for predictability when situations are ambiguous |
| Individualism and collectivism | Individual priorities and group loyalty |
| Masculinity and femininity | Achievement and material success; care and quality of life |
| Long and short term orientation | Future persistence and investment; near-term results and traditions |
| Indulgence and restraint | Freedom to satisfy desires; control through social norms |

| Trompenaars dimension | Main contrast |
|---|---|
| Universalism and particularism | General rules; relationships and circumstances |
| Individualism and communitarianism | Individual; group responsibility |
| Neutral and emotional | Restrained; openly expressed emotion |
| Specific and diffuse | Separate; overlapping work and personal relationships |
| Achievement and ascription | Status earned through performance; status attached to identity or position |
| Sequential and synchronous time | One activity and strict schedules; parallel activities and flexible schedules |
| Inner and outer direction | Control the environment; adapt to it |

Do not confuse uncertainty avoidance with power distance, or achievement with ascription. Country examples in the slides are general models, not descriptions of every individual. Guanxi concerns relationship networks and reciprocal obligations in the course's discussion of China; inwa concerns harmony in unequal relationships in its discussion of Korea.

## Political and legal environment

**Macropolitical risk** affects business broadly in a country. **Micropolitical risk** targets particular firms or industries. Transfer risk concerns movements such as capital and dividend remittances; operational risk concerns running the business; ownership risk concerns control and property.

| Legal term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Civil law | Written legal codes are central |
| Common law | Judicial precedent and interpretation are central |
| Theocratic law | Religious teachings provide the legal foundation |
| Antitrust law | Protects competition and restricts collusion |
| Caveat emptor | Buyer bears responsibility for checking safety |
| Caveat venditor | Seller bears responsibility for safety or warnings |
| Litigation | Resolution through a court |
| Arbitration | Resolution by an agreed neutral decision maker |
| Patent | Protection of an invention |
| Trademark | Protection of a distinctive brand identifier |
| Copyright | Protection of original expressive work |

The legal-system categories describe the teaching models; actual jurisdictions can combine them. Expropriation concerns taking property; a restriction on transferring profits is a transfer risk. Integrative responses to political risk include local relationships, employment, sourcing and joint ventures. Protective responses can include keeping key activities elsewhere and spreading production across countries.

## Ethics and corporate responsibility

Ethics concerns right and wrong conduct. Integrity means acting consistently with moral principles. A legal action can still raise ethical concerns.

**Imperative principle:** act according to what is right. **Utilitarian principle:** consider the greatest overall good. **Generalisation argument:** consider the consequences if everyone acted in the same way.

CSR considers the company's obligations to people and places affected by its activities. It includes employees, customers, owners, suppliers and the environment. Internal controls improve the reliability of records and help prevent or detect errors and fraud. The FCPA discussion has two themes: anti-bribery and accounting/internal controls. SOX concerns corporate reporting, accountability and related controls.

## Entry strategies and foreign investment

| Entry mode | Key distinction |
|---|---|
| Exporting | Sell goods across borders without building a foreign operation |
| Licensing | Permit another firm to use rights or technology for a fee |
| Franchising | Provide a brand and operating format with associated standards |
| Strategic alliance | Cooperate; no separate jointly owned entity in this course's classification |
| Joint venture | Create or operate a jointly owned business and share risk |
| Acquisition | Buy an existing foreign business |
| Greenfield investment | Build a new foreign operation |
| Wholly owned subsidiary | The parent owns the foreign subsidiary completely |

A wholly owned subsidiary can be established through acquisition or greenfield investment. The session focuses on greenfield subsidiaries, but the concepts are not identical. In the slides, exporting, licensing and franchising use relatively little parent capital; alliances and joint ventures share risk; acquisitions and wholly owned operations require greater commitment.

MNE motives: increase revenue, reduce costs and diversify risk. **Dunning OLI:** Ownership advantages, Location advantages and Internalisation advantages. Ownership means firm-specific strengths; location means benefits of a particular country; internalisation means benefits from keeping activities within the firm.

## Strategy and control

Mission describes the organisation's purpose; vision describes its desired future; goals state intended outcomes; values guide conduct. Shareholders are owners. Stakeholders also include other affected groups such as employees, customers, suppliers and communities.

| International strategy | Cost reduction pressure | Local responsiveness pressure |
|---|---|---|
| International | Low | Low |
| Multidomestic | Low | High |
| Global | High | Low |
| Transnational | High | High |

Entry mode and international strategy answer different questions. A joint venture is an entry mode, not one of the four strategies above. Cost leadership requires low operating costs; a low selling price alone does not establish it.

**Planning horizons in Session 8:** operational, less than one year; tactical, one to three years; strategic, five or more years. Use the course's definitions when a question invokes them.

**Miles and Snow:** prospectors seek new opportunities; defenders protect existing operations; analysers combine stable operations with selected new opportunities; reactors respond to competitors without a consistent proactive approach.

**Tacit knowledge** is difficult to express; **explicit knowledge** can be written or codified. **Absorptive capacity** is the ability to recognise, absorb and apply new knowledge.

**Bureaucratic control:** rules and procedures. **Interpersonal control:** personal contact. **Output control:** results measured against goals. Supplementary slides distinguish direct face-to-face control from indirect written reports. Organisational culture comprises shared norms and values; a strong culture can support change or resist it. Six Sigma targets about 3.4 defects per million opportunities in the conventional model.

| Eiffel Tower | Hierarchical | Tasks |
| Guided Missile | Egalitarian | Tasks |
| Incubator | Egalitarian | People and self-development |

## Errors and dated wording in the slides

- Session 1 slide 15 blends the WTO with its predecessor. GATT operated from 1948; the WTO was established on 1 January 1995. [WTO explanation](https://www.wto.org/english/thewto_e/whatis_e/tif_e/utw_chap1_e.pdf).
- Session 3 slide 12 associates adoption of the euro with 1992. The euro launched in 1999; cash followed in 2002. [ECB history](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/intro/html/index.en.html).
- Session 6 slide 12 says FCPA of 1997. The Act dates to 1977. [US Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/criminal/criminal-fraud/foreign-corrupt-practices-act).

These factual dates were checked on 18 October 2026. If a question explicitly quotes an inconsistent slide, retain the exact wording and options before resolving the conflict. Older country statistics and broad cultural examples should not be silently treated as current facts.

## Reading each question

Read the full stem and every option. Identify **NOT**, **EXCEPT**, **least**, **most**, **always** and **only**. Match the defining feature rather than one familiar word. For an exchange-rate calculation, write the units first. Where two choices look plausible, identify the condition that makes one the better answer. Keep the complete options because a letter alone can be misleading if order changes.
