I'm Faiaz.
I chose the article Shopper AI by Kennedy and co-authors, from twenty twenty-six.
My example is Carrefour.

The study uses design science.
The authors built a way to compare shopping AI.
They used earlier studies, expert interviews, patents and real AI tools.
Three hundred and twelve people helped check it.

This system is called a taxonomy.
It has fourteen features in two groups.
Capabilities are what AI can do, such as using information and protecting data.
Parasocial skills are how AI creates a feeling of human contact.
AI can sound caring but has no real feelings.

Both groups can affect the shopping experience.
Advice that fits your needs may require more personal data.

I think the study helps shops find problems.
For example, AI may understand you but suggest a product the shop does not have.

One strength is the use of several sources.
They looked at both the technology and the customer.
But the study mainly focuses on the USA.
We need to test these ideas with UAE customers.
Also, checking the taxonomy does not prove that AI makes customers happier or increases sales.

In twenty twenty-one, Majid Al Futtaim opened Carrefour City Plus in Dubai.
It used AI so shoppers could skip the normal checkout.

In France, Carrefour announced Hopla in twenty twenty-three.
This chatbot could suggest food based on a budget and food preferences.
They do not prove that customers were happier, or that every UAE store has these tools.

My idea for Carrefour UAE is an AI food shopping assistant.
This is a proposal, not a service I have tested.

A customer asks for a vegetarian dinner for two, under fifty dirhams.
The AI checks today's prices and what is in stock.
If a product is missing, it explains another option and asks the customer first.
If the wrong item arrives, the customer can speak to a staff member.

This links knowledge with personalisation.
The customer keeps control.

I would test this with a small group first.
Customers should choose what personal data to share.
Paid product suggestions should be clearly marked.

I would compare the AI with normal product search.
Do shoppers stay within their budget?
Can they complete their basket?
Does the AI make fewer mistakes?

For me, friendly AI is not enough.
Carrefour should give useful advice and leave customers in control.
