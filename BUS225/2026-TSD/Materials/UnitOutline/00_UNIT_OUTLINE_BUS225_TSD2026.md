## 1 Unit Details

Welcome to:

**BUS225**

**Services Marketing**

### 1.1 Unit Description

The marketing of services and tangible goods differs, requiring different strategies to be employed in marketing service organisations. Students will analyse and critically examine services marketing together with relationship and retail marketing. They will be asked to reflect on contemporary marketing trends and insight. Students will evaluate the activities of the service strategy of organisations, including retail, and the broader implications it presents in areas of ethical conduct and varying customer expectations or situations.

### 1.2 Learning Outcomes

This unit enables you to demonstrate your achievement of the following unit learning outcomes.

## 3 Overall design and expectations

### 3.1 Approach to learning and teaching

This unit applies the core characteristics of services as a framework to explore strategies for the marketing of services. This is achieved by examining the application of marketing concepts, models and frameworks in services using practical examples and experiences. This unit also draws on topics in relationship management and retailing as key elements of services. Students will engage in experience-based exercises and cases for their learning activities.

This unit is delivered in a blended learning format, which requires you to review material presented on the unit website in addition to attendance at the university. You are expected to read and understand all textbook chapters relevant to the workshops. This material includes workshop questions. You should be familiar with this material and complete the workshop questions prior to attending the workshops.

There are 12 weeks, with a 2-hour session each week.

Students are expected to work on their workshop materials, attend every workshop, and to engage and communicate consistently and effectively with their groups on their group projects. Students also have to read their textbooks.

### 3.2 Expected time commitment for studying this unit

As this is a 3-credit point unit, we expect you to spend on average 10 hours per week for the total weeks of this teaching period (or 150 hours overall) working on this unit. Generally speaking, students will benefit from spending more time rather than less. However, students should not dedicate more than a maximum of 150 hours to this unit overall.

### 5.2 Assessment information

#### 5.2.1 Assessment 1: Service Expert Video Presentation (Individual) 30%

**ASSESSMENT OVERVIEW**

You are a services marketing expert YouTuber who often applies new thinking for service organisations through research insights. You are using your video presentation, based on evidence, to provide content that helps service industries stay up to date with new knowledge in the field.

You will be provided with two key service-related journals and you are to pick one only. You are required to understand and apply the content from one of the journals, and to add your expert perspectives on the topic. In your video, you can include major points of the article, recommendations, and contemporary trends or success and failure examples from service industries and major brands.

The video is expected to attract an audience such as business owners and people who are keen to learn about new research in the field that can benefit their work. The video will be assessed on how well the research paper is understood and critiqued, its application to the service industry, and the video's professionalism, engagement and creativity. You can include images and visuals that may be relevant to engage viewers.

The video presentation should not be more than 5 minutes. You have to be visible and seen speaking in the video in person, or you will not be marked on the presentation component and will receive zero marks for it. You can use tools such as Canva to develop the video.

It is your responsibility to find a suitable platform and learn how to develop a video. You are to submit a link to your video via LMS.

**WHERE TO SUBMIT**

To submit a video link via LMS. You may choose to do so by uploading your video to YouTube.

**ASSESSMENT CRITERIA**

You will be assessed on these key criteria:

- Your understanding and critique of the journal article.
- The application of the article to a service organisation.
- Creativity in your visuals and professional skill.
- Professionalism and technical quality of the presentation.
- Audience engagement throughout the presentation.

The assessment includes a moderation process to ensure reliable, just, and fair outcomes.

Your assessment outcome will be provided to you via LMS grades.

**ESSENTIAL ADDITIONAL INFORMATION**

Students have to synthesise the literature and be able to apply local business examples for the audience, providing expert perspectives and recommendations. The video should be professional, engaging and creative.

Students can consult with their local affiliate lecturer about their learning. Feedback on the assessment is provided through the return of the marking and rubric.

See myMurdoch Learning for further details, such as instructions, communication, resources, guides, exemplars.

#### 5.2.2 Assessment 2: Reflective Essay and Service Blueprint (Group) 40%

**ASSESSMENT OVERVIEW**

The reflective journal and service blueprint is to be completed in groups of 3 to 4 students. Students will be required to prepare a reflective essay on specific contemporary services and retail experiences and trends involving robotics, and the ethical implications involved for employees and consumers. In Part B, students have to create and include a service blueprint for the organisation with the introduction of robotics. Students have to work on a retailing organisation.

It is important that each group explains how the relevant services marketing theory or practice can be applied to the selected local service organisation. The group will receive a Fail grade for the particular report if it does not explain the relevance and practical application of the services marketing theory to the selected service organisation.

**Component A, Service Blueprint (10%).** This part of the assignment requires you to form groups of up to 3 to 4 people in your workshop or tutorial class and develop a service blueprint for a service company of your choice. The lecturer makes the final decision on groupings and organisation choice. Each group has to submit a service organisation profile of the chosen service organisation to their tutor by Workshop 3.

For the chosen service organisation, students have to create and include a service blueprint for a service organisation that is a retailer, and reflect on their customer journey with the introduction, either current or potential, of robotics or artificial intelligence. The blueprint will be developed through an in-class activity discussion of their customer journey. The service blueprint is a flowchart that provides a service organisation with the means of managing and controlling individual parts of the service delivery system, identifying weak points and opportunities for improving or enhancing the efficiency and productivity of the system, and preventing service failures. Students have to include a key summary of implications and recommendations developed based on the blueprint, to be included in a section of the reflective essay.

The key components of a service blueprint are customer actions and the line of external interaction; frontstage, or visible, customer contact employee action and the line of visibility; backstage, or invisible, contact employee action and the line of internal interaction; support processes; and physical evidence.

**Component B, Reflective Journal Report (30%).** This part of the assignment requires members of the group to reflect on particular retail experiences and trends. Choose a retailer that has a major service component involving interactions with customers. A reflective journal is a personalised account of how learning in this unit can be applied to any service and retail interactions students may encounter in the future, and outside the classroom.

The questions to reflect upon are: first, referencing what you have learned in this course and supporting academic literature, outline the importance and trends in the implementation of robotics and intelligent service systems in the particular service industry, analysing an industry close to the one in your blueprinting. Second, reflect on the role of robotics and intelligent systems in the firm and how it impacts the customer journey and ethical implications for various stakeholders, using your service blueprint and supporting material to analyse the customer journey. Third, based on the service blueprint and overall analysis, provide key insights and recommendations for the retail service company.

The combined word limit for the Service Blueprint and Reflective Journal is 2,000 words, excluding references.

The reflective journal and service blueprint are group assignments, so students should plan and work together to ensure an equitable workload. Students should utilise the strengths of individual group members and actively resolve potential group conflict prior to submitting the assignment.

**WHERE TO SUBMIT**

Both components, A and B, are to be submitted via LMS by one member of the group.

**ASSESSMENT CRITERIA**

You will be assessed on these key criteria:

- Service blueprint structure and completeness.
- Identification of service failures and improvement opportunities.
- Presentation and clarity of the service blueprint.
- Analysis of robotics and intelligent systems in the service industry.
- Reflection on the customer journey.
- Ethical implications for stakeholders.
- Critical analysis and recommendations for the retailer.
- Presentation, clarity, referencing and professional skill.

The assessment includes a moderation process to ensure reliable, just, and fair outcomes.

The final grade will automatically take into account a peer evaluation conducted by all team members using LMS scoring. Your assessment outcome will be provided to you via LMS grades.

**ESSENTIAL ADDITIONAL INFORMATION**

Evidence of individual contribution. To document each member's contribution, each group member must be allocated and write a distinct component of the report and email it to all group members as evidence of their contribution.

Students can request activation of a peer review evaluation. All group members are expected to attend classes and meetings and contribute effectively to earn a fair grade. Based on peer evaluations and evidence of contribution, for example emailed work, the marker will decide whether to moderate up to 50% of the final mark. A mark of 0 may be awarded where there is no evidence of work or meeting participation.

Working in a team helps students develop essential professional skills such as collaboration, communication, problem-solving and adaptability. Each team member brings unique insights, knowledge and experiences, which can enhance the depth and quality of the reflective journal and service blueprint.

Students have to integrate the literature and provide a clear understanding of the topic. The blueprint and essay reflections should be well thought through and detailed, and present innovative insights.

Students can consult with their local affiliate lecturer about their learning. Feedback is given initially via the submission of the service organisation profile and group charter early in the trimester. Assessment feedback is provided through the return of the marking and rubric.

See myMurdoch Learning for further details, such as instructions, communication, resources, guides, exemplars.

The final exam may consist of multiple-choice, discussion questions, or a case study, or a combination of these. Further details will be provided towards the end of the trimester. The exam has been developed to test your understanding of the key topics in BUS225 Services Marketing. The final exam contributes 30% to the total marks for this unit. A formal briefing on the final exam will be provided in the final session of the unit. All materials in this unit are examinable.

Exam timetables are published through MyInfo. Internal students, and many external students, will be required to come on campus at South Street to sit the exam. **WHERE TO SUBMIT**

Exams are conducted online or face-to-face, depending on conditions.

**ASSESSMENT CRITERIA**

The assessment includes a moderation process to ensure reliable, just, and fair outcomes.
