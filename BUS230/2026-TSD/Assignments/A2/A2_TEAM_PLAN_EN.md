This proposed allocation gives each member a presentation section, five academic sources to find, and one practical contribution to the modified tool. Confirm the roles, shared case, presentation date and uploader together.

## Our model and the assignment

We will use **Kotter's original eight-stage model from Leading Change (1996)**. Kotter is explicitly included in Unit Outline section 5.2.2, Topic 7 and the course toolkit list, so it is suitable for this assignment. Keep the original model separate from Kotter's later Accelerators and from our own proposed modifications.

Our task is to critically analyse the model's limitations, develop a modified tool that addresses them, and deliver a formal simulated training session for managers. Managers should leave knowing how to use our tool. Each limitation we identify needs a clear response in the modified version.

Working title: **Using a Modified Kotter Model for Organisational Change**.

Use one shared case throughout. Suggested case for agreement: a fictional retail chain introducing a digital inventory system. Label the case as a training scenario and distinguish assumptions from published evidence.

## Faiaz Modified model and manager guidance

**Presentation: slides 13-20, 7 minutes 30 seconds.**

Combine the four members' contributions into one usable tool. Show a recognisable connection to the original eight stages and identify exactly which mechanisms or rules we change. Each part needs inputs, manager actions, an owner, a decision and a way to check the result.

**Your contribution to the modified tool:** rules for continuing, revising or pausing the change. After limited testing, the manager reviews agreed indicators and concerns before deciding what to do. If assumptions change, explain when to revisit diagnosis, the vision or implementation, and how to record the reason. Justify these specific rules; piloting and feedback alone are not inventions of our group.

Check alignment between strategy, processes, training, authority and rewards. Specify organisational roles such as sponsor, change lead, line manager and employee representatives. Keep these separate from the student roles in this plan.

Explain required resources and practical risks, including delays, paperwork and superficial participation. Identify limitations the tool cannot fully resolve. Proposed benefits should be evaluated rather than presented as proven results.

**Materials to read:** Topics 5 and 6 Podcast and Workshop slides and transcripts; Topic 7 on Stouten, the process perspective and Change Kaleidoscope; Mazin's and Aram's completed contributions. Read full academic texts supporting the design choices.

**Slide content:** 13 purpose of modification; 14 overall diagram; 15 initial diagnosis; 16 participation and responses; 17 continue/revise/pause rules; 18 follow-up evaluation; 19 actions, roles and resources; 20 evidence, feasibility, risks and boundaries.

**Additional responsibility:** consistent terminology and a clear link between the analysis, modified tool and demonstration.

## Beryl Application and manager training

**Presentation: slides 21-27, 8 minutes, including the exercise and debrief.**

Demonstrate the tool using the same case. Show what information a manager gathers, whose views are considered, the decision taken and what gets revised. Include an employee concern that leads to a meaningful adjustment.

**Your contribution to the modified tool:** a follow-up evaluation form. Specify the owner, review dates, a work outcome indicator, an indicator of actual adoption and a trigger for reassessment. A 30/60/90-day schedule is a possible design choice, not a university requirement. Send the form to Faiaz.

Prepare a communication plan covering audience, message, channel, feedback, owner and response. Explain why the approach fits the situation and its resource limitations.

Run a **two-minute exercise**: give a short scenario, ask managers to choose continue, revise or pause and state what evidence they need, then explain the answer using our tool. Check the learning objectives and conclude. Add a short reflection after the team has actually completed the work. Do not invent audience responses, results or experience.

**Materials to read:** all Topic 8 slides and transcript; Topic 2 on readiness; Topic 3 on constructive resistance; Topic 7 on sustaining and evaluating change; Faiaz's final model.

**Slide content:** 21 case application; 22 decision justification; 23 communication; 24 worked demonstration; 25 exercise; 26 debrief and learning check; 27 conclusions and recommendations.

**Additional responsibility:** coordinate the timed rehearsal and check the exercise. Everyone rehearses their own section and transition.

## Developing the tool together

The following are proposed research directions. Verify them against the original book and academic evidence before treating them as confirmed limitations.

| Research direction | Proposed practical contribution | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Context, readiness and previous change experiences | Diagnosis form linking the answers to the manager's approach | Mazin |
| Participation and constructive criticism | Concern log with responsibility, decisions and responses | Aram |
| Changing assumptions and implementation decisions | Conditions for continuing, revising or pausing, with a documented reason | Faiaz |
| Sustaining results while allowing further adaptation | Named owner, review schedule, indicators and reassessment triggers | Beryl |

Check the extent of diagnosis already provided by Kotter, existing coalition and empowerment provisions, flexibility within the stages, and what cultural anchoring already includes. Every limitation in the analysis must receive an explicit response in the modified tool. If the evidence changes a claim, update the analysis, design and exercise together.

The indicators explain how managers could evaluate our proposal. They are not results we have already achieved or proof that the modified model is superior.

## Research and references

Each member finds **five distinct quality academic sources** relevant to their section. Aram records author and year, DOI or library link, the passage read, the supported claim and the slide number. Members may use each other's sources, but each publication is counted once. Aim for **20 unique academic sources actually used**. Course slides and ordinary websites do not replace this collection.

The Unit Outline requires 15-20 academic references, with 20 as the maximum and websites excluded from that count. The HD rubric says 20+. Choosing exactly 20 respects the Outline's limit. The number alone does not guarantee a high mark. Use relevant recent research alongside essential original works.

Use **Chicago 17 author-date** as the working style, following the explicit A2 instruction. The Outline's example closing slide also mentions APA. Ask the lecturer to clarify this inconsistency before finalisation and keep one consistent style.

### Starting points for research

- Kotter, John P. 1996. Leading Change. Obtain and check the original book. The 1995 article can help with comparison but does not replace it.
- Sveningsson and Sörgärde. 2023. Managing Change in Organizations, 2nd edition. This is the textbook listed in the Unit Outline.
- Appelbaum et al. 2012. Back to the future: revisiting Kotter's 1996 change model. DOI: 10.1108/02621711211253231.
- Hughes. 2016. Leading changes: Why transformation explanations fail. DOI: 10.1177/1742715015571393.
- Stouten, Rousseau and De Cremer. 2018. Successful Organizational Change: Integrating the Management Practice and Scholarly Literatures. DOI: 10.5465/annals.2016.0095.

These are starting points, not a completed bibliography. Read full texts before using detailed arguments; publisher abstracts alone do not establish every proposed limitation.
