4. Required Slide Structure

SLIDE 19 — Why ADKAR Scores Are Not Enough

Time: 1 minute

Title: “Beyond the ADKAR Score”

Explain the fourth limitation of the standalone ADKAR model:

A high self-reported score does not necessarily prove actual workplace performance.

Visual concept:

Show three connected elements:

SELF-REPORT → TASK DEMONSTRATION → WORKING CONDITIONS

Highlight the rule:

“If evidence conflicts, readiness is NOT YET VERIFIED.”

Explain that this is our team’s proposed Evidence Disagreement Rule, not an official Prosci feature.

SLIDE 20 — Knowledge vs Ability

Time: 1 minute

Title: “Knowing Is Not the Same as Doing”

Create a strong visual comparison between Knowledge and Ability.

Left side:

KNOWLEDGE

* Understands the procedure
* Can explain the steps

Right side:

ABILITY

* Performs the task correctly
* Uses real equipment
* Works under realistic conditions

Use the fictional Cedar Retail inventory system as the example.

Include the takeaway:

“Knowledge must be explained. Ability must be demonstrated.”

SLIDE 21 — Communication and Audience Preparation

Time: 45 seconds

Title: “Now It’s Your Turn!”

This slide introduces the interactive activity.

Start with a brief message about the importance of two-way communication, employee feedback, and psychological safety.

Then transition into the workshop.

Display:

“Welcome to Cedar Retail Management Training”

“Your team is now responsible for making a management decision.”

Use a visually engaging corporate workshop layout.

Speaker instruction:

Invite the volunteer student group to participate from their seats.

Do not require participants to move to the front of the classroom.

SLIDE 22 — INTERACTIVE CHALLENGE

Time: 2 minutes

Title: “You Are the Manager”

This is the most important interactive slide.

Design it as a professional decision-making simulation.

Scenario:

Cedar Retail is introducing a digital inventory system.

An evening-shift employee understands the new procedure, but:

1. The scanner is unreliable.
2. There is no protected practice time.
3. The employee fears unfair penalties for previous stock errors.

The manager suggests another motivational talk.

Display the main question prominently:

“WHAT WOULD YOUR TEAM DECIDE?”

Show three large decision cards:

A — CONTINUE (green)

B — REVISE (amber)

C — PAUSE (red)

Underneath:

“30 seconds to discuss. Choose one decision and explain why.”

Interactive sequence:

1. Presenter introduces the scenario.
2. Volunteer group discusses for 30 seconds.
3. Presenter asks the group to choose A, B, or C.
4. One participant explains the decision.
5. Presenter thanks the participants and moves to the next slide.

Design the decision cards to look like interactive buttons.

If technically feasible, make the buttons clickable in PowerPoint. Otherwise, use simple click-to-reveal animations or a straightforward slide transition.

Do not require internet access or external polling applications.

SLIDE 23 — Reveal the Correct Decision

Time: 1 minute 15 seconds

Title: “The Evidence-Based Decision”

Reveal the recommended answer:

C — PAUSE THE AFFECTED TASK

Important: This does not mean stopping the entire organisational change project.

Explain the four management actions:

1. IT Lead — Provide reliable scanner access.
2. Store Manager — Arrange protected practice time.
3. Operations Sponsor — Review the disputed penalty policy and communicate a reasoned decision.
4. Manager — Observe a realistic task to verify Ability after the conditions are addressed.

Use a visual decision flow or four professional action cards.

Highlight:

“A motivational talk alone cannot solve missing organisational conditions.”

Also explain that revising the penalty policy is necessary, but the affected task should remain paused until its essential prerequisites are met.

SLIDE 24 — Final Conclusion

Time: 1 minute

Title: “From ADKAR Scores to Better Decisions”

Summarise the four key principles:

1. Identify individual ADKAR barriers.
2. Check organisational conditions.
3. Listen and respond to employee concerns.
4. Verify real workplace performance.

Create a visually powerful final slide.

Main closing statement:

“DON’T ASSUME READINESS. VERIFY IT.”

Add:

“Thank you — Questions?”

This slide should provide a strong conclusion to the entire group presentation, not just my section.
