# Beryl: BUS230 Assessment 2 speaker script

Prepared 14 October 2026. Draft for rehearsal, with team approval still unknown.

## Slide 7: L1: shared working conditions (70 seconds)

The first limitation concerns shared working conditions. Ability can include external constraints, so we are not arguing that ADKAR ignores resources. The narrower gap is that the five outcome labels do not provide a complete shared record of conditions, owners, and prerequisites across roles.

Weiner distinguishes organisational readiness from individual readiness. Rafferty and colleagues also examine readiness at individual, group, and organisational levels. Oreg and colleagues show that responses to change involve both personal and contextual factors. These works support examining the wider setting. They do not test our record.

At Cedar Retail, more instruction cannot repair a scanner or protect time in the roster. The manager must connect those conditions to the task and assign them to people with authority. Our first addition makes that ownership and prerequisite explicit.

Sources: Weiner 2009; Rafferty, Jimmieson, and Armenakis 2013; Oreg, Vakola, and Armenakis 2011

## Slide 8: L2: a reasonable objection (75 seconds)

The second limitation concerns reasonable objections. A colleague can support accurate stock records while disagreeing with a penalty rule. Piderit describes responses to change as multidimensional and potentially ambivalent. Oreg's research connects resistance with context as well as personal characteristics. The recent review by Oreg and Sverdlik also draws attention to ambivalence and timing.

A low Desire score therefore does not tell the manager whether the proposed requirement is fair or workable. Ford and colleagues argue that change agents can contribute to resistance through their own actions and inactions. This supports treating a concern as information rather than automatically as a problem inside the employee.

We retain the Desire conversation, but add an accountable sponsor response. The sponsor must explain whether to retain, revise, or stop the disputed requirement. This rule does not guarantee agreement or remove unequal power.

Sources: Piderit 2000; Oreg 2006; Ford, Ford, and D'Amelio 2008; Oreg and Sverdlik 2026

## Slide 9: L3: dependencies across roles (70 seconds)

The third limitation concerns the order of actions across roles. The earliest individual barrier helps target support for the colleague. It does not, by itself, provide a project schedule for IT, the store manager, and the sponsor.

Burke and Litwin describe links between organisational systems, management practices, motivation, and performance. Herold and colleagues investigate how contextual and personal factors interact in commitment to change. Pettigrew and colleagues emphasise context, levels of analysis, time, and process. Together, these perspectives justify making dependencies visible.

In the scenario, the manager can discuss the colleague's concern while IT works on access. Those activities can happen in parallel. The affected receiving task still pauses if an essential condition is missing. We keep personal prerequisites and add a separate rule for coordinating organisational work.

Sources: Burke and Litwin 1992; Herold, Fedor, and Caldwell 2007; Pettigrew, Woodman, and Cameron 2001; BUS230 Topic 7

## Slide 10: L4 and the analysis summary (70 seconds)

The fourth limitation concerns evidence. A brief score is a useful snapshot, but it cannot automatically prove workplace performance. ADKAR already distinguishes Knowledge from Ability and supports observation. Our remaining question is what the manager should do when self-report, task performance, and working conditions disagree.

Holt and colleagues developed a multidimensional readiness instrument through a systematic process. Jo and Hong's newer scale also involves substantial development and testing. Their work reminds us that a readiness judgement needs a clear construct and appropriate evidence. Our classroom record has not undergone that validation.

The table brings all four bounded gaps together. Each has a specific addition, and each addition has a limit. We are examining a use case, not claiming four proven defects in every application of ADKAR.

Sources: Holt et al. 2007; Jo and Hong 2023; Prosci n.d.-d

## Slide 11: Modified tool overview (65 seconds)

Our modified tool is a digital decision record attached to the individual ADKAR assessment. We retain the five outcomes and the support they guide. Around that baseline, we add four connected records: working conditions, a response to a challenge, cross-role dependencies, and an evidence disagreement rule.

The output is a specific task decision. It names what can continue, what needs revision, and what must pause. It also names the people responsible and the evidence needed for review.

This is a teaching adaptation informed by academic work. It is not an official Prosci product or a replacement for the full methodology. Its contribution is the explicit connection between the records and the decision. The ideas of dialogue, learning, and reassessment already exist, so we do not present those alone as new.

Sources: Prosci n.d.-a, n.d.-b, n.d.-c, n.d.-d; design proposal

## Slide 12: Conditions and challenge records (70 seconds)

Here are the first two records filled with our fictional case. The IT lead owns reliable scanner access. The store manager owns protected practice time. Each entry needs completion evidence rather than a promise that someone will fix it.

The concern record preserves the colleague's meaning: inherited errors may reduce their performance rating. It asks for evidence and an alternative, such as separating inherited errors from new receiving mistakes. The operations sponsor owns the response because that role controls the policy.

For demonstration, the record requests a decision before the affected task goes live. We have not invented a sponsor approval. A staff representative can check that the concern is represented accurately, without implying everyone consents. Faiaz will now connect these records to the actions and the final decision.

Sources: Ford, Ford, and D'Amelio 2008; design proposal; fictional entries
