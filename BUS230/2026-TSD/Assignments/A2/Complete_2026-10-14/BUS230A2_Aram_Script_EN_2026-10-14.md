# Aram: BUS230 Assessment 2 speaker script

Prepared 14 October 2026. Draft for rehearsal, with team approval still unknown.

## Slide 19: Research and benchmarking (40 seconds)

Our benchmark combines existing Prosci provision with change research. Stouten and colleagues connect practitioner models with scholarly evidence. Errida and Lotfi review change models and apply success factors in a construction-company case.

We use these works to justify examining diagnosis, enabling conditions, participation, and review. Their settings and methods also limit the conclusion. Neither source tests our retail record. They support the design rationale without establishing the adaptation's effectiveness.

Sources: Stouten, Rousseau, and De Cremer 2018; Errida and Lotfi 2021; Rafferty, Jimmieson, and Armenakis 2013

## Slide 20: Evidence disagreement rule (55 seconds)

The evidence rule keeps self-report, task performance, and conditions separate. The colleague understands the procedure, but the task has not been observed with usable inputs. Ability remains not yet verified.

Baldwin and Ford explain training transfer as application and maintenance at work. Edmondson links psychological safety with learning behaviour. Kluger and DeNisi show that feedback effects vary. These findings support representative, safe practice and careful feedback.

The manager investigates disagreements rather than averaging them into a pass. This rule has not been validated as a readiness measure.

Sources: Baldwin and Ford 1988; Edmondson 1999; Kluger and DeNisi 1996; Holt et al. 2007; Jo and Hong 2023

## Slide 21: Worked case: a different decision (40 seconds)

The initial suggestion is another motivational talk followed by launch. The same scenario instead calls for a pause to the affected task.

IT verifies access, the manager protects practice time, and the sponsor reviews the rating rule. The manager then observes realistic practice and records actual evidence.

This worked example compares decisions, not business outcomes. Individual support and reinforcement continue.

Sources: Fictional application; Prosci n.d.-d; design proposal

## Slide 22: What the research does and does not establish (40 seconds)

ADKAR identifies individual support needs. Wider Prosci already provides organisational process and sponsorship. Research adds perspectives on context, voice, learning, and evidence.

Our record makes owners and decision reasons explicit. It draws on those existing ideas. Nilsen helps distinguish a guide to action from an approach to evaluation.

The next step is a local usability check, followed by suitable evaluation before wider use. The classroom exercise can check understanding, but cannot establish business impact.

Sources: Nilsen 2015; Stouten, Rousseau, and De Cremer 2018; Prosci n.d.-b, n.d.-c

## Slide 23: Manager workflow (30 seconds)

The manager defines a task, diagnoses the person's support needs, and connects them to conditions and responsibilities. They then verify evidence, record a decision, and set a review trigger.

This workflow keeps both individual support and organisational responsibility visible. Each decision stays linked to the affected task and its evidence.

Sources: Design synthesis; BUS230 Topic 7

## Slide 24: Communication and employee voice (55 seconds)

Colleagues on both shifts receive an explanation, practice, and a chance to question the design. IT and the manager confirm dependencies. The sponsor explains the policy decision.

Lewis examines information-giving and input-seeking channels. Morrison reviews employee voice and silence. Oreg and Sverdlik highlight context and timing. These works support asking how communication affects a decision.

Following Topic 8's underscore and explore approach, the manager explains the purpose and invites useful questions. An invitation alone does not guarantee meaningful participation. The record therefore includes a response owner.

Sources: Lewis 1999; Morrison 2023; Oreg and Sverdlik 2026; BUS230 Topic 8

## Slide 25: Your manager decision (35 seconds)

Here is the manager scenario. An evening colleague understands stock receiving, but lacks reliable scanner access and protected practice time. The colleague is also worried about penalties for inherited errors.

Would you continue, revise, or pause the affected task, and why?

[Read the scenario for about 20 seconds. Allow up to 15 seconds for thinking or a brief oral answer. Move to the prepared explanation even if the lecturer does not answer.]

Sources: Fictional training exercise

## Slide 26: Worked answer and learning check (85 seconds)

The prepared answer is to pause the affected receiving task until reliable access and protected practice time are usable. IT owns access. The store manager owns the time. The sponsor should review the rating rule and explain whether it is retained, revised, or stopped.

After the essential inputs are addressed, the manager observes a realistic receiving task and records the actual evidence. Another motivational talk alone cannot resolve the missing inputs. The manager still continues the individual Desire conversation and relevant learning support.

The learning check is this: why does understanding the procedure fail to prove Ability here? Because Knowledge describes understanding, while Ability requires performance in the relevant conditions. The second check is to name who must answer the policy concern. That is the sponsor, with a reasoned response.

[Point to each owner and the evidence entry. Together, slides 25 and 26 form a two-minute demonstration. No audience reply is required.]

Sources: Prosci n.d.-a; fictional application of the proposed rules

## Slide 27: Conclusion and next action (40 seconds)

The manager can now identify an individual barrier, connect it to organisational conditions, and explain a task decision using evidence.

The design lesson is that a clear checklist still needs judgement about context and responsibility. The next action is to test the record on one task, inspect usability, and review actual evidence. Benefits remain proposals to evaluate. Continued support and the adaptation's limits stay visible.

Sources: Design reflection; BUS230 Topic 7

## Slide 28: Questions and source acknowledgements (90 seconds)

Thank you. We welcome your questions.

[Allow up to 90 seconds for questions. If there are no questions, use the short recap below and allow time to review the decision. Display reference slides 29 and 30 when relevant. Keep the total session within 29 minutes and 30 seconds.]

The key practical point is to identify the affected task, name the people who control its conditions, and verify suitable evidence before continuing. A concern may call for a design response as well as individual support. Our record is a teaching proposal that needs local testing. It credits the existing Prosci practices and does not establish business results.

[Decision check after the recap if there are no questions]

We can also check how the decision would change. If scanner access becomes usable but practice time is still missing, the affected task stays paused. If both conditions become usable, the manager still needs suitable task evidence and a reasoned response to the concern. A completed action is evidence for that action, not proof of readiness everywhere.

Continue applies to the specified task when its prerequisites and evidence are acceptable. Revise addresses a disputed or unsuitable requirement. Pause holds a task with an essential input missing. That distinction gives the manager a clear next action while preserving judgement and continued support.

Sources: Prosci n.d.-a, n.d.-b, n.d.-c, n.d.-d; Prosci 2022; Creasey 2025; Murdoch University 2026
