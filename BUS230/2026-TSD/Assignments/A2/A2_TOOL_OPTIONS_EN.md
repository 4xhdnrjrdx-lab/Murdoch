# BUS230 A2: simpler tool options

Prepared on 7 October 2026, Asia/Dubai, after checking the course toolkit, Unit Outline section 5.2.2, Topic 7 materials and the A2 rubric. This is a comparison for discussion. The existing Kotter allocation remains the current proposal; Faiaz has asked about alternatives but has not selected a replacement.

## Eligibility and the task

All four options below are explicitly included on page 1 of the BUS230 toolkit list. Lewin is also an explicit example in the Unit Outline. The list supports their eligibility as tools to analyse and modify; it does not confirm the lecturer's approval of a particular proposed modification.

A2 requires critical analysis of a chosen tool or process, a new or modified version addressing the identified limitations, and a formal simulated manager training session. A simple diagram alone does not meet these requirements. The 30-minute presentation, maximum 30 slides and academic research requirements still apply.

## Comparison

The difficulty ratings are our planning judgment about explaining and demonstrating each tool. They are not research findings or a prediction of marks.

| Option | Core idea | Ease of explanation | Main A2 consideration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Force Field Analysis | Identify forces supporting and restraining a particular change | Very easy: two sides of one diagram | Select a specific version and critically examine how evidence and different perspectives inform the identification and weighting of forces |
| Lewin's three-stage model | Unfreeze, Change, Refreeze: prepare, change and stabilise | Very easy: three stages | Turn a high-level outline into a usable manager process without treating Lewin's wider theory as only a simple diagram |
| Stakeholder Mapping: power–interest matrix | Map stakeholders by influence and interest to plan engagement | Very easy: four quadrants | Define this specific version, since stakeholder mapping is a family of tools; examine whose needs or views a two-axis map may overlook |
| Prosci ADKAR | Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability and Reinforcement for individual change | Easy: five memorable elements | Keep the ADKAR model distinct from the wider Prosci Methodology; evaluate its scope rather than claiming the full methodology lacks organisational planning |

## Possible research and design directions

These are questions to investigate, not established limitations or completed original contributions. Check the selected original version and academic literature before claiming that an addition is new.

- **Force Field Analysis:** How are forces identified and scores justified? Do different groups agree? A proposed adaptation could require an evidence record for each judgment, document disagreement, and show how revised evidence changes priorities. NHS guidance already includes prioritisation, action planning and revisiting forces, so merely adding actions or a review is not enough to demonstrate a new contribution.
- **Lewin:** How is the simplified three-stage representation translated into local decisions? A proposed adaptation could specify stage review criteria, responsibilities and reassessment rules for the selected case. Do not assume that stabilisation means stopping all future change or that Lewin ignored group participation. His wider work and the contested interpretation of the model need to be read.
- **Stakeholder Mapping:** Does prioritising only power and interest give enough attention to people strongly affected by the change? A proposed adaptation could record impact and the reasons for engagement decisions, with a defined process for responding to concerns. Check this against existing stakeholder tools before presenting it as new.
- **ADKAR:** How do individual adoption barriers interact with workload, resources and organisational decisions? A proposed adaptation could link diagnosed individual needs to specific managerial resource decisions. Repeated assessment and barrier identification already exist in ADKAR; they are not new modifications by themselves.

Use one shared, clearly labelled fictional case for demonstration. An exercise can ask managers to identify forces, place stakeholders or diagnose an adoption barrier, then apply the modified tool and explain their decision.

## Recommendation

For the simplest visual tool and classroom exercise, **Force Field Analysis** is the first option to consider. If the team prefers a model describing the whole change over time, **Lewin's three-stage model** is the first alternative to Kotter. This recommendation concerns the ease of preparation and demonstration; research and a well-supported modification are still substantial work.

Stakeholder Mapping is a good option for a focused engagement problem. ADKAR is a good option for a case centred on employees adopting a new way of working.

SWOT and PESTLE are also in the course toolkit, but their broad diagnostic scope requires careful work to build a focused change-management training session. They are not our first suggestions for replacing Kotter in the current plan.
