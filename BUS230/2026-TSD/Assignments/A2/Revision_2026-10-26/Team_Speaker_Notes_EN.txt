SLIDE 5: The original ADKAR model
Mazin / 04:00–05:00

SAY THIS
ADKAR describes five outcomes of individual change. Awareness means understanding why change is needed. Desire concerns willingness to participate. Knowledge concerns how to change. Ability means applying the relevant skills and behaviours. Reinforcement supports continued use. A manager assesses the person’s needs and provides suitable support, starting with the earliest barrier. Training, coaching and repeated assessment already belong to the baseline approach. We do not present those existing provisions as inventions of our team.

SLIDE 7: 01 / Shared working conditions
Beryl / 06:00–07:00

SAY THIS
Our first limitation concerns shared working conditions. ADKAR recognises that Ability can depend on external conditions. However, a five-outcome checklist does not itself allocate responsibility across IT, a store manager and a sponsor. At Cedar Retail, the IT lead owns scanner access and the manager owns practice time. Our added conditions record makes those responsibilities explicit for the affected task. Weiner and the multilevel readiness literature support considering collective conditions. They do not validate our particular record.

SLIDE 9: 03 / Dependencies across roles
Beryl / 08:00–09:00

SAY THIS
The third limitation concerns dependencies across roles. Individual support and organisational work may need to happen together. A colleague can learn the procedure while IT repairs access and the manager arranges practice. We keep the individual ADKAR assessment and add a route for work that crosses organisational roles.

SLIDE 11: Our modified decision tool
Beryl / 10:00–11:00

SLIDE 14: One complete decision record
Faiaz / 13:00–14:00

SAY THIS
The complete record asks five sets of questions. What is the task and whose work is affected? Which individual ADKAR need requires support? What condition or concern needs an owner? What must happen first, and what evidence is available? Finally, what decision follows and when will the manager review it? Keep the record focused on one task so it remains usable. The record supports a reasoned discussion. It does not calculate a validated readiness score.

SLIDE 16: Continue, revise or pause
Faiaz / 15:00–16:00

SAY THIS
Our proposed decision rules have three outcomes. Continue when the necessary conditions and task evidence support proceeding with follow-up. Revise when the design needs a reasoned change. Pause the affected task when an essential prerequisite is missing. A task-level pause does not stop the entire change project. Training and repairs may continue while the task remains on hold. The decision record must explain the scope and what evidence would allow the manager to reconsider it.
