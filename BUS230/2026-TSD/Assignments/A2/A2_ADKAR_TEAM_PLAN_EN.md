## The original model we will analyse

The baseline is the five-outcome **ADKAR model formalised by Jeff Hiatt in 2006**, interpreted using Prosci's public model and assessment guidance. The five outcomes are individual change requirements, not a complete organisational transformation method or five fixed calendar phases.

| Outcome | Meaning | A manager asks |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Understanding the need for change | Can the person explain why this change is needed? |
| Desire | Willingness to participate and support it | What supports or weakens their willingness? |
| Knowledge | Knowing how to change | Do they understand the new task? |
| Ability | Applying the required skills and behaviours | Can they perform it in working conditions? |
| Reinforcement | Sustaining the change | What helps the behaviour continue? |

Source: Hiatt (2006); Prosci (n.d.-a). ADKAR offers a memorable language and separates understanding, willingness, learning and performance. This can help managers choose more relevant support instead of prescribing the same training to everyone.

Prosci describes the elements as sequential prerequisites (Creasey 2025). Its public assessment uses scores from 1 to 5 and identifies the first element at 3 or below as a barrier point (Prosci 2022). Treat this as a diagnostic conversation prompt, not a proven universal prediction of success.

The **full Prosci Methodology** combines ADKAR with the PCT Model and the 3-Phase Process. PCT addresses success, leadership and sponsorship, project management and change management. ## Limitations and modifications one and two

These are four bounded limitations of using the five-outcome model on its own. They combine a model scope analysis with academic design rationale; they are not four empirically proven defects of every ADKAR application.

### L1 Individual outcomes do not specify organisational dependencies

The five outcomes can reveal a person's difficulty but do not provide a complete map of coupled staffing, workflow, authority, rewards and technology. Ability can include external constraints, so it would be inaccurate to say ADKAR ignores resources. The gap is an explicit shared decision about which organisational condition must change, who can change it and what individual action depends on it.

Collective readiness differs from individual attitudes (Weiner 2009; Rafferty, Jimmieson, and Armenakis 2013). Burke and Litwin (1992) provide a systems perspective; these sources justify examining context, not a claim that they tested our worksheet.

**Mazin's modification:** a joint conditions record. For each affected task, record a technical, political or cultural condition, evidence, the accountable owner and a prerequisite for proceeding. Example: no protected practice time is a manager-owned condition; low Ability must not automatically become an employee training failure. A missing essential condition blocks the affected task from going live until an owner resolves or explicitly redesigns it.

**Check and boundary:** verify the owner, resource and prerequisite for every affected role. This can expose a constraint; it cannot create a budget or remove a sponsor's power. The added governance record complements organisational tools already present in wider Prosci.

### L2 A Desire barrier does not decide whether the change itself is appropriate

Desire is a person's choice to support the change. A low score can reflect a reasonable objection to workload, fairness or the proposed design. The five outcome labels do not themselves require the sponsor to reconsider that design. The risk is a manager using a low score only to increase compliance. This is a misuse risk, not a claim that ADKAR bans participation.

Responses can be ambivalent and shaped by context (Piderit 2000; Oreg 2006; Oreg, Vakola, and Armenakis 2011). Change agents can contribute to resistance (Ford, Ford, and D'Amelio 2008). Topic 3 asks us to consider resistance as useful input.

**Aram's modification:** an accountable response to a challenge. Record the concern in the employee's terms, its evidence, the alternative proposed and a sponsor decision: retain, revise or stop the disputed requirement. The sponsor must provide a reason and response date. An employee representative checks that the concern was represented accurately; this does not mean everyone consents. Unresolved serious fairness or operational concerns go to the sponsor before pressure is applied to raise Desire.

**Check and boundary:** count reasoned responses and actual design revisions, not just messages sent or higher Desire scores. Dialogue already exists in Prosci; our addition is this explicit decision and accountability rule. It does not guarantee agreement or prevent retaliation without supportive management.

## Limitations and modifications three and four

### L3 The first individual barrier is not a complete cross role action order

Prosci's earliest-barrier rule helps target individual support. It does not, by itself, tell a manager how to coordinate tasks across roles when several constraints interact. An IT access problem may need work now while an employee's first barrier is Desire. Treating the personal sequence as a strict project schedule can delay the action needed to improve that Desire.

The model need not be abandoned or described as unable to revisit outcomes: reassessment already exists. Our concern is the missing cross-role dependency rule in a standalone five-outcome checklist. Context, time and interactions matter (Pettigrew, Woodman, and Cameron 2001; Herold, Fedor, and Caldwell 2007). Topic 7 distinguishes checklist advice from judgement about the process in context.

**Faiaz's modification:** a dependency register linked to each individual barrier. Record the upstream condition, downstream task, owner, completion evidence and decision. Address personal prerequisites in the ADKAR order, while organisational owners can work on enabling conditions in parallel. When a prerequisite changes, update the linked action and explain the route taken. A missing essential condition means **pause the affected task**; a disputed or unsuitable design means **revise**; verified prerequisites and acceptable evidence mean **continue**.

**Check and boundary:** the manager must explain which dependency caused the choice and which role owns it. The contribution is the linked routing rule, not a new feedback loop or repeated assessment. Parallel work can increase coordination costs and does not prove the model's individual prerequisite sequence is false.

### L4 A brief score is not enough evidence of learning and performance

The public 1-5 assessment is a practical snapshot. A score is not automatically a validated readiness measure, proof of workplace Ability or a causal forecast. Different roles and tasks may produce different answers; averaging them can hide a blocked role. ADKAR already distinguishes Knowledge from Ability and allows observation. The remaining gap is how this small worksheet resolves conflicting evidence before a readiness decision.

Readiness measures require explicit construct and validation work (Holt et al. 2007; Jo and Hong 2023). Transfer means applying and maintaining learning at work (Baldwin and Ford 1988). Feedback design matters (Kluger and DeNisi 1996), and a safe learning setting supports speaking about errors (Edmondson 1999).

**Beryl's modification:** an evidence disagreement rule. Keep self-report, a realistic task demonstration and the conditions check side by side. If they disagree, record **not yet verified** and investigate the task or conditions; do not average them into a pass. Confirm Knowledge by explanation and Ability by performance with usable equipment and time. Use a non-punitive practice task, not a public ranking of staff.

**Check and boundary:** count evidence disagreements found and resolved, and check the task under representative conditions. These are proposed indicators, not achieved results. This rule supplements existing assessment and observation. It has not been psychometrically validated and needs local calibration before real use.

## Shared demonstration case

**Proposed fictional training scenario for team agreement.** Cedar Retail has two stores and plans to replace paper stock counts with a digital inventory system. Staff will scan deliveries and record discrepancies. This company, its people, numbers and events are invented for teaching. The team has not yet approved this case. No survey or company research has been conducted.

The training focus is a stock colleague's ability to complete a delivery task. Assume the colleague understands the purpose, wants reliable records, has completed a short demonstration, but worries about being blamed for inherited stock errors. The evening shift shares one working scanner and has no protected practice time. A past launch was described as “blame first, fix later.” These facts are scenario inputs, not research findings.

**Illustrative baseline profile:** Awareness 4, Desire 2, Knowledge 4, Ability 2, Reinforcement 3. Under the public assessment convention, Desire is the first barrier. A competent baseline ADKAR response would ask why Desire is low and arrange relevant support. It need not ignore equipment or employee concerns. Our adaptation makes the related decisions explicit and traceable.

### Walk through the four additions

| Addition | Example record | Manager decision |
|---|---|---|
| Joint conditions | Scanner access and 20 minutes of protected practice are missing. Store manager owns roster time; IT lead owns access. | Pause independent evening-shift rollout until the conditions are usable. |
| Challenge response | Staff request separation of inherited errors from new recording mistakes. Operations sponsor checks the proposed penalty rule. | Revise the pilot procedure to review causes before assigning blame; explain the decision to staff. |
| Dependency route | Access and protected time enable a realistic task demonstration. The sponsor can review the penalty rule while IT works. | Work on conditions in parallel; retain the individual Desire discussion and required learning support. |
| Evidence disagreement | Self-reported Knowledge is high, but the colleague cannot complete the task using the available scanner. | Ability is not yet verified. Investigate equipment and practice, rather than averaging the scores. |

After owners address the conditions, observe a practice delivery task. For the demonstration, ask the colleague to scan a delivery, find a deliberately mismatched quantity and explain how to report it. Choose task criteria with the team; they are local teaching criteria, not Prosci validation thresholds.

If the task succeeds under representative conditions and concerns receive reasoned responses, the manager can consider continuing. Record the actual evidence instead of inventing improved ADKAR scores. Keep coaching and reinforcement, and review when the scanner, roster or process changes.

**Proposed evaluation:** track task completion accuracy, unresolved condition dependencies, reasoned responses to concerns and evidence disagreements. A later workplace trial could compare decisions using the same scenario with and without the added fields. A classroom demonstration can check usability and learning; it cannot establish causal superiority or real business benefits.

## Two minute manager exercise

**Beryl facilitates slide 22.** Show this card:

“An evening stock colleague says: I understand the new system and support accurate stock records. I can explain the procedure, but the shared scanner fails during deliveries and I have no practice time. I am worried that old stock errors will reduce my performance rating. The manager says we should give another motivational talk.”

Ask pairs to write four short items: the missing condition; the concern that needs a sponsor response; the decision and its organisational owner; the evidence needed before continuing.

| Seconds | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-20 | Explain the card and questions. |
| 20-80 | Pairs discuss and complete one worksheet row. |
| 80-100 | Invite one brief answer; use the prepared example if nobody responds. |
| 100-120 | Debrief the decision and link it to the four additions. |

**Answer guide:** scanner reliability and protected practice time are missing conditions. Fair treatment of inherited errors is a design concern. Pause the affected delivery task, assign access to IT and time to the store manager, and ask the operations sponsor to review the rating rule. Observe a realistic delivery task after the conditions are usable. A motivational talk alone does not resolve these inputs. Accept other answers when they connect evidence, an owner and a justified route.

**Learning check on slide 23:** ask why a high Knowledge score does not establish Ability, and which added rule makes a sponsor respond to a concern. The facilitator checks whether the answer separates conditions from personal support and explains a decision. Do not pre-write audience responses as achieved results.

Stouten et al. and Errida and Lotfi provide comparisons of change approaches.
