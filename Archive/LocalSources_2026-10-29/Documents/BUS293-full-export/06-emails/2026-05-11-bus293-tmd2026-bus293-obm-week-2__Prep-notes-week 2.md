# Organisational Theory and Behaviour-Week 2

## Introduction to Teams and Groups

Explain that teams are more structured than general groups because members share accountability and work toward organisational goals. Example: A hospital emergency response team works together with defined roles, unlike a casual friendship group at work.

## Advantages of Teams

Discuss how teams combine skills and perspectives to improve decisions and innovation. Example: A marketing team combining designers, analysts, and writers can create stronger campaigns than one individual.

## Disadvantages of Teams

Explain coordination losses and social loafing. Example: In a university group assignment, some members may rely on others to complete the work.

## Types of Teams

Describe self-directed, multicultural, and virtual teams. Example: Remote software developers collaborating online form a virtual team.

## Team Effectiveness Model

Explain that effective teams benefit both organisations and members. Example: Companies with supportive leadership often have higher employee satisfaction and productivity.

## Team Design Factors

Describe how tasks and interdependence influence teamwork. Example: In manufacturing, one worker’s output may become another worker’s input.

## Team Composition

Explain the Five C’s and diversity. Example: Diverse healthcare teams improve patient care by combining different expertise.

## Team States

Discuss norms, cohesion, trust, and efficacy. Example: Sports teams often perform better when players trust one another.

## Tuckman’s Stages

Walk through forming, storming, norming, performing, and adjourning. Example: New project teams usually experience conflict before becoming productive.

## Team Building

Discuss brainstorming and trust-building activities. Example: Team retreats can improve communication and relationships.

## Decision Making Challenges

Explain groupthink and evaluation apprehension. Example: Employees may avoid challenging a manager’s idea during meetings.

## Improving Decision Making

Describe brainwriting and nominal group technique. Example: Anonymous idea sharing often encourages quieter employees to contribute.

## Conclusion

Summarise that successful teams require planning, trust, communication, and positive norms. Encourage students to apply these concepts in assignments and workplaces.
