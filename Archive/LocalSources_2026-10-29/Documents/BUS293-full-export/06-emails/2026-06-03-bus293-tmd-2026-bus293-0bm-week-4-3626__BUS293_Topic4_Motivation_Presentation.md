# Employee Motivation
BUS293 Organisational Theory and Behaviour
Topic 4: Motivation Theories and Applications

<!-- Slide number: 2 -->
# Learning Outcomes
Understand content and process theories of motivation
Apply motivation theories to workplace situations
Explain goal setting and feedback

<!-- Slide number: 3 -->
# What is Motivation?
Motivation drives behaviour and performance.
Employee engagement = emotional commitment + focused effort.
Example: A staff member volunteers for extra projects because they enjoy contributing.

<!-- Slide number: 4 -->
# Content Theories
Focus on WHAT motivates people.
Examples: Maslow, McClelland, Four-Drive Theory.
Example: An employee seeks promotion to satisfy achievement needs.

<!-- Slide number: 5 -->
# Maslow's Hierarchy
Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-Actualisation.
Example: Job security motivates a new employee more than leadership opportunities.

<!-- Slide number: 6 -->
# Equity Theory
People compare inputs and rewards with others.
Negative inequity: feeling under-rewarded.
Example: Two employees do similar work, but one receives a larger bonus.

<!-- Slide number: 7 -->
# Organisational Justice
Distributive Justice = fairness of outcomes.
Procedural Justice = fairness of processes.
Example: Transparent promotion criteria improve motivation.

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
# Expectancy Theory
Motivation depends on:
Expectancy (effort→performance)
Instrumentality (performance→reward)
Valence (value of reward)
Example: Sales staff work harder when bonuses are achievable and valued.

<!-- Slide number: 9 -->
# Reinforcement Theory
Positive Reinforcement: reward good behaviour.
Negative Reinforcement: remove unwanted condition.
Punishment: discourage behaviour.
Extinction: remove rewards for poor behaviour.

<!-- Slide number: 10 -->
# Examples of Reinforcement
Positive: Employee receives recognition.
Negative: Extra supervision removed after improvement.
Punishment: Written warning for misconduct.
Extinction: Stop rewarding lateness.

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
# Social Cognitive Theory
People learn by observing others.
Example: A new employee learns customer service skills by shadowing a senior colleague.

<!-- Slide number: 12 -->
# Goal Setting Theory
SMARTER Goals:
Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound, Exciting, Reviewed.
Example: Increase customer satisfaction from 80% to 90% within 6 months.

<!-- Slide number: 13 -->
# Effective Feedback
Feedback should be specific, timely, credible and supportive.
Example: 'Your report was accurate and submitted early; keep using this approach.'

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
# Limitations of Goal Setting
Overemphasis on measurable goals.
Can encourage stress or unethical behaviour.
May hinder learning for new employees.

<!-- Slide number: 15 -->
# Key Takeaways
Motivation is critical for performance.
Fairness, rewards, goals and feedback influence behaviour.
Managers should build capability, provide support and ensure justice.
