### Notes:
Transformational leadership views leaders as change agents. They develop, communicate and model a shared vision for the team or organisation, and encourage experimentation. In developing the vision, they create and champion an image of the companies projected, positive, future state, which motivates and bonds employees. The vision is communicated by framing the message around a grand purpose, using symbols, metaphors, and stories to create a shared mental model of the future. Transformational leaders model the vision through walking the talk, symbolising and demonstrating the vision through their behaviour, which increases employee trust in the leader. They then encourage experimentation in new ways of operating by questioning current practices and supporting a learning orientation. Building commitment to the vision is increase through the communication and modelling of the vision, and through employee involvement in shaping the vision.

Some leadership models say charismatic leadership is essential for transformational leadership, however research suggests that charisma differs from transformational leadership as charisma is a personal trait that provides referent power, it does not necessarily attempt to change the organisation, and it is associated with narcissism.

Categorises leadership style into either task-oriented, or people-oriented
Neither of these orientations is more effective than the other, each with advantages and disadvantages

Task-oriented leaders:
Assign work and clarify responsibilities
Set goals and deadlines
Evaluate and provide feedback on work quality
Establish well-defined best work procedures
Plan future work activities
People-oriented leaders:
Show interest in others as people
Listen to employees
Make the workplace more pleasant
Compliment employees for their work

### Notes:
Managerial leadership theories include leadership style orientation, which clusters leadership behaviours into task, and people, oriented. Task-oriented leadership includes behaviours that define and structure work roles such as assigning employees to specific tasks, clarifying their work duties and procedures, ensuring they follow company rules and pushing them to reach their performance capacity, whereas people-oriented leadership includes behaviours that focus on developing relationships with employees such as showing mutual trust and respect for subordinates, demonstrating a genuine concern for their needs and having a desire to look out for their welfare. Neither of these orientations is more effective than the other, each with advantages and disadvantages, and recent research shows that effective leaders rely on both styles, but in different ways.

Leader-Member Exchange (LMX) Theory focuses on the relationship that exists between leaders and members, rather than the style the leader is using
Looks at relationships as a series of exchanges, differentiated based on their quality
Low LMX relationships are based on economic exchanges, e.g. leaders providing organisational resources in return for task performance
High LMX relationships are based on social exchange of resources, e.g. liking, trust, and commitment.

Benefits:
Research shows high LMX relationships lead to increased employee commitment and organisational citizenship behaviours, enhanced job performance, and employee retention

Limitations:
Potential to alienate some employees
Does not considering the effects of team dynamics and social identity
Does not provide specific advice on how leaders can develop high-quality relationships

LMX Theory

### Notes:
Leader–member exchange theory focuses on the relationship that exists between leaders and members, rather than the style the leader is using. This theory looks at relationships as a series of exchanges, that may lead to developing trust and mutual commitment. LMX relationships are differentiated based on their quality with low LMX relationships based on economic exchanges like leaders providing organisational resources in return for task performance, and high LMX relationships based on social exchange of resources such as liking, trust, and commitment. High-quality LMX leads to improved organisational outcomes such as increased employee commitment and organisational citizenship behaviours, enhanced job performance, and employee retention. Limitations of this theory include its potential to alienate some employees, not considering the effects of team dynamics and social identity, and not providing specific advice on how leaders can develop high-quality relationships.

Follower-Centric Perspective

### Notes:
The Follower-centric perspective recognises that leaders don’t operate in a vacuum, and it proposes that leaders emerge because of the attributional and cognitive processes of their followers. Organizations that use follower-centric styles tend to have a flat structure, where leaders and followers are treated equally. Employees have a ‘voice’ and can greatly impact organizational decision-making.

The romance of leadership effect, where leaders are seen to have a sole impact on organisational success is based in people wanting to believe that leaders make a difference, so they engage in fundamental attribution error and other perceptual distortions to support this belief in the leader’s impact. This simplification of life events is done to minimise cognitive processing (discussed in topic three) and is supported by the Western belief that life events are generated more by people than by uncontrollable natural forces.

The implicit leadership theory poses that people have preconceived beliefs about the features and behaviours of effective leaders, known as leadership prototypes, which they use to evaluate the leader’s effectiveness. People are more likely to think a leader is effective when they fit their established idea of what a leader SHOULD be. Leadership exists in the mind of the followers.

### Notes:

They also contribute to increased follower satisfaction and organisational citizenship behaviours, and lower turnover and counterproductive work behaviours. Ethical leadership can change organisational and team norms, as well as increase willingness to speak up about ethical issues and reduce interpersonal conflicts in the workplace.

# Topic 9 Summary

### Notes:
There are many perspectives on leadership, and related theories. The Transformational perspective looks at leadership on a macro-scale, visioning for the future, whereas managerial perspective focusses on day-to-day requirements of a leader and contains a myriad of theories which try to predict which behaviours and styles will be effective in a given situation. The follower-centric perspective recognises that leaders don’t operate in a vacuum, and that followers' beliefs and expectations impact whether a leader is seen as effective, and finally the competency perspective acknowledges that there are some key predictors of effective leaders. Culture and gender, in addition to other contingency factors identified in theories, impact the way a leader's effectiveness is perceived, and it is important to reflect on the context of leaders - their situation, team contingencies, work contingencies, their relationship with followers, when looking at what might make them more, or less, effective.
