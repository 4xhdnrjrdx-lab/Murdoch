# Communication Problems Between Teams at Westpac Banking Corporation (WBC)

Communication issues between teams have been identified as a critical organisational issue for Westpac Banking Corporation (WBC), which is one of the top financial institutions on the ASX200 index of listed Australian firms. Efficient communication is crucial in banking as departments like Technology, Risk, Compliance, Customer Service, Operations, and Retail Banking often operate and depend heavily on each other. Team dynamics research has indicated that the level of communication within a team will impact a team’s level of trust, cohesion, coordination and decision-making abilities (Mathieu et al 2008). If communication systems are not properly implemented, the result is often fragmented or misinterpreted information which hinders performance of an organization and increase operational risk.

One of the key communication issues for WBC is how to keep information consistent across different departments that have contrasting perspectives and objectives. The objective of Technology team members tends to be more about innovative new products and digital transformations, whereas risk and compliance teams are driven more by governance, regulation, and the avoidance of risks. The differing objectives of various departments are a contributing factor to the lack of cooperation and communication barriers. As suggested in the team mental model theory, higher team performance can be achieved when members share similar understandings about the team's goals, their roles, and tasks (Kozlowski and Ilgen 2006). If departments have differing expectations and assumptions, this will more than likely lead to misunderstanding and lack of coordination.

It also relates to team trust and cohesion. Team trust is employees' positive beliefs about whether or not colleagues can be relied upon and are knowledgeable. Trust between teammates has been found to be vital in effective teamwork, and the basis for an information-sharing environment and the willingness to work together (Edmondson 1999). Breakdowns in communication may also reduce the level of trust between departments if employees believe that information is incomplete, contradictory or simply slow in its transmission. Cohesion may also be impacted on by insufficient interaction with teammates outside of immediate working groups. Team Dynamics discusses how member interaction and shared success impact on team cohesion; in a large organization such as WBC, departmental barriers can inhibit this and also reduce the collaborative spirit between groups.

Furthermore, remote and hybrid working conditions can exaggerate the issues with communication. Studies into remote teams have shown how virtual environments may limit informal communication, interpersonal bonds, and opportunities for informal knowledge sharing (Allen, Golden, and Shockley 2015; Gilson et al. 2015). Although WBC has invested in technology for digital communication,virtual communication cannot replace face-to-face interaction. Evaluation apprehension, and the need to conform, are both discussed in Team Dynamics as influences which inhibit effective communication. This is the apprehension which individuals have of being judged by managers or peers; this means employees may be reluctant to express problems, contradict decisions or present alternative solutions, leading to innovation stifled and poor decision-making enhanced.

Poor communication in WBC has consequences for organizational performance. Slow transmission of communication will affect the speed at which decision are made, quality of services provided and cause inefficiencies between different departments. In the highly competitive banking industry this may also have a negative impact on customer perception of services provided and the company's image. Numerous studies prove that effective communication is linked positively to team efficiency, performance and employee satisfaction (Salas, Sims and Burke, 2005). Therefore, poor communication at WBC may negatively impact strategic objectives.

This challenge also has significant consequences for sustainability of the organization. In the long term WBC is dependent on positive relationships with its stakeholders, compliance with regulations and ethical decisions. Poor communication can contribute to employee stress, lower levels of job satisfaction, and affect turnover rates negatively. It will also increase the risk factors for the organization, regarding operational and compliance risks. This problem is linked to principle 10 of the United Nations Global Compact: it focuses on promoting integrity, accountability and anti-corruption actions. Effective communication will help ensure that employees comply with the rules, feel safe when reporting problems and take decisions that are morally correct (United Nations Global Compact, 2024). Therefore, poor communication should not be perceived only as a performance but also as a sustainability issue.

Recommendations

WBC should improve inter-departmental collaboration through setting regular meetings, inter-departmental project teams and performance metrics across the departments. Through increased interaction employee teams build trusting relationships and shared mental models that allow for better coordination (Mathieu et al. 2008; Kozlowski and Ilgen 2006).

The company should invest in developing their leaders through leadership training in psychological safety and open communication. By developing the psychological safety within teams employees will feel safer to share concerns and raise concerns, share information and come up with solutions. The employee will feel safer in expressing opinions and feelings without fear of being criticized (Edmondson 1999). Psychological safety helps reduce evaluation apprehension and increase the quality of the decisions made by the team.

WBC needs to improve their communication systems for teams that work hybrid or remotely. Using more structured protocols for communication, incorporating virtual collaboration technologies, and holding frequent virtual team engagement activities help develop trusting relationships, reduce misconceptions among employees who work remotely and develop shared mental models (Allen, Golden and Shockley 2015; Gilson et al. 2015).

Finally, WBC needs to expand team building activities that encourage interaction and boost team cohesion. Research into Team Dynamics suggests that through interaction employees build strong bonds through their shared goals and collective success, in turn, increasing team cohesion (Beal et al. 2003; Salas, Sims, and Burke 2005).
