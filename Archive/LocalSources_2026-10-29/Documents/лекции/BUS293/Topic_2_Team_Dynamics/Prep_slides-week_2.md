**SLIDE 1 — Title**
Organisational Theory and Behaviour: Teams
BUS293 Topic 2 Summary

**SLIDE 2 — Introduction to Teams and Groups**

- Teams have complementary skills and shared accountability.
- Groups may be informal and focus on social interaction.
- Teams are aligned with organisational objectives.

**SLIDE 3 — Advantages of Teams**

- Better decision making and innovation.
- Improved coordination and customer service.
- Higher motivation and accountability among members.

**SLIDE 4 — Disadvantages of Teams**

- Coordination process losses increase with team size.
- Social loafing reduces individual effort.
- Integrating new members requires extra time and energy.

**SLIDE 5 — Types of Teams**

- Self-directed teams manage their own work.
- Multicultural teams bring creativity and diversity.
- Virtual teams rely on communication technology.

**SLIDE 6 — Team Effectiveness Model**

- Effective teams support organisational goals.
- Benefits include member satisfaction and long-term survival.
- Supportive environments improve performance.

**SLIDE 7 — Team Design Factors**

- Task complexity and structure affect effectiveness.
- Task interdependence can be pooled, sequential, or reciprocal.
- Team size should balance skills and coordination.

**SLIDE 8 — Team Composition**

- The Five C's: Cooperating, Coordinating, Communicating, Comforting, Conflict resolving.
- Diverse teams make better decisions.
- Roles may be formal or informal.

**SLIDE 9 — Team States**

- Norms regulate behaviour.
- Cohesion motivates members to stay committed.
- Trust and efficacy improve team performance.

**SLIDE 10 — Tuckman's Stages of Team Development**

- Forming – understanding expectations.
- Storming – conflict and role negotiation.
- Norming – cohesion and agreement.
- Performing – efficient teamwork.
- Adjourning – task completion and transition.

**SLIDE 11 — Team Building**

- Experiential activities build trust.
- Brainstorming encourages free flow of ideas.
- Challenging viewpoints improve team performance.

**SLIDE 12 — Team Decision Making Challenges**

- Production blocking limits participation.
- Evaluation apprehension reduces idea sharing.
- Groupthink prioritises consensus over quality decisions.

**SLIDE 13 — Improving Team Decision Making**

- Brainwriting reduces production blocking.
- Electronic brainstorming allows anonymous contributions.
- Nominal group technique improves evaluation of ideas.

**SLIDE 14 — Conclusion**

- Teams outperform individuals when managed effectively.
- Positive norms and communication are essential.
- Effective team design improves organisational success.
