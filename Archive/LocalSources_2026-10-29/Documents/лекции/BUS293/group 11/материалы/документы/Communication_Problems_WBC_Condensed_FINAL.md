# Communication Problems Between Teams at Westpac Banking Corporation (WBC)

Communication problems are a considerable problem for WBC, the largest financial company trading as part of the ASX200; in particular due to the close interaction between the departments such as Technology, Risk, Compliance, Operations and Customer Service and the close relationship between the parts of the company. Communication transmits information necessary for trust, cohesion, coordination and decisions and it creates operational risk and reduces organisational performance in case of communication breakdowns (Mathieu et al 2008).

One of the main issues is with effective communication where each department has a different focus; for example, technology teams concentrate more on innovative or digital transformation objectives whereas risk and compliance teams would more likely focus on governance requirements. This divergence in understanding the main expectations is likely to lead to conflicts or communication issues. As per team mental model theory, performance will increase where everyone involved in a project has the same understanding of the goals, roles and processes (Kozlowski and Ilgen 2006).

Communicationissues further impact on trust and cohesion. Trust establiseases of what information needs to be shared, and efforts to work with others in collaborative activities, and is actually harmed by the breakdown in communication resulting from inconsistent and delayed information transfers, potentially damaging the positive interdepartmental relationships within the organisation (Edmondson 1999). In a large organisation like WBC, the different department...siloes...may inhibit contacts between departments and restrict opportunities to construct cohesion with FOCUSyears of shared experience and achievement.

Communication becomes even more problematic in hybrid and telecommuting workplaces. Virtual work, for example, may lead to decrease in informal communication and knowledge transfer, making collaboration more complicated (Allen, Golden, and Shockley 2015; Gilson et al. 2015). Even though WBC applies digital communication technologies, virtual communication is unable to substitute face to face communication completely.

Communication problems have direct effect on organizational performance. Information sharing time lag may have resulted in slowing down decision-making, service delivery quality and waste of resources. In a competitive banking industry such an impact may cause poor customer satisfaction and organizational image and reputation. Good communication practices are highly correlated to team effectiveness and employee commitment (Salas, Sims and Burke 2005).

Finally, communication challenges have impact on sustainability. Trust, adherence to regulation and ethical decision-making among stakeholders are crucial to WBC's sustainability. Poor communication may contribute to increased stress and pressure on employees, higher operating risks, and violation of regulations. Such a conclusion is also related to Principle 10 of United Nations Global Compact that encourage business to be transparent and accountable. In addition, we should also see communication as a matter of performance as well as of sustainability.

Recommendations

Cross-functional cooperation: WBC can enhance cross-functional cooperation via frequent meetings among departments, shared goals and teamwork initiatives. Collaboration improves trust, communication and shared mental models (Mathieu et al., 2008; Kozlowski and Ilgen, 2006).

The organization must also place effort on developing leadership on the topics of psychological safety and open communication. People who feel comfortable stating their concerns and new thoughts are most likely to generate more appropriate solutions and decisions (Edmondson 1999).

Secondly, WBC should establish new procedures for communication of hybrid and remote teams, i.e. By using communication standards,virtual interaction tools and consistent interaction opportunities (Allen, Golden and Shockley 2015; Gilson et al. 2015).

Finally, team-building initiatives should be expanded to strengthen cohesion and collaboration across departments, improving both employee engagement and organisational performance (Beal et al. 2003; Salas, Sims, and Burke 2005).
