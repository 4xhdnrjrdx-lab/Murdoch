## Slide 14 — Casualisation: A Definition

Casualisation is the ongoing increase in employees identified as 'casual'; a broad trend towards an increasing percentage of a country's population being employed in casual/part-time work.
Campbell (2004) argues that this sort of work, depending on the socio-cultural context, is essentially employment in which the worker does not have certain rights and benefits, such as:

- Paid sick leave
- Paid public holidays
- Paid annual leave
- Notice of dismissal and redundancy pay

Importantly, Bill Martin and Jocelyn Pixley (2005) argue that perceived job security is one of the four crucial factors that inform job satisfaction.
Casual work often lacks job security.

## Slide 15 — Casualisation: Precarious work

Walsh, Huynh and Longmuir (2025) state:
"One key feature of precarious work is a push for flexibility. At an organisational level, employment practices have become more flexible, reducing the ability of workers to collectively organise to resist precarity. This increased flexibility has led to employment being increasingly vulnerable to uncertainty, instability, and insecurity, with workers and their families expected to manage the risks associated with precarious work" (Walsh, Huynh & Longmuir, 2025).

## Slide 16 — Casualisation and 'employability'

"[T]he detrimental effects of precarious work may have become normalised by many young Australians … young people may have largely accepted individual responsibility for navigating precarious work. … Sofritti et al. (2020) found that young precarious workers engage with discourses of an entrepreneurial self, employability, and self-responsibilisation, each of which acts on and dictates to young people about the sorts of workers they should be in order to live in a precarious labour market. Notably, these discourses focus on the individual and place the responsibility for taking and managing risks on young people" (Walsh, Huynh & Longmuir, 2025).

## Slide 20 — Advantages and disadvantages of casual work (For Employers)

**Advantages**

- Businesses do not have to pay sick leave or annual leave
- Under-performing employees can be fired without any major costs
- One could argue that casual employment improves productivity and efficiency because workers are afforded a healthier work/life balance

**Disadvantages**

- How difficult is it to plan for the future with an unstable and malleable/fluid workforce?
- Workers are paid at a higher hourly rate
- One could argue that an unsettled and unstable casual workforce can inform unproductive work
- Businesses may lose talented and skilled workers who may be looking for more permanent employment

## Slide 21 — Advantages and disadvantages of casual work (For Employees)

**Advantages**

- Flexible working hours
- Higher hourly rate than full-time work (25% loading sometimes)
- Employees are not tied down by a contract … they are free to travel, take on other work, pursue study goals and take on hobbies
- May lead to permanent work

**Disadvantages**

- No sick leave and annual leave benefits
- Instability
- Invisibility – not part of work culture
- Irregular working hours
- Irregular pay
- No job security
- Less superannuation
- No redundancy
- Immediate termination (no notice)

**Open questions on the slide**

- Harder work/life balance? High peak periods of work and then low?
- Physical and mental health?
- Easier work/life balance? Social and community roles easier to balance (e.g., care for ill, disabled, ageing family members)?

## Slide 24 — Long-term casual work?

In Australia, workplace laws have changed so that it is more difficult for employers to keep employees in long term casual work.
Since December 2023, employers are no longer able to offer more than two-year fixed term contracts when there is work certainty.

## Slide 25 — Remember last week we looked at gig work.

- What has been your experience of 'gig' work?
- How does it compare to being a casual employee? Is there a difference?
- In Australia, casual employees are 'employees': the employer is responsible for their pay, taxes, superannuation, and compliance with relevant awards or enterprise agreements.
- Gig workers, on the other hand, are not employees. They are engaged to deliver a result or output – not hired as a member of staff. They don't receive typical employee entitlements.

## Slide 28 — Financial literacy

"[Financial literacy is] a combination of skills and knowledge needed to be able to make informed decisions about a person's financial resources on a day-to-day basis … having a broader understanding of the financial system itself; how it works on a global scale and how banks or the stock market can affect people on an individual level". (Foundation for Young Australians, 2020)
Financial literacy includes negotiating pay, consultancy fees and employment conditions, researching tax and salaries, etc., personal budgeting skills, understanding finance for small business, self employment, and so on.

## Quick reference (summary)

- **Workshop theme:** Casualisation (World of Work theme) + skills focus on **Financial Literacy**; also revisits gig work and Assessment 2.
- **Key definition:** casualisation = rising share of casual/part-time work, lacking entitlements (paid sick/annual leave, public holidays, notice/redundancy) (Campbell 2004); casual work often lacks job security, a key driver of job satisfaction (Martin & Pixley 2005).
- **Precarity:** precarious work pushes "flexibility" that shifts risk onto workers (Walsh, Huynh & Longmuir 2025); "self-responsibilisation" of young workers via employability discourses (Sofritti et al. 2020, in Walsh et al. 2025).
- **AU law update:** since December 2023, employers can't offer fixed-term contracts beyond two years where there's work certainty (Slide 24).
- **Casual vs gig (AU):** casual workers are employees (employer handles pay/tax/super/awards); gig workers are engaged for an output, not employees, and don't get employee entitlements (Slide 25).
- **Financial literacy (FYA 2020):** informed day-to-day financial decisions + understanding the wider financial system; includes negotiating pay/fees/conditions, tax/salary research, budgeting, small-business/self-employment finance.
- **Linked readings (already in your library):** Stambe et al. 2025 (Gatta/Ablaza/Stambe "Not all insecure work…") and Vassiley 2022 ("Why insecure work is finally being recognised as a health hazard…").
