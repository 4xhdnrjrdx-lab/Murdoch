## Core message (paraphrased)

We're in an era of growing anxiety about AI, automation, Big Data, VR and AR disrupting work. The article argues that one of the most practical ways to cope with "AI anxiety" — and stay employable — is to deliberately build **uniquely human skills** (non-digital, non-technological capabilities), and to reframe AI as something that can **augment** rather than only replace human work.

## Framing: AI anxiety is real

- Opens with psychologist **Mary Alvord** (via a _Scientific American_ article) describing how AI-related anxieties show up in clients — fears about the future of work and one's place in it.
- These concerns aren't abstract: the disruption and transformation of work by generative AI is well documented.

## The future of work (data)

- **WEF 2025 Future of Jobs Report:** **85.7%** of surveyed employers see AI, information processing, Big Data, VR and AR as forces shaping their plans. Long-term productivity gains remain uncertain, but some jobs are hit harder — clerical/routine roles especially.
- **ILO + Poland's National Research Institute** joint report: **25%** of global jobs are exposed to generative AI (with the figure higher in high-income countries).
- **Gendered impact:** in high-income countries, **9.6% of jobs held by women** are at high risk of automation, vs **3.5% of jobs held by men**.
- Beyond clerical work, the ILO flags **"highly digitised cognitive jobs"** in media and similar fields as exposed — e.g., securities/finance dealers and brokers, software developers, financial advisers, authors and writers, translators and interpreters. (Note: exposure means tasks can be affected, not that the whole job disappears.)

## Work in a time of disruption

- **COVID-19** and the **"great resignation"** pushed workers to re-evaluate job expectations; trends like remote work and flexible hours (already emerging pre-pandemic) accelerated.
- **Policy Horizons Canada** futurists identify several "game changers" reshaping work — including generative AI and automation.
- Another major force: the **fraying social contract between employers and employees** — eroding benefits, the rise of the **gig economy**, and rising cost of living were already leaving workers feeling vulnerable and disengaged before AI added to the pressure.

## How to cope with AI anxiety

- Acknowledge the feelings; avoid becoming overwhelmed.
- Psychologists' strategies for managing generative-AI anxiety include: **trying out AI tools** to see where they're useful; and **building new skills**.
- The author expands on **building skills**. In a research study, he and colleagues identified the **10 skills most frequently named as key for the future of work**:
  1. Collaboration
  2. Communication
  3. Creativity and innovation
  4. Critical thinking
  5. Cross-cultural competency
     _(the article lists 10; the remaining items continue this set of "human" skills — adaptability/lifelong learning, problem solving, emotional/social intelligence, leadership, and self-management type capabilities)_
- **Key point:** all of these are **"human" skills** — not digital or technological. Focusing on them is a practical, future-proof response because they're likely to stay in demand as AI advances.

## Rethinking our relationship with AI and work

- Researchers describe **three channels** for AI's workplace impact: **replacing** aspects of human work; **complementing/augmenting** human work; or creating **new** kinds of work.
- The author favours the **second — augmentation**: treat generative AI as a **tool** that extends human capacity rather than purely a threat.
- Exploring **"collaborative intelligence"** (humans + generative AI working together) can be an antidote to AI anxiety, and may help people **re-imagine their relationship to work** and strengthen their sense of purpose.

## Key terms (for your assignment)

- **AI anxiety** — distress/fear about AI's effect on work and one's role in it.
- **Human skills** — non-technological capabilities (collaboration, communication, creativity, critical thinking, cross-cultural competency, etc.).
- **Three channels of AI impact** — replace / complement (augment) / create new work.
- **Collaborative intelligence** — humans and AI working together, each contributing strengths.
- Sources cited: WEF 2025 Future of Jobs Report; ILO + Poland NRI report; Policy Horizons Canada; Scientific American (Alvord).
