2. Industry Analysis Report
Introduction
For this report I chose three marketing job advertisements from Dubai. I picked them because they fit my degree, a Bachelor of Business in Marketing and Management, and my interest in how marketing is changing with data and AI. The three roles sit at different levels, so together they show what employers want from a marketing graduate now and where this career can go in the future.
The first job is Market Research Analyst at Interlink HR Consultancy (Interlink HR Consultancy, 2026). I chose it because it is a marketing role that is built around collecting and understanding data. The second is Digital Marketing Trainee at Febno Technologies (Febno Technologies, 2026). I chose this one because it is an entry-level role made for a fresh graduate like me, and it covers the core digital marketing skills. The third is Marketing Supervisor at Sparklaxy Technology FZCO (Sparklaxy Technology FZCO, 2026), a technology company. I chose it as a more senior, future-facing role because it shows where the marketing industry is heading, especially with AI tools.
Job A – Market Research Analyst (Interlink HR Consultancy)
This role asks the candidate to conduct field research, collect and analyse data, develop questionnaires, and make recommendations from the findings. Some of the employability skills are stated clearly, and some are implied. Critical/Creative Thinking & Problem Solving is the most obvious skill: the advert asks the analyst to “identify patterns and solutions,” which means looking at raw data and turning it into useful answers. Digital Literacy appears in the requirement for “strong analytical and statistical skills” and the use of “advanced research techniques,” since modern research is done with software and data tools. Communication & Interpersonal Skills is stated directly through “excellent communication skills,” an “extrovert personality,” and “strong presentation skills.” Self-Management is implied, because the role is “90% fieldwork” and the person must work under pressure, so they need to organise themselves without close supervision.
In this job the skills are used to gather information from people and then explain what it means to clients. The most prioritised skills seem to be analytical thinking and communication, because the whole role is about understanding data and presenting clear recommendations to support business decisions.
Job B – Digital Marketing Trainee (Febno Technologies)
This is an entry-level role that supports website optimisation, SEO, PPC, marketing automation, and affiliate marketing. Digital Literacy is the strongest skill here. The advert names tools and channels such as Google AdWords, search engine marketing, analytics, and landing-page tests, which all need confidence with digital tools. Critical/Creative Thinking & Problem Solving is also important, because the trainee must analyse best practices, gather competitive insights, and recommend strategies to counter competitor moves. Communication & Interpersonal Skills shows up when the trainee has to “translate business goals into marketing objectives” and communicate marketing briefs. Teamwork & Leadership is implied through partnering with the Marketing and Sales team. Learning & Applying Knowledge is also implied, since the role asks the person to study emerging technology and benchmark the company against others, which means always learning and using new information. Planning & Organisation appears in managing several campaigns at the same time.
Here the skills are applied to run digital campaigns and to measure their results through reports and dashboards. The most prioritised skills are Digital Literacy and analytical thinking, because the role is about using digital tools and data to improve engagement and conversion. As an entry-level role, it values a willingness to learn more than years of experience, which makes it a realistic first step for a graduate.
Job C – Marketing Supervisor (Sparklaxy Technology FZCO)
This is a more senior role at a technology company, leading marketing for several brands. Teamwork & Leadership is clearly prioritised, since the person must mentor talent, manage complex workflows, and lead a high-performance team. Digital Literacy is explicit and modern: the advert asks for “AI and Digital Fluency,” meaning the person must be comfortable with AI-driven marketing tools, analytics, and martech. Critical/Creative Thinking & Problem Solving appears in “strategic depth,” “creative leadership,” and turning customer insights into business-driving strategies. Initiative, Enterprise & Entrepreneurship is implied through finding opportunities that competitors miss and a growth-first mindset. Communication & Interpersonal Skills is stated through “exceptional written and verbal skills” and presenting to senior leaders. Financial Literacy is implied through KPIs, ROI, and acquisition costs, and Planning & Organisation through strong multitasking and decision-making skills.
In this role the skills are used to set strategy, lead people, and connect marketing to business results. The most prioritised skills are leadership, AI and digital fluency, and strategic thinking, because the company expects measurable business impact and wants someone who can use new technology to grow faster than competitors.
Commonalities and Industry Comment
When I look at the three jobs together, three employability skills appear in all of them: Communication & Interpersonal Skills, Digital Literacy, and Critical/Creative Thinking & Problem Solving. Even though the roles are at different levels, every employer wants someone who can use digital and data tools, think clearly about problems, and communicate the results to other people.
I believe these skills are preferred because marketing in Dubai has become very digital and data-driven. Businesses now run most of their campaigns online and measure almost everything, so they need people who are comfortable with analytics and AI tools. At the same time, marketing is still about people, so clear communication stays essential at every level. The senior role shows the next step for the industry: AI fluency is moving from a “nice to have” into a real requirement. This is the main reason I chose professional development courses in exactly these three skills, which I explain in Task 3.

I chose this course because it will help me make decisions based on data, and this is what employers want.

These three courses match my own goals and the skills that employers want. I believe they will help me become a better marketing graduate.
