# Chapter 2: Analysing the world of work and digital literacy

### 2. Digital Literacy

#### 2.3. Workplace digital literacy skills

**Digital literacy** is
a critical and strategic resource valued by employers and is a critical
skillset that employees need to function and perform duties in the
workplace.

Employers are looking more and more to hire
new workforces with a high degree of digital literacy. Due to rapid
changes and digitalisation, they need workforces that a highly-skilled
with advanced decision-making and problem-solving abilities and are
competent and adaptive. Nikou, De Reuver & Kanafi, 2022 argue that there are a **broad array of literacies that enable workers to interact with technology**and understand how to use it including information literacy, ICT (Information and Communications Technology) literacy, technological literacy, media literacy, net/online literacy.

How
employees (and prospective employees and contractors) perceive their
skill levels and have access to information and development impacts
their engagement in work and willingness to learn new platforms and
approaches.

A highly literate employee would be:

#### "more confident to adopt the technology and establish a positive attitude towards the use of technology [...] they may be more familiar with the content, interfaces, access choices, terminology and norms of new tools" (ibid).

#### **DIGITAL LITERACY**

Proficiency
with digital technology is not just being able to navigate a wide range
of applications from Microsoft Office to Social Media Email Services,
e-Learning platforms, design tools, etc for a variety of purposes.

 Digital
creation through coding and programming (Python or Javascript),
computational, systems and design thinking alongside AI &
communication with data analytics is increasingly important.
