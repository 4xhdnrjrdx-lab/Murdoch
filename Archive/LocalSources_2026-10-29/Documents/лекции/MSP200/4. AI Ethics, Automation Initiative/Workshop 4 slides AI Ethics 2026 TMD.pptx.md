# The Five R’s Framework for ReflectionReporting, Responding, Relating, Reasoning, ReconstructingTable below: based on that developed by Pauline Roberts (2016)

| Stage | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Reporting | Report what happened in this circumstance and what the situation involved. Why is it important or relevant? |
| Responding | Respond to the situation (or career learning experience) by observing, expressing your views, and asking questions. Examine your emotional reaction. |
| Relating | Relate and make connections between the situation (or career learning experience) and your own skills, professional experience, or discipline knowledge. Ask: Have I been through this before? Were the conditions the same last time, or different? Do I currently have the skills or knowledge to deal with this? |
| Reasoning | Identify significant factors underlying the circumstance or issue (or career learning experience). Explain and show why they are important in understanding your experience. Consider different perspectives. |
| Reconstructing | Reframe or reconstruct future practice or professional understanding. How would I address this next time? What might work and why? Are there different options for me to pursue next time? Why would happen if …? |

# Automation and Generative AI

“Automation, from industrial robots to automated document processing systems, continues to be the biggest factor in changing the demand for various occupations. Generative AI is both accelerating automation and extending it to an entirely new set of occupations.
“Automation has taken a leap forward with the recent introduction of generative AI tools. “Generative” refers to the fact that these tools can identify patterns across enormous sets of data and generate new content—an ability that has often been considered uniquely human. Their most striking advance is in natural language capabilities, which are required for a large number of work activities. While ChatGPT is focused on text, other AI systems from major platforms can generate images, video, and audio.”
(McKinsey, 2023)

# The future of work with AI (Cheok, Edirisinghe & Shrestha, 2025)

There is an emphasis on efficiency and productivity, with AI systems automating routine and repetitive tasks. This shift allows human workers to focus on more creative and strategic tasks.

# Automation and the importance lifelong learning

“The 21st century [has seen] digital transformation across numerous industries world-wide, leading to the emergence of new sectors and jobs that demand fresh skill sets while displacing many existing roles” (Lee & Nie, 2024, 3)
“These developments underscore the swift evolution of work, necessitating continuous upskilling, reskilling, and lifelong learning to enhance employability (…). A resilient workforce is demonstrated by its ability to adapt to change, recover swiftly from setbacks, and learn from past experiences” (Lee & Nie, 2024, 4)
In this context, we promote a culture of lifelong learning – equipping people with the skills, knowledge and attitudes that will help them address the challenge of increasing automation.

# Possible ways to address bias…

# A more positive view: AI-enabled leisure society

Cheok et al. (2025) refer to the idea of an emergence of an AI-enabled leisure society, where a reduced need for human labour will lead to a societal shift in focus from work to leisure.
Cheok at al. point to a possible redefinition of work, and the need to support lifelong learning (138).
Discussion:
In such a society, how would we manage the distribution of wealth, with a shift from full-time work to much less work, and increased leisure?
