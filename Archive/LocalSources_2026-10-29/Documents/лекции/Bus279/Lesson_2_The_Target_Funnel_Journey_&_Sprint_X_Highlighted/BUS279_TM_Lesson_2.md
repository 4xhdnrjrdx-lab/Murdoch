**СЛАЙД 16 — описание элементов персоны** _(подробный текст)_

- **Name:** Giving your persona a name (e.g., "Sarah the Student," "David the Dad") humanises them and makes them more relatable. It helps your team think of them as a real person.
- **Occupation:** What does this person do for a living? This gives you insight into their income level, daily routines, and potential needs related to your product or service. (e.g., "Software Engineer," "Stay-at-Home Parent," "Small Business Owner")
- **Brief Overview (Demographics & Psychographics):** This is a short summary of the persona's key characteristics, including:
  - **Demographics:** Age, gender, location, education, income, family status. (e.g., "35-year-old female, lives in a suburban area, has a master's degree, household income of $100,000, married with two children")
  - **Psychographics:** Values, interests, lifestyle, attitudes, and personality traits. (e.g., "Values family time, enjoys outdoor activities, environmentally conscious, early adopter of technology")
- **Motivations:** What drives this person? What are their goals and aspirations? What problems are they trying to solve? Understanding their motivations helps you tailor your marketing messages and product offerings to their needs. (e.g., "Wants to save time and money, seeks convenience, desires to improve their health, wants to impress their peers")
- **Purchase Patterns:** How does this person typically make purchasing decisions? Are they impulsive buyers or do they carefully research products before buying? What channels do they use to shop (online, in-store, mobile)? (e.g., "Researches products online before buying, reads reviews, compares prices, prefers online shopping with free shipping")
- **Pain Points:** What are this person's frustrations and challenges related to your product or service category? Addressing these pain points is a key way to attract and retain customers. (e.g., "Struggles to find time to cook healthy meals, frustrated with complicated software interfaces, concerned about the environmental impact of their purchases")
- **Purchase Frequency:** How often does this person typically buy products or services like yours? This helps you understand their customer lifetime value and tailor your marketing efforts accordingly. (e.g., "Buys groceries weekly, purchases new clothes quarterly, subscribes to a monthly service")
- **Social Influences:** Who or what influences this person's purchasing decisions? Do they rely on recommendations from friends and family, online reviews, social media influencers, or expert opinions? (e.g., "Influenced by online reviews, follows health and wellness influencers on social media, values recommendations from friends")

**СЛАЙД 22 — «Why is the marketing funnel so important?»**
The marketing funnel is a crucial concept in understanding how customers interact with a brand and make purchasing decisions. It helps visualise the journey a potential customer takes from initial awareness to becoming a loyal customer.
Why it's important:

- **Understanding Customer Behavior:** The funnel helps marketers understand the different stages customers go through, allowing them to tailor their marketing efforts at each stage.
- **Identifying Bottlenecks:** By analysing the funnel, businesses can identify where customers are dropping off. This allows them to pinpoint areas where they need to improve their marketing or customer experience.
- **Measuring Effectiveness:** The funnel provides a framework for tracking the effectiveness of marketing campaigns. By measuring conversions at each stage, businesses can see which strategies are driving the most leads and sales.
- **Optimising the Customer Journey:** The funnel highlights opportunities to optimise the customer experience at every stage. By addressing pain points and improving the overall journey, businesses can increase customer satisfaction and loyalty.

---

**СЛАЙД 23 — «The "Funnel" Analogy»**
The term "funnel" is used because, ideally, a large number of potential customers enter the top of the funnel (awareness), but as they move through the stages, the number decreases. Marketing plays a crucial role in setting customer expectations by communicating the benefits and features of a product or service. **СЛАЙД 57 — «Action/Inaction and Call to Action…»**
The "Action" phase of the sales funnel is the crucial point where potential customers take a desired step, moving them closer to becoming actual customers. However, the nature of this "action" varies significantly depending on the business, its goals, and the specific stage of the customer journey. It's not always about an immediate purchase. _(визуал: красная лестница вверх)_

---

**СЛАЙД 58 — «What is an 'action'?»**

- **Diverse Definitions of Action:** For an e-commerce store, "action" might be completing a purchase. But for a business focused on lead generation, it could be submitting a contact form, signing up for a newsletter (providing their email address), downloading a resource like an ebook, or requesting a demo. For content-driven websites, it could be clicking on a specific link to read a related article or watch a video. The key is that the "action" should align with the overall marketing objectives.
- **Calls to Action (CTAs) and Their Purpose:** CTAs are the prompts that encourage this desired action. They can be buttons, links, or even verbal cues. Effective CTAs are clear, concise, and compelling, telling the user exactly what to do and what they'll get in return. Examples include "Shop Now," "Sign Up for Free," "Download Your Guide," or "Learn More."
- **The Cart Abandonment Problem:** A customer might take the "action" of adding items to their online shopping cart, indicating strong purchase intent. However, they don't complete the checkout process. This is where the importance of subsequent CTAs and strategies comes into play.

---

**СЛАЙД 59 — «Driving Final Purchase»**
To combat cart abandonment, businesses employ various tactics:

- **Reminder Emails:** Automated emails reminding customers about the items in their cart, often including incentives like free shipping or a discount.
- **Retargeting Ads:** Displaying ads to users who have previously visited the website or added items to their cart, keeping the brand and product top-of-mind.
- **Streamlined Checkout Processes:** Simplifying the checkout process to reduce friction and minimise the chances of customers abandoning their purchase due to complexity or inconvenience.
- **Urgency and Scarcity:** Creating a sense of urgency through limited-time offers or low stock warnings can motivate customers to complete their purchase.

**СЛАЙД 66 — «Expectations vs Perception Outcomes — When we don't meet expectations…»** _(три колонки с иконками — текст не в экстракте)_

- **Complaints are more common than compliments:** It's generally accepted that people are more likely to voice their dissatisfaction than their satisfaction. This is often attributed to the negativity bias, where negative experiences tend to have a stronger impact on our emotions and memories than positive ones.
- **The "silent majority" of unhappy customers:** Studies indicate that a significant portion of unhappy customers don't even bother to complain. They simply take their business elsewhere. Estimates suggest that for every customer who complains, there are many others who remain silent.
- **Word-of-mouth spreads faster for negative experiences:** Research shows that dissatisfied customers tend to share their negative experiences with more people than satisfied customers share positive ones. This can have a significant impact on a business's reputation.

---

**СЛАЙД 67 — «Factors influencing complaining and complimenting behaviour»**

- **Severity of the issue:** People are more likely to complain about serious issues that have a significant impact on them.
- **Ease of complaining:** If it's easy to complain (e.g., through a simple online form or social media), people are more likely to do so.
- **Customer expectations:** If a customer has high expectations and those expectations are not met, they are more likely to complain.
- **Personal characteristics:** Some people are simply more likely to complain than others, regardless of the situation.
