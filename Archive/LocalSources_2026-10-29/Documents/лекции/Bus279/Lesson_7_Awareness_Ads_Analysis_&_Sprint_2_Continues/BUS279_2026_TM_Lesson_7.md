## Часть 1 — AWARENESS

### Slide 3 — Awareness

Awareness isn't just about being seen; it is about being seen by the right person, at the right time, with the right chemical trigger. We're going to use **Systems Thinking** to see how a single ad is actually part of a massive, data-driven organism.

### Slide 4 — Awareness vs. Attention

The Battle for the **Reticular Activating System (RAS)**.

### Slide 5 — The Core Concept

In a world of infinite scrolling, "Reach" is a vanity metric. Just because an ad appeared on a screen (Awareness) does not mean it was processed by the human brain (Attention). As an expert marketer, your goal isn't just to be "seen" — it is to penetrate the user's mental filters.

### Slide 6 — 1. The Novice Trap: The "Bill Board" Mentality

The novice thinks that if they pay for 100,000 impressions, they have "reached" 100,000 people. They treat social media like a digital billboard on a highway. But in the digital age, users have developed **"Banner Blindness."** Their brains are trained to filter out anything that looks like a traditional advertisement.

### Slide 7 — 2. The Expert Insight: The RAS Filter

The human brain has a gateway called the **Reticular Activating System (RAS)**. It is a bundle of nerves in our brainstem that filters out "noise" and only lets in "signals" that are important, dangerous, or rewarding.

- **Awareness** is hitting the filter.
- **Attention** is passing through it.

### Slide 8 — 3. The Chemical Trigger

To turn Awareness into Attention, you must trigger a micro-dose of **Dopamine** (Curiosity/Reward) or **Cortisol** (Urgency/Problem). If your Velocity Fit ad looks like every other fitness ad, the RAS marks it as "Noise" and the user scrolls. To get Attention, you need a **Pattern Interrupt**.

### Slide 9 — 4. Systems Thinking Application

Awareness is the **Input** of your marketing system. If the quality of your Attention is low at Slide 1, your Conversion at Slide 15 will be zero, no matter how good your product is. You cannot fix a "Conversion Problem" if you actually have an "Attention Problem."

### Slide 10 — A few interesting concepts here to be aware of

### Slide 11 — The "Diderot Effect"

Ever bought a new pair of shoes, and then realised your old jeans didn't look good with them, so you bought new jeans, then a new belt, then a new shirt?

- **The Origin:** Named after the French philosopher Denis Diderot, who was gifted a beautiful scarlet robe.
- **The Spiral:** He felt his old desk looked "shabby" next to the robe, so he replaced it. Then he replaced his chairs, his prints, and his clock. He ended up in debt and miserable.
- **The Takeaway:** In Marketing, this is the **"Complementary Goods"** strategy. We don't just buy a product; we buy an identity. Once you buy one piece of that identity, your brain feels "incomplete" until you buy the rest.

This, alongside the **halo effect**, forms a symmetry in which people will buy more of your products if they are very happy with one. As marketers we can use this to our advantage. However, this can also be problematic. For example, if your other products aren't as good as your hero product, and the difference in quality is above the **just noticeable difference (JND, also called Weber's Law)**, there will be a **reward prediction error**, which could backfire.

### Slide 12 — The "Mere Exposure" Effect

- **The Concept:** People tend to develop a preference for things merely because they are familiar with them.
- **The Experiment:** In a study, a stranger sat in a university lecture hall for several weeks but never spoke. At the end of the term, students rated that stranger as more "likeable" and "trustworthy" than other people, simply because they had seen them more often.
- **The Lesson:** This is why companies spend millions on billboards and logos. They don't need you to read the ad; they just need your brain to "register" the logo so that when you're at the supermarket, you'll subconsciously choose the familiar brand over the unknown one.

But be careful here. If, as mentioned before, they go completely unnoticed, they will not have the same impact.

### Slide 13 — The "Baader-Meinhof" Phenomenon

Have you ever learned a new word or a fact, and then suddenly you see it everywhere for the next three days? This is called **Frequency Illusion**. Your brain isn't actually seeing it more; it's just that now that the information is "tagged" as important, your reticular activating system (the brain's filter) stops ignoring it and starts pointing it out to you.

### Slide 14 — The Signal and the Static: Navigating the Attention Economy

We live in a state of **Continuous Partial Attention**. Every day, the average person is bombarded by thousands of "Noise" inputs: notifications, billboards, emails, and social media ads. To survive this sensory overload, our brains have become masters of Invisibility. We don't just ignore ads; our Reticular Activating System (RAS) prevents us from even seeing them.

### Slide 15 — 1. The Invisible Filter (Noise)

A brand like Oppo faces an **"Attention Tax."** Because they haven't yet established a "Constellation of Variables" in the average Western consumer's mind, their ads are treated as **Environmental Noise**. Unless they spend massive amounts of money to "Push through the filter" with a loud pattern interrupt, the brain simply categorises them as "unimportant static" and discards the visual data before it reaches conscious thought.

### Slide 16 — 2. Brand Equity: External vs. Internal

The breakthrough happens when a company shifts its Brand Equity from the external (what they tell you they are) to the internal (what you know they are).

- **External Equity:** A company yelling for attention, trying to prove their value. This creates **Visual Friction**.
- **Internal Equity:** When the brand lives inside the consumer's "Mental Map." You aren't just seeing a logo; you are triggering a history of trust, quality, and status.

### Slide 17 — 3. The "Constellation of Variables" (The Apple Effect)

When you see an Apple ad, your brain doesn't have to work hard. It already holds a "Constellation" of data points: Sleek UI, Privacy, High Resale Value, Blue Bubbles, and Innovation. Because this constellation already exists, the ad doesn't have to fight to be noticed; it glows against the noise. The brain recognises the "Signal" immediately, triggering a hit of **Oxytocin** (Trust) and **Dopamine** (Anticipation). For Apple, awareness is almost "free" because the internal equity has already done the heavy lifting.

### Slide 18 — 4. The Expert Lesson

As an expert marketer, you must realise that you aren't just designing an ad; you are trying to add a new star to your brand's constellation.

- If you are a new brand, you are currently "Invisible".
- You cannot rely on people "recognising" you.
- You must use **Pattern Interrupts** that are directional and **Consistent Value** to slowly build that internal equity until you finally break through the noise floor.

We have a 'constellation' for everything. When you see a chair, your conscious mind thinks 'chair' but subconsciously you see legs, arm rests, the seat; you already have an idea of how to sit on it, whether you can trust sitting on it, whether it looks comfortable or not, etc. We do this with everything. The world is made up of variables. If you take two brands — Apple vs Oppo — if you haven't interacted with Oppo, but you have heard of them, you will still have a constellation of variables (looks cheap/expensive, easy/difficult to use, high/low quality). However, this constellation hardly does anything when someone says the word 'Oppo'. For Apple, you've interacted with it, so even though it's also a constellation of variables, because you've used it and feel more comfortable with it, your 'Apple constellation' lights up when someone says 'Apple'.

### Slide 19 — Signal vs. Noise — The "Reach" Trap

**The Core Concept:** A novice marketer asks, "How many people saw it?" An expert marketer asks, "Who actually noticed it?" In Awareness, massive numbers are often a mask for massive failure. We must distinguish between **Broad Noise** (accidental views) and **High-Intent Signals** (conscious attention).

### Slide 20 — 1. The Novice View: The "Volume" Fallacy

Novices hunt for "Virality." They believe 1 million views on a generic TikTok dance is better than 10,000 views on a product demo. They equate Reach with Revenue. However, if those 1 million people are "scrollers" who don't fit your target market, you haven't gained awareness; you've just created a momentary flicker of light in a dark room.

### Slide 21 — 2. The Expert View: The "Quality" Filter

Experts value **High-Intent Views**.

- **1,000,000 Low-Intent Views:** People who saw your ad because the algorithm forced it on them. They have zero "Itch" for your product. (High Noise).
- **10,000 High-Intent Views:** People who stopped scrolling because the "Hook" identified a specific problem they have. (High Signal).

### Slide 22 — 3. Why 10k > 1M

- **Data Integrity:** 1 million random views feed "Dirty Data" into your Meta/Google pixel. The AI starts trying to find more random people, ruining your targeting.
- **The CPA Trap:** Low-intent views have a 0% conversion rate, which drives your Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) through the roof.

### Slide 23 — 4. Neuromarketing: Avoiding the "Glaze"

When users scroll past "Noise," their brains are in a low-energy, **theta-wave** state, essentially "glazing over." To create a Signal, you need to trigger an active **beta-wave** state by presenting a Pattern Interrupt that matters only to your specific market.

### Slide 24 — The Omnichannel Organism — Connecting the Dots

**The Core Concept:** Awareness does not exist in a vacuum. In a Systems Thinking model, your brand is an organism with multiple "sensors." TikTok might be the **Eyes** (Awareness), but Google Search is the **Hands** (Intent/Action).

### Slide 25 — 1. The Awareness-Intent Bridge

When a Murdoch student sees a 'Velocity Fit' ad on TikTok, they rarely click "Buy" immediately. Instead, the ad creates a mental "Star" in their constellation.

- **The Trigger:** TikTok Awareness ad spikes Dopamine.
- **The Search:** Two hours later, the student goes to Google and types "AI Gym App CBD."

### Slide 26 — 2. Feeding the System

If you have high awareness on Social Media but you don't "own" your search terms on Google, you are essentially paying to send customers to your competitors.

- **Awareness (TikTok/IG):** Creates the "Itch."
- **Search (Google/YouTube):** Provides the "Scratch."

### Slide 27 — 3. Cross-Channel Attribution

As an expert, you don't just look at TikTok's dashboard. You look at the **Direct Search Volume** on your website. If your TikTok ad goes live and "Google Searches" for your brand name increase by 20%, your Awareness System is working, even if the TikTok "Click" count is low.

### Slide 28 — The Awareness Flywheel — Building the Strategic Fortress

**The Core Concept:** Consistency in awareness builds a **Flywheel Effect**. The harder you work at the start to "push" through the noise, the easier it becomes to stay visible later. This creates a **Strategic Fortress** that protects you from competitors.

### Slide 29 — 1. Lowering the "Friction Tax"

The first time a user sees Velocity Fit, there is high Cognitive Friction (Who are they? Can I trust them?). The 10th time they see you, that friction is gone. **The Result:** Your CPA drops over time because you no longer have to "introduce" yourself. You are already part of their internal "Constellation."

### Slide 30 — 2. The Fortress of Trust (Oxytocin)

Consistent awareness builds Oxytocin. If the market sees you everywhere (Influencers, Ads, PR), they subconsciously assume you are the "Market Leader." This "Social Proof" makes it harder for a new competitor to break in. They would have to spend 3x more than you just to be noticed.

### Slide 31 — Something to be aware of (CAC vs LTV)

The focus on Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) over Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) is a classic "Short-Term" glitch driven by the pressure of Market Penetration. While it is scientifically proven that retaining an existing customer is **5 to 25 times cheaper** than acquiring a new one, corporations often prioritise the "New Lead" because it provides a visible Stimulus of Growth to shareholders and boards. In their eyes, a "Happy" existing customer is a static data point, whereas a new acquisition is a "Victory" that proves the brand is expanding its Constellation.

However, this "Growth at all costs" strategy often leads to a **Service Vacuum**, where the Friction for existing customers increases, leading to a **Defection** that the company then tries to fix by spending even more on acquisition. It's a **leaky bucket** problem: they are so obsessed with the "River" of new traffic that they forget to maintain the "Dam" that keeps the value in. The goal is to realise that true Market Power comes from **Density, not just Reach**; making your current circle "happy" lowers your Indirect CAC naturally through the most powerful pattern interrupt of all: **Word of Mouth**.

### Slide 32 — 3. The Flywheel Math

- **Phase 1:** High spend, low recognition (Pushing the wheel).
- **Phase 2:** Moderate spend, high recognition (The wheel spins itself).
- **Phase 3:** Awareness becomes "Internal Brand Equity." You can now launch new products (e.g., Velocity Fit Smart Watch) with 0% awareness cost.

### Slide 33 — 4. Systems Thinking: Cumulative Advantage

Every dollar spent on awareness today is an investment in making tomorrow's ads 10% cheaper. You aren't just buying "views"; you are buying **Market Gravity**.

### Slide 34 — The Anatomy of Success — High-Equity Awareness

When a brand moves from "External Noise" to "Internal Equity," awareness becomes a superpower. Global giants use systems and neuromarketing to own the "Signal."

### Slide 35 — 1. Red Bull: The Content System

Red Bull doesn't sell a drink; they sell "Extreme Lifestyle."

- **The Awareness Play:** They sponsor high-dopamine stunts (like Felix Baumgartner's space jump).
- **The Strategy:** They created a media house. They don't just "run ads"; they provide the content that news outlets want to share.
- **The Result:** By the time you see a Red Bull can in a shop, you've already associated it with courage and adrenaline. The "Itch" for energy is pre-scratched by the content.

### Slide 36 — 2. Liquid Death: Pattern Interrupt Mastery

They took the most boring product (water) and put it in a beer-style tallboy can with a heavy metal aesthetic.

- **The Pattern Interrupt:** In a supermarket aisle full of blue, serene plastic bottles, a skull-branded can creates Visual Friction. The brain has to look at it because it doesn't fit the "Water" category.
- **The Awareness Logic:** They use dark humour to trigger Dopamine in a demographic (Gen Z/Millennials) that usually hates corporate ads.

### Slide 37 — The "Lightning in a Bottle" — Managing Virality

**The Core Concept:** Virality is not a strategy; it is a **System Input**. You cannot force it, but you must be ready to capture it. Virality without a conversion path is just "Expensive Noise."

### Slide 38 — 1. The Virality Strategy: "The Catch Basin"

If the Velocity Fit app goes viral, what happens to those 5 million viewers?

- **The Sensor:** You need a "Lead Magnet" (e.g., a free 7-day AI Form Check).
- **The Path:** You must have your HubSpot/CRM automation ready to tag those viral leads immediately.
- **The Remarketing:** Once they leave the viral video, do you have a retargeting ad ready to follow them?

### Slide 39 — When Virality Fails — The "Static" Graveyard

**Example A: Nathan Apodaca (The Ocean Spray Skateboarder)**

- The Moment: He went viral skating to Fleetwood Mac while drinking Ocean Spray. It was pure Oxytocin (Trust/Chill).
- The Failure: Ocean Spray was slow to react. By the time they bought him a truck, the "Signal" had already started to fade. They didn't have a system to turn that momentary awareness into a long-term subscription or loyalty program. They got the "Noise," but missed the "Equity."

**Example B: Sunny Co Clothing (The Red Swimsuit)**

- The Moment: They offered a free red swimsuit to everyone who reposted their photo. It went too viral.
- The Failure: They didn't have the Systems Thinking to handle the logistics. They couldn't fulfil the orders, the comments turned toxic, and the brand's reputation was destroyed.

**The Lesson:** They focused on Reach but forgot that scaling can be a 'monster'.

### Slide 40 — The Final Takeaway: Awareness as Gravity (1–2)

1. **The Reach Fallacy:** Reach is simply the opportunity for a human brain to ignore you. Awareness is only achieved when the "Noise" of your brand becomes a "Signal" that the consumer can no longer filter out. If you are not building a Constellation of Variables, you are just making noise.
2. **The Equity Shift:** Success is measured by the transition of your brand from the External (where you have to pay to be noticed) to the Internal (where you are already a part of the consumer's mental map). Once you live inside their head, the "Friction" of selling to them evaporates.

### Slide 41 — The Final Takeaway: Awareness as Gravity (3–4)

3. **The Systems Rule:** An ad is never a solo performance; it is part of an Omnichannel Organism. Awareness on TikTok is the "Itch," and Google Search is the "Scratch." If your system has a gap between awareness and intent, you are essentially paying to educate your audience for your competitor's benefit.
4. **The Virality Lesson:** Virality is a lightning strike. You cannot predict it, but you can build the Lightning Rod. The difference between a brand that survives a viral moment and one that fades into the "Static Graveyard" is the presence of a System (CRM, Lead Magnets, and Retargeting) ready to capture that energy and turn it into Internal Equity.

## Часть 2 — ADS

### Slide 42 — The Ad Ecosystem — The High Cost of "Guessing"

Ads are the **Engines** of your marketing system. Like any engine, they require fuel (Money) and tuning (A/B Testing). A novice sees ads as a "cheap" way to get sales; an expert sees ads as a high-stakes auction where the wrong strategy leads to immediate financial haemorrhaging.

### Slide 43 — 1. The CAC Reality Check (Cost Per Acquisition)

"Cheap" ads are a myth. As more brands flood the digital space, the cost to "buy" a customer (CAC) is rising across all platforms.

- **Meta (Facebook/Instagram):** Typically $20–$60 per acquisition for apps/consumer goods. Relies on Interruption Marketing; you are stopping their scroll.
- **Google Search:** Often $30–$100+. You pay a premium because the user has High Intent (already looking for the "Scratch").
- **TikTok:** Highly variable ($10–$50). Lower cost but much higher "Creative Decay"; the ads stop working faster because the audience craves novelty.

### Slide 44 — 2. The "No One Size Fits All" Rule

An ad is **User-Dependent**.

- **The 19-year-old student:** Needs a high-Dopamine, fast-cut TikTok ad that feels like User-Generated Content (UGC).
- **The 45-year-old Manager:** Might respond better to a high-Oxytocin, benefit-driven LinkedIn sponsored post that promises "Efficiency."

If you send the "Manager ad" to the "Student," your CAC will be $200 because the RAS Filter will reject it as "Noise" and cutting through that noise will be much more expensive.

### Slide 45 — 3. Strategy: What is the Goal?

Before you spend $1, you must decide the ad's job:

- **Awareness:** "I exist." (Metric: CPM — Cost per 1,000 impressions).
- **Attention:** "Look at this specific feature." (Metric: Watch Time).
- **Conversion:** "Buy/Download now." (Metric: CPA — Cost Per Action).

### Slide 46 — 4. The Expert Weapon: Strategic A/B Testing

Because ads are expensive, we don't guess. We use A/B testing as **Financial Insurance**. The Variable Test: You test two headlines. If Headline A has a 2% click rate and Headline B has a 4% rate, you just halved your costs by simply looking at the data.

### Slide 47 — 5. Future Trends: AI and Hyper-Personalisation

The future of ads is Generative and Dynamic.

- **AI-Driven Creative:** Tools now create 1,000 variations of an ad, changing the background, music, and actor to match the specific person watching it.
- **The Death of the "Standard" Ad:** In 5 years, we won't make "an ad." We will provide the AI with "Brand Assets" (Logos, Benefits, Tone) and the AI will assemble a unique ad in real-time for every individual user.

### Slide 48 — Real-World Success: The "Frankenstein" Strategy

Large companies like Amazon and Airbnb use **"Dynamic Creative Optimisation."** They don't just show you a house; they show you a house with a pool because they know you searched for pools last week. They use data to remove every ounce of Visual Friction. **The Expert Takeaway:** If your ad strategy is "I like this picture," you aren't a marketer; you're an artist. If your strategy is "Version B is outperforming Version A by 12% in the 18-24 demographic," you are an Expert.

### Slide 49 — The Anatomy of a Successful Ad — Beyond the Platform

**The Core Concept:** There are too many social platforms to master each one individually. If you try to learn "The TikTok Trick" or "The Meta Hack," you are chasing ghosts. Instead, we must take a step back and look at the **Universal Pattern** of successful advertising. An ad is not a picture; it is a psychological and systemic intervention.

### Slide 50 — 1. The Universal Chemistry of Persuasion

Regardless of the platform, expert ads follow a biological sequence:

- **The Hook (Dopamine & Hype):** We trigger curiosity or anticipation. We sell the "Dream" or the "Result."
- **The Bridge (Oxytocin & Story):** We build trust. We don't just show a product; we weave a story or show a relatable human experience.
- **The Scratch (Action):** We provide the solution to the "Itch."

**The Golden Rule:** When ads try to simply sell, they become Noise. When ads try to solve or entertain, they become Signal.

### Slide 51 — 2. The Attention Caveat: The "Vampire Effect"

Humorous, shocking, or sexy ads are great at drawing attention, but they carry a massive risk: **The Vampire Effect**. **Ad-Focused vs. Brand-Focused:** People often remember the hilarious joke or the beautiful model, but they cannot remember the brand. If the ad is too far removed from the brand's core function, the "Constellation of Variables" never forms. The ad "sucks the life" out of the brand. This is why **Marketing Communications (Comms)** is a specialised field; it's the art of ensuring the "Hook" actually leads back to the "Brand."

### Slide 52 — 3. Cutting the Clutter: The Toolset

To cut through the noise floor, we use a variety of tactical "Sensors":

- **Sponsorships & Affiliations:** Borrowing trust (Oxytocin) from someone the audience already likes.
- **Google Ads:** Capturing existing intent (The "Scratch").
- **Remarketing:** Following the user to remind them of the "Itch" they haven't scratched yet.

### Slide 53 — 4. Systems Thinking: The Ad is Just One Gear

An expert never looks at an ad in isolation. If an ad has a high Click-Through Rate (CTR) but people "fall off" at the checkout, the ad isn't the problem, the **System** is.

- **The Drop-Off:** If they click the ad but stall at the payment gate, you might have a **Friction Wall** (too many forms, high shipping costs, or lack of trust).
- **The Machine:** Your ad is the "Input." If the "Output" (Sales) is low, you must audit the entire machine, not just the first gear.

### Slide 54 — 5. The "Expert" Methodology: Test, Test, Test

We don't "know" what people want; we let the data tell us. We test Headline A vs. Headline B. We test Video A vs. Video B. We look at the "Fall Off" points in the customer journey to see where the Visual or Cognitive Friction is highest.

## Часть 3 — ANALYSIS

### Slide 55 — The "Truth" in the Machine

**The Core Concept:** In an expert marketing system, Analysis is the **feedback loop** that prevents you from wasting money. A novice looks at "Vanity Metrics" (likes, shares) and feels good. An expert looks at "Value Metrics" (conversions, CAC, retention) and makes adjustments. Analysis is how we find the "9" and the "11" in our data sequence.

### Slide 56 — 1. The Hierarchy of Metrics

Not all data points are created equal. Separate the Signal from the Noise.

- **Vanity Metrics (The Noise):** Likes, Follows, Impressions. Feel good but don't pay the bills. Potential attention, not actual intent.
- **Performance Metrics (The Signal):**
  - **Hook Rate:** (3-Second Views / Impressions). Tells you if your Awareness strategy is working. Did you stop the scroll?
  - **Hold Rate:** (Average Watch Time). Tells you if your Content is valuable. Did you keep them after the hook?
  - **CTR (Click-Through Rate):** Tells you if your Call to Action (CTA) was effective.
  - **CPA (Cost Per Acquisition):** The final truth. How much did it cost to get one person to subscribe to Velocity Fit?

### Slide 57 — 2. Systems Thinking: Identifying the "Leak"

Analysis lets us see the marketing funnel as a pipe. If you pour 1,000 people in the top and only 2 come out the bottom, you have a leak.

- **Leak at the Top:** High impressions, low Hook Rate. (Your creative is "Noise").
- **Leak in the Middle:** High Hook Rate, low Click-Through. (Your story didn't build enough Oxytocin to drive action).
- **Leak at the Bottom:** High Clicks, zero Sales. (You have a Friction Wall at the checkout or the app's UI is confusing).

### Slide 58 — 3. The "Why" Behind the Numbers

Data tells you **what** happened; Neuromarketing tells you **why**. If your analysis shows a massive drop-off at the "AI Form Check" screen, the data says "People are leaving." The expert analysis says "The camera request is triggering a **Cortisol Spike** (Privacy fear/Self-consciousness). We need to change the copy to build more trust."

### Slide 59 — 4. Real-World Example: Netflix's Granular Analysis

Netflix doesn't just look at "Views." They look at:

- **Completion Rate:** Did you finish the episode?
- **Rewatch Value:** Did you replay a specific scene?
- **The "Drop-Off" Point:** If 40% of people stop watching a show at the 12-minute mark of Episode 1, they know exactly where the script failed to "Scratch the Itch."

### Slide 60 — On innovation

In marketing, there is a dangerous romance with the word "Innovation." We often picture a lone genius in a lab creating a "disruptor" that changes the world. But for established giants like Kellogg's and Hershey's, the strategy has shifted from the "Big Bang" of innovation to the "Precision Engine" of data.

### Slide 61 — The Pivot: From Innovation to Data

For decades, Kellogg's and Hershey's lived or died by **New Product Development (NPD)**. Innovation meant a new cereal flavour or a new candy bar shape: a high-stakes gamble on grocery store shelf space. Today, they have shifted their "Brand Equity" from external guessing to internal data.

### Slide 62 — Kellogg's

They now use a system called **KUBE (Kellogg Unit Brand Engagement)**. Instead of just inventing a new cereal and hoping people like it, they use real-time data to see exactly which blogs and social content their "Nutri-Grain" audience is reading. In one case, they used machine learning to optimise search terms for their "Bare Naked" granola, increasing sales by **112%** simply by using data to outmanoeuvre competitors in search results.

### Slide 63 — Hershey's

Hershey's has built an **"Intelligence Engine"** that pairs decades of retail experience with real-time consumer data. They use AI to "interrogate" their media spend. If an ad isn't working in a specific city, the AI automatically pulls the budget and moves it to a location where sales are underperforming. They aren't trying to "innovate" a new way to eat chocolate; they are using data to ensure the chocolate is in front of the right person at the exact moment they have an "Itch."

### Slide 64 — Innovation is a Luxury; Iteration is a Strategy

Innovation is essential, but it is brutally expensive. For a new disruptor (like Tesla in 2010), innovation is the only way in. But for an established business, pure innovation has a high "Miss Rate." **The Expert Insight:** One wrong move in "Radical Innovation" can end a business. However, a business that focuses on **Data-Driven Iteration** is virtually unkillable.

**Iteration > Innovation:** Iteration is the process of making small, data-backed improvements to things that already work. Small Innovations (the "Quiet" Wins):

- **Process Innovation:** Using data to make your checkout 10% faster (The "Easy Path").
- **Service Innovation:** Offering a "Snooze" button on your subscription instead of a "Cancel" button.
- **Marketing Innovation:** Changing a headline from "Get Fit" to "Fix Your Form" because A/B testing showed a 40% higher click rate.

### Slide 65 — The Power of Granular Data (The Meta/Google "Scalpel")

In 2026, we don't just "advertise to people interested in fitness." We use a scalpel:

- **Retention Bidding (Google Ads):** Tell Google AI to specifically bid higher for "Lapsed Customers" — people who used to love you but haven't bought in 3 months.
- **Omni-channel Bidding:** Track a user who saw an ad on their phone, went into a physical store in the CBD, then bought later on their laptop. The data bridges the physical and digital world.
- **Dynamic Creative (Meta):** Provide the AI with 10 headlines and 10 images. The AI assembles a custom ad for every user. The 19-year-old sees the high-Dopamine, fast-cut video; the 45-year-old sees the high-Oxytocin, trust-based testimonial. The AI does the A/B testing in real-time.

### Slide 66 — The 2026 Data Reality: Privacy as a Friction Point

In 2026, the marketing "Scalpel" is sharper than ever, but the walls are getting thicker. Between increasingly harsh crackdowns on data collection, Apple's privacy lockdowns, and the Privacy Sandbox, the era of "Passive Tracking" is dead. If you rely solely on an AI's "guess" to find your audience, you are building your brand in a fog.

### Slide 67 — 1. Data is Difficult (And That's a Good Thing)

- **The Privacy Arms Race:** Users are retreating into Sovereign Shells. They are opting out of tracking and ignoring "Cookie Walls."
- **The Trust Tax:** To get data in 2026, you must pay for it with Value. Users perform a "Privacy-Cost Audit" before every click.
- **The Glitch:** Most companies are still trying to "rent the rain" (buying ads) rather than "owning the soil" (building their own database).

### Slide 68 — 2. The Shift: First-Party vs. Modelled Data

- **Renting the Rain:** Third-party tracking is now "Modelled Data" — an AI's best guess. High-noise and high-friction.
- **Owning the Soil:** First-Party Data is information given explicitly to you. When someone signs up to your newsletter, that is a **High-Fidelity Signal**.
- **The Value Exchange:** In 2026, data is a **Social Contract**. You don't "take" data; you earn it by offering a Resolution (e.g., exclusives or better products).

### Slide 69 — 1. Data Removes the "Founder Bias"

Many founders fail because they "love" their innovation too much. They ignore the "9" and "11" in the sequence because they are stuck on the "5." Data is the "cold shower" that tells you your idea might be bad; but it also tells you how to fix it.

### Slide 70 — 2. The "Fall-Off" Audit

If you focus on innovation but ignore the System, you are wasting money. If your ad gets 1 million clicks (Innovation!) but your landing page takes 4 seconds to load (Process Failure), your data will show a 90% Drop-Off. An expert uses analysis to find these "leaks" in the pipe. You don't need a new ad; you need a faster website.

### Slide 71 — 3. The "Strategic Fortress"

Data allows you to build a fortress. When you know your customer's **Lifetime Value (LTV)**, you know exactly how much you can afford to spend on an ad (CAC). If a competitor tries to move in, you can outspend them because your "Data Engine" is more efficient. **The Final Rule:** Unless you are a brand-new disruptor with nothing to lose, stop trying to "Reinvent the Wheel." Start using data to make the wheel 1% smoother every single day.

### Slide 72 — Data as the Strategy — The "Precision" Pivot

**The Core Concept:** For established giants like Kellogg's or Hershey's, "Innovation" isn't a guess in a test kitchen; it's a data-led iteration. Unless you are a brand-new disruptor with zero to lose, pure innovation is a high-stakes luxury. For everyone else, Data-Driven Iteration is the safer, faster, and more profitable path to growth.

### Slide 73 — 1. The Corporate Shift: Innovation vs. Iteration

- **The Problem with Pure Innovation:** It is brutally expensive. One "Big Bang" failure (a new product that doesn't sell) can bankrupt a small business or sink a CEO's career.
- **The Iteration Strategy:** Instead of reinventing the wheel, we use data to make the wheel 1% smoother every day.

### Slide 74 — 2. Types of "Small" (and Better) Innovation

Innovation isn't just a new product. Data allows for:

- **Process Innovation:** Making the "Easy Path" faster (e.g., reducing checkout clicks from 5 to 2).
- **Service Innovation:** Changing how you deliver value based on user fall-off.
- **Marketing Innovation:** Using A/B testing to find the "Pattern Interrupt" that cuts through the noise.

### Slide 75 — 3. The Precision Scalpel: Granular Targeting

We no longer "blast" ads; we perform surgery:

- **Retention Bidding:** Find "Lapsed Customers" (used the app for 3 weeks and stopped) and send a specific "We miss you" trigger.
- **Custom Audience Lookalikes:** Give Meta the data of your top 1,000 "Bec's Journey" subscribers and say, "Find me 100,000 more people exactly like these."
- **Dynamic Creative Optimisation (DCO):** The AI assembles a unique ad for every user — background, person, music — based on that user's historical data. _(Big caveat: this is very behaviour-specific, which ignores a lot of the psychographic elements we look out for. An astounding leap in advertising, but with pitfalls.)_

### Slide 76 — 4. Data is Your "Founder Bias" Cure

Many founders fail because they "love" their initial innovation too much. They ignore the "9" because they're emotionally attached to the "5." **The Data Truth:** Data is a cold shower. It tells you your favourite ad is failing. It tells you your "innovative" form-check is causing Cortisol Spikes. **The Pivot:** A business with a data strategy can pivot toward what works before the money runs out.

### Slide 77 — The "Nervous System" — Marketing Technology (MarTech)

**The Core Concept:** Analysis is impossible without the right infrastructure. If your data is scattered across spreadsheets, you don't have a system; you have a mess. To act like an expert, connect your "Sensors" (Social Media) to your "Brain" (CRM). This lets you track a user from the very first ad they see to the moment they become a long-term subscriber.

### Slide 78 — 1. The CRM: The "Brain" (Salesforce / HubSpot)

A CRM platform like Salesforce is where the "Constellation of Variables" lives for every individual user.

- **Tracking Progress:** Not just about the sale. Salesforce tracks the "Lead Status." Example: "User 402 watched the Bec's Journey video, downloaded the app, but stalled at the AI Form Check."
- **The Expert Move:** Use this data to trigger an automated email or a "Remarketing" ad specifically for people stuck at that stage. You are using the software to perform Process Innovation in real-time.

### Slide 79 — 2. Social Listening & Management: The "Ears" (Hootsuite / Sprout Social)

You can't manually check every comment on every platform. Tools like Hootsuite use **sentiment analysis** to flag whether conversations are mostly positive, neutral, or negative.

- Hootsuite's social listening tracks brand mentions and keywords. If many users start calling the Velocity Fit app 'too glitchy' on Android, it can surface that trend early so you can respond before it escalates.
- You can benchmark competitors' reach and engagement. If a rival app's posts suddenly spike in the CBD, monitoring shows that lift and signals it may be time to defend your 'Strategic Fortress' with more local ad spend.
- **One big thing to keep in mind:** Not everyone is on social media. You have a large 'middle' cohort who don't leave reviews, don't comment online, and just silently 'quit' your brand. While there might be a lot of 'noise' regarding your brand, don't act too hastily in any direction.

### Slide 80 — 3. The Integrated Organism: Bridging the Gap

The real magic happens when these tools talk to each other. This is Systems Thinking in practice:

- Hootsuite identifies a viral trend or a common "Itch" in the fitness community.
- Meta/Google Ads launches an ad addressing that specific "Itch."
- Salesforce tracks who clicked and who dropped off.
- The Analysis tells you the CAC of that specific campaign.

### Slide 81 — 4. Don't Get "Tool-Drunk"

Novices buy every piece of software available and hope it solves their problems. Experts know that **Software is just an accelerator**. If your strategy is bad, Salesforce will just help you fail faster and more expensively. Use the software to find the "Fall-Off" points. If Salesforce shows that 80% of your users quit after Day 3, you don't need more ads; you need to iterate on your app's "Dopamine" triggers for new users.

### Slide 82 — The Takeaway

In 2026, the best marketer isn't the best artist; they are the best **Systems Architect**. You use Hootsuite to listen, Ads to speak, and Salesforce to remember.

## Часть 4 — SYNTHESIS

### Slide 83 — The Synthesis: The "Full-Stack" Marketer

The final piece of the puzzle is **Synthesis**: understanding that a marketing system is only as strong as its weakest link. You can have the best ad in the world, but if your data analysis is wrong, or your CRM isn't tracking the lead, you are just "leaking" money into the digital void.

### Slide 84 — 1. The Three-Pillar Check

As an expert, before you launch a campaign, you perform a **System Audit**:

- **Awareness Check:** Is this a "Pattern Interrupt" or am I just adding to the Noise Floor? Does this help build my Internal Brand Equity (The Constellation)?
- **Ads Check:** Am I A/B testing my creative variables? Have I calculated my maximum allowable CAC based on my customer's value?
- **Analysis Check:** Do I have my "Sensors" (Hootsuite) and "Brain" (Salesforce) connected? Do I know where the Fall-Off point is in the customer journey?

### Slide 85 — 2. The "Iteration" Mindset vs. The "Ego" Mindset

A big takeaway: data is crucial. Remove the emotion and work on logic. The **Novice** gets angry when their "innovative" ad fails. The **Expert** thanks the data for showing them the failure early. By focusing on Iteration (like Kellogg's or Hershey's), you aren't looking for a "one-hit-wonder" viral video; you are building an **Evergreen Growth Machine** that gets 1% better every week.

### Slide 86 — 3. Systems Thinking: The "Invisible" Value

Much of an expert marketer's work is invisible — hours spent in Salesforce cleaning data, in Hootsuite listening to sentiment, or in the Ads Manager killing underperforming creatives. **Awareness** is the flashy front-end. **Analysis** is the engine room. Without the engine room, the ship doesn't move.

### Slide 87 — Failure is a Feature, Not a Bug

**The Core Concept:** In the "Expert" mindset, there is no such thing as a failed ad; there is only a **Data Collection Exercise**. Fear of failure is the greatest barrier to growth because it prevents the experimentation necessary to find your "11." You will never reach everyone, nor should you want to.

### Slide 88 — 1. The "Everyone" Fallacy

Novices are terrified of negative comments or low reach because they want to please "everyone." Experts realise that "everyone" is a distraction. If you try to speak to everyone, you end up speaking to no one. **The Goal:** To become the "Signal" for your Target Market and remain "Noise" for everyone else.

### Slide 89 — 2. Redefining Failure: The Iteration Mindset

If you launch a Velocity Fit ad and it gets zero traction, a novice calls it a "failure." An expert calls it a **"Negative Data Point."**

- **The Logic:** You haven't lost money; you've purchased the knowledge of what your target market doesn't like.
- **The Feature:** Failure is a "Feature" of the system because it forces you to narrow your focus. Each "failed" A/B test is a brick in the Strategic Fortress of your database.

### Slide 90 — 3. A/B Testing as Market Discovery

A/B testing isn't just about picking a winner; it's about **Understanding the Human**. If Version A (Dopamine) fails and Version B (Oxytocin) wins, you haven't just picked an ad — you've discovered that your target market values Trust over Hype. Every iteration makes your "Constellation of Variables" more accurate. You are building a "Profile" of your customer that your competitors don't have.

### Slide 91 — 4. Systems Thinking: The Learning Loop

In a system, failure is simply a **Feedback Loop**.

- **Input:** $500 ad spend.
- **Output:** Low engagement.
- **Feedback:** "The Hook didn't interrupt the RAS."
- **Adjustment:** Iterate the Hook.

The system is working because it showed you the flaw. The only true failure is a campaign that provides no data.

### Slide 92 — 5. The Expert Takeaway: Courage through Data

When you realise that every "miss" makes the next "hit" more likely, the fear of failure disappears. You stop being an artist waiting for inspiration and start being an **Architect building a database**.
