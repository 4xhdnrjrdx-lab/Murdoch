**СЛАЙД 4 — «What is Email Marketing?»**
Essentially, email marketing involves using email to promote your products or services, build relationships with your audience, and drive specific actions. It's a form of direct marketing that allows you to communicate directly with individuals who have opted in to receive your messages. This can include sending newsletters, promotional offers, announcements, and personalised messages.

---

**СЛАЙД 5 — «Why is Email Marketing Important?»**

- **High Return on Investment (ROI):** Email marketing consistently delivers a high ROI compared to other marketing channels. This is because it's cost-effective and allows for targeted communication.
- **Direct Communication:** Email provides a direct line of communication with your audience, allowing you to bypass the algorithms and noise of social media.
- **Personalisation:** Email marketing allows for a high degree of personalisation, enabling you to tailor your messages to specific segments of your audience. This can significantly increase engagement and conversion rates.
- **Building Relationships:** Email marketing is a powerful tool for building and nurturing relationships with your customers. By providing valuable content and personalised offers, you can foster loyalty and trust.
- **Driving Traffic and Sales:** Email campaigns can effectively drive traffic to your website, landing pages, and online stores. By including clear calls to action, you can encourage recipients to take specific actions, such as making a purchase.
- **Measurable Results:** Email marketing provides detailed analytics, allowing you to track key metrics such as open rates, click-through rates, and conversion rates. This data enables you to optimise your campaigns and improve their effectiveness.
- **Customer Retention:** It is a great tool to keep in contact with existing customers, which is very important for repeat business.
- **Opt-in nature:** People who receive your emails have generally opted into receiving them. This means that they have shown some level of interest in your products or services.

---

**СЛАЙД 6 — «Email in an attention economy»**

- Capturing and monetising attention, often through intrusive or interruptive tactics.
- Email marketing in this context might prioritise high-volume, generic blasts, focusing on click-through rates and short-term sales. The goal is to grab attention, even if it means sacrificing long-term relationships.

---

**СЛАЙД 7 — «Email in a trust economy»**

- This prioritises building genuine relationships and fostering trust with customers. Email marketing in this context emphasises personalised, valuable content, and transparent communication.
- The goal is to cultivate loyalty and long-term engagement.

---

**СЛАЙД 8 — «How the Trust Economy Changes Email Marketing»**

- **Shift from Quantity to Quality:** Instead of sending mass emails, marketers focus on sending targeted, relevant messages to specific segments of their audience. This means prioritising quality content that provides value to the recipient rather than simply pushing sales.
- **Emphasis on Personalisation:** Personalisation goes beyond just using the recipient's name. It involves understanding their needs, preferences, and behaviours and tailoring messages accordingly. This can include sending personalised product recommendations, content based on past purchases, or messages triggered by specific actions.
- **Transparency and Authenticity:** In the trust economy, transparency is crucial. Marketers must be upfront about their intentions and avoid using deceptive tactics. Authenticity is also key. Customers are more likely to trust brands that are genuine and relatable.
- **Building Relationships, Not Just Transactions:** Email marketing becomes a tool for building and nurturing relationships, rather than just driving sales. This means focusing on providing valuable content, engaging with recipients, and fostering a sense of community.

---

**СЛАЙД 9 — «How the Trust Economy Changes Email Marketing» (продолжение)**

- **Consent and Privacy:** In a trust economy, respecting user privacy is paramount. Marketers must obtain explicit consent before sending emails and provide clear opt-out options. This is very important, because if a customer feels that you are violating their privacy, you will loose all trust.
- **Valuable Content:** Content that is educational, entertaining, or solves a problem for the receiver is paramount.
- **Two way communication:** Email marketing is no longer a one way street. Encouraging replies, and feedback, builds trust.

**СЛАЙД 26 — «1. Strong Visual Hierarchy & "The Hero"»**

- The email follows the "Inverted Pyramid" model. The most important information is at the top, grabbing attention within milliseconds.
- **The Primary Offer:** "2 FOR 1 LARGE PIZZAS" is the largest text in the email. It uses high-contrast white text on a red background, making it impossible to miss.
- **The Hero Image:** The pizzas are shown in high resolution with vibrant colours, triggering a visceral "hunger" response.
- **A "Human" Element:** The inclusion of the "tradie" character makes the brand feel relatable and grounded, specifically targeting a large demographic in the market.

---

**СЛАЙД 27 — «2. Psychological Triggers»**

- **Loss Aversion & Urgency:** The subject line mentions "2 for 1 Tuesday." By tying the deal to a specific day, it creates a "now or never" mentality.
- **Value Perception:** The word "UNREAL" is used repeatedly. This reinforces the idea of extreme value, making the consumer feel like they are "winning" by ordering.
- **The Power of Free:** Buy-one-get-one (BOGO) deals are statistically more effective than "50% off" because the word "Free" (implied by 2-for-1) has a much stronger emotional pull.

---

**СЛАЙД 28 — «3. Frictionless Path to Purchase»**
Marketing is only as good as its ease of use. This email removes "friction" (obstacles to buying):

- **Direct CTA:** The "ORDER NOW" buttons are bright, distinct, and placed frequently throughout the scroll.
- **Segmented Offers:** Below the main hero, the email offers "Lunch Pizzas" starting at $3. This captures a different segment of customers who might not want two large pizzas but still want a deal.
- **Bundle Options:** The "$34.95" family bundle provides a quick "one-click" solution for groups, saving the user the cognitive effort of building an order from scratch.

---

**СЛАЙД 29 — «4. Brand Consistency & Trust»**

- **The "All You Can Eat" Mention:** By highlighting the "Locate Now" for dine-in, they drive foot traffic to physical locations, not just delivery.
- **Clear T&Cs:** Including the terms clearly at the bottom (validity dates, surcharges) builds trust and reduces customer frustration during the checkout process.

**СЛАЙД 32 — «Consider the recency bias…»**
**Pizza:**

- Scenario: You're hungry and deciding on dinner.
- Recency Bias via Email: If you just opened an email from "Pizza Hut" with a vibrant picture of their new gourmet pizza and a "20% off tonight only" offer, you're far more likely to order from them. The visual and the deal are fresh in your mind. Even if "Joe's Pizza" is your usual go-to, the recent, enticing email from Pizza Hut overrides your usual preference. If an email from "Pizza Hut" just arrived with a "buy one get one free" deal, you are more likely to order from them, even if you were thinking about making pasta.
- Impact: The recent visual and promotional email directly influence your immediate dinner choice. This is why timing is important.

**СЛАЙД 33 — «Recency bias in tech…»**

- Scenario: You're thinking about upgrading your headphones.
- Recency Bias via Email: If you just received an email from "JBHifi" showcasing the latest noise-cancelling headphones with a detailed video demonstration, you're more likely to consider buying them from JBHifi. If an email from "Electronics Direct" shows up with a sale on a specific brand of headphones, you are more likely to buy that brand, even if you were considering a different brand. Even if you've been researching a specific brand on other websites, the recent, visually compelling email from TechWorld or Electronics Direct can sway your decision.
- Impact: The recent email, with its product visuals and information, becomes the primary driver of your purchasing decision, overriding potentially more thorough prior research.
- It's not a 1:1 correlation, meaning this isn't what will always happen, but remember, convenience is important. A person is more likely to click through your email than go find a previous email to click through.

**СЛАЙД 34 — «What does an email consist of?»**

1. **Subject Line:** This is the first impression, so make it count! It should be concise, engaging, and create a sense of urgency or curiosity.
   - Good Example: "🔥 Hot deals on pizzas this week! 🍕"
   - Why it works: Uses emojis for eye-catching appeal, mentions something that might appeal to your target market, and has a clear timeframe (this week).
2. **Preheader:** A short snippet of text that appears after the subject line in many email clients. Use it to expand on the subject and entice the reader further.
   - Good Example: "Don't miss out on 2-for-1 deals and new menu items!"
   - Why it works: Adds detail to the offer, creating further incentive to open.

---

**СЛАЙД 35 — «What does an email consist of?» (продолжение)** 3. **Header:** The top section of the email, usually containing your logo and branding.

- Good Example: A clean design with your company logo, perhaps a simple image related to the email's theme (like a delicious pizza).
- Why it works: Reinforces brand identity and sets the visual tone.

4. **Body Content:** This is where you deliver your message. Keep it concise, focused, and use visuals to break up text.
   - Good Example: "Hey [Name], Craving a delicious feast? This week at [Your Pizzeria], we're serving up…" (Use personalisation if you have the data) Then, showcase a few specific pizza deals or new options with mouthwatering descriptions. Include high-quality images of the food.
   - Why it works: Friendly, direct, and highlights what's in it for the reader.

---

**СЛАЙД 36 — «What does an email consist of?» (продолжение)** 5. **Call to Action (CTA):** Tell your readers what you want them to do. Make your CTA button stand out with contrasting colours and clear wording.

- Good Example: "Order Now" or "See the Full Menu" buttons.
- Why it works: Guides the reader towards the desired action.

6. **Footer:** This usually contains contact information, unsubscribe links, and social media buttons.
   - Good Example: Your company address and contact details / Clear and easy-to-find unsubscribe link / Links to your Facebook, Instagram, etc.
   - Why it works: Provides essential information and ways to connect further.

**СЛАЙД 40 — «CRM Focus»**
In modern digital marketing, a Customer Relationship Management (CRM) system is the "brain" of the entire operation. It is far more than just a digital Rolodex; it is the strategic engine that allows a business to stop guessing and start knowing. To understand why every brand is currently obsessed with capturing your email and phone number, we have to look at how a CRM transforms raw data into a "Strategic Fortress."

---

**СЛАЙД 41 — «1. The Death of Anonymity: Why We Need Your Number»**
In the "Old World" of marketing, a brand would put a billboard on the Kwinana Freeway. They knew thousands of people saw it, but they had no idea who they were, if they ever actually bought a meat pie because of it, or if they were even interested in meat pies to begin with.
In the "CRM World," the goal is to move a customer from being an anonymous browser to a known lead. This is why every website—from a high-street retailer to a niche energy drink brand offers you a 10% discount in exchange for your email. That email address is the "Unique Identifier" (the key) that unlocks a person's entire history with the brand.

---

**СЛАЙД 42 — «2. Tracking the "Easy Path": Who is Converting and Why?»**
A CRM allows a marketer to view the Customer Journey in high definition. Instead of seeing a generic "sale," a CRM shows the sequence:

- **The Origin:** Did they find us via a TikTok ad, a Google search, or a physical QR code at a 7-Eleven?
- **The Consideration:** How many times did they visit the site before buying? Did they read the info page or go straight to the shop?
- **The Conversion:** What was the final "Scratch" that made them buy?
  By looking at the people who do convert, a CRM identifies patterns. If the data shows that 80% of buyers are university students who clicked an ad at 11 PM, the marketer knows exactly where to double their budget. Conversely, if people from a certain segment are "abandoning" their carts, the CRM highlights exactly where the Friction is.

---

**СЛАЙД 43 — «3. Personalisation at Scale (Dopamine & Oxytocin)»**
Human beings are biologically wired to respond to personal recognition. A CRM allows a brand to deliver this at scale:

- **Oxytocin (Trust):** When a brand remembers your name, your previous order, or your birthday, it builds a sense of belonging and safety.
- **Dopamine (Reward):** A CRM can trigger a perfectly timed "We miss you" discount exactly when its predictive algorithms suggest you are running low on a product.
  Without a CRM, a brand is just shouting at a crowd. With a CRM, the brand is having a one-on-one conversation with thousands of people simultaneously.

---

**СЛАЙД 44 — «4. The "Value of Information" Cycle»**
Everyone asks for your data because information is the only hedge against waste. Resources (money and time) are finite. If a company knows that a specific customer hasn't opened an email in six months, the CRM tells them to stop spending money on that person and pivot to someone who is "Warm."
This creates a feedback loop:

- Capture Information: (Email/Phone/Preferences).
- Analyse Behaviour: (What do they click? What do they ignore?).
- Optimise the Process: (Remove the friction points they get stuck on).
- Increase ROI: (Spend money only on the people and tactics that convert).

---

**СЛАЙД 45 — «Unique identifier»**

- When you provide a company with your email address or phone number, you aren't just giving them a way to contact you; you are handing them a unique identifier that acts as a key to a massive vault of data.
- Companies use those two pieces of information to build a "high-definition" profile of you, including your age, interests, and habits.

---

**СЛАЙД 46 — «1. The Unique Identifier (The "Digital Fingerprint")»**

- Think of your email and phone number as a digital ID card. Because you use the same email for Netflix, your bank, social media, and online shopping, companies can use Data Brokers (like Experian or Acxiom) to "stitch" your activity together.
- If Company A has your email, they can buy data from a broker that says, "This email belongs to a person who recently bought baby clothes, lives in XYZ, and has a high interest in RPG games."

---

**СЛАЙД 47 — «2. How Companies Know Your Age (and More)»**
Companies rarely "guess" your age; they either already have it or buy the fact from someone who does.

- **Account History:** If you've ever signed up for a service that requires a date of birth (like Facebook, Google, or a betting app) using that email, that data is often shared or sold through advertising networks.
- **The "Veblen" Indicator:** Marketers use your purchase history to estimate your life stage. If your email is associated with a university student discount, they know you are likely 18–24. If you start buying "Veblen goods" (luxury items that signal status), they move you into a higher income and older age bracket in their CRM (Customer Relationship Management) system.
- **Public Records:** Your phone number is often linked to your name in public directories, white pages, or even property records, which are easily searchable by automated marketing tools.

---

**СЛАЙД 48 — «3. CRM Tracking: "Who is Converting and Why?"»**
Inside a company's CRM, your email acts as a tracker for your Customer Journey. Every time you open an email or click a link, the system logs:

- **Your Latency:** How long you waited before clicking.
- **Your Triggers:** Did you click because of a "limited time offer" (Dopamine/Urgency) or a "scientific report" (Oxytocin/Trust)?
- **Your Value:** They assign you a Lead Score. If you always open emails but never buy, you are a "Cold Lead." If you buy every time a specific influencer is mentioned, you are "Warm," and they will automate more of that specific content to you.

---

**СЛАЙД 49 — «4. The "Value of Information" Cycle»**
Companies are obsessed with numbers and subscriptions because information is a hedge against waste.

- **Predictive Analysis:** If they have enough data on people "like you," they can predict when you are about to have an "energy slump" or when you are likely to switch brands.
- **Removing Friction:** By knowing your age and location, they can simplify your "Easy Path." For example, they won't show you ads for a store in Sydney if your phone number's area code or your shipping history shows you live in Joondalup.

---

**СЛАЙД 50 — «5. Biological Markers (The Neuro-Marketing Angle)»**
Even without your "data," companies can infer your state of mind. If you are browsing a site at 2 PM on a Tuesday, they know you are likely experiencing a Cortisol (stress) spike or an energy dip. They use your email to send a "Dopamine-triggering" discount code at exactly that time to provide the "Scratch" to your biological "Itch."

**СЛАЙД 51 — «1. Major Data Brokers (The "Big Three")»**
These are the industrial-scale aggregators. They collect data from public records (e.g., property deeds, electoral rolls), court records, and commercial sources (e.g., credit card issuers, loyalty programs).

- **Acxiom:** One of the largest, holding data on approximately 2.5 billion people worldwide. They provide the "identity links" that help companies connect an email address to a physical home address or an estimated income.
- **Experian & Equifax:** While known as credit reporting agencies, they also operate massive marketing divisions. They sell "lifestyle data" that helps brands understand your financial health and spending power.

**СЛАЙД 52 — «CRM Software»**
Software like Salesforce and HubSpot are the industry-leading examples of CRM platforms. In our agency scenario, these platforms act as the "Operational Engine Room" where the strategy meets the data. Here is how they fit into the ecosystem and how a marketing manager actually uses them to manage the entire machine.

---

**СЛАЙД 53 — «1. The Hub: Where Data Lives»**
Think of the website you designed in the sprint as the Capture Point. When a customer enters their email for a discount or buys a trial pack, that information doesn't just sit in the website's backend; it is instantly pushed into HubSpot or Salesforce.

- **Lead Scoring:** The CRM automatically assigns a "Temperature" to the lead. A student who has visited the "L-Theanine Science" page three times but hasn't bought yet is a "Warm Lead." The CRM flags them for a specific follow-up.
- **The Single Customer View:** When the CEO looks at a contact in Salesforce, they see every touchpoint: every email opened, every ad clicked, and every purchase made.

---

**СЛАЙД 54 — «2. Automation: Managing the "Easy Path" at Scale»**
You cannot manually email 10,000 customers. This is where Marketing Automation (within HubSpot/Salesforce) takes over.

- **The Trigger:** A customer abandons their cart (a "Friction" event).
- **The Workflow:** The CRM detects this and automatically sends a "Dopamine-triggering" email 2 hours later with a 5% discount code.
- **The Result:** The system "scratches the itch" of the customer without a human being ever lifting a finger. This ensures the "Easy Path" is always active, 24/7.

---

**СЛАЙД 55 — «3. Managing the B2B Relationship»**
For Quasar Rush, the CRM isn't just for the end consumer (B2C); it's vital for the B2B side (selling to 7-Eleven or Ampol).

- **Sales Pipeline:** Sales reps use Salesforce to track their conversations with P&C category managers. They can see exactly which managers haven't been contacted in 30 days.
- **Proof of Concept:** The agency can pull a report from HubSpot to show the 7-Eleven manager: "Look, we have 5,000 people in your specific postcode who have signed up for our newsletter. If you stock us, we have a ready-made market." This is the ultimate "Oxytocin" (trust) signal for a retailer.

---

**СЛАЙД 56 — «4. How it is all Managed: The Marketing Stack»**
Management happens through Integration. You don't manage these in a vacuum; you connect them:

- **Advertising (Meta/Google):** Feeds the "Awareness" data in.
- **Website (Shopify/Wix):** Feeds the "Conversion" data in.
- **CRM (HubSpot/Salesforce):** Aggregates everything, runs the automation, and provides the "Analytics" (the "Aha!" moments).

---

**СЛАЙД 57 — «Why HubSpot vs. Salesforce?»**

- **HubSpot:** Often preferred by smaller agencies or start-ups because it is "Inbound" focused and very user-friendly. It's built for content marketing and automation.
- **Salesforce:** The "Heavyweight." It is highly customisable and better for massive corporations with complex sales teams. It's more of a "Strategic Fortress" that requires specialist management.
