## Часть 1 — Recap & The Itch/Scratch as a Data System

### Slide 3 — 1. The Recap: Signal vs. Noise

Before moving forward, we must look back.

- **The Itch and the Scratch:** In Week 6 and 8, we learned that the ad creates the "Itch" (Awareness) and the app/search provides the "Scratch" (Action).
- **Expert vs. Novice:** A novice is blinded by Vanity Metrics (likes and views that look good but don't pay the bills).
- **The Expert Signal:** We look for KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) that indicate real intent: CPA (Cost Per Acquisition), CTR (Click-Through Rate), and actual subscriptions.

### Slide 4 — Itch and Scratch

In the realm of Advanced Analytics, the "Itch and Scratch" philosophy evolves from a simple creative concept into a data-driven system for predicting and shaping human behaviour. It is no longer about guessing what people like; it is about using complex data sets to identify invisible needs and engineer new desires.

The itch and scratch is just a way to think about wants/needs and how we satisfy them. When your arm itches, there are a lot of ways you can make the itch go away — you can scratch it, you can use medication, you can ignore it — so we have to find the itch (problem) and then provide a scratch (solution).

### Slide 5 — 1. Understanding the Itch: The Data-Driven Discovery

Before you can scratch the itch, you must use your "sensors" to find it. This phase is about identifying the friction in a customer's life that they haven't even articulated yet.

- **Behavioural Mining:** We use tools like **Hotjar** and **Amplitude** to look "inside" the user journey. If the data shows a 45% drop-off at a specific step, that is a "Micro-Friction" — a point of friction where the user is frustrated but hasn't complained. This used to be a huge problem as those who didn't complain simply left and we had no idea why. Now, using tools like Hotjar and Amplitude, we can detect where they fall off in the user journey and check what the friction is. Remember, the **tax** is what people are willing to 'pay', the **friction** is what kills the experience. We need to identify the friction.
- **Sentiment Analysis:** Using tools like **Sprout Social**, we listen to the "noise" of the market to find unmet desires. We look for patterns of scepticism or confusion in the metro area to understand where the current market is failing the consumer.
- **Predictive Modelling:** Advanced analytics allows us to "formulate reasons" for behaviour. We move beyond _what_ they did to _why_ they did it, using **Google Analytics 4** to see the path they took before they even realised they were looking for a solution. This is where things get really tricky and we use advanced data analysis.

### Slide 6 — 2. Creating the Itch: Engineered Desire

Before the smartphone, no one "needed" to be connected 24/7. This is the "Elite" level of marketing (using analytics to prove that a problem exists so you can sell the solution).

- **The "Pattern Interrupt":** We use **Dragonfly AI** to predict human attention. By identifying what bypasses the Reticular Activating System (RAS), we can "force" a new itch into the user's brain in the first 2 seconds of a scroll.
- **Bypassing the "Friction Wall":** When creating a new itch (like "You need an AI to correct your squat form"), the user will naturally resist. We use **Attribution Modelling** to see which series of ads (from high-energy "Dopamine" hooks to trust-building "Oxytocin" content) successfully "sold" the idea over several days.
- **The 16 Basic Desires:** We map our analytics against deep psychological drivers. If the data shows our audience values "Order" or "Social Standing," we engineer the itch to make them feel that our product is the only way to satisfy that specific, deep-seated need.

It's important to remember that marketing is about perception, not fact. There is no 'fact' in marketing. We shape people's perceptions. Luxury brands are luxury brands not because of functional value, but because of social and emotional value, which are built using marketing.

### Slide 7 — 3. The Role of the "Expert Marketer"

A novice waits for the market to tell them what it wants; an Expert Marketer uses the analytics "Engine Room" to find the sequence (the "9" and "11") that triggers a new behaviour.

- **Strategy vs. Furniture:** Having the software isn't enough. The "Expert" uses **Cometly** or **Klaviyo** not just to track sales, but to identify the moment a user is likely to quit, then creates a new "Itch" (like a personalised discount or a new challenge) to keep them in the loop.
- **The Strategic Fortress:** Once you have engineered the itch and provided the scratch, you use the **Brand Style Guide** to standardise that experience. This ensures the "Aha!" moment becomes a repeatable, reliable pattern that competitors cannot easily copy.

## Часть 2 — The Integrated Platform Strategy

### Slide 8 — 1. The Integrated Platform Strategy

An expert marketer knows that a customer's journey isn't linear. We use these tools in combination to move users past the Friction Wall.
**Facebook & Instagram (Meta Business Suite):**

- Function: These are "Demand Generation" sensors. We use them to create the Itch by using high-energy, Dopamine-driven visuals that bypass the brain's Reticular Activating System (RAS).
- Advanced Logic: We use **Dynamic Creative** to A/B test different "hooks" (such as a lifestyle-focused ad ("The Dream") versus a functional ad ("The Science")) to see which version lowers the Cost Per Click (CPC).

### Slide 9 — Google Ads (The "Scratch" Capture)

- Function: Google is a "Demand Capture" sensor. When a user searches for a solution (e.g., "AI personal trainer"), they are looking for the Scratch to the itch we created on social media.
- Advanced Logic: We use **Retention Bidding** and **SEMrush** data to "spy" on competitor keywords, ensuring our brand is the first "Safe Bet" they see.

### Slide 10 — Google Analytics 4 (The Foundational Sensor)

- Function: GA4 tracks the "Digital Glue" of the entire journey. It tells us if a user from a Facebook ad stayed on our site or hit a Friction Wall and bounced.
- Advanced Logic: We move beyond vanity metrics like "page views" to track **Events** (e.g., completing 50% of the AI Assessment), allowing us to find the "9" and "11" in our conversion sequence.

### Slide 11 — 1. Google Ads: The "Intent" Sensor

Google Ads is primarily a Demand Capture tool. It targets users who are already feeling the "Itch" and are actively searching for a "Scratch".

- **Keyword Intelligence:** We use tools like SEMrush to "spy" on what keywords competitors are buying to identify market gaps.
- **The "Scratch" Capture:** We bid on high-intent search terms (e.g., "AI personal trainer CBD") to ensure our brand is the first "Safe Bet" the user sees. This becomes quite tricky and needs some testing. The shorter the tail (Gym CBD), the more expensive it is. However, the longer the tail (Gym that has pilates at night for women in the CBD), the smaller the number of people who actually search for it. You need to find a balance here.
- **Search vs. Display:** We use the Search Network to capture existing demand and the Display Network to place visual "Pattern Interrupts" on websites our target persona visits.
- **Conversion Tracking:** We track "Actions" (like a subscription) rather than just "Clicks" to avoid wasting our Ad Spend (Fuel).

### Slide 12 — 2. Meta (Facebook/Instagram): The "Demand" Sensor

Meta Business Suite is a Demand Generation tool. It is used to create an "Itch" in users who didn't even know they had a problem.

- **Bypassing the RAS:** We use high-energy, Dopamine-driven creative (Version A: 'The Dream') to bypass the brain's Reticular Activating System (RAS) in the first 2 seconds of a scroll.
- **A/B Testing Variables:** We never rely on "gut feeling". We test single variables (like a "How-to" hook vs. a "FOMO" hook) to see which version lowers our Cost Per Lead.
- **Audience Segmentation:** We use Meta's data to target specific Personas, such as "The Busy Mum" (Sarah-Jane) or "The Tradie-Trainer" (Big Mike), ensuring our message resonates rather than becoming "background noise".
- **Social Proof:** We leverage influencer partnerships to build Oxytocin (Trust) and prove our "Social Proof" to a sceptical audience.

### Slide 13 — 3. Google Analytics 4 (GA4): The "Foundational" Sensor

GA4 is the Digital Glue that connects all your offline and online touchpoints. It tells you what happened after the click.

- **Behavioural Tracking:** It monitors where users from a CBD-based Facebook ad go on your site and which pages they stay on the longest.
- **Identifying the Friction Wall:** We use GA4 to find "drop-off" points, such as users who open the app but quit at the AI Camera Assessment.
- **Attribution Modelling:** We use GA4 to track a customer across multiple touchpoints, proving that a sale today might have been triggered by a "Dopamine" ad three days ago.
- **Events over Hits:** We move beyond "page views" (a vanity metric) to track Events, such as completing a "Fitness Discovery Quiz," which shows real intent.

## Часть 3 — When / Why / How to Use Each Tool

### Slide 14 — 1. Google Ads (formerly AdWords) — your "Intent Sensor"

Designed to capture people who are already looking for a solution.

- **When to Use It:** When you have identified a clear Search Intent. Most effective when users are in the "Action" phase of the marketing funnel and actively searching for keywords like "AI personal trainer" or "best fitness app in the country".
- **Why to Use It:** To provide the immediate Scratch to an existing Itch. Lets you "spy" on competitor keywords using tools like SEMrush to ensure your brand is the first "Safe Bet" a user sees.
- **How to Use It:** Keyword Bidding — bid on high-intent terms to appear at the top (can get very expensive). Negative Keywords — exclude terms that signal "low intent" (like "free" or "cheap") to protect your Ad Spend (Fuel). Conversion Tracking — link it to your HubSpot CRM to ensure a "click" actually results in a long-term paying subscriber. (All customers are not equal spenders. Remember CLV? Some customers are Platinum customers — they spend regardless of ads. Some spend because of ads and some spend only with discount codes etc.)

### Slide 15 — 2. Meta Business Suite (Facebook & Instagram) — your "Demand Sensor"

Designed to create a problem the user didn't even know they had.

- **When to Use It:** At the start of your campaign to generate Awareness and Desire. The best tool for Pattern Interrupts (stopping a user who is "Passively Entertained" and making them feel a new Itch).
- **Why to Use It:** Because its visual nature allows for Dopamine-driven storytelling. Bypass the brain's RAS using fast-paced, high-energy visuals appealing to specific Personas like "The Busy Mum" or "The Tradie".
- **How to Use It:** A/B Testing — run "Challenger" ads (Version A vs. Version B) to test a single variable, like a "How-to" hook vs. a "FOMO" hook. Influencer Integration — partner with "Relatable Peers" (like Sarah-Jane or Bec's Journey) to build Oxytocin (Trust) and lower the Friction Wall. Pixel Tracking — use the Meta Pixel to feed data back into your Ad Attribution software to see which creative is "winning".

### Slide 16 — 3. Google Analytics 4 (GA4) — your "Foundational Sensor"

The Digital Glue that tracks what happens after the user leaves the social platform or search engine.

- **When to Use It:** Constantly, to monitor the health of your Strategic Fortress. Active from the moment your website or app landing page goes live.
- **Why to Use It:** To identify where users hit the Friction Wall. If users click your Meta ads but bounce once they see the "AI Camera Assessment," GA4 tells you exactly where the "leaky bucket" is in your funnel.
- **How to Use It:** Event Tracking — move beyond "Page Views" (vanity metric) and track Events, such as someone starting a "Fitness Discovery Quiz" or clicking "Subscribe". Attribution Modelling — look at the User Journey to see the sequence; did they find you on TikTok but convert via a Google Search? Audience Insights — see where visitors come from to ensure your Micro-Budget is spent in the right geographic market.

### Slide 17 — 4. Attribution Modelling: Tracking the Sequence

A common mistake for a novice is "Last-Click" bias (giving all the credit to the final link a user clicked). An expert uses advanced analytics to map the entire Constellation of Variables. We don't go into correlation and causation much, but essentially we look at correlation more in marketing. More often than not there are a host of factors that contribute to a purchase.

### Slide 18 — Correlation _(диаграмма)_

This is an oversimplification, but essentially there's a need, a person would then look at brand and price (assuming they already know they want a type of product), they would then consider, see an ad, then purchase. Most of the reasons we buy are variables — you go to Woolies because it's convenient, they have a wide variety, and they have good prices and promotions, for example. It's not one thing but multiple things that add to the purchase. If they just had good prices but were far away, you wouldn't shop there.

However, there's a trigger that moves you from consideration to purchase, which is the **causal factor**.

Therefore, you need to know both your **corollary factors** (the reason why people consider your brand) and the **impetus** (the causal factor that is the response to the call to action).

**Схема (слева направо):** **Need** → ветвится на **Price** и **Brand** → обе стрелки сходятся в **Consideration** → **Ad** → **Purchase**. (Price/Brand = corollary факторы, питающие Consideration; стрелка Ad→Purchase = causal/impetus.)

### Slide 19 — 4. Attribution Modelling: Tracking the Sequence (cont.)

- **When to Use It:** When your campaign spans multiple platforms (e.g., a TikTok ad leads to a Google Search).
- **Why to Use It:** To see the "9" and "11" in your sequence. It proves that while Google Ads might provide the Scratch (the conversion), the Meta ad was the essential Itch that started the journey.
- **How to Use It:** In GA4 or Cometly, select a "Data-Driven" or "Linear" attribution model to see how every touchpoint contributed to the final subscription. (Remember, correlation for the various touchpoints and causation for call to action).

### Slide 20 — 5. CRM Integration: The "Digital Glue"

Tools like **HubSpot** and **Salesforce** act as the central "Fortress" where all your tool data is stored.

- **When to Use It:** From the very first "Lead" captured via a Facebook Form or Google Search.
- **Why to Use It:** To connect your social media ad spend directly to long-term revenue. See if a specific ad creative resulted in a "Vanity" click or a high-value subscriber.
- **How to Use It:** Use **Zapier** or **Make** to automatically move data from your "Sensors" (Google/Meta) into your CRM. (You don't necessarily need to memorise this, but it's useful to come back to when you need it. Here is where marketing becomes much more intense because of the sheer number of tools available.) This allows for "Personalisation at Scale," where you send a "Supportive" **Klaviyo** email to a user who hit a Friction Wall at the AI Assessment.

### Slide 21 — 6. Budget Allocation & Analysis: The "Fuel"

Advanced use of these tools requires managing the Ad Spend (Fuel) based on data, not a "gut feeling". This is important because in marketing you will sometimes look at something you made (ad, copy, picture, video) and think it's amazing, but if it goes against your brand style guide it might not be a good idea. A/B testing is crucial, so is iteration, but you also have a brand to protect.

For example, if you're always quiet and tend to just listen in your friend group, that's how people get to know you, that's the person they like. Suddenly becoming the life of the party, constantly making jokes, taking over conversations, would confuse everyone and make them feel like they never knew you. The same happens for a brand. We like a high level of predictability with a hint of mystery or novelty.

- **When to Use It:** Once you have enough data from an A/B test to identify a "Winner".
- **Why to Use It:** To avoid wasting your Micro-Budget on "Passive Entertainment" that doesn't convert.
- **How to Use It:** Review the CPA (Cost Per Acquisition) in Meta Business Suite or Google Ads. If Sarah-Jane has a higher conversion rate than Jaxson Fit, you shift your "Fuel" to her content to scale the success.

## Часть 4 — Advanced Analytics (Predictive / Retargeting / Sentiment)

### Slide 22 — 1. Predictive Analytics: Seeing the "Itch" Before it Starts

Advanced data isn't just about what happened yesterday; it's about using historical patterns to predict what the user will do tomorrow.

- **Churn Prediction:** Using Amplitude or Klaviyo, we look for "Micro-Signals" of disengagement (like a user who hasn't opened the app in three days) to predict they are about to hit a Friction Wall.
- **Pre-emptive Scratching:** Once the data predicts a drop-off, we trigger an automated "Supportive" message via HubSpot to solve the problem before the user even articulates it.

If you look at Black Friday for example, it's a very basic view of how companies predict what will happen. The trend has been that during Black Friday, people tend to shop a lot more. Now, companies have made Black Friday a whole month event with multiple sales etc. The idea is to sell to a large swathe of their audience even before the large sale starts. Predictive analytics might sound extremely difficult (and can be), but we're essentially looking at past behaviour, sentiment, and then predicting what might happen. Predictions are never 100% accurate, but help us to prepare.

### Slide 23 — 2. Segmented Retargeting: Personalisation at Scale

We have to move beyond "blunt" marketing (sending the same ad to everyone) and use Segmented Retargeting based on specific user actions.

- **Segment A (The Awareness Gap):** Users who clicked a Meta ad but didn't download the app. We retarget them with a "Relatable" Sarah-Jane video to build Oxytocin (Trust).
- **Segment B (The Onboarding Gap):** Users who downloaded the app but quit at the AI Camera Assessment. We retarget them with a "How-to" video or a Typeform quiz to lower the Friction Wall.
- **Custom Audiences:** We upload our CRM data from Salesforce into Google Ads to create "Lookalike Audiences," finding more people in the CBD who behave exactly like our most successful "Elite ROI" subscribers.

### Slide 24 — 3. Data Integration & The "Digital Glue"

The final stage of advanced data is System Interconnectivity. You must ensure your "Sensors" are talking to each other so your data isn't "Poisoned" by silos.

- **The Zapier/Make Workflow:** We use "Digital Glue" to ensure that a lead captured in a Facebook ad is instantly moved to our Mailchimp drip campaign without manual entry.
- **Ad Attribution (Cometly):** We use advanced attribution to "solve the Data Gap," proving which specific creative (like Big Mike's authenticity) actually resulted in a sale, even if the conversion happened across different devices or platforms.

### Slide 25 — 4. Advanced Sentiment Analysis

We move beyond counting "likes" to understanding the emotional state of the market.

- **Sprout Social & Brand24:** We use these tools to perform "Digital Listening," crawling news, forums, and blogs to see if the conversation around our brand is becoming a PR Liability or building Brand Equity.
- **The "Chemical" Feedback Loop:** If the sentiment analysis shows high levels of Cortisol (stress/anger) regarding a "No Excuses" ad, the data tells us to pivot immediately to a "Supportive" Oxytocin-based message.

### Slide 26 — 1. Predictive Analytics: The "Early Warning" System

We have moved past _what happened_ to _what will happen_.

- **Churn Prediction:** We use tools like Amplitude or Klaviyo to spot "Micro-Signals" of disengagement (like a user failing to open the app for three days) predicting they are about to hit a Friction Wall.
- **Pre-emptive Scratching:** Once the data predicts a drop-off, we trigger an automated "Supportive" message via HubSpot to solve the problem before the user even articulates it.

### Slide 27 — 2. Segmented Retargeting: Precision Engineering

Instead of "blunt" marketing, we use data to treat different drop-off points with specific surgical precision.

- **The Awareness Gap (Segment A):** Retarget users who clicked a Meta ad but didn't download with "Relatable" content to build Oxytocin (Trust).
- **The Onboarding Gap (Segment B):** Retarget those who quit at the AI Camera Assessment with "How-to" videos or interactive quizzes to lower the Friction Wall.
- **Custom Audiences:** Upload CRM data into Google Ads to create "Lookalike Audiences," finding new people in the area who behave exactly like our most successful "Elite ROI" subscribers.

### Slide 28 — 3. Advanced Sentiment Analysis: The Chemical Audit

We move beyond counting "likes" to understanding the emotional health of your brand in the market.

- **Digital Listening:** Using Sprout Social or Brand24 to crawl news and forums to see if the conversation is building Brand Equity or becoming a PR Liability.
- **The "Chemical" Feedback Loop:** If data shows high Cortisol (stress) regarding an ad, the strategy dictates an immediate pivot to a supportive, Oxytocin-based message.

## Часть 5 — Who Does the Work (Roles)

### Slide 29 — 1. The Marketing Intern: The "Sensor" Monitor

Responsible for the "ground-level" sensors. Their job is to ensure the data flowing into the fortress isn't Poisoned Data.

- **Platform Management:** Manages the daily health of the Meta Pixel and Google Analytics 4 events.
- **Vanity vs. Signal:** Distinguishes between Vanity Metrics (likes/views) and actual Signal (clicks/add-to-cart) to provide the "Expert Signal".
- **Behavioural Mining:** Uses tools like Hotjar to record sessions and identify the specific point where users hit the Friction Wall during onboarding.
- **Sentiment Tracking:** Conducts initial "Digital Listening" using Sprout Social to flag if the CBD market is reacting positively or sceptically to a campaign.

### Slide 30 — 2. The Marketing Agent: The "Engine Room" Operator

The Agent (or Specialist) turns the data into a Systemic Fortress. They take the raw sensors and build the "Easy Path" for the consumer.

- **A/B Testing:** Designs and executes "Challenger" ads to test single variables, such as a "Dopamine" hook vs. an "Oxytocin" hook, to lower the CPA.
- **Attribution Modelling:** Uses Cometly or GA4 to solve the "Data Gap," proving which specific creative resulted in a sale, even if the user switched devices.
- **Segmented Retargeting:** Sets up specific "Recovery" campaigns for Segment B (the onboarding gap) to lower friction for users who quit the app.
- **CRM Integration:** Connects the sensors to the "Digital Glue" of HubSpot, ensuring leads are nurtured through automated drip campaigns based on their specific behaviour.

### Slide 31 — 3. The Marketing Manager: The "Strategic Architect"

The Manager doesn't just look at the tools (the "Furniture"); they own the Strategic Logic. They are responsible for overall ROI and Brand Equity. Remember, the tools are there to give more insight and to 'make life easier', but you are still the strategic lead. Moreover, having all the tools would be too expensive. You don't need specialised tools for everything, so just like with everything else, you can A/B test your way to using tools by seeing what gives the best ROI.

- **Predictive Strategy:** Uses Amplitude or Klaviyo to monitor "Micro-Signals" of churn, identifying the moment a user is likely to quit before it actually happens.
- **Budget Allocation:** Manages the Ad Spend (Fuel) by shifting resources from "Passive Entertainment" (low ROI) to "Statistical Goldmines" (high ROI).
- **Fortress Protection:** Uses Advanced Sentiment Analysis to ensure the brand isn't becoming a PR Liability in the local news cycle.
- **Standardisation:** Takes the winning data patterns (the "9" and "11") and locks them into the Brand Style Guide to ensure the "Aha!" moment is repeatable across the entire agency. Standardisation removes a lot of cognitive dissonance and decision fatigue.

### Slide 32 — Who does all of the aforementioned?

### Slide 33 — 1. The Marketing Intern: The "Friction Audit"

The intern's primary physical task is to find the "leaky bucket" in the user journey using observational data.

- **Hotjar Session Review:** "Watch" recorded user sessions (or mock scenarios) to identify where users hesitate. If a user hovers over a button for 10 seconds but doesn't click, they have found a "Micro-friction point".
- **Sentiment Tagging:** Use Sprout Social or Brand24 to categorise 20 comments from a recent campaign. Tag Dopamine (Excitement), Oxytocin (Trust), or Cortisol (Stress/Confusion).
- **Verification of Sensors:** Ensure the Meta Pixel and GA4 events are firing correctly. An intern's failure to catch "Poisoned Data" early can lead to a $10,000 mistake later.

### Slide 34 — 2. The Marketing Agent: The "A/B Engine Room"

The Agent physically manipulates the variables to find the "9" and "11" in the sequence.

- **Split-Testing Creative:** Take one high-performing ad and change one specific variable (the hook, the colour, or the CTA). Use Canva Pro or Adobe to quickly iterate these versions for a live test. And, as much as AI promises, there is still a clear distinction between what a professional can achieve vs AI. AI is still the tool, not the professional. This means you shouldn't be thinking of things in terms of cost, but in terms of ROI. If you create an image for your company using AI and it costs you nothing, you might find you also don't get much in return because there's so much AI 'slop' out there. Using a professional, albeit expensive, can increase your ROI greatly. In marketing, you have to spend money to make money.
- **Setting the Digital Glue:** Physically map out a Zapier or Make workflow. For example: "If a user submits a Lead Form on Facebook → automatically add them to the 'Relatable' Drip Campaign in HubSpot".
- **Attribution Mapping:** Use Cometly to trace a sale back through the "Constellation of Variables". Prove that a conversion wasn't just "Last-Click" luck but the result of a deliberate multi-touch strategy.

However, with all the tools available, the most basic and important remains **CRM**. You can have all the data and do all the A/B testing, but if you don't know whether it's moving the needle for your target market, it doesn't help.

### Slide 35 — 3. The Marketing Manager: The "Budget & Pivot"

The Manager makes the high-level calls on where to spend the Ad Spend (Fuel).

- **The Churn Response:** Look at Amplitude data showing a drop in active users. The Manager must physically draft a "Pre-emptive Scratch" email in Klaviyo or HubSpot to re-engage those users before they quit. This is much easier said than done, but remember our goal isn't always to have every email opened. Two important things are:
  1. To remind people about us (top of mind awareness).
  2. To be there 'just in time' — to send out emails when people need something specific (think JB Hi-Fi with their emails targeting a large range of products, or Coles doing the same, or Dominos sending out emails around lunch or dinner time).

## Часть 6 — The Tool Stack (Categorised)

### Slide 36 — 1. Demand & Intent Sensors (The Funnel Entrants)

Used to create or capture the initial interest of your Target Persona.

- **Meta Business Suite (Facebook/Instagram):** A Demand Generation sensor used to create an "Itch" using high-energy, Dopamine-driven visuals that bypass the RAS. We talk about this a lot because it plays such an important part in marketing. We see thousands of ads/brands per day — how many do we actually remember or pay attention to? Very few. The brain conserves energy, so it only pays attention to things it thinks are important. When a person opens TikTok, the brain has already set an expectation for what will be seen. A pattern interrupt is required to get people to pay attention.
- **Google Ads:** An Intent Sensor designed for Demand Capture; targets users actively searching for a "Scratch" to an existing problem.
- **TikTok Ads Manager:** Similar to Meta, used for high-dopamine "Pattern Interrupts" and reaching younger demographics via fast-paced video content.
- **SEMrush / Ahrefs:** "Spy" tools used for Keyword Intelligence to see what competitors are bidding on and identify market gaps. (The trap here is to get too 'stuck into' what your competitors are doing. Strategy is built on information, but your 'move' is very important here, so the competitor's strategy is only a part of the equation you need to make your next move.)

### Slide 37 — 2. Foundational & Behavioural Sensors (The Logic Engine)

Once a user clicks, these tools track what happens "inside" the journey to identify points of Cognitive Friction.

- **Google Analytics 4 (GA4):** The Digital Glue of the entire journey; tracks Events (like starting a quiz) rather than just "hits" to find the "9" and "11" in your conversion sequence.
- **Hotjar / FullStory:** Tools for Behavioural Mining; provide heatmaps and session recordings to find Micro-Friction points where users hesitate or quit.
- **Amplitude:** An advanced product analytics tool used for Predictive Modelling and identifying Churn Signals before a user actually leaves. Prediction is difficult as there are many reasons why people drop off. Here you need to consider levels of customers again. If you keep focusing on those one-off purchases that happen during sales, your overall customer base might become lower ROI.

### Slide 38 — 3. Attribution & Integration Tools (The Strategic Glue)

Ensure your data isn't Poisoned by silos and prove exactly which "star" in your constellation is driving ROI.

- **Cometly / Rockerbox:** Advanced Ad Attribution software that solves the "Data Gap," proving which specific creative resulted in a sale, even if the user switched devices.
- **HubSpot / Salesforce:** The central CRM Fortress where all lead data is stored, allowing for Personalisation at Scale.
- **Zapier / Make:** The Digital Glue that automates the flow of data between your sensors (e.g., sending a Facebook lead directly to your CRM).

### Slide 39 — 4. Sentiment & Market Sensors (The Chemical Audit)

Monitor the emotional health of the brand to ensure it doesn't become a PR Liability.

- **Sprout Social / Brand24:** Used for Digital Listening and Sentiment Analysis; crawl news and forums to see if the market feels "Excitement" (Oxytocin) or "Stress" (Cortisol).
- **Dragonfly AI:** A predictive visual tool that identifies what will bypass the RAS and grab human attention in the first 2 seconds. This still needs strategy, data, etc.
- **Klaviyo:** An email/SMS automation tool used for Pre-emptive Scratching (sending supportive messages to users who show signs of disengagement).
