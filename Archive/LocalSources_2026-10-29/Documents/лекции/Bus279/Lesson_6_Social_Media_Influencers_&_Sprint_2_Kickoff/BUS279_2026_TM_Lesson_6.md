**СЛАЙД 31 — «Let's do a deeper dive here»**

**СЛАЙД 32 — «The Science of Certainty: Why A/B Testing is Non-Negotiable»**
In marketing, there is a dangerous temptation to believe in "Universal Design"—the idea that a "good" ad or a "beautiful" website will work for everyone. The reality is that design is entirely subjective. What triggers Dopamine in a 19-year-old gamer at Murdoch University might create Visual Friction and Cortisol for a 45-year-old manager. As marketers, we are not the target audience. We cannot simply "know" what people like based on our own intuition. To succeed, we must move from subjective opinions to objective data. A/B Testing is the tool that allows us to do this.

**СЛАЙД 33 — «1. Data Over Dogma»**
A/B testing (or split testing) is the process of showing two versions of the same piece of content to similar audiences to see which one performs better. It removes the "I think" from the conversation and replaces it with "The data shows."

- **Version A (The Control):** Your current best-performing content.
- **Version B (The Challenger):** The same content with one specific change (e.g., a different headline, a different price, or a different influencer).

**СЛАЙД 34 — «2. Understanding Your Specific Market»**
Different target markets have different "biological buttons." A luxury brand might find that Even Pricing ($100) converts better because it signals prestige. A convenience brand might find that Odd Pricing ($99) works because it signals a bargain. Without A/B testing, you are guessing which signal your market prefers. By gathering data, you are essentially "interviewing" thousands of customers simultaneously. Their clicks, views, and purchases tell you exactly what scratches their specific "Itch."

**СЛАЙД 35 — «3. Maximising ROI (The Financial Logic)»**
Marketing budgets are finite. If you spend $5,000 on an influencer campaign that hasn't been tested, you are gambling. A/B testing allows you to run "micro-tests" first. Test two different "Hooks" with a small spend. Identify the winner (the one with the lowest Cost Per Click). Pour the remaining budget into the winner.

**СЛАЙД 36 — «4. Iteration: The Goal of Constant Improvement»**
A/B testing isn't a "one and done" task; it is a cycle of constant iteration. Once you find a winner, that winner becomes the new "Control," and you try to beat it with a new "Challenger." This process of incremental improvement is how a small brand eventually takes market share from giants. **The Bottom Line:** In a world of infinite content, the only way to ensure your message breaks through the noise is to let the data speak. A/B testing turns your social media strategy into a scientific process, ensuring that every dollar spent is backed by the biological preferences of your target market.

**СЛАЙД 37 — «The Novice: The Trap of Intuition»**
The novice marketer operates on "gut feel." They believe they are the target audience, assuming that because they like a specific aesthetic, a high-octane video, or a witty caption, the rest of the world will too. They chase trends and "universal" beauty, convinced that a "good" design is objectively good for everyone. To the novice, marketing is an art form driven by personal taste.

**СЛАЙД 38 — «The Experienced: The Application of Theory»**
The experienced marketer has moved past personal bias and into the world of frameworks. They understand the "Itch and the Scratch" and can identify the difference between UI (the skin) and UX (the skeleton). They know how to apply theories like the Left-Digit Effect in pricing or the Z-Pattern in layout. They have a toolkit of "what works," but they often apply these rules as rigid laws rather than flexible strategies.

**СЛАЙД 39 — «The Expert: The Humility of Data»**
The expert marketer has reached the most powerful realisation in the industry: "I don't know what the customer wants; only the customer knows." The expert understands the why behind the theory (the neurochemistry of Dopamine, Oxytocin, and Cortisol) but they also know that every target market is a unique biological ecosystem. They recognise that there is no such thing as "Universal Design." An interface that removes friction for a 19-year-old student might create a "Friction Wall" for a 45-year-old FIFO worker. Because the expert knows they don't have the answers intrinsically, they rely on A/B Testing. They treat every campaign as a scientific experiment, using data to "interview" the market. To the expert, the only truth is the one backed by statistical significance. They don't fall in love with their own ideas; they fall in love with the data that proves what actually works.

**СЛАЙД 40**
A/B testing (or split testing) is the scientific method of marketing. In the context of influencers and social media content, it is the only way to move from "I think this looks good" to "I know this converts." When you are paying an influencer or boosting a post, you are spending finite resources. A/B testing ensures you aren't gambling those resources on a hunch.

**СЛАЙД 41 — «1. The Core Mechanics: Controlled Variables»**
To run a true A/B test, you change one variable while keeping everything else identical. If you change the image and the caption at the same time, you won't know which one caused the change in performance. Common Variables to Test in Social Media:

- The "Hook": Testing a "How-to" headline vs. a "Fear of Missing Out" (FOMO) headline.
- The Creative: A high-production studio shot vs. a raw, "User-Generated Content" (UGC) style video.
- The CTA (Call to Action): "Shop Now" vs. "Get the Discount."
- The Influencer: Sending the same brief to two different influencers to see whose audience has a higher Oxytocin (Trust) connection to the product.

**СЛАЙД 42 — «2. Statistical Significance: Don't Trust Small Numbers»**
If you show an ad to 10 people and 2 click on Version A, but only 1 clicks on Version B, you cannot say Version A is 100% better. That is likely just "statistical noise."

- **Sample Size:** You need a large enough audience to ensure the results aren't a fluke.
- **Confidence Level:** Most professional tools look for a 95% confidence level. This means if you ran the test 100 times, the same version would win 95 times.
- **The "Aha!" Moment:** Until a test reaches statistical significance, the results are irrelevant. Never pivot your entire strategy based on a 4-hour trend.

**СЛАЙД 43 — «3. The Neuromarketing of A/B Testing»**
A/B testing is essentially a search for which chemical trigger works best for your specific persona.

- **Testing for Dopamine (The Reward):** Does a "Flash Sale" graphic get more clicks than a "New Collection" graphic? The Flash Sale targets the quick reward centre of the brain.
- **Testing for Oxytocin (The Trust):** Does a testimonial from a peer perform better than a celebrity endorsement? A student seeing another student study successfully might trigger more trust than a generic athlete.
- **Testing for Cortisol (The Itch):** Does an ad that highlights the "Energy Slump" problem perform better than an ad that shows the "Full of Energy" solution? Often, the "Itch" (Pain Point) is a stronger motivator than the "Scratch."

**СЛАЙД 44 — «4. Applying A/B Testing to Influencers»**
When working with influencers, you can't always control their content, but you can A/B test the Traffic Flow:

- **Unique Tracking Links (UTM):** Give Influencer A and Influencer B different links. This allows your CRM (HubSpot/Salesforce) to track exactly who is converting and why.
- **Landing Page Splitting:** Send half the influencer's traffic to a "Lifestyle" landing page and half to a "Scientific/Solution" landing page.
- You might find that Influencer traffic has a high bounce rate on heavy video pages but stays longer on fast-loading, informational pages.

**СЛАЙД 45 — «5. The "Winner-Takes-All" Process»**
The goal of A/B testing is Iteration. Test: Run Version A vs. Version B for 48–72 hours. Analyse: Find the winner using statistical significance. Kill: Stop the losing ad immediately. Evolve: Take the winner (the new "Control") and create a new Version B to try and beat it. The Result: Over time, your Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) drops because you are only spending money on content that is biologically and statistically proven to work.

**СЛАЙД 46 — «The Power of Data: From Guesswork to Certainty»**
Imagine I show you a single number: 5. If I ask you what the next number in the sequence is, you are essentially gambling. You might guess 6, or 10, or 55. Without context, a single data point is just "noise"; it tells you where you are, but it cannot tell you where you are going. This is the Novice Marketer mindset: making massive strategy decisions based on one good day of sales or one positive comment.

**СЛАЙД 47 — «Adding the Second Point»**
Now, I give you a second number: 7. Suddenly, the fog begins to lift. You can start to form a hypothesis. You might think, "Perhaps we are counting by twos?" and guess 9. Or you might think the numbers are prime and guess 11. Your guess is now "educated," but the risk of being wrong is still high. This is the Experienced Marketer: using a bit of theory to start predicting trends.

**СЛАЙД 48 — «The 'Aha!' Moment: Establishing the Pattern»**
Then, I reveal the third number: 9. Now, you aren't just guessing; you are predicting with confidence. The sequence is clear. The more data points we add (11, 13, 15) the more certain you become. The "noise" has become a "signal."

**СЛАЙД 49 — «How this applies to A/B Testing»**
In Social Media and Content marketing, every "Like," "Share," and "Click" is a number in your sequence.

- One Post (The 5): You had a post go viral. Was it the lighting? Was it the time of day? Was it the influencer's hair? You don't know. You have one data point.
- Two Posts (The 7): You try a second influencer. Sales go up again. You're starting to see a trend, but is it a fluke?
- The A/B Test (The 9 and 11): You systematically change one variable. You test Version A against Version B. Now you are finding the "logic" of your target market.
- **The Expert Lesson:** An expert knows that they can't see the pattern until they have enough points on the graph. A/B testing is simply the act of creating those points so you can stop guessing what the customer wants and start providing exactly what they're asking for.

**СЛАЙД 87 — «Influencer Marketing > $23 B Globally»** _(плашка «THE WHY»)_

- **Authenticity:** Influencers often come across as more authentic and relatable compared to traditional advertising. Their content feels less like a sales pitch and more like a recommendation from a friend.

**СЛАЙД 93 — «Influencer Marketing Done Wrong:»**

- **Lack of Authenticity:** When influencers promote products they clearly don't use or believe in, it damages their credibility and the brand's reputation. Audiences are very good at spotting inauthenticity. Examples include influencers promoting products that are contradictory to their normal online persona.
- **Failure to Disclose Sponsorships:** Not clearly labeling sponsored content as ads can lead to legal issues and erode trust. Transparency is crucial. This has been a recurring issue, with many influencers facing backlash for not properly disclosing partnerships.
- **Mismatched Influencers:** Partnering with an influencer whose values or audience doesn't align with the brand can lead to negative reactions. For example, a family-friendly brand partnering with an influencer known for controversial content.
- **Influencer Misbehavior:** If an influencer engages in behavior that contradicts the brand's values, it can reflect poorly on the company. This can include anything from offensive social media posts to legal issues.

**СЛАЙД 94 — «Influencer Marketing Done Right»**

- **Authentic Partnerships:** Brands that partner with influencers who genuinely love their products create more engaging and believable content. Example: Gymshark's long-term partnerships with fitness influencers who actually use and promote their apparel.
- **Creative and Engaging Content:** Influencer campaigns that go beyond simple product placements and create entertaining or informative content are more successful. Example: brands that create social media challenges that encourage user participation.
- **Targeted Campaigns:** Brands that carefully select influencers whose audience aligns with their target market achieve better results. Example: niche brands partnering with micro-influencers who have a highly engaged, specific audience.
- **Long-Term Relationships:** Building ongoing relationships with influencers, such as brand ambassadorships, can create a stronger sense of trust and authenticity. Example: brands that have created "athlete" or "ambassador" programs with influencers.
- **Utilising multiple influencer tiers:** Brands that utilise a combination of macro, micro, and nano influencers to reach the largest and most specific audiences.

**СЛАЙД 97 — «Product driven vs Market driven»** _(встроенное видео)_
Are we going to release a product that is in direct competition to others? So, we're product driven in that we want to create something similar to what's out there already and then highlight what makes ours different? Or, are we focusing on market needs, so we're trying to understand the underlying reason for purchases? Both have pros and cons. Being product-driven means you can focus on what competitors are doing and then try to improve or differentiate on their offering. Being market-driven means you want to understand your customers at a more fundamental level and try to satisfy those underlying needs. Think about cars — They convey status, luxury, technology, environmental intention etc. Being product focused in the car market means you look at what competitors are doing and you improve, longer range on EVs, quicker charging times, more premium interiors etc. Being market focused means you know why people are buying, so you add the tech needed to enhance the experience, you listen and watch for behaviour and add and remove and iterate accordingly. **СЛАЙД 99**
Regardless of the route you choose, it starts with data. Being product-focused means you need to understand behaviour. Look at what people are and aren't buying. Being market-focused means understanding both psychographics and behaviour and how they interact.

**СЛАЙД 100 — «When we focus on understanding, we have to mediate this through PESTLE, meaning…»** _(пример «drone car»)_

- **Politics:** can be very divisive these days, so we want to ensure that our offering will still be valuable should political parties change etc.
- **Economic:** Is it economically viable? Are things getting better or worse, do people FEEL better or worse off? Focussing on a drone car that might cost $150,000 might not be the best idea when most people are struggling to pay rent.
- **Social:** What are the shifts in society like? There are tons of fads now… More people want to be healthy; more people want to care for the environment. How does this fit into our drone car idea? People might like the idea because it can save time, can be kinder to the environment, but it can also be dangerous. It takes very little to see this as a 'toy for the wealthy' and have people turn against it. You need to understand societal pressures.
- **Technological:** research the available technology. Does the tech exist? If not, can we create it? What costs? Are there more feasible technologies? Do we have first mover advantage? Could this lead to first mover disadvantage?
- **Legal:** Arguably the first question is "Is this legal?" With drone transport there will have to be a lot of legislative changes. Legality is also a question of resources — with enough resources we can build, iterate, and lobby for legalisation through testing. A very expensive endeavour.
- **Environmental:** will there be a steady supply of natural resources for this? Where are we going to build these?

**СЛАЙД 101 — «Which then leads us to Porter's»**

- How easy would it be for others to copy us? (first mover advantage/disadvantage)
- Could I find suppliers for the drone? Many suppliers or just one? Stable and reliable?
- Are there enough buyers? Do buyers even want this? How easy is it for a buyer to walk away?
- Are there substitutes? Even if legal, cars/public transport remain. Do we have a strong enough value proposition?
- Are there rivalries? Larger companies doing the same that might outspend us?

**СЛАЙД 102**
If we have positive answers to many aspects here, we have to use design thinking to create. This means really understanding the 'why' of it. And the 'why' here refers to both why would people want to use this and why are we the company to make this happen?

**СЛАЙД 103**
The design process should be iterative and should consider things like UX and UI. The experience as a whole needs to be a positive one, and the UI needs to be such that it's easy to use and easy to retain.

**СЛАЙД 104**
We also want to look at what a customer funnel will look like. We can do this before the completion of the product to get people hyped for it. This means promos, taking people for flights so they can talk about it, getting influencers onboard etc. New technologies get a lot of PR! However, you have to balance this out with what you want to hide from competitors.

**СЛАЙД 105**
When the product is released, we want to keep momentum going. Thus, subscriptions to newsletters, posting videos etc., as we want: Top of mind awareness · Relationship building (putting a face to the company) · Controlling the message (ensuring there's a lot of positive content, ensuring we deal with negative reviews etc.)

**СЛАЙД 106**
With our company off to a good start we can't sit back and relax. We need to keep innovating. Strive to understand your customers, assess their behaviour, look at analytics, improve content, and focus on R&D. We want to focus on iteration, improving in small increments. From here we can also look at diversifying our target market, establishing hero-products etc., branch out internationally etc. etc. However, it all starts with data.
