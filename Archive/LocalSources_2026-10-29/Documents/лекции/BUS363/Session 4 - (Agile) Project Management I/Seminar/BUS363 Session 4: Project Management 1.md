## Group Activity (30 mins): The Marshmallow Challenge

### Setup

- Divide into **teams of 4–6 students**.
- Each team can **only use the supplies provided** in the Challenge kit, which contains:
  - 20 sticks of spaghetti
  - 1 meter of masking tape
  - 1 meter of string
  - 1 marshmallow
- **Time: 18 minutes** to complete the exercise.
- Online / External students: do this activity at home with friends and family.

### Rules and objectives

- Build the tallest freestanding structure.
- Place the entire marshmallow on top.
- Use as much or as little of the provided materials as needed.
- 18-minute time limit.

### Assessment criteria

- The tower must be **freestanding**.
- The tower must **support the weight of a single marshmallow**.
- The marshmallow must be the **highest point** on the structure.

### Who performs best at the Marshmallow Challenge?

- According to **Tom Wujec** (founder/populariser of the challenge), **school children performed the task better than business students**.
- An in-depth study showed that **Architects, Executive Administrators (EAs) and Engineers performed even higher than school children**.

### Related video

**Build a tower, build a team | Tom Wujec** (TED talk, ~7:26 mins). Referenced on the slides as "Gallery 1 — Tom Wujec".

### What does this activity have to do with Project Management & Assessment 1?

The activity surfaces five PM lessons, each to be connected back to the student's everyday-task project:

- **Prototyping and flexibility** — the importance of early testing and adapting strategies based on feedback. Relate this to how you can prototype and refine your everyday-task project.
- **Time management** — how did you balance planning and execution under time pressure? Connect this to the need for effective time management in your assessment project.
- **Communication and collaboration** — how did you exchange ideas and delegate tasks? Highlight the importance of these skills in optimising your chosen everyday task.
- **Problem-solving** — how did your team approach the challenge and overcome obstacles? Apply similar problem-solving techniques to your assessment.
- **Assumptions and testing** — what assumptions about the activity affected your structures? Make sure you identify and test the assumptions you're making with your project task.

### Marshmallow Challenge — Key Skills Developed

- **Collaboration and teamwork** skills, such as listening, valuing others' ideas and co-operating.
- **Leadership**, including the ability to influence others or allocate tasks.
- **STEM skills**, exploring stable structures, geometry, evaluating materials.
- **Innovation and prototyping**, trial and error (testing, prototyping, iteration).

### What is Project Management — PMBOK definition (6th edition)

_Project management is the application of processes, methods, skills, knowledge and experience to achieve specific project objectives according to the project acceptance criteria within agreed parameters. Project management has final deliverables that are constrained to a finite timescale and budget._

_A key factor that distinguishes project management from just 'management' is that it has this final deliverable and a finite timespan, unlike management which is an ongoing process._

_Because of this a project professional needs a wide range of skills; often technical skills, and certainly people management skills and good business awareness._

(Source: PM Body of Knowledge — PMBOK 6th edition.)

## The Project Canvas (Project Scope Canvas — A1 tool)

A one-page planning template (by **BEDENK**, "create change from within", licensed Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivs 3.0). It has fields for **Title** and **Project target** and is organised into 10 numbered blocks:

1. **Scope** — four nested zones: _must have_, _nice to have_, _if possible_, _out of scope_.
2. **Users** — What are the main user groups? For whom do we do it?
3. **Benefits** — What problem do we solve for the user?
4. **Stakeholders** — Who are the stakeholders and what is their position on the project? Plotted on a quadrant of _Proponent ↔ Opponent_ (vertical) against _High Engagement ↔ Low Engagement_ (horizontal).
5. **Risks** — What events could have a negative impact on the project in the (near) future? Identify the impact. How can you avoid / reduce / redirect the major risks? Plotted on a **Risk Level (Low/Medium/High)** vs **Risk Impact (Low/Medium/High)** grid.
6. **Team** — Sponsor; Steering Group (only decision makers); Project Leader; Project Team; Sounding Board.
7. **Resources** — Who and/or what do we need to make our project succeed?
8. **Budget** — Expenditure / Revenue.
9. **Actions** — What must we do to develop and launch the project?
10. **Planning** — First determine the milestones, then the actions needed to get to each milestone, then determine the dates for completion. (Shown on a timeline starting at "NOW".)

## The PM Hierarchy (cascade)

A cascading chain (each element feeds the next):

- **Project Manager** — Manages project work & team. _The PM has the responsibility, authority and accountability for the project management and project delivery._
- **Project Management** — Application of PM standards & best practices. _Work follows specific PM processes, policies, tools and techniques._
- **PM Process** — A repeatable, predictable & manageable approach to managing projects. _Includes practices, techniques, templates and tools; serves as a baseline for continuous improvement._
- **Project Plan** — Documentation. _Shows project work, milestones, deliverables, costs, resources, risks and issues._
- **Project** — the outcome of the above.

## Project Characteristics

1. Temporary work with specific **start and end dates**.
2. Performed by **resources**.
3. Delivery of unique products, services or results to **meet a specific business goal**.
4. Constrained by **scope, time and cost**.
5. Has an **identified priority** within the organisation's portfolio of work.

## Project vs Program vs Portfolio Management

### Comparative overview (synthesised from the two comparison tables on slide 34)

| Dimension                      | Project Management                                                                                                                     | Program & Project Portfolio Management                                                                                     | Portfolio Management                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Purpose**                    | Provide deliverables by executing a network of tasks                                                                                   | Deliver benefits by executing a network of projects                                                                        | Support strategy formulation and execution from an investment perspective                                                                                                                                 |
| **Temporal nature (duration)** | Temporary: has a finite start and end                                                                                                  | Longer time frames (compared to projects), with termination as a decision to disinvest (e.g. NASA's Space Shuttle program) | Enduring process, with regular balancing of investments                                                                                                                                                   |
| **What is aggregated?**        | Collection of related tasks or deliverables (smallest level of aggregation)                                                            | Collection of projects and other components                                                                                | Collection of assets / things of value (largest level of aggregation)                                                                                                                                     |
| **Strategic alignment means…** | Accomplishing specific requirements and objectives; projects execute strategies at functional / business level, seldom corporate level | Accomplishing specific requirements on a grander scale; programs accomplish functional, business or corporate strategies   | Adjusting the balance of investments to increase probability of achieving financial & business outcomes in a dynamic external environment; functional strategies seldom benefit from risk diversification |
| **Success defined by**         | Meet narrowly defined objectives — typically metrics of quality, cost and timing                                                       | Meet broadly defined objectives — typically streams of benefits to different stakeholders                                  | Focus on long-term value to investors; comparison to portfolios of external benchmarks                                                                                                                    |
| **Risk management approach**   | Risks typically regarded as threats                                                                                                    | Risks often framed as opportunities, but threats also important                                                            | Risks are deviations from investor expectations, managed through portfolio balance                                                                                                                        |
| **Decisions**                  | Made continually; more focused on uncertainty rather than ambiguity                                                                    | Made continually; ambiguity addressed first, then uncertainty                                                              | Made at specific decision events (gates), primary outcome to invest or disinvest; mostly focused on uncertainty                                                                                           |
| **Key competencies**           | Management skills                                                                                                                      | Leadership skills                                                                                                          | Analysis and decision making                                                                                                                                                                              |

### PMBOK 7th-edition style comparison (slide 34, right-hand table)

By **Scope, Change, Planning, Management, Success, Monitoring**:

- **Projects** have defined objectives; scope is progressively elaborated through the life cycle; managers expect and implement change-control processes; progressively elaborate high-level information into detailed plans; manage the project team to meet objectives; success measured by product/project quality, timeliness, budget compliance and customer satisfaction; monitor and control the work of producing the products, services or results.
- **Programs** have larger scope and provide more significant benefits; managers expect change from inside and outside and are prepared to manage it; develop the overall program plan and create high-level plans to guide detailed planning at the component level; manage program staff and project managers, providing vision and leadership; success measured by the degree to which the program satisfies the needs and benefits it was undertaken for; monitor the progress of program components to ensure overall goals, schedules, budget and benefits will be met.
- **Portfolios** have an organisational scope that changes with the strategic objectives of the organisation; managers continuously monitor changes in the broader internal and external environment; create and maintain necessary processes and communication relative to the aggregate portfolio; may manage or coordinate portfolio management staff (or program/project staff with reporting responsibilities into the portfolio); success measured in terms of aggregate investment performance and benefit realisation of the portfolio; monitor strategic changes and aggregate resource allocation, performance results and risk of the portfolio.

(Sources cited: PMBOK 2013, p.8; leadingstrategicinitiatives.com; lajevardpm.wordpress.com.)

### Nested relationship (slide 35 — Jerry Bucknoff, PM Best Practices, 2009)

Three concentric domains, each linked to a value type:

- **Portfolio Management** (→ _Business Value_): Led by the business. Business goal alignment. Business value alignment (risk/benefits). Program selection. Portfolio optimisation. ("Doing the right thing.")
- **Program Management** (→ _Stakeholder Value_): Sponsored by the business. Ownership of benefits. Multiple projects. Alignment of projects with overall program benefits. Compliance with project management standards. ("Realising the benefits.")
- **Project Management** (→ _Quality Deliverables_): Delivery of product or service. Scope, cost, schedule. Responsible for quality of deliverables. ("Doing things right.")
  Source: https://governance.business/2019/01/16/portfolio-program-and-project-management-what-are-the-difference/

### Worked example — hierarchy (slide 36)

- **Portfolio:** 6 Year Airplane Manufacturing Portfolio
  - **Program:** Airbus 921 Program → Projects: Avionic Systems Project, …
  - **Program:** Airbus 922 Program → Projects: Communication Systems Project, …
  - **Program:** Airbus 923 Program → Projects: Entertainment System Project, …

### Worked example — manager focus areas (slide 37, i2a)

In busy organisations change is slow and tricky, often involving tens or hundreds of individual projects executed under limited resources. To increase efficiency and impact, projects are commonly ordered under programs and portfolios.

- **Portfolio** (achieve overall strategic goals of the organisation): predominantly about choosing the right projects (aligning resource demand with resource availability) to achieve a set of strategic business transformation goals.
- **Program** (coordinating projects that share a business aim; optimise use of **shared resources**): strategic reflection focused on achieving greater change and impact not available by managing projects individually.
- **Project** (the building blocks of a business's change-management system; create the highest-quality result within the project scope): coordinates the creation/enhancement of a product, service or capability.
- **Upstream information:** change requests on other programs or projects; performance reports.
- **Downstream information:** prioritising decisions; change management; governance.
- Focus areas — **Portfolio Managers:** Portfolio return, Portfolio risk, Resource access (Core Goal: doing the right thing). **Program Managers:** Coordination, Communication, Resource allocation (Core Goal: realising the benefits). **Project Managers:** Time efficiency, Cost reductions, Quality assurance (Core Goal: doing things right).

## Design Thinking + Lean Startup + Agile (combined diagram, slide 42)

A combined model split into two phases:

- **Problem solving phase — Customer PROBLEM (DESIGN THINKING):** Empathize → Define → Ideate (moving from Concrete to Abstract). Followed by the **Lean Startup** loop: Learn → Try experiments → Pivot / Persevere? → Measure → (Iterate / Build).
- **Execution & solution phase — Customer SOLUTION (LEAN STARTUP → AGILE):** the Agile loop of Sprint planning → Product Backlog → Sprint execution → Sprint Review → Shipable [shippable] increment.

Axes: ABSTRACT (top) ↔ CONCRETE (bottom). The flow links Design Thinking, Lean Startup and Agile into one continuous problem-to-solution cycle.

## The Iron Triangle (Lock, D., 2017, _The essentials of project management_)

Three triangle variants shown:

1. Classic **Quality / Cost / Time** triangle (Quality at apex, Cost and Time at the base corners).
2. **Cost / Schedule / Quality** triangle with **People** at the centre.
3. **Scope / Cost / Time** triangle with **Quality** at the centre.

The point: scope, time, cost (and quality/people) are interdependent constraints that must be balanced.

### Starting Out in Project Management: What is the TIME, COST, QUALITY triangle? (video, 1:31 mins)

Time, cost, quality is at the heart of project management. For example: the project must be completed by a certain date; must not spend more than an agreed budget; and the products/services created must meet a defined specification. Because projects are unique and we don't know exactly what will happen, there's a good chance you'll need to adjust the balance between these parameters over time. This means a **trade-off** — to take more time, spend more money, or agree to reduce scope and/or the quality specification. (APM — Chartered Body, Project Profession.)

### Discussion questions

### Group-activity list of challenges (slide 50)

- Limited budget
- Government regulation (e.g. EV cars)
- Time & costs
- Poor communication
- Technology (evolution, e.g. integration of AI & rapid changes to technology)
- Environmental challenges (eco-friendly) — more sustainable projects & impact on funding — wider/broader outcomes
- Accommodate employees' changing needs, e.g. working from home
- Motivation & reward systems
- Current trends constantly changing (e.g. digital, AI, etc.)
- Diversity — inclusion of different ideas & approaches
- Adapting to rapid change (VUCA-world challenges)
- Data Vortex (if no paper records, what happens if online data is lost?)
- Seasonal changes — weather / climate-change impact, e.g. disaster risk & recovery (flood / fire, etc.)
- COVID-19 & future pandemics
- Job satisfaction (shorter hours, paid more, do less, work from home)
- Cost of supplies, supply-chain challenge & quality of supplies
- Workplace clarity & interpretation of client needs
- Expected outcome doesn't always meet customer needs
- Lack of people on board — labour shortage
- Poor risk management (low/med/high — cybersecurity)

### Five grouped challenges (pyramid, slide 51)

- Change
- Risk management
- Emerging technologies
- Timescales vs Resources (OB)
- Industrial landscape / International markets

### Detail on the grouped challenges (slides 52–54)

**Change** — Quick market and ability to change is valued (time is money). The shift is from Waterfall to Agile/Scrum. For organisations to be successful, they have to change, so the approach to running the business or projects has to be flexible too.

**Leadership (OB)** — _Hubristic leadership._ Reference to Hogan, Owen & Davidson's work. This has been a real problem; hubris has been cited as a reason for the demise of several corporations.

**Risk Management** — Every management textbook discusses how important risk management is, yet risks are still often overlooked. It is not the risk/issue itself that pushes a business to bankruptcy, but poor risk management and not anticipating risks in the first place.

**Emerging Technologies** — Links with change: we live in a world of constant change and successful management practices should take that into consideration. Examples include Blockchain and Bitcoin (Stanford University launched a course on cryptocurrencies; University of Edinburgh & Oxford's business school followed).

**Industrial Landscape / International Markets** — Development of international markets is extremely important as the nature of project/product delivery (or any business) changes. This brings a range of issues — cultural differences, different governance and regulations. For example, GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) affects business, national and international sales.
