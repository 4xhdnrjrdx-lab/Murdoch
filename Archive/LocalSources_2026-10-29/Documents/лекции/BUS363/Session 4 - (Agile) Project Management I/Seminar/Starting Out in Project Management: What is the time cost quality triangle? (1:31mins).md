## Part 1 — The Core Parameters

The three core parameters at the heart of project management are **time, cost, and quality**:

- **Time:** the project must be completed by a specific, agreed date.
- **Cost:** the project must not exceed the defined, agreed budget.
- **Quality:** the resulting products/services must meet a specific, defined specification.

Because projects are unique and outcomes aren't fully predictable, PMs often need to adjust the balance between these three over the project's life cycle.

## Part 2 — The Iron Triangle and the PM's Trilemma

- These three parameters are collectively the **Iron Triangle**; balancing them is the **project manager's trilemma**.
- **Trade-offs** when circumstances change mean choosing to:
  - take more **time**, or
  - spend more **money** (cost), or
  - reduce the **scope** and/or the **quality specification**.
- **Case study — sporting facility** (for a major competition):
  - **Scenario A (fixed deadline):** if the date is critical, spend more money or reduce the specification to hit it.
  - **Scenario B (flexible deadline):** if the date isn't critical, take more time and spend more money rather than trade off quality/scope.

## Part 3 — Prioritisation

- The PM's key "trick": understand the **relative priority of objectives** — decide whether finishing on time, staying on budget, or meeting the exact quality specification matters most to keep the customer/sponsor happy.
- **Reference:** the book _Starting Out in Project Management_, published by the **APM**; more on the APM website.

## Key terms & references

- **Iron Triangle:** the relationship between time, cost, and quality.
- **Project manager's trilemma:** the challenge of balancing the three competing parameters.
- **Time / Cost / Quality:** completion date / agreed budget / defined specification.
- **Trade-off:** adjusting one parameter (e.g. cost or scope) to satisfy another (e.g. time).
- **APM:** professional body and publisher of _Starting Out in Project Management_.
