## Part 1 — The lesson of sliced bread

- **Otto Frederick Rohwedder** invented (machine-)sliced bread in the 1910s. Despite being a breakthrough, it **flopped for ~15 years** because nobody knew about it.
- **The shift:** success came only when **Wonder (Bread)** figured out how to **spread the idea**.
- **Core principle:** success doesn't come from the patent or the factory — it comes from whether you can make an **idea spread**. We live in a "century of idea diffusion": those who spread ideas win.

## Part 2 — The death of the TV-Industrial Complex

- The old model — the **TV-Industrial Complex (TIC):** buy ads to interrupt people → use that reach to sell more product → use the profit to buy more ads.
- **The problem:** it's broken — consumers now have **too much choice and too little time**.
- **The result:** people have become **expert at ignoring marketing**. A company might spend $100M advertising a pain reliever, and consumers ignore it simply because they don't have that problem right now.

## Part 3 — The Purple Cow and being remarkable

- The **Purple Cow** metaphor: a normal cow is boring (you've seen it), but a purple cow is **remarkable**.
- **Remarkable** literally means **"worth a remark."**
- **The new rule:** in a world where everyone ignores the "average," you must be **remarkable** to be seen.

## Part 4 — Target the edges and "Otaku"

- Mass marketing targets the **average people in the centre** of the curve — but they're the **best at ignoring you**.
- **Target the edges** instead: the **innovators** and **obsessed** people at the tails.
- **Otaku** — a Japanese word for an intense obsession (e.g. a "hot-sauce otaku").
- **Strategy:** find a group that has an **otaku** for what you're saying, talk **directly to them**, and make it **easy for them to tell their friends**.

## Part 5 — Examples of remarkability

- **Silk** (soy milk): **tripled sales** by putting a shelf-stable product into the **refrigerated section next to the milk**.
- **Dutch Boy** (paint): saved the company by **redesigning the paint bucket** to be easier to carry and pour — making it worth talking about.
- **Aeron chairs** (Herman Miller): turned a chair into a **status symbol**, selling **over a billion dollars'** worth.
- **Apple / Steve Jobs:** people from many countries will travel to watch a two-hour "commercial" (a keynote) because they're **devoted to the idea** and will spread it.

## Part 6 — Why "very good" is bad

- One of the most dangerous things a creator can be is **"very good"** — it's boring, average, and **nobody notices it**.
- **The paradox:** today the **safest** thing is to be **remarkable and take risks**. Playing it safe and trying to appeal to everyone is the **riskiest** strategy of all.

## Key terms & references

- **Purple Cow** — being extraordinary in a field of average things.
- **Remarkable** — "worth a remark."
- **TV-Industrial Complex (TIC)** — the old model of buying mass attention through interruption.
- **Otaku** — an obsession/deep interest that drives people to share ideas.
- **Idea diffusion** — spreading the idea (not the patent/factory) is what wins.
- **Examples:** Otto Rohwedder & Wonder Bread (sliced bread); Silk (placement); Dutch Boy (bucket redesign); Aeron chair (status symbol); Apple keynote (devotion); Soap Lake, WA giant **lava lamp** (a "Purple Cow" idea to revitalise a town).
