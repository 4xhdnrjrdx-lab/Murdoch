## What the diagram is

The original is a single large flow chart that lays out all **49 project management processes** of PMBOK 6th Edition, with arrows showing the typical flow/dependencies between them. Each box names a process, its number (e.g. "5.3"), and its Knowledge Area (e.g. "SCOPE"). The processes are organised along two dimensions:

- **5 Process Groups** (the horizontal logic / project lifecycle phases): **Initiating → Planning → Executing → Monitoring & Controlling → Closing**.
- **10 Knowledge Areas** (the subject-matter grouping): Integration (4), Scope (5), Schedule (6), Cost (7), Quality (8), Resource (9), Communications (10), Risk (11), Procurement (12), Stakeholder (13).

The 49 processes sit at the intersections of these two dimensions. Counts per Process Group: **Initiating 2, Planning 24, Executing 10, Monitoring & Controlling 12, Closing 1** (= 49).

## How to read the "flow" (typical sequence logic)

The diagram's arrows reflect the standard PMBOK logic, useful as a mental model:

- A project starts in **Initiating** — author the **Project Charter** (4.1) and **Identify Stakeholders** (13.1).
- The bulk of work is **Planning** (24 processes) — culminating in the **Develop Project Management Plan** (4.2), which integrates the subsidiary plans (scope, schedule, cost, quality, resource, communications, risk, procurement, stakeholder).
- **Executing** carries out the work (**Direct and Manage Project Work**, 4.3) and produces deliverables, while managing the team, quality, communications, procurements, risk responses and stakeholders.
- **Monitoring & Controlling** runs in parallel across the whole life cycle — measuring performance, validating/controlling scope, schedule, cost, quality, resources, and running **Perform Integrated Change Control** (4.6) so changes flow back into the plan.
- **Closing** finishes with **Close Project or Phase** (4.7).

## Key terms & references

**5 Process Groups:** Initiating · Planning · Executing · Monitoring & Controlling · Closing.

**10 Knowledge Areas:** Integration (4) · Scope (5) · Schedule (6) · Cost (7) · Quality (8) · Resource (9) · Communications (10) · Risk (11) · Procurement (12) · Stakeholder (13).

**Process counts:** 49 total — Initiating 2 · Planning 24 · Executing 10 · Monitoring & Controlling 12 · Closing 1.

**Notable named artefacts/tools referenced in process names:** Project Charter (4.1); Project Management Plan (4.2); WBS — Work Breakdown Structure (5.4); Integrated Change Control (4.6); qualitative vs quantitative risk analysis (11.3 / 11.4).

**Note on PMBOK 7th Edition:** BUS363 transitions from PMBOK 6th → 7th. The 7th Edition moves _away_ from this 49-process / 5-group / 10-knowledge-area structure toward **12 Principles** and **8 Performance Domains** (plus tailoring and a models/methods/artifacts section). This 6th-Edition process flow is still the clearest map of the underlying _mechanics_ and remains examinable as the predecessor framework.

## Relevance to BUS363

This is a **foundational reference for A2 — Individual Project Plan (35%)**, and the single most useful cheat-sheet for the **"Integration of PM Concepts (20%)"** criterion.

- **A2 — Integration of PM Concepts (20%):** This grid is essentially the menu of named PM concepts the rubric wants you to weave in. When your Project Plan covers scope, schedule, cost, quality, resourcing, communications, risk, procurement and stakeholders, you can cite the specific process (e.g. "Create WBS (5.4)", "Determine Budget (7.3)", "Plan Risk Responses (11.5)") to show you're applying the standard, not just narrating. Mapping each section of your plan to the relevant process number is a fast way to demonstrate breadth.
- **A2 — Project Plan Design (15%):** The Process Group flow (Initiating → Planning → Executing → M&C → Closing) is a ready-made backbone for structuring the plan and its lifecycle, and pairs naturally with the Session 5/A2-example delivery lifecycle.
- **Course framing:** Sits at the heart of the PMBOK 6th→7th transition theme — use the 49-process structure to show the "traditional/predictive" baseline, then contrast with Agile/adaptive approaches (Agile Practice Guide) and the 7th-Edition principles/performance-domains model in your integration discussion.
- Lower direct relevance to A1/A3/A4, but the vocabulary (charter, WBS, change control, stakeholder engagement) is reusable anywhere you need precise PM terminology.
