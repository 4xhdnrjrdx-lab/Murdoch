## Part 1 — Reverse Brainstorming (in-class activity technique) (Slides 12–19)

### What it is (Slide 13)

Reverse brainstorming is a creative problem-solving technique that inverts conventional brainstorming. Instead of directly seeking solutions to a problem, participants are asked to consider the problem _in reverse_ — thinking about how the problem could be made worse, or how to achieve the opposite of what you want. The purpose of this inversion is to explore the problem from different angles, surface hidden assumptions, and generate ideas that traditional brainstorming might miss. Once a list of reverse ideas exists, those ideas are flipped back to identify possible solutions.

### How it works — 5 steps (Slide 14)

1. **Define the Problem** — clearly state the problem you're trying to solve.
2. **Reverse the Problem** — instead of asking how to solve it, ask how you could _cause_ the problem or make it worse.
3. **Brainstorm Reverse Ideas** — generate ideas based on the reversed problem; encourage creativity and wild suggestions without judgment.
4. **Flip the Ideas** — review the reverse ideas and flip them into solutions for the original problem.
5. **Evaluate & Develop Solutions** — assess the flipped ideas for feasibility and effectiveness, then develop them into actionable solutions.

### Worked Example Scenario 1 — Securing Initial Funding for a Start-up (Slide 15)

- **Original problem:** How can a start-up secure initial funding?
- **Reversed problem:** How can a start-up ensure it _never_ gets any funding?

| Reverse Ideas (how to fail)                                                            | Flipped Solutions (how to succeed)                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Have a vague business model with no clear revenue stream.                              | Develop a clear, concise business model with a well-defined revenue stream.                                       |
| Present a pitch deck filled with jargon and unrealistic financial projections.         | Create a compelling, realistic pitch deck that clearly communicates the value proposition.                        |
| Network exclusively with people who have no interest or experience in venture funding. | Network strategically with experienced entrepreneurs, angel investors, and venture capitalists in the industry.   |
| Ignore feedback from potential investors and advisors.                                 | Actively seek and incorporate feedback from potential investors and industry experts to refine the business plan. |

### Class activity — the funding version (Slides 16–19)

- **Original problem:** How can businesses at any stage explore various traditional and alternative funding strategies?
- **Reversed problem:** How can businesses ensure they _NEVER_ find or succeed with any funding strategy, traditional or alternative?
- Slide 17 is a blank template (Reverse Ideas → arrow → Flipped Solutions) for students to fill in. Slides 18–19 give the model answer.

**Reverse Ideas — 10 ways to fail (Slide 18):**

1. **Ignore Personal and Professional Networks** — assume friends, family, and professional contacts have no interest or ability to invest or offer financial support.
2. **Dismiss Crowdfunding** — treat platforms like Kickstarter or Indiegogo as irrelevant, believing your business isn't suited for public contributions.
3. **Overlook Grants and Government Programs** — assume you don't qualify for any grants, subsidies, or government/non-profit financial aid.
4. **Neglect Angel Investors and Venture Capitalists** — believe your business is too small, too niche, or not innovative enough to attract them.
5. **Avoid Bank Loans and Credit Lines** — presume you won't meet the criteria, or that such instruments are too risky.
6. **Bypass Strategic Partnerships** — disregard partnerships with other businesses that could offer financial support or investment for mutual benefit.
7. **Ignore Revenue-Based Financing** — skip options that let you repay investors with a percentage of future revenue instead of giving up equity.
8. **Discount Asset-Based Financing** — assume asset-based financing (invoice financing, equipment loans) doesn't apply to your model.
9. **Sidestep Bootstrapping** — assume self-funding is out of reach, underestimating starting small and reinvesting profits.
10. **Reject Pre-Sales and Advance Payments** — overlook securing funds via pre-sales, subscriptions, or advance payments from interested customers.

**Flipped Solutions — 10 funding routes (Slide 19):**

1. **Leverage Networks** — tap personal/professional networks for angel investors or soft loans, gaining both capital and advice.
2. **Utilize Crowdfunding** — use crowdfunding as a dual strategy to raise funds _and_ validate product/market fit with a community of early supporters.
3. **Pursue Grants and Programs** — actively search and apply for grants, government programs, and non-profit initiatives in your sector/region.
4. **Engage with Investors** — craft compelling pitches for angels and VCs, highlighting your unique value proposition and growth potential.
5. **Explore Bank Financing** — evaluate eligibility for bank loans, SBA loans, or credit lines with a solid business plan and financial projections.
6. **Form Strategic Partnerships** — seek mutually beneficial partnerships for access to new markets, technologies, or capital.
7. **Consider Revenue-Based Financing** — for steady-revenue businesses, retain equity while gaining growth capital.
8. **Assess Asset-Based Financing** — use assets (invoices, equipment) as collateral for a cash-flow boost without significant equity loss.
9. **Bootstrap** — start lean with self-funding, build via reinvested profits, minimise debt, and keep control.
10. **Encourage Pre-Sales** — run pre-sale campaigns or subscriptions to generate upfront revenue, reducing external funding needs and validating demand.

## Key terms & references

**Standards / methodologies:** PMBOK Guide 6th → 7th Edition; Agile Practice Guide (PMI + Agile Alliance).

**Ideation technique:** Reverse Brainstorming (5 steps: Define → Reverse → Brainstorm Reverse Ideas → Flip → Evaluate & Develop).

**Funding options / instruments:**

- Family & Friends ("Call Mum") — informal early funding.
- Bank Loan / Credit Lines — needs business plan + security; interest-bearing.
- Grants & Government Support / Programs.
- Start-up Accelerators — provide investment + mentorship + education + network; competitive; team-focused (e.g. Plus Eight, muru-D, Spacecubed).
- Seed Funding.
- Angel Investment — private investors; equity for funding; early-stage; one-time (e.g. Perth Angels: $100k–$500k for 15–40% holding).
- Venture Capital (VC) — later-stage; equity for capital; ROI-driven; high failure rates (e.g. Sequoia Capital, Felicis Ventures, Blackbird Ventures).
- Strategic Partnerships.
- Revenue-Based Financing — repay via % of future revenue, retain equity.
- Asset-Based Financing — invoice financing, equipment loans (assets as collateral).
- Bootstrapping — self-funding, reinvested profits, keep control (e.g. Atlassian).
- Crowdfunding — "alternative finance"; many small contributions; platforms take a % (Kickstarter, Indiegogo, GoFundMe; reward/donation models).
- Pre-Sales / Advance Payments / Subscriptions.

**Key concepts & metrics:**

- Equity vs debt funding; return on investment (ROI).
- **Unicorn** = private company valued at ≥ US$1B (≈ A$1.3B).
- The **"Valley of Death"** — the early operating-but-pre-revenue phase where ~10% survive; innovation-funnel survival rates (20% found → 10% survive → 65% of VC-backed survive → 0.1% become a success story); funding stages: Potential Founders → Seed/Startup ($0–$1M) → Growth ($1–$5M) → Expansion ($5–$10M) → Mature ($100M+) → Unicorns ($1B+).
