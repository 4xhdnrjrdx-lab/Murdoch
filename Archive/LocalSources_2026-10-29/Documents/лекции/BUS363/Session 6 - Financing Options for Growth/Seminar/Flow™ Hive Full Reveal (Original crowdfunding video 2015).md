## Part 1 — Beekeeping and the problem with traditional harvesting

- Opens on the long symbiotic relationship between humans and honeybees: humans care for bees; bees pollinate and produce honey.
- Traditionally, harvesting honey is **difficult and invasive** — opening the hive, disassembling it, and disturbing the colony for a whole day to extract honey.
- The speaker and his father spent ~a decade looking for a better way. Traditional harvesting is labour-intensive:
  - wear protective gear, light a smoker to calm the bees;
  - open the hive, lift heavy boxes;
  - remove frames without crushing bees;
  - clear bees off frames, carry them to a centrifuge / honey extractor;
  - cut off wax cappings, filter the honey, clean up the mess;
  - return the empty frames to the hive.

## Part 2 — The invention: "honey on tap"

- Flow Hive lets a beekeeper **turn a tap** and have fresh honey flow straight out of the hive, ready to use — no heavy lifting, smoker, expensive processing gear, or mess.
- Benefits claimed:
  - **Minimal disturbance** — bees aren't disturbed during extraction.
  - **Real-time visibility** — transparent ends on the Flow frames show when honey is ready, without opening the hive.
  - **Health monitoring** — the windows also help check colony strength and hive health.
  - **Flavour isolation** — harvest individual frames (**up to ~3 kg per frame**) for seasonal variety, or **20 kg+ from a whole hive**. [Product claims — verify.]

## Part 3 — How it works (the mechanism)

- The core is the **Flow frame** — partially formed honey cells.
  1. **Preparation:** bees complete the cells with wax, fill them with honey, and cap them.
  2. **Activation:** turning the key triggers a **patented system** that splits the cells vertically, creating a drainage channel inside the frame.
  3. **Extraction:** honey flows down and out of the hive while bees stay undisturbed on the comb surface.
  4. **Reset:** turning the key again resets the cells; bees chew the wax and refill them.

## Part 4 — Professional applications and field testing

- The inventors spent ~three years testing the final version with beekeepers worldwide.
- For commercial operations, frames can use a **pneumatic system** to harvest an entire apiary simultaneously at the push of a button.
- Beekeeper testimonials express amazement at how easily honey flows and how little the bees are bothered versus the hours extraction used to take.

## Part 5 — The crowdfunding mission and vision

- Over 10 years in development; working with top Australian manufacturers for quality.
- Turned to **crowdfunding** to fund expensive **tooling** and large-scale production — which will lower cost and make Flow Hive accessible to everyone.
- Aim: build a new global community of beekeepers. The system is pitched as:
  - **Versatile** — backyards, rooftops, school/community gardens;
  - **Efficient** — more efficient and bee-friendly than standard hives;
  - **Cost-effective** — cheaper than a standard hive plus all the extraction equipment; [claim — verify]
  - **Adaptable** — existing beekeepers can buy individual Flow frames for their current hives.
- Early supporters are offered the product at **discounted prices** to help launch the startup.

## Key terms & references

- **Flow™ Hive:** the honey-harvesting system ("honey on tap").
- **Flow™ frame:** the internal frame with partially formed cells and the split-cell drainage mechanism.
- **Patented split-cell system:** the mechanism that creates a honey channel without disturbing bees.
- **Pneumatic system:** optional automated harvesting upgrade for commercial beekeepers.
- **Crowdfunding:** the method used to raise capital for tooling and large-scale production.
- **Smoker / centrifuge (honey extractor):** traditional tools/equipment the system aims to make unnecessary.
- **Australia:** location of the manufacturing partners.
- **Founders (context):** Cedar & Stuart Anderson [verify].
