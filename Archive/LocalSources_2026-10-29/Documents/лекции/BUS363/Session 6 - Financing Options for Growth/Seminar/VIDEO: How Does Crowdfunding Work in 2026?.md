## Part 1 — The "science" of crowdfunding

- Crowdfunding is often mistaken for luck but is presented as a **science**. Mark Pecota [verify], CEO of **LaunchBoom**, says his team has helped creators raise **over $100M** (with **$26M** in the last year). [LaunchBoom's own marketing figures — verify.]
- Crowdfunding generates capital from **backers** — strangers online inspired by a pitch. It's a two-sided relationship: backers usually expect **tangible rewards, early access, or other incentives** for their pledges.

## Part 2 — Primary perks of crowdfunding

- **Avoid upfront costs** — generate revenue without carrying inventory or huge production costs before knowing the product sells.
- **Market validation** — test an idea before major investment.
- **Early-adopter feedback** — a community that helps refine the product.
- **Attracting big deals** — successful campaigns draw major retailers and larger investment.

## Part 3 — The five models of crowdfunding

1. **Debt crowdfunding** — a loan via online investors instead of banks; faster and more flexible than slow bank protocols.
2. **Equity crowdfunding** — selling ownership shares; avoids debt and creates invested partners, but means loss of control and heavy reporting.
3. **Reward-based crowdfunding** — the most common; backers get perks/rewards (e.g. early access); best for validating ideas and building community.
4. **Donation-based crowdfunding** — relies on generosity for a cause/social/environmental project; no rewards.
5. **Profit/revenue sharing** — a lower-risk version of equity: investors share future profits without taking ownership; harder to attract investors (they only get paid if it succeeds).

## Part 4 — Major platforms

- **Kickstarter** — creative projects and innovative tech; **"all or nothing"** funding; popular for games, design, technology.
- **Indiegogo** — welcomes unconventional/"weird" ideas; **flexible goals** (keep what you raise).
- **GoFundMe** — personal causes, medical bills, emergencies, non-profits; mainly donation-based, no rewards.
- **Patreon** — membership platform for creators (artists, musicians, podcasters); recurring monthly income from a loyal community rather than project-based funding.

## Part 5 — Crafting a winning narrative

- **Share the "why"** — your personal connection and how it improves backers' lives.
- **Grab attention fast** — open with a compelling anecdote, surprising stat, or powerful question.
- **Show, don't tell** — sensory detail, evocative language, visuals.
- **Personalize** — introduce a "protagonist" (creator or community facing a challenge) to build empathy.
- **Highlight the journey** — authentic voices, testimonials, struggle and triumph.
- **Transparency** — communicate funding needs, fund use, and a **roadmap**.

## Part 6 — The marketing secret: the Reservation Funnel

- Core idea: **"launches are won in the pre-launch."** Build a marketing **Reservation Funnel** in five steps:
  1. Person sees a Facebook/Instagram ad.
  2. Clicks through to a landing page.
  3. Gives an email to be notified at launch.
  4. Is offered a **$1 deposit** to reserve the product at the best deal.
  5. Is invited into a **closed VIP Facebook group** with exclusive founder access.

## Part 7 — Testing mindset and reward strategies

- Use pre-launch data to **test assumptions** about positioning (vary ad imagery, copy, landing-page elements).
- Three reward strategies:
  1. **Discount stacking** — tiers for the same product where the discount shrinks over time (urgency/scarcity).
  2. **The no-brainer deal** — a low-cost, high-value add-on as a reward.
  3. **Value stacking** — bundle progressively more value into higher tiers (logistically complex).

## Part 8 — Essential campaign-page information ("meat and potatoes")

- **Product description** (idea, purpose, impact); **realistic funding goal** (covers all costs); **project timeline**; **reward descriptions**; **team introduction** (expertise); **risk mitigation** (obstacles + plan); **budget breakdown** (transparent allocation).
