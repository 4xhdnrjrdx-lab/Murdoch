## The big structural shift: 6th → 7th Edition

The 7th Edition moves **away** from the 6th Edition's process-based model (5 Process Groups × 10 Knowledge Areas = 49 processes) toward a **principles-based, outcome-oriented** model built on two halves:

- **The Standard for Project Management → 12 Principles** (the _foundation of how we should approach_ projects — they guide behaviour and actions).
- **The PMBOK® Guide → 8 Performance Domains** (groups of related activities critical to delivering outcomes).

Plus an emphasis on **Tailoring**, and a wide universe of methods/approaches that can be mixed.

**What PRINCIPLES are:** the foundation for how to approach something. They guide our behaviour and actions; they are open and applicable in different ways/contexts; they are interconnected; there is **no specific order or ranking**.

**What PERFORMANCE DOMAINS are:** groups of related activities critical for delivering outcomes. There is **no prescriptive sequence**; they work with any delivery approach (predictive, agile, hybrid); they are **not processes**; they are interactive, interdependent and interrelated.

**Methods/approaches universe shown in the infographic (the "umbrella"):** PMBOK® Guide (past editions), PRINCE2®, Disciplined Agile®, PMI Citizen Developer®, Critical Chain, Front-End Loading (FEL), Scrum, Agile, Hybrid, DevOps, ITIL®, Adaptive Project Framework (APF), Kanban, Extreme Programming (XP), Scaled Agile Framework® (SAFe®), and Others. (The point: the 7th Edition is method-agnostic — tailoring picks the right mix.)

## The 12 Principles

Each: one-line principle statement → definition → keywords → key comments.

### 1. Stewardship — "Be a diligent, respectful, and caring steward"

You should care about the project and feel responsible and committed to delivering value, overseeing it with the same level of care you'd use in your personal life.

- **Keywords:** Care · Honesty · Trustworthiness · Compliance
- **Comments:** PMI's Code of Ethics is _related_ but not the same thing; demonstrates duty of care and behaviour toward the project's interest; about being transparent, avoiding conflicts of interest, and following the law.

### 2. Team — "Create a collaborative project team environment"

You can't deliver sustainable outcomes without a team that collaborates toward the same objective; members apply their specific skills to meet team needs and project goals.

- **Keywords:** Collaboration · Teamwork · Culture · Roles & Responsibilities
- **Comments:** Team collaboration doesn't necessarily mean friendship; organizational culture affects how teams work together; working as a team is more efficient and effective than working alone.

### 3. Stakeholders — "Effectively engage with stakeholders"

Stakeholders directly and indirectly influence the project; failing to engage them creates threats and misses opportunities to optimize value.

- **Keywords:** Engagement · Individuals · Groups · Organizations
- **Comments:** They can support or oppose the work and their opinion can change over time; can be internal or external; can come and go during the life cycle.

### 4. Value — "Focus on value"

Successful projects are about delivering value, which goes beyond simple delivery; benefits are realized when target stakeholders perceive positive value from the outcomes the deliverables enable.

- **Keywords:** Outcomes · Success · Business results · Benefits
- **Comments:** No point being on-schedule/on-budget if you don't deliver value; value can be realized during or after the project; the project should **NOT** continue if it won't deliver the proposed outcome/value.

### 5. Systems Thinking — "Recognize, evaluate, and respond to system interactions"

Your project is not a bubble; it's a "gear" in a larger system of interdependent agents, and you must consider and plan for those interactions.

- **Keywords:** Interdependency · Systems of systems · Integration · Holistic view
- **Comments:** Balance internal and external perspectives; systems change over time due to internal/external volatility; systems operate beyond the project life cycle and continue afterward.

### 6. Leadership — "Demonstrate leadership behaviors"

Projects are complex and need strong leadership; motivation, focus, self-awareness and facilitation align the team and overcome the natural conflict that arises.

- **Keywords:** Motivation · Empowerment · Example · Influence
- **Comments:** Anyone on the team can demonstrate leadership; leadership is **different from authority**; there is no single universal leadership style.

### 7. Tailoring — "Tailor based on context"

Your approach cannot be "one size fits all"; adapt it to the work, organization and culture to increase benefits and maximize value.

- **Keywords:** Predictive (waterfall) · Iterative (agile) · Hybrid · "Swiss Army knife"
- **Comments:** There is no "best" methodology — only a "right" methodology for a specific case; the delivery method can be a mix of different approaches/methods.

### 8. Quality — "Build quality into processes and deliverables"

Quality is how deliverables connect to objectives and the value chain; your probability of delivering business benefits drops sharply if deliverables don't meet requirements.

- **Keywords:** Satisfy needs · No defects · Fit for purpose · Conformity
- **Comments:** Quality is about fulfilling requirements; quality can be measured; focus on detection **and** prevention of errors.

### 9. Complexity — "Navigate complexity"

Accept the complexity of your environment; VUCA (Volatility, Uncertainty, Complexity, Ambiguity) is part of any project — shift focus from _avoiding_ complexity to _assessing and reacting_ when needed.

- **Keywords:** VUCA · Ambiguity · Uncertainty · Volatility
- **Comments:** Complexity arises from many factors, most outside our control; it can increase or decrease at any point; indicators and triggers are critical tools to navigate it.

### 10. Risk — "Optimize risk responses"

Risks are part of every project; proactively assess exposure and respond effectively to threats **and** opportunities that may affect the ability to deliver value.

- **Keywords:** Threats · Opportunities · Risk tolerance · Risk appetite
- **Comments:** Each organization has a different risk tolerance, which affects actions; probability and impact change over time; risks can be inside or outside your control.

### 11. Adaptability and Resiliency — "Embrace adaptability and resiliency"

Resilience lets you "build back better" after a setback; without adaptability and resilience it's hard to accept change and recover from problems.

- **Keywords:** Recovery · Adaptability · Reaction · Diversity
- **Comments:** Resiliency is built by improving capacity to absorb impact and recover; adaptability increases by focusing on outcomes rather than a specific delivery; team diversity improves adaptability and resiliency.

### 12. Change — "Enable change to achieve the envisioned future state"

Change is the only permanent feature of any project; act as a change agent and create conditions for others to adapt to a rapidly evolving environment.

- **Keywords:** Psychological safety · Enablement · Comprehensive · Resistance
- **Comments:** Change is **NOT** necessarily bad; enabling change is the opposite of avoiding it; keep the desired outcomes in mind when evaluating changes.

## The 8 Performance Domains

Each: description → key comments (including how it maps back to the 6th Edition).

### 1. Stakeholder

Activities related to stakeholders, including the engagement process, to ensure productive working relationships.

- Contains relevant elements of communications; addresses both supporting and opposing stakeholders; it's about a productive relationship, not friendship; stakeholders change during the project; not all stakeholders are the same (different interests and influence).

### 2. Team

Activities addressing the responsibilities tied to the work needed to deliver business value.

- Includes roles and responsibilities; team culture (norms and behaviours) must be built; strongly connected to the **Leadership** principle; includes emotional intelligence, critical thinking and motivation; strong emphasis on **shared ownership**.

### 3. Development Approach and Life Cycle

The domain where the delivery approach (predictive, iterative, hybrid) is selected and the life cycle, phases and cadence are defined.

- A project may have a single deliverable or multiple (periodic or continuous); predictive is based on defined requirements; iterative/adaptive suits high uncertainty; hybrid combines both; selection is driven by factors like risks, innovation, stability and funding.

### 4. Planning

Activities for the coordination and organization needed to produce the deliverables.

- Strongly related to the Development Approach; **predictive planning uses WBS and decomposition** to break down scope; iterative planning evaluates multiple options until costs may exceed benefits; covers estimating, scheduling and budgeting in different approaches; maps to some aspects of the **Planning process group of PMBOK 6th**.

### 5. Project Work

All activities related to the actual work: physical resources, contracting, managing change, and continuous learning capability.

- Includes reviewing work via lessons learned, retrospectives and lean methods; actions to reduce **work in progress (WIP)** occur here; several **procurement/contracting** processes happen here; closely connected to the **Tailoring** and **Change** principles; maps to some elements of the **Executing process group of PMBOK 6th**.

### 6. Delivery

Activities and work associated with the project's delivery, including requirements, quality and change.

- Requirements can be clear in predictive development; in adaptive approaches requirements may only become clear at later stages; includes **Cost of Quality (COQ)** (prevention and correction); stakeholder acceptance and satisfaction is a key factor for evaluating deliverables; a "fail fast, learn fast" approach helps handle suboptimal outcomes.

### 7. Measurement

Activities that measure project performance so actions can be taken to ensure desired results.

- You can't evaluate results you don't measure; measurement goes beyond reporting and data — it's about conversations and decisions using the data; covers **leading and lagging indicators (KPIs)** and what makes an effective metric; includes **Earned Value Analysis**, dashboards, Kanban and other visual reporting.

### 8. Uncertainty

Ensures the project addresses risks, uncertainty, and VUCA (volatility, uncertainty, complexity, ambiguity) and its environment.

- Though not named in the guide, **VUCA** is central here; strongly connected to **Systems Thinking, Complexity and Risk** principles; early warning and environmental scanning are relevant activities; closely related to the **Risk knowledge area of PMBOK 6th**; uncertainty and risks are not necessarily negative — they can be opportunities.

## Key terms & references

**Two halves of the 7th Edition:** The Standard for Project Management (→ 12 Principles) + the PMBOK® Guide (→ 8 Performance Domains), plus Tailoring.

**12 Principles:** Stewardship · Team · Stakeholders · Value · Systems Thinking · Leadership · Tailoring · Quality · Complexity · Risk · Adaptability and Resiliency · Change. (No ranking; interconnected.)

**8 Performance Domains:** Stakeholder · Team · Development Approach and Life Cycle · Planning · Project Work · Delivery · Measurement · Uncertainty. (Not processes; no prescribed sequence; approach-agnostic.)

**Cross-cutting concepts:** VUCA (Volatility, Uncertainty, Complexity, Ambiguity); Tailoring (predictive/waterfall, iterative/agile, hybrid); Value & benefits realization; Risk appetite vs risk tolerance; Threats vs opportunities; Cost of Quality (COQ); Work in Progress (WIP); Leading vs lagging indicators (KPIs); Earned Value Analysis; WBS & decomposition; "fail fast, learn fast"; shared ownership; psychological safety; leadership ≠ authority.

**6th → 7th mapping (handy for exams/A2):**

- 6th _Planning_ process group ↔ 7th _Planning_ domain.
- 6th _Executing_ process group ↔ 7th _Project Work_ domain.
- 6th _Risk_ knowledge area ↔ 7th _Uncertainty_ domain.
- 6th processes (charter, WBS, change control, etc.) still describe the _mechanics_; 7th reframes them as outcomes guided by principles.

**Methods universe (tailoring options):** PRINCE2®, Disciplined Agile®, PMI Citizen Developer®, Critical Chain, FEL, Scrum, Agile, Hybrid, DevOps, ITIL®, APF, Kanban, XP, SAFe®, and others.

## Relevance to BUS363

This is a **core conceptual reference for the whole unit** and pairs directly with the 6th-Edition processes flow — together they let you discuss the **PMBOK 6th → 7th transition**, which is an explicit course theme.

- **A2 — Integration of PM Concepts (20%):** The 12 Principles and 8 Domains are exactly the named, current concepts to weave into your Project Plan. Citing principles (e.g. "Tailoring," "Focus on Value," "Optimize risk responses") and domains (e.g. "Planning," "Delivery," "Uncertainty") shows you're applying the _latest_ standard, and contrasting them with the 6th-Edition processes demonstrates depth. The **Tailoring** principle is especially useful for justifying _why_ you chose a predictive/agile/hybrid approach in your plan.
- **A2 — Project Plan Design (15%):** The 8 Performance Domains are an excellent outcome-oriented backbone for structuring a modern plan (stakeholders, team, development approach, planning, project work, delivery, measurement, uncertainty), as an alternative or complement to the lifecycle-stage structure in the A2 example.
- **Agile leadership in a VUCA world (Session 11) & A3:** The **Complexity, Adaptability & Resiliency, Leadership, Systems Thinking and Change** principles, plus the **VUCA** framing, map directly onto the leadership-in-VUCA topic and the "scaling globally" presentation — ready vocabulary for arguing how to lead/scale under uncertainty.
- **Course-wide:** Because the 7th Edition is method-agnostic and explicitly embraces agile/hybrid, it's the bridge between "operations vs agile PM" (early sessions) and "scaling agile" (the unit's title). Use it whenever you need to justify an adaptive choice against a traditional baseline.
