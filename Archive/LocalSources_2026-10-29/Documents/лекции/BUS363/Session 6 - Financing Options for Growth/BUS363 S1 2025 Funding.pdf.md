Reverse Brainstorming

• Reverse brainstorming is a creative problem-solving technique that

turns the conventional brainstorming approach on its head.

•

Instead of directly seeking solutions to a problem, participants are

asked to consider the problem in reverse.

• This involves thinking about how the problem could be made worse

or how to achieve the opposite of what you want.

• The purpose of this inversion is to explore the problem from

different angles, uncovering assumptions and generating ideas

that might not emerge through traditional brainstorming.

• Once a list of reverse ideas is generated, these ideas are then flipped

back to identify possible solutions.

Reversed Problem:
How can businesses ensure they NEVER find or succeed with
any funding strategy, traditional or alternative?

REVERSE IDEAS:

1.

Ignore Personal and Professional Networks: Assume that
friends, family, and professional contacts have no interest or
ability to invest or offer financial support.

2. Dismiss Crowdfunding: Consider platforms like Kickstarter or

Indiegogo as irrelevant, believing that your business is not suited
for public contributions.

3. Overlook Grants and Government Programs: Assume your

business does not qualify for any grants, subsidies, or financial aid
programs offered by the government or non-profit organizations.

4. Neglect Angel Investors and Venture Capitalists: Believe that

your business is too small, too niche, or not innovative enough to
attract the attention of angel investors or venture capitalists.

5. Avoid Bank Loans and Credit Lines: Presume that your business
won’t meet the criteria for bank loans or credit lines, or that such
financial instruments are too risky.

6. Bypass Strategic Partnerships: Disregard the potential of

strategic partnerships with other businesses that could offer
financial support or investments in exchange for mutual benefits.

7.

Ignore Revenue-Based Financing: Skip over the possibility of
revenue-based financing options that allow you to pay back
investors with a percentage of future revenue instead of giving up
equity.

8. Discount Asset-Based Financing: Assume that asset-based

financing, such as invoice financing or equipment loans, is not
applicable or advantageous to your business model.

9. Sidestep Bootstrapping: Assume that self-funding or

bootstrapping is entirely out of reach, underestimating the
potential of starting small and reinvesting profits.

10. Reject Pre-Sales and Advance Payments: Overlook the strategy
of securing funds through pre-sales, subscriptions, or advance
payments from customers interested in your product or service.

[OFFICIAL]

Reversed Problem:
How can businesses ensure they NEVER find or succeed with
any funding strategy, traditional or alternative?

FLIPPED SOLUTIONS:

1.

Leverage Networks: Tap into personal and professional networks for
potential angel investors or soft loans, utilizing these connections for
both capital and advice.

2. Utilize Crowdfunding: Explore crowdfunding as a dual strategy to both

raise funds and validate your product or market fit, engaging a
community of early supporters.

Pursue Grants and Programs: Actively search for and apply to grants,
government programs, and non-profit initiatives that support
businesses in your sector or region.

6.

Form Strategic Partnerships: Seek partnerships with businesses
or organizations where there’s a mutual benefit, potentially gaining
access to new markets, technologies, or capital.

7. Consider Revenue-Based Financing: For businesses with steady
revenue streams, explore revenue-based financing to retain equity
while gaining the capital needed for growth.

8. Assess Asset-Based Financing: Utilize assets like invoices or

equipment as collateral for financing, providing a cash flow boost
without significant equity loss.

Engage with Investors: Craft compelling pitches for angel investors
and venture capitalists, highlighting your business's unique value
proposition and growth potential.

9. Bootstrap: Start lean with self-funding, gradually building your
business through reinvested profits, minimizing debt, and
maintaining control over your venture.

Explore Bank Financing: Evaluate your eligibility for bank loans, SBA
loans, or credit lines, presenting a solid business plan and financial
projections to potential lenders.

10. Encourage Pre-Sales: Launch pre-sale campaigns or offer

subscriptions to generate upfront revenue, reducing the need for
external funding and validating market demand.
