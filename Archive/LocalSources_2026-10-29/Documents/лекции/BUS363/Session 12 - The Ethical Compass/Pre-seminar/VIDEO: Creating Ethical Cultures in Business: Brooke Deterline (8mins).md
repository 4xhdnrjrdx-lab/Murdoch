## Part 1 — Courage and systemic failure

- **Brooke Deterline** defines **courage** as acting **from the heart in the face of fear** — a **skill** that can be built and strengthened through practice.
- The opposite is shown by the **Enron fraud**, which she calls **"synergistic corruption":** the built-in checks and balances failed because the **lawyers, accountants, and bankers** who were supposed to say "no" didn't.
- She asks whether anything has really changed in the ~10 years since such scandals — implying the system is still vulnerable.

## Part 2 — Case study: the illegal trading ring ("Ted")

- "**Ted**" discovered an **illegal trading ring** in his department but at first did nothing under intense stress, deciding he'd just **quit**.
- A friend from another department asked: _"If you went into the system, could you find the program?"_ When Ted said yes, the friend said, **"Then you have no choice"** — mirroring Ted's own values back to him.
- Ted came forward, and **"justice prevailed."**

## Part 3 — Deterline's own situational test

- She discovered her company's **CEO was involved in illegal activities** and called a meeting with the **CFO (her boss)** and his reports to report it — expecting the CFO to struggle (he was a close friend of the CEO).
- The CFO suggested they **"do some more research and give it a little time."**
- In "slow motion" she watched **good people** nod or look down — and felt the **bystander pull** herself ("maybe it's not a big deal, I don't have to say anything").
- When the CFO briefly left, she remembered she'd been **hired specifically to act on shared values even when hard**. On his return she pushed back — **"We should go to the board"** — and the CFO eventually agreed.

## Part 4 — The psychology of ethical failure

- References **Dr. Phil Zimbardo** and the **1971 Stanford Prison Experiment** — used to argue that even **ethical, compassionate people** can betray their values in challenging situations.
- There's a natural human pull to be a **bystander** or to follow a group/leader doing wrong, to keep a sense of **security or acceptance**.
- Cites **ethical fading**: when people are so focused on operational targets and under such pressure that the **ethical implications literally fade** from mind.
- A red-flag phrase: **"This is business"** — often signals ethics are being set aside.

## Part 5 — Social Fitness Training and solutions

- The leverage point is the **"space" between stimulus and response** — used to reconnect with values and wisdom; she calls it the **"magic pause button."**
- The core method is **Social Fitness Training**, developed over ~25 years by **Dr. Lynne Henderson** — retraining the brain to **override the fear response** and act consistently with one's values.
- A key tool is **Social Flight Simulations:** practising scary ethical scenarios to build **"muscle memory."** By rehearsing _with_ some fear, you can turn the **adrenaline shot** into a cue for **mindful action** rather than avoidance — what neuroscience calls **priming the brain**.

## Part 6 — Creating courageous corporate cultures

- Become a **"pattern interrupter":** notice where you typically stand up and where you don't.
- Given today's economic/political challenges, the world needs a **"bigger pause button."**
- Courage is an **innate capacity** in everyone — but ultimately a **choice** that must be made.
