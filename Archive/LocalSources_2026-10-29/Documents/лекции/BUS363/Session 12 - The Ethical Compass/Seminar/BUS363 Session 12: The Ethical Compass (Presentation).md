## Ethics vs Morals vs Law

**Britannica framing (paraphrased):** Both morality and ethics broadly concern telling "good from bad" / "right from wrong." Morality is often seen as personal and normative (held by the individual), whereas ethics is the standard of good/bad set by a particular community or social setting.
_Source: https://www.britannica.com/story/whats-the-difference-between-morality-and-ethics_

**The Ethics Centre video — "Ethics, morality, law – what's the difference?" (5:13)** — https://youtu.be/Xki2fRA0bY8 ; article: https://ethics.org.au/ethics-morality-law-whats-the-difference/
Three different demands on how we should live (ethics, morality, law):

- Inside us is a mix of **beliefs, claims, rules and norms** telling us how to live and behave.
- We can explore potential **ethical pressures** with more awareness and make sense of them.
- We must address challenges when facing **contradicting requirements**.

**Three-column comparison (The Ethics Centre, paraphrased):**

| **Ethics**                                                                                                            | **Morals**                                                                                                                          | **Law**                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A branch of philosophy answering the basic question **"What should I do?"**                                           | Many find morality very useful; a reflection on **"What kind of life do I want to live?"**                                          | In democratic nations, law is **not** a morality                                                                       |
| A **process of reflection**                                                                                           | Weighs all the various values, principles and purposes                                                                              | Aims to set basic **"standards of behaviour"** needed for a community to function and for people to be treated equally |
| Decisions shaped by **values, principles and purpose** — not by unthinking habit, social convention, or self-interest | Usually **inherited** from family/community/culture; people rarely "shop around" for a morality; usually an **unconscious process** |                                                                                                                        |

## An ethics framework (Purpose, Values, Principles)

**The Ethics Centre video — "Purpose, Values, Principles – An Ethics Framework" (3:34)** — https://youtu.be/KLvWMoFUboo ; article: https://ethics.org.au/purpose-matters/
An ethics framework (paraphrased) is: a statement of an organisation's **purpose, values and principles**; a clear statement of what it believes and the **standards** it upholds; a **roadmap to good decision-making**; and a guide to making the organisation the best version of itself.

## Unconscious bias

**The Ethics Centre video — "Unconscious Bias" (2:36)** — https://youtu.be/zJh20Wi2dMg ; article: https://ethics.org.au/confirmation-bias-ignoring-the-facts/
Key idea (paraphrased): brains evolved to help us survive, so they take shortcuts (heuristics / **unconscious bias**) to get through the day. These shortcuts are vital — **but they carry a cost.** The aim is to learn what unconscious bias is and to become more aware of where, when and how it operates. (Directly relevant to the algorithm/AI-bias theme later in the deck.)

## Ethical principles (quote)

Attributed to **Major Thomas C. Linn** (paraphrased): you can't excuse failing to follow an ethical principle by pointing to a superior's unethical behaviour — that just makes ethics "someone else's problem." Ethics involves everyone. Competent service to society can put both career and life at risk; if this ethic isn't internalised, the consequences may be severe, including externally-imposed reforms while one's ethical commitment still remains in doubt.
_Source: https://josephsononbusinessethics.com/2017/02/5463/_

## Ethical dilemmas — discussion videos

**1. The Trolley Problem — "Would you sacrifice one person to save five?" (TED-Ed, Eleanor Nelsen, 4:55)** — https://youtu.be/yg16u_bzjPE ; lesson: https://ed.ted.com/lessons/would-you-sacrifice-one-person-to-save-five-eleanor-nelsen
Classic setup: a runaway trolley is heading toward five workers; you stand by a switch that would divert it onto a second track where only one worker stands. Do you sacrifice one to save five? (Utilitarian vs deontological framing.)

**2. The Ethical Dilemma of Self-Driving Cars (TED-Ed, Patrick Lin, 4:15)** — https://youtu.be/ixIoDYVfKA0 ; lesson: https://ed.ted.com/lessons/the-ethical-dilemma-of-self-driving-cars-patrick-lin
Premise: autonomous vehicles will ultimately be safer and cleaner, but can't avoid all accidents. Discussion questions:

- How should the car be programmed for an **unavoidable accident**?
- How do we ensure the driving **algorithm is free of unconscious bias**?
- Would you buy a car that prioritises **your** safety over the safety of the many?

**3. "Universal basic income is a brilliant idea" — Yanis Varoufakis (Big Think, 3:46)** — https://youtu.be/O8B4U7o9kvg ; source: https://bigthink.com/u/yanis-varoufakis
Frame: with job losses from accelerating tech innovation across industries, how do people still afford to live? Varoufakis (ex-Greek Finance Minister) argues (paraphrased) the welfare state is costly and ineffective and can harm the poor more than it helps, and that **UBI** is a better alternative — leaving the open question of **where the funding would come from**.

**4. "Meet the biohacker using CRISPR to teach everyone gene editing" — Josiah Zayner (Quartz, 6:06)** — https://youtu.be/4vmHweDC5SY
Zayner believes anyone should be able to edit their DNA using **CRISPR**. He founded **ODIN**, selling DIY biotech kits that teach people to genetically modify bacteria and frogs (DIY gene therapy); the company has sold tens of thousands of CRISPR experiments. Raises the ethics of democratised, unregulated gene-editing.

## Emerging ethical issues (discussion lists)

_Source: The Millennium Project — http://107.22.164.43/millennium/ethics-rd1.html_

**Ethical issues of 2010–2025 (paraphrased prompts):**

- Should "information pollution" become a crime, the way environmental pollution now is?
- Is it ethical for society to recreate extinct species?
- Is it right to computer-augment or genetically enhance the brains of pets and other animals?
- In a hyper-connected world where ideas, people and resources can be brought together to solve a problem, is it unethical to **do nothing** to connect them when it's clearly within one's power?

**Ethical issues by 2025–2050 (paraphrased prompts):**

- If technology develops a mind of its own, what ethical obligations do we have for its behaviour — and should **artificial life** (life-mimicking software, sentient robots) have rights?
- Do we have the right to genetically change ourselves into one or several new species?
- Is it right for humans to **merge with technology** to avoid technological hegemony? (e.g. "bionic soldiers" — the tension between enhanced military troops and the non-enhanced, and pushback against a two-tiered society of enhanced vs "ordinary" citizens.)

## Ethical discussion scenarios

### Scenario A — New technology + privacy

_Source: Crone & Wright (2014), https://www.dhi.ac.uk/san/waysofbeing/data/data-crone-wright-2014b.pdf_
Paraphrased prompts: Should policy-makers and privacy advocates worry that ordinary citizen-consumers don't treat privacy as very important — certainly less important than the conveniences they get from large-scale data aggregation, data mining and profiling? Or is asking a policy-maker that question like asking the fox whether the henhouse needs more protection? The scenario also raises **freedom to opt out** and **function creep** (technologies later used for purposes never originally specified).

### Scenario B — "Emails Exposed"

_Case & questions authored by Akshay Vyas, on the Markkula Center for Applied Ethics (Santa Clara University) website; via https://www.unodc.org/e4j/en/integrity-ethics/module-1/exercises.html_

Summary (paraphrased): Robert is a high-profile basketball player at a Perth university with a big social-media following. Because of his profile, the athletic board decides to monitor his social accounts; with no WA law preventing it, he's required to hand over his personal social-media logins. Officials say monitoring is to catch compliance/behavioural issues early and coach athletes on their online presence. After Robert tweets about skipping class (hashtags referencing a TV show), officials flag it and quietly begin monitoring his **university email** too — without telling him. The university's handbook lets administrators access student email to safeguard the system or ensure compliance with university rules, but is silent on whether owners must be notified. In his email, officials find messages showing Robert's tutor had been supplying answers to homework and quizzes. Result: Robert is placed on athletic probation and the tutor is fired.

**Discussion questions:**

- Should universities be allowed to monitor student email and social media? If so, under what circumstances?
- Where is the line between campus safety and invasion of privacy?
- Are university email/social-media monitoring rules too vague? How could they be clarified?
- Should Robert be punished for cheating if he didn't know his email was being monitored? What about the tutor?

**Activity:** discuss the case from three perspectives — (1) Robert the student, (2) Robert's tutor, (3) the university officials — then debate as a group.

## Key terms & references

**Core concepts**

- **Ethics** (philosophy; "What should I do?"; reflective; values/principles/purpose)
- **Morals** (inherited, often unconscious; "What kind of life do I want to live?")
- **Law** (community standards of behaviour; not itself a morality in democracies)
- **Ethics framework** = purpose + values + principles → roadmap for decisions
- **Unconscious bias / heuristics** (useful shortcuts with a cost) → links to **algorithmic bias**
- **Ethical leadership** / ethical decision making ("four key drivers")
