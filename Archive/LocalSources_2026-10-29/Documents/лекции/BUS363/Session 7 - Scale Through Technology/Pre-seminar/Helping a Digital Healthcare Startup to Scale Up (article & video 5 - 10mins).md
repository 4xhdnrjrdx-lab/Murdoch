## Overview / context

- **uMotif** is a **UK-based digital-health startup**, cofounded in **2012** by **Bruce Hellman**.
- **Origin story:** Hellman was inspired after tracking his own sleep when his baby was born, and the self-awareness that gave him.
- **What the product does:** uMotif gives people tools to record and track their **mood, sleep, and clinically relevant symptoms** (e.g. dizziness, breathlessness) on a **mobile phone app**, then share that data with their **doctor and care team** to better inform appointments and treatment plans.
- **The thesis behind it:** Hellman's framing is that the "quantified self" (sensors measuring steps, weight, blood pressure) has existed for years, but focusing only on the **quantitative** misses the whole picture of a person.

**Traction / credibility at the time:**

- Named one of **50 UK "scale-ups"** that could grow to over **£100m in revenue**, by the not-for-profit **Silicon Valley Comes to the UK**.
- Software/apps deployed across **dozens of hospitals**.
- Profiled in _The Guardian_, _The Daily Telegraph_, and _The London Times_.

---

## The engagement — IDEO Europe "Startup in Residence"

- To scale strategically, Hellman applied to **IDEO Europe's first "Startup in Residence"** — a **three-month program** that accelerates companies through **design thinking**.
- Scope was deliberately total: Hellman said they brought **all of uMotif** — product, organization, branding, approach, and business model — wanting to reconsider just about everything.
- During the residency, the uMotif team **embedded with IDEO's London studio**, working with a mixed team of **interaction, business and communication designers, plus design researchers**, to examine every aspect of the business.

---

## Key insight #1 — Narrow the focus (the core "scaling" move)

- The team found uMotif's **organic growth had mirrored the fast-expanding digital-health market**, but as a result the offering had become **fragmented**.
- **Decision:** tighten the sales focus from **14 branches of medicine down to just two — oncology and cardiology** (with **mental health kept as an R&D area**).
- This is the central lesson of the case: **scaling came from focusing/narrowing, not from doing more** — concentrating effort on fewer, higher-value segments.

## Key insight #2 — Reframe the value proposition: "Qualified Self"

- In-depth interviews with **patients, nurses, doctors, and executives** surfaced a clear opportunity: _simple symptom tracking for people going through an acute or serious period in their health, to activate and extend the patient–care-team relationship._
- The team coined a reframe: from the **"Quantified Self" → the "Qualified Self"**, to capture uMotif's distinctive approach of measuring **how people feel** (qualitative), not just metrics.

## Key insight #3 — Redesign for the real care context (multiple caregivers)

- The **original** uMotif product was designed around a **single clinician**.
- The team designed a **new dashboard** built for the reality of modern healthcare: **multiple caregivers** / a whole care team.
- The deeper aim: not just helping patients understand their own health, but giving them **meaningful insights to have richer conversations with their care teams**, so they can jointly improve care plans and outcomes.

---

## Outcome

- Hellman's stated result: uMotif became a **more focused, confident and clear organization**, prepared to scale **sales, delivery and clinical impact**.
- Back in its own HQ, the team carried away a **toolkit of IDEO systems and processes** to run the business and apply to new projects, products and services.
- Forward thesis: health systems worldwide are rapidly adopting digital channels; uMotif aims to be at the forefront of that shift, using **design thinking to put people at the centre of their care** — improving quality of life, treatment outcomes, and healthcare experience.
