Project management is the process of organizing tasks to achieve project objectives. A project manager will plan these tasks, monitor their progress, and ensure that results are delivered.

They might use a methodology to help them with this. There are many different project management methodologies to choose from. Some are traditional and follow a linear model, while others are more flexible in nature.

There is no one-size-fits-all approach — a project manager must choose the best methodology for their team. In this video, we will outline the main project management methodologies and discuss their various advantages and disadvantages.

## [≈00:00–02:15] Introduction and the Waterfall Methodology

- **Why methodologies matter:** project management uses specific frameworks to make effective use of resources, time, and team coordination.
- **Waterfall (traditional):**
  - The most traditional and common PM method.
  - **Process:** linear and sequential — each phase must finish before the next begins.
  - **Best for:** highly structured projects with clearly defined requirements and a restricted scope where changes are unlikely.
  - **Drawbacks:** time-consuming and inflexible; a mistake made early is difficult and expensive to fix later.

## [≈02:15–04:30] Critical Path Method (CPM) and Resource Management

- **CPM:** determines the shortest possible time to complete a project by identifying the "critical path."
- **The critical path:** the longest sequence of scheduled activities that must finish on time for the whole project to finish on schedule.
- **Resource leveling:** balancing demand for resources against available supply.
- **Time management:** identifies which tasks are "critical" and which have "float" (scheduling flexibility).
- **Best for:** complex projects with many interdependent tasks where timing is critical.

## [≈04:30–07:00] Agile, Scrum, and Kanban

- **Agile philosophy:** unlike Waterfall, Agile is flexible and iterative.
- **Scrum:**
  - A subset of Agile using short, repeatable work cycles called **sprints**.
  - Emphasises small, self-organising teams and regular **stand-up** meetings to track progress.
- **Kanban:**
  - Focuses on **visualising work** and managing the **flow** of tasks.
  - Uses a **Kanban board** to move tasks through stages (e.g., To-Do → In Progress → Done).
  - **Goal:** prevent bottlenecks and avoid overloading the team at any one time.

## [≈07:00–09:15] Extreme Programming (XP) and Lean

- **Extreme Programming (XP):**
  - A methodology built specifically for **software development**.
  - Emphasises high-quality code and responsiveness to changing customer requirements via frequent **releases** and short development cycles.
  - **Core practices:** pair programming, code reviews, continuous testing.
- **Lean Project Management:**
  - Originates from manufacturing (Lean); focuses on **eliminating "waste"** and maximising **customer value**.
  - Encourages **continuous improvement** — constantly finding ways to improve the process and cut unnecessary steps.

## [≈09:15–11:30] Six Sigma and Lean Six Sigma

- **Six Sigma:**
  - A **data-driven** methodology aimed at reducing **defects** and variability in processes.
  - **Goal:** near-perfect quality through statistical analysis.
- **DMAIC framework** (core Six Sigma process):
  1. **Define** the problem.
  2. **Measure** key aspects of the current process.
  3. **Analyze** the data to find root causes.
  4. **Improve** the process.
  5. **Control** the future state.
- **Lean Six Sigma:** combines Lean's waste-reduction focus with Six Sigma's defect-reduction focus for a highly efficient process.

## [≈11:30–12:49] PMBOK and Choosing a Methodology

- **PMBOK (Project Management Body of Knowledge):** considered a **standard**, not a specific methodology. It outlines **10 Knowledge Areas** — including Communications, Human Resources, and Risk Management — essential for any project manager to master.
- **Conclusion:** there is **no "one size fits all"** methodology. Managers must choose the framework that best fits the specific project, team size, and organisational goals to deliver successfully and keep customers happy.
