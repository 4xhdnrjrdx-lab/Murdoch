Agile is a project management methodology based on four core values and 12 principles outlined in the Agile Manifesto. The Agile project management philosophy encourages early and continuous delivery, flexible and adaptive planning, collaboration with stakeholders, and a supportive working environment.

Though Agile has its roots in software development, it is used across many industries to manage and execute a variety of project types. Agile is also interpreted through different frameworks like Scrum and Kanban.

In this video, we’ll explore the history of Agile project management, the frameworks within Agile, and the different roles and processes of an Agile team.

## [≈00:00–03:15] The Agile Philosophy and Manifesto

- **Ubiquity:** Agile is one of the most widely used PM philosophies, especially in IT/software, though its adaptive nature appeals to many industries.
- **Statistic:** ~**71% of organisations** report using Agile methods at least some of the time. [Wrike/survey figure — verify before citing.]
- **The Agile Manifesto:** articulated through **4 core values** and **12 principles**.
- **The four core values:**
  1. **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools.
  2. **Working software** over comprehensive documentation.
  3. **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation.
  4. **Responding to change** over following a plan.
- **The 12 principles:**
  1. Satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.
  2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.
  3. Deliver working software frequently (a couple of weeks to a couple of months).
  4. Ongoing daily collaboration between the project team and stakeholders.
  5. Build projects around motivated individuals; give them the environment and support they need.
  6. Convey information through face-to-face interaction (most effective method).
  7. Working software is the primary measure of progress.
  8. Promote sustainable development — sponsors, developers, and users maintain a constant pace indefinitely.
  9. Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility.
  10. Simplicity — maximising the amount of work not done — is essential.
  11. The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organising teams.
  12. At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective and adjusts accordingly.

## [≈03:15–05:15] The Scrum Framework

- **Prevalence:** Scrum is the most popular framework/interpretation of Agile's values and principles.
- **Sprints:** short, fast-paced cycles, typically **one to four weeks**.
- **Why short cycles:** teams don't plan too far ahead and can refine processes to optimise customer delivery; ideal where deliverables ship in increments or need consistent feedback.
- **Scrum practices:** daily stand-up meetings; sprint retrospectives at cycle end; rapid feedback and close collaboration with customers/stakeholders; self-organising and adaptive (reacting to needs rather than predicting them up front).
- **Roles in a Scrum team:**
  - **Team Lead / Scrum Master:** coaches the group and "paves the way," helping the team clear obstacles (resource limits, poor communication, other roadblocks).
  - **Team Member:** engineers, creatives, or designers doing the bulk of day-to-day work.
  - **Product Owner:** owns product vision and priorities — "if the project is a plane, the product owner is the captain."

## [≈05:15–07:45] The Kanban Framework

- **Flow, not cycles:** unlike Scrum's cyclical sprints, Kanban is "a river that flows" — tasks are **pulled** based on requirements/completion rather than **pushed**.
- **Origins:** adapted from the **Toyota** production process in the **1940s**; a leaner take on Agile.
- **The Kanban board:** a visual tool where tasks are **cards / sticky notes** moving through columns/phases.
- **Standard columns:** **Backlog / To Do** (ranked tasks to attend to) → **In Progress** (being worked on) → **Completed** (finished).
- **Customisation:** teams add workflow-specific columns, e.g., a **"Waiting for sign-off"** approval step.
- **5 steps to successful implementation:**
  1. Visualise the workflow.
  2. Limit **Work In Progress (WIP)** (said "whip").
  3. Measure and manage the flow.
  4. Make process policies explicit.
  5. Use models to spot improvement opportunities.

## [≈07:45–09:29] Comparison and Tools

- **Scrum vs Kanban:**
  - **Scrum** = incremental delivery via short cycles; more "ritualistic" (daily meetings, reviews, retrospectives).
  - **Kanban** = continuous workflow / moving tasks; more flexible and less formal.
- **Choosing:** depends on project requirements, clients, and how the team works best.
- **Wrike:** supports both via Kanban boards, custom workflows, and dashboards for sprint planning and backlog organisation.

## Key terms & references

- **Methodologies/frameworks:** Agile (philosophy), Scrum, Kanban, Lean (leaner approach).
- **Foundation:** Agile Manifesto — 4 values, 12 principles.
- **Scrum terms:** sprints (1–4 weeks), Scrum Master / Team Lead, Product Owner, Team Member, daily stand-up, sprint retrospective, incremental delivery, self-organising teams.
