In our day to day live we routinely deal with both physical goods, as well as services. But where do these products or services come from? How are these goods and services made?

Operations Management can be defined as the management of the entire process that transfers inputs into outputs. Outputs are as said physical goods or services. Inputs can be material, manpower and machines.

## Part 1 — Definition and the "Black Box" of Production

- Operations management explains where the physical goods and services we encounter in daily life originate.
- It lets you "peek inside the black box" — i.e., the production process.
- **Definition:** operations management is the "management of the entire process that transforms inputs into outputs." [The raw output said "demand management of the entire process" — the phrasing is likely a transcription artifact; verify if you quote it.]
- **Inputs** fall into three categories ("the 3 Ms"): **material, manpower, and machines**.
- **Outputs:** physical goods or services.

## Part 2 — The Transformation Process (Car-Industry Example)

The transformation process is illustrated with a car-manufacturing example, in sequence:

1. **Material selection:** metal is the material input.
2. **Machinery:** a machine — here a "press" — reshapes the metal.
3. **Manpower:** a machine operator runs the press.
4. **Transformation:** the metal is reshaped through the process.
5. **Finished component:** the metal becomes a finished component.
6. **Final output:** the component is assembled onto a car — the final output.

## Part 3 — Process Improvement and Methodologies

- A central aspect of operations management is improving the transformation process.
- This is done through specific management concepts and frameworks:
  - **Total Quality Management (TQM)** — improving the production process.
  - **Kanban** — a methodology for process improvement.
  - **Total Productive Maintenance (TPM)** — maintaining and improving the production process.

## Key terms & references

- **Core concepts:** operations management, the "black box" (production process), transformation process, inputs → transformation → outputs.
- **Inputs (3 Ms):** material (e.g., metal), manpower (e.g., machine operator), machines (e.g., press).
- **Outputs:** physical goods (e.g., assembled car), services.
- **Improvement frameworks:** Total Quality Management (TQM), Kanban, Total Productive Maintenance (TPM).
- **Organization:** Frankfurt School of Finance & Management.
