## Part 1 — Defining Agile and its Core Philosophy

- **Agile** means different things to different people, but fundamentally it aims to simplify ways of working.
- It's designed to **empower people** to improve processes — making them more repeatable and quicker.
- **The Lean analogy:** Agile is to development and project management what **Lean** is to manufacturing processes.

## Part 2 — Agile vs. Waterfall and Customer Value

- **Early value:** a primary driver of Agile is delivering value early in the process.
- **Voice of the customer:** Agile relies on listening to the "voice of the customer" to stay highly responsive to change.
- **Comparison to Waterfall:**
  - **Waterfall** starts at the beginning and moves through sequential steps, producing a result only at the very end.
  - Many major projects fail under Waterfall because they take **three to five years** to deliver — by which time the world has moved on and requirements have changed.
- Agile is positioned as more effective because it can adapt as the world and customer needs evolve.

## Part 3 — Evolution and Industry Impact

- **Origins:** Agile emerged from the **IT development world**, but has gained significant interest for adoption outside tech.
- **Global adoption:** seen as a "sensible way of doing things," increasingly applied across non-tech contexts.
- **Human factor:** noted for delivering high **job satisfaction** for the people involved.

## Key terms & references

- **Methodologies:** Agile, Waterfall, Lean (manufacturing).
- **Core concepts:** early value; voice of the customer; responsiveness to change; empowerment; process repeatability; job satisfaction.
- **Sectors mentioned:** IT development, manufacturing.
- **Organisation:** Association for Project Management (APM).
