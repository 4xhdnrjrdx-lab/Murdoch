The Fortune 500 companies are the highest-revenue-generating companies in the United States by rank of total revenue. The "Fortune 500" name is derived from "Fortune Media", a magazine company. In 2024, the Fortune 500 companies cumulatively included $18,843,981,300,000 (18.84 trillion) in revenue and consisted of 31,002,174 (31 million) employees. This is official data gathered from each company's annual 10-K reports, which are required to be released within 90 days of the company's fiscal year end, usually December 31. All information is public.

## Industry representation in the top 100 (answers Activity 2: "Are there industries most represented?")

Grouping the 100 companies by sector cluster:

- **Financials & Insurance — ~23 (largest cluster):** Commercial Banks (JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citi, Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, Capital One = 7); Diversified Financials (Fannie Mae, StoneX, Freddie Mac, American Express = 4); Insurance P&C (Berkshire, State Farm, Progressive, Allstate, Nationwide, Liberty Mutual, Travelers, USAA = 8); Insurance Life/Health (MetLife, New York Life, Prudential, TIAA = 4).
- **Health Care & Pharma — ~16:** Insurance/Managed Care (UnitedHealth, Elevance, Centene, Humana = 4); Pharmacy & Other Services (CVS, Cigna = 2); Medical Facilities (HCA = 1); Wholesalers: Health Care (McKesson, Cencora, Cardinal Health = 3); Pharmaceuticals (J&J, Eli Lilly, Merck, Pfizer, AbbVie, Bristol-Myers Squibb = 6).
- **Technology — ~14:** Internet Services & Retailing (Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Uber = 4); Computer Software (Microsoft, Oracle = 2); Computers/Office Equipment (Apple, Dell, HP = 3); Semiconductors (Nvidia, Broadcom, Intel = 3); IT Services (IBM = 1); Network Equipment (Cisco = 1).
- **Energy / Oil & Gas — ~8:** Petroleum Refining (Exxon, Chevron, Marathon, Phillips 66, Valero = 5); Mining/Crude-Oil (ConocoPhillips = 1); Pipelines (Energy Transfer, Enterprise Products = 2).
- **Aerospace & Defense — 5:** Boeing, RTX, Lockheed Martin, General Dynamics, General Electric.
- **Retail & Consumer staples — spread across several categories:** General Merchandisers (Walmart, Costco, Target = 3); Food & Drug Stores (Walgreens, Kroger, Albertsons, Publix = 4); Specialty Retailers (Home Depot, Lowe's, TJX = 3); Food Production/Consumer Products (PepsiCo, ADM, Tyson = 3); Beverages (Coca-Cola); Apparel (Nike); Household & Personal Products (P&G).
- **Telecommunications — 4:** Verizon, AT&T, Comcast, Charter.
- **Motor Vehicles & Parts — 3:** Ford, GM, Tesla.
- **Airlines — 3:** Delta, United, American.
- **Logistics/Delivery — 2:** UPS, FedEx.

**Takeaway:** the Fortune top 100 is dominated by **financial services/insurance, health care/pharma, big tech, and energy** — large, mature, capital-intensive firms competing largely on scale, efficiency and cost. The very top (Amazon, Walmart, UnitedHealth, Apple, McKesson) are revenue giants driven by retail/logistics, managed care and distribution.

## How this maps to Activity 2 (Slide 28) and the B Corp comparison (Slide 29)

Activity 2 asks you to compare these Fortune firms with the B Corps and answer set questions. Useful contrasts to draw:

- **What they have in common (Fortune 100):** huge scale, public-market listing (mostly), focus on revenue growth, operational efficiency, and shareholder value; sectors skew to finance, health, tech, energy.
- **How B Corps differ (Slide 29 list — Patagonia, KeepCup, Eileen Fisher, T2 Tea, Aesop, Bank Australia, Coursera, etc.):** mostly smaller, often private, concentrated in apparel, personal care, food/beverage and ethical banking/investment; defined by **certified social/environmental performance** and stakeholder (not just shareholder) value. Industries most represented among the B Corps are apparel and personal-care/consumer goods plus ethical finance — quite different from the finance/health/energy/tech tilt of the Fortune 100.
- **OM angle (the part graded for A1/Operations Management):** when you "choose one Fortune 100 and one B Corp company and explain how they use operations management functions," map each to the Session 2 OM decision areas — design, supply chain, quality management, forecasting, location/capacity planning, job design, inventory management, scheduling. A Fortune giant (e.g., Walmart or Amazon) will show OM optimised for scale, cost and logistics; a B Corp (e.g., Patagonia or KeepCup) will show OM where sustainability/ethics are designed into supply chain, materials and product design — tying back to the unit's "positive impact" / SDG theme.
