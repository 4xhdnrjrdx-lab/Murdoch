## Slide 13 — Operations Management (intro)

- In day-to-day life we routinely deal with both physical goods and services. But where do these products/services come from? How are these goods and services made?
- This is where Operations Management comes in: by looking closely at the production of such goods, we can "take a peek inside the 'black box'" — the production process.

Diagram (Asana, "Functions of operations management and skills needed") pairs each OM function with a skill:

- Operational planning → Resourcefulness
- Finance → Financial planning
- Product design → Data interpretation
- Quality control → Conflict management
- Forecasting → Data-driven decision making
- Strategy → Critical thinking
- Supply chain management → Problem solving

## Slide 14 — Operations Management (definition)

**Operations Management** can be defined as **the management of the entire process that transfers inputs into outputs.** Outputs are physical goods or services. Inputs can be **material, manpower and machines** (the "3 Ms").
(Slide shows the same operations-management explainer cartoon — press, materials, worker — as the Frankfurt School video.)

## Slide 15 — What is 'Project Management'? (1)

- Everywhere you look, projects are underway. Whether recruiting a new staff member or constructing the world's tallest building, every project needs a manager to ensure success — someone watching every detail, at every stage, from inception to completion.
- Put simply, project management is about getting things done.
- It's about knowing exactly what you want to achieve, how you'll achieve it, and how long it will take; ensuring everyone involved shares and understands those aims before the first steps are taken, and continues to as the end of the project draws closer.

## Slide 16 — What is 'Project Management'? (2)

- Because successful projects don't just happen. They're not just a long list of jobs — they are a masterpiece of planning, management, organisation and communication; a carefully choreographed sequence of events where progress is smooth and steady, one step leads seamlessly to the next, and every possible hitch has been considered and countered.
- Video link: https://youtu.be/Jk-JwtScIlw

## Slide 17 — What is a 'Project'? (1)

- Projects come in all shapes and sizes. Projects need project managers and teams to help plan, monitor, and achieve goals. It's important to distinguish projects from day-to-day activities we call **'business-as-usual' (BAU)**.
- The video gives examples of what a project might look like and how it differs from BAU. Project definitions vary by scope, budget, and size, but all share certain common features.
- Video link: https://youtu.be/j72niEHuk-0

## Slide 18 — What is a 'Project'? (2) — the five project phases

- **Initiation** — the pre-planning phase; even plans need a little planning to ensure success.
- **Planning** — after initiation, figure out exactly how to make the project succeed.
- **Execution** — the "doing" phase; by now everyone should know what needs to be done and their role.
- **Monitoring** — project managers form a bridge between stakeholders and project developers, running back and forth passing on information and updates.
- **Closure** — the PM's job doesn't end when development is over; they review the details, seeing what went wrong and what went right.
  (These map to the PMBOK process groups: Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring & Controlling, Closing.)

## Slide 20 — Operations Management (definition expanded)

- Every business is managed through **three major functions: finance, marketing, and operations management.**
- **Operations management (OM)** is the **business function responsible for planning, coordinating, and controlling the resources needed to produce a company's goods and services.**
- Involves: **managing people, equipment, technology, materials, and information** to produce products and services.

## Slide 21 — Key OM components

List of OM activities:

- Designing the product
- Deciding what resources are needed
- Arranging schedules, equipment, and facilities
- Managing inventory
- Controlling quality
- Designing the jobs to make the product
- Designing work methods

Side table — **Project Management vs Operations Management** (Basis of: comparison):
| Basis of | Project Management | Operations Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | Projects have a definitive start and a definitive finish. | Operations are continuous. |
| **Task Type** | Project tasks are specific to that project and have never been done before. | Operational tasks are repetitive and cyclical. |
| **Success / Improvement Criteria** | Project work success is based on project objectives identified specifically and uniquely for that project. | Operational work success is based on previous indicators (i.e., system availability). |

## Slide 33 — Watch: What is Project Management?

- Project management is the process of organizing tasks to achieve project objectives. A project manager plans tasks, monitors progress, and ensures results are delivered, often using a methodology.
- There are many PM methodologies: some traditional/linear, others more flexible. There is **no one-size-fits-all** approach — the PM must choose the best methodology for their team.
- The video outlines the main PM methodologies and their advantages/disadvantages.
- Video link: https://youtu.be/8UJ_q0pwMDA

## Slide 34 — Watch: What is 'Agile Project Management'?

- Agile is a project management methodology based on **four core values and 12 principles** outlined in the **Agile Manifesto**.
- The Agile philosophy encourages early and continuous delivery, flexible and adaptive planning, collaboration with stakeholders, and a supportive working environment.
- Though Agile has roots in software development, it's used across many industries for a variety of project types.
- Agile is interpreted through different frameworks like **Scrum and Kanban**.
- Video link: https://youtu.be/x-nX23hCwRE

## Key terms & references

- **Three core business functions:** finance, marketing, operations management.

- **Inputs (3 Ms):** material, manpower, machines. **Outputs:** physical goods or services. **Concept:** the "black box" (production process).
- **OM functions & matched skills (Asana):** operational planning/resourcefulness; finance/financial planning; product design/data interpretation; quality control/conflict management; forecasting/data-driven decision making; strategy/critical thinking; supply chain management/problem solving.
