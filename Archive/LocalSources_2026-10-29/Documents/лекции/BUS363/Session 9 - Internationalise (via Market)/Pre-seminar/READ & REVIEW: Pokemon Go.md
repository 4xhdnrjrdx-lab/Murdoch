## Lead claim: biggest US mobile game ever

Pokémon GO is reported as officially the biggest mobile game in the United States, with **21 million active daily users**, narrowly overtaking _Candy Crush Saga's_ **20 million** peak active-user count in the US. Other strong 2016 mobile contenders — Supercell's _Clash Royale_ and _Slither.io_ — were far behind. The data is attributed to **SurveyMonkey Intelligence**, which published charts illustrating the game's scale.

## Bigger Than Snapchat?

SurveyMonkey also estimated that Pokémon GO was on track to overtake **Snapchat's** active daily users on **Android** devices within a couple of days. If that happened, the augmented-reality game would also pass **Google Maps'** active daily users on Android — meaning more people would be opening maps to find Pokémon than for almost any other purpose. The article frames this as an opportunity: business owners should capitalise on the trend, and using Pokémon GO to attract customers is presented as a viable new business strategy.

## More Engaging Than Facebook

The article argues that **average user engagement** matters even more than active daily users. Data from **SensorTower** showed Pokémon GO was more engaging — taking up more daily user time — than Facebook.

### Chart 3 — "Daily Time Spent in Pokémon GO by Average iOS User, Compared to Top Mobile Apps"

_Based on U.S. iOS usage for July 11, 2016. Source: SensorTower._

| App            | Daily time spent (avg iOS user) |
| -------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Pokémon GO** | **33m 25s**                     |
| Facebook       | 22m 8s                          |
| Snapchat       | 18m 7s                          |
| Twitter        | 17m 56s                         |
| Instagram      | 15m 15s                         |
| slither.io     | 10m 8s                          |

Important caveat from SensorTower: while Pokémon GO beat the social-media apps on time spent, it still trailed other dedicated mobile **games** — _Game of War_ averaged about **2 hours** of daily usage, and _Candy Crush Saga_ about **43 minutes**. Even so, 33 minutes for Pokémon GO is significant.

## Good For The Gander (strategic implications)

- The phenomenon was a net win for **Nintendo**, though the author cautions the company's rapid market-value gains may have been **hype-driven** rather than reflecting actual added value. Regardless, Nintendo's move into mobile gaming launched strongly, and sentiment around the company shifted from pessimism to hype and buzz.
- Developer **Niantic** was credited with creating a genuine phenomenon with Pokémon GO, effectively putting **augmented-reality (AR) gaming** on the map. The author predicted a wave of imitators, would-be competitors, and outright copycat games.
- The hope expressed: that new entrants would do innovative things with interactive AR gaming, and that more impressive uses of the technology would follow.
- The game's long-term user base would depend on **how Niantic and its partners managed the game's future** — handling server issues and rolling out new content. At the time of writing, the game was positioned to make a great deal of money. Either way, the author concludes, it had changed how people think about mobile and social gaming.
