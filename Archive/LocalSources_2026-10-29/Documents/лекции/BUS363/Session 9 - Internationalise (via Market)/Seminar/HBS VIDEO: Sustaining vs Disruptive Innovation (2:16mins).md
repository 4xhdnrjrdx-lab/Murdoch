## Part 1 — Why innovation matters

- Innovation is essential in any industry: it lets a company meet customer needs, **differentiate** in the market, and outperform competitors.
- It generally falls into two categories: **sustaining** or **disruptive**.

## Part 2 — Sustaining innovation

- **Sustaining innovation** = improving **existing** products.
- **Who it targets:** developing **superior versions** of a company's top offerings to sell to its **best, most profitable customers** for higher profit.
- **Best suited to:** businesses already **well-established** in their industry.

## Part 3 — Disruptive innovation

- **Disruptive innovation** = a company with **fewer resources** challenges and eventually disrupts established **incumbents**, moving **upmarket** over time. Two primary types:
  - **Low-end disruption:** enter the **bottom** of an existing market with a product that is **"good enough" / satisfactory but much cheaper** than incumbents'. Because those low-margin segments lack a strong profit incentive, incumbents often **retreat to more lucrative areas**, letting the disruptor take the space.
  - **New-market disruption:** create an **entirely new market segment** to reach **underserved** customers — by taking something previously **expensive or unattainable** and making it **affordable and accessible** to a much larger population.

## Part 4 — Strategic positioning for growth

- **Established companies:** to protect a competitive advantage and **avoid being disrupted**, focus on **improving current offerings** (sustaining).
- **Emerging companies:** help **create new products/segments** and work to **maintain their "disruptive scope"** as they grow.

---

## Key terms & references

- **Sustaining innovation** — improving existing products for top-tier customers to raise profit.
- **Disruptive innovation** — a smaller/under-resourced entrant challenges incumbents and moves upmarket.
- **Low-end disruption** — cheaper, "satisfactory" products at the bottom of an existing market.
- **New-market disruption** — making previously unattainable products affordable/accessible to new customers.
- **Incumbents** — the established, dominant firms in an industry.
- **Differentiation** — achieving a distinct market position vs. competitors.
- **Framework origin (context):** Clayton Christensen, _The Innovator's Dilemma_.
