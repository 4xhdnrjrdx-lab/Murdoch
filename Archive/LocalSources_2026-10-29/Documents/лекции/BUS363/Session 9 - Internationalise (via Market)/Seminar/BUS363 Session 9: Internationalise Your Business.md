## Main Points: Internet-based Business Models

Three drivers underpinning internet-based business models:

- **Technology** — i.e., new technologies reducing the cost of communication.
- **Collaboration** — i.e., new ways of interaction between different parties have enabled innovative transaction and exchange mechanisms.
- **Adaptability** — i.e., the high **'clock speed'** (the high speed of change in the business environment) of information technology fosters the development of new organisational forms.

---

## Business Scalability

Two lenses on scalability as a growth/success factor:

- **New venture growth** — Gilbert et al. (2006) conclude that the most important predictors of new venture growth are the _entrepreneur's characteristics, resources, strategy, industry, and organisation structure and systems_ (p. 928). [paraphrased from the cited quotation to preserve the listed predictors]

- **New business model innovation** — treated as an important growth / scalability success factor. Business model scalability is defined as <q>a business model's ability to increase revenues faster than the corresponding cost base</q> (Hallowell, 2001).

### Examples of Business Model Scalability

Companies cited as having scaled quickly and become important contenders on the web:

- **Dropbox**
- **Zynga**
- **Salesforce.com**

Key observations:

- Today, firms with the **ability / agility to grow fast** and that are **responsive / adaptable to change** seem to be succeeding.
- Investors often look for **'infinite scalability'**.
- **Effective and efficient use of technology** plays an important role in internet-based business models, but there is more to success than technology alone.

### Harvard Business Review — on scalability (block quote, paraphrased for copyright)

HBR's point: on the internet, the inability to scale — to get very big very fast — leaves a venture nowhere. It is not enough for the _technology_ alone to be scalable; the _entire business model_ must also be scalable. A venture needs to be able to quickly extend into new markets, either horizontally or vertically. "Will it scale?" is one of the first questions venture capitalists ask. (Source: _Harvard Business Review_.)

## Consumer Decision-Making

### Framing I — "Which gamble would you rather play?"

Demonstrates framing effects / prospect-theory intuitions (risk-seeking in the loss domain, risk-averse in the gain domain).

_Chance to Win…_

- A. 100% chance to win $10
- B. 50% chance to win $20 and 50% chance to win $0

_Chance to Lose…_

- A. 100% chance to lose $5
- B. 50% chance to lose $10 and 50% chance to lose $0

### Framing II — equivalent options framed differently

Demonstrates attribute framing (logically identical options feel different based on wording).

_Which frozen yoghurt would you rather buy & eat?_

- A. 20% fat
- B. 80% fat free

_Which grade would you rather get?_

- A. below the top 25% in class
- B. in the top 75% in class

### Values

Definition: values are <q>enduring beliefs [that] a given behaviour or outcome is desirable or good.</q>

Four types of values:

1. **Global** — enduring & abstract values.
2. **Domain-specific** — apply only to a specific domain (e.g., family).
3. **Terminal** — desired end states (e.g., true friendship).
4. **Instrumental** — values needed to achieve end states (e.g., being honest).

### Changing Values — "Which values change over time?"

Visual prompt contrasting shifts in social/cultural values over time, illustrated by paired images:

- "Fascism" vs "Capitalism" imagery — shifting political/economic value framings.

- Nuclear / fossil power vs solar & wind energy — shift toward environmental/renewable values.

Discussion prompt: **Which values change over time?**

### Why (some) values change? — Influences on values

Culture (according to **Hofstede**) — cultural dimensions:

- Individualism vs. collectivism
- Uncertainty avoidance (structured vs. unstructured situations)
- Masculinity vs. Femininity
- Power distance (equality in status)

Additional influences on values:

- Ethnic identification (e.g., European vs. Asian ethnicity)
- Social class (e.g., upper class more prosocial?)
- Age (generations stick together)

Recurring discussion prompt: **Which values change over time?**

### Other Considerations (Personality, Lifestyle, AIO)

**PERSONALITY** — Can personality affect consumer behaviour? (**Yes**)

- Optimal stimulation level
- Dogmatism (resistant to change)
- Need for uniqueness & creativity
- Susceptibility to influence
- Frugality (saving money)
- Self-monitoring:
  - High self-monitoring (others are important)
  - Low self-monitoring (others are not important)
- Competitiveness

**LIFESTYLE** — Lifestyles affect consumption and marketing in many ways.

- Activities, interests, & opinions (**AIO**)
- Market segmentation (e.g., golfers)
- Communication (e.g., blogs for wine enthusiasts)
- New product ideas (e.g., toe shoes)
