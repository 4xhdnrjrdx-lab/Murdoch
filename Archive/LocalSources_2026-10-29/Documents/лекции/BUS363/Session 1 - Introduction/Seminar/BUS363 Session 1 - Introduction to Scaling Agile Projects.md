## Slide 26 — Factors that contribute to Project Success

- **A clear goal / objectives (scope) !!!**
- Support from senior management
- Realistic schedule (timeframe)
- End user commitment (stakeholder)
- Adequate funds/resources
- Effective leadership / conflict resolution
- Flexible approach to change
- Clear communication channels
- Taking account of past experience, historic records; and
- Effective management of uncertainty (risk / opportunity)

## Slide 29 — Project Management Institute (PMI) — PMBOK timeline

Timeline of the **PMBOK (Project Management Body of Knowledge) Guide** editions:

- **1996 — PMBOK 1st edition:** First edition released by PMI; consisted of 9 Knowledge Areas and 37 processes.
- **2000 — PMBOK 2nd edition:** Update reflecting the progress and development of project management; consisted of 9 Knowledge Areas and 39 processes.
- **2004 — PMBOK 3rd edition:** Revised to include 44 processes and 592 ITTO (Inputs, Tools, and Techniques, & Outputs).
- **2008 — PMBOK 4th edition:** Project management constraints increased from 3 to 6; consisted of 42 processes and 517 ITTO.
- **2013 — PMBOK 5th edition:** Revised to include 47 processes and 619 ITTO.
- **2018 — PMBOK 6th edition:** Revised to include a few drastic changes including the introduction of agile methodologies.
- **2021 — PMBOK 7th edition:** A new edition of the PMBOK Guide.

(Key terms: **PMI**, **PMBOK**, **ITTO** = Inputs, Tools, Techniques & Outputs. Source: Prothoughts.)

## Slide 31 — Project Management Knowledge Areas (6th Edition) — mind map

Mind map of the 10 PMBOK 6th-edition Knowledge Areas (centre: **Project Management**) with their processes:

- **Integration:** Develop Project Charter; Develop Project Management Plan; Direct and Manage Project Execution; Monitor and Control Project Work; Perform Integrated Change Control; Close Project or Phase.
- **Scope:** Collect Requirements; Define Scope; Create WBS; Verify Scope; Control Scope.
- **Time (Schedule):** Define Activities; Sequence Activities; Estimate Activity Resources; Estimate Activity Durations; Develop Schedule; Control Schedule.
- **Cost:** Estimate Costs; Determine Budget; Control Costs.
- **Quality:** Plan Quality; Perform Quality Assurance; Perform Quality Control.
- **Human Resources:** Develop Human Resources Plan; Acquire Project Team; Develop Project Team; Manage Project Team.
- **Communications:** Identify Stakeholders; Plan Communications; Distribute Information; Manage Stakeholder Expectations; Report Performance.
- **Risk:** Plan Risk Management; Identify Risks; Perform Qualitative Risk Analysis; Perform Quantitative Risk Analysis; Plan Risk Responses; Monitor & Control Risks.
- **Procurement:** Plan Procurements; Conduct Procurements; Administer Procurements; Close Procurements.

(Note: the mind map credits a PMBOK 4th-edition-based "Knowledge Area Processes" map; Conceptualized & Developed © Babou Srinivasan; Process Info © 2008 Project Management Institute, Inc. — http://www.pmi.org. Key term: **WBS** = Work Breakdown Structure.)

## Slide 32 — PMBOK 6th vs 7th Edition (structural comparison)

**PMBOK Guide – Sixth Edition:**

- _A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge:_ Introduction, Project Environment, and Role of the Project Manager.
- **Knowledge Areas:** Integration; Scope; Schedule; Cost; Quality; Resources; Communications; Risk; Procurement; Stakeholders.
- _The Standard for Project Management_ (process groups): Initiating; Planning; Executing; Monitoring and Controlling; Closing.
- Appendixes, Glossary, and Index.

**PMBOK Guide – Seventh Edition:**

- _The Standard for Project Management:_ Introduction; System for Value Delivery.
- **Project Management Principles (12):** Stewardship; Team; Stakeholders; Value; Systems Thinking; Leadership; Tailoring; Quality; Complexity; Risk; Adaptability and Resiliency; Change.
- _A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge_ — **Project Performance Domains (8):** Stakeholders; Team; Development Approach and Life Cycle; Planning; Project Work; Delivery; Measurement; Uncertainty.
- Tailoring; Models, Methods, and Artifacts.
- Appendixes, Glossary, and Index.

**PMIstandards+™ Digital Content Platform:** Links to the PMBOK Guide via the Models, Methods, and Artifacts section while expanding on that content; incorporates content from all PMI standards plus platform-specific content; content reflects "how to…" in actual practice, including emerging practices.

(Teaching point: shift from a **process/knowledge-area** based standard (6th) to a **principles + performance-domains** based standard (7th).)

## Slide 33 — Different Project Management Approaches etc.

Two approaches contrasted:

**Lean Six Sigma:**

- Six Sigma is a methodology that adds value by improving process efficiency through use of specific tools.
- Allows the current process performance to be taken to its true potential level.
- "…Six Sigma consists of developing a culture that demands perfection and provides executives with the tools to measure performance and implement improvements…" — Jack Welch (1995).
- **6 Sigma Tools & Methods** include: Process Focus, Critical Variables, SPC Software, Statistical Tools, Quality Tools, DMAIC, Facts and Data, Variation.
- **DMAIC** phases: **Define** (define problem, team, scope, process) → **Measure** (determine current performance; identify possible root cause; obtain root causes data) → **Analyze** (validate cause & effect; identify opportunities) → **Improve** (implement improvement) → **Control** (control & continue improving).
- Source: Flevy.com – Six Sigma Basics | PPT.

**Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe):**

- Centre: **Scaled Agile Framework**. Seven surrounding elements (01–07): 01 Lean Portfolio Management; 02 Organizational Agility; 03 Continuous Learning Culture; 04 Lean-Agile Leadership; 05 Team and Technical Agility; 06 Agile Product Delivery; 07 Enterprise Solution Delivery.
- Source: SAFe® Certification Training Courses.

(Key frameworks/terms: **Lean Six Sigma**, **DMAIC**, **SPC**, **Scaled Agile Framework / SAFe**.)

## Summary of frameworks, tools, methodologies, models & key terms

**Project management standards / bodies:**

- **PMI** (Project Management Institute) and the **PMBOK Guide** (1st 1996 → 7th 2021); **ITTO** (Inputs, Tools, Techniques & Outputs); **PMIstandards+™** digital platform; **Agile Practice Guide** (PMI/Agile Alliance).
- PMI (2017) definition of projects: occur at all org levels; fulfil objectives via deliverables; drive change from current to future beneficial state; enable business value creation.

**PMBOK 6th edition — 10 Knowledge Areas:** Integration, Scope, Schedule (Time), Cost, Quality, Resources (Human Resources), Communications, Risk, Procurement, Stakeholders. **Process groups:** Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring & Controlling, Closing. (**WBS** = Work Breakdown Structure.)

**PMBOK 7th edition — 12 Principles:** Stewardship, Team, Stakeholders, Value, Systems Thinking, Leadership, Tailoring, Quality, Complexity, Risk, Adaptability & Resiliency, Change. **8 Performance Domains:** Stakeholders, Team, Development Approach & Life Cycle, Planning, Project Work, Delivery, Measurement, Uncertainty.

**Other methodologies/frameworks:**

- **Lean Six Sigma** with **DMAIC** (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control); SPC, statistical/quality tools.
- **Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe)** — 7 core competencies (Lean Portfolio Management, Organizational Agility, Continuous Learning Culture, Lean-Agile Leadership, Team & Technical Agility, Agile Product Delivery, Enterprise Solution Delivery).

**Concept models:**

- **VUCA** (Volatile, Uncertain, Complex, Ambiguous).
- **Systems thinking** / SDG "wedding cake" (Biosphere → Society → Economy → Partnerships), per Folke et al. (2016).
- **Psychological contract** (WORK vs PAY iceberg).
- **危機 wēijī** (crisis = risk + opportunity).
- "Tree swing" requirements-miscommunication cartoon.
- "Straight line vs tangled line" project-reality model.
- E&I major **"Can Do" infinity-loop journey** (incubation → acceleration → scale → impact; Self/Other/Groups/Systems).

**Project success factors:** clear goal/scope; senior management support; realistic schedule; end-user/stakeholder commitment; adequate funds/resources; effective leadership/conflict resolution; flexible approach to change; clear communication channels; using past experience/historic records; effective management of uncertainty (risk/opportunity).
