## Components of leadership (Northouse, 2016: 32)

**Leadership** is characterised as:

- It is a **process**
- Involves **influence**
- Occurs within a **group context**
- Attends to **common goals**

**Leaders (and the leader–follower relationship):**

- Leaders and followers are **involved together**
- Leaders and followers **need each other**
- Leaders often **initiate and maintain** the relationship
- Leaders are **not above or better than** followers

---

## Assigned vs Emergent leadership

**Assigned leadership** — leadership based on **occupying a position** within an organization.

**Emergent leadership** — an individual perceived by others as the **most influential member** of a group or organization, **regardless of the individual's title.**

- Emerges over time through **communication behaviours**
- Affected by **personality and gender**
- Tied to **identity** (fitting in with the group)

---

## Leaders vs managers — traditional view (Kotter, 1990: 85; reprint 2001)

Framing: leadership and management are **distinctive but complementary "systems of action."** Management "produces order and consistency"; leadership "produces change and movement."

**Management activities → Leadership activities (paired):**

- Planning & Budgeting → Establishing direction
- Organizing & Staffing → Aligning people
- Controlling & Problem Solving → Motivating / Inspiring

---

## Are leaders and managers different kinds of people? (Zaleznik, 1977)

Contrast table presented in the lecture:

| Managers                                      | Leaders                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Unidirectional authority                      | Multidirectional influence                                                           |
| Are reactive                                  | Are emotionally active & involved                                                    |
| Prefer to work with people on problem solving | Shape ideas (rather than just responding to them)                                    |
| Have low emotional involvement                | Act to expand available options / change the way people think about what is possible |

---

## Reconciliation — these are skills the same person can develop (Kotter, 1990, _What leaders really do_, HBR)

A single individual ("LEADER") can hold both skill sets:

- **Leadership skills:** Align people; Motivate people; Set direction and create change.
- **Management skills:** Organise and staff; Control and problem-solve; Plan and budget.

---

## Two types of skills leaders must master

Leaders in organizations must master two skill types, shown as a balance/seesaw:

- **(EQ) People skills**
- **(IQ) Intellectual skills**

The implied message: both must be balanced, not one at the expense of the other.

## The Strataplex Model (Mumford, Campion & Morgeson, 2007)

_Reference: Mumford, Campion & Morgeson (2007) "The leadership skills strataplex: Leadership skill requirements across organizational levels." The Leadership Quarterly, 18, 154–166._

Four general leadership skill categories with their sub-skills:

| General Skill     | Sub-skills                                                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cognitive**     | Speaking, active listening, writing, reading comprehension, active learning                                |
| **Interpersonal** | Social perceptiveness, coordination, persuasion                                                            |
| **Business**      | Manage resources (material, personnel, financial); operations analysis                                     |
| **Strategic**     | Visioning; systems perception; identification of problems, key causes and consequences; solution appraisal |

## The dark side of leadership — Hubris

(Lecture showed a 2:10-min video "Dark Side of Leadership" referencing Narcissistic Personality Disorder / sense of entitlement — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FRTgvq-Wfi4.)

### The path to hubristic leadership (5-step progression)

1. **Leaders influence followers' behaviour.**
2. **Power is the ability to influence others.**
3. **Leadership and power are closely related.**
4. **However, this dynamic can be perilous** — both for the leader themselves and for followers.
5. **Leaders can become intoxicated by power** (and become affected by hubris).

### What is hubris? (equation)

**Leadership + Power → Hubris.**

## Pride vs Arrogance (Tracy & Robins, 2007; Guy Winch)

Source cited: Psychologists **Jessica Tracy and Richard Robins**, June 2007 issue of _Current Directions in Psychological Science_. Their evidence supports the idea that pride has **two different faces**: **"authentic" pride** and **"hubristic" pride**.

**Authentic pride** arises when (per Guy Winch, Ph.D., licensed psychologist, author of _Emotional First Aid: Healing Rejection, Guilt, Failure, and Other Everyday Hurts_):

> "we feel good about ourselves, confident, and productive; and related to socially-desirable personality traits such as being agreeable, conscientious, and emotionally stable."

**Dr. Winch's distinction:**

- **Arrogance** = bragging about oneself, then thinking no one can do better, that they are the best, and that they could never fail at anything.
- **Pride** = doing something and feeling pleased with oneself, or feeling proud of someone else's accomplishments.

**Hubris and hubristic pride:**

- **Hubris** is extreme pride and arrogance; it occurs when those in power **lose their connection to reality and vastly overestimate their capabilities.**
- **Hubristic pride** tends to involve egotism and arrogance, and is related to **socially undesirable traits**: being disagreeable, aggressive, having **low or brittle self-esteem**, and being **prone to shame.**
