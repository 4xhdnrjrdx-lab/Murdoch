## SCOPE

### Defining a Project Scope

- The work and resources that go into the creation of the product or service are essentially the things that **frame the scope of the project**.
- The scope of the project **outlines the objectives** of the project and the **goals** that need to be met to achieve a satisfactory result.

### Effective Project Scope Planning (video, 2 mins)

Key points from the slide:

- Defining a project scope is essential for completing a project effectively and on time.
- Project scope involves determining all the things involved in a project — from goals, to deliverables, to costs and deadlines — to inform stakeholders and keep the team on task.
- A **scope statement** is the document listing these boundaries; it articulates project details, provides a project roadmap, helps focus team members, and prevents projects from expanding (scope creep).
- The better a project is "scoped" at the beginning, the better the team can manage change and complete the project effectively.

Link: https://youtu.be/nXOCnDQ05kA?si=vaS1dy5G2GhxoS8X

### Project Scope — Management

PMI (2013; 2017) identified **5 major processes** within scope management:

1. Collect Requirements
2. Define Scope
3. Create WBS
4. Validate Scope
5. Control Scope

_(Note: the slide lists 5 — it omits "Plan Scope Management", which is the 6th process in PMBOK 6th Ed. Ch.5. See the separate PMBOK Ch.5 dump for all six.)_

### Define Scope (key elements)

- **Project Deliverables** — the primary purpose is to define the deliverables as clearly as possible; project characteristics are set out, and a full and satisfactory deliverable represents the project's completion.
- **Acceptance Criteria** — measurable criteria for objectives, which may include cost, time and quality (performance).
- **Project Exclusions** — items that are out of scope.
- **Project Constraints** — factors that affect the execution of the project or process.
- **Project Assumptions** — factors that at the time of planning are considered true, real or certain, without proof or demonstration.

## Why is Project Management important?

- In 2021, according to PMI, **9.4% of every dollar invested was wasted** due to poor project performance — i.e., $94 million for every $1 billion invested.
- This underscores the need for project management: PM reduces project costs by improving efficiency, mitigating risks, and optimising resources.

### According to PMI — "Why Effective Project Management is Important?" (infographic, source: PMI via nimble)

Five statistics shown as donut charts:

- **9.9%** of every dollar is wasted due to poor project performance.
- **32%** of the budget is lost when a project fails to reach its goals.
- **39%** of projects fail due to change in organisation's priorities.
- **52%** of completed projects experienced high scope creep.
- **68%** of organisations outsource their projects to third parties.

### Project Objectives (incl. SMART)

- What is the **project vision** (overall aim / purpose)?
- What are **3–5 key goals & objectives**?
- What are **3–5 key deliverables**?

**SMART Project Objective** — worked example breaking a single objective into SMART parts:

- _Specific / Achievable:_ "Finalize the migration of old CRM to new CRM"
- _Measurable:_ "completely, so we can stop using the old CRM"
- _Time-bound / Realistic:_ "by the end of Q4 with a group of five devs and one analyst."
  (SMART = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Realistic, Time-bound.)

### Scope

- What are you going to deliver?
- What are your milestones? (i.e., the key "parts" of the project)

### Assumptions vs Constraints

- What are some key **assumptions**?
- What are some **constraints** — i.e., scope, time, cost, quality, resources, risk, etc.?

**Project Assumptions Surfacing Matrix (template) — described:** a 2×2 grid plotting assumptions by **Known ↔ Unknown** (horizontal axis) against **High Probability ↔ Low Probability** (vertical axis). Instructions: as assumptions are gathered, place one note per assumption in the matrix according to likelihood and criticality. Example notes shown: "Installations must be moved after geological tests" (known/high); "Spring run-off floods creek and washes out road" (unknown/high); "Supply chain problems delay replacement parts for broken-down equipment" and "There's a shortage of repair people to finish repairs on time" (unknown, lower).

### Project Plan (timeline)

- What is going to be delivered by when?
- Timeline & responsibilities.
- _Note:_ you may use Excel, PowerPoint, MS Project, or other software to prepare the timeline.

**WBS / Project Plan table template — columns:** WBS ID | WBS Task | Immediate Predecessor | Duration (Days, Weeks, Months) | Start Date | Finish Date | Responsible Officers.

### Risks / Issues & Mitigation

Questions: What are your risks? Are there any issues? How will you address (mitigate) risks & add value?

**5-step risk process (diagram):**

1. **Risk Planning** — DEFINE: Scope; Team; Strategy
2. **Risk Identification** — IDENTIFY: Risk Event; Impact; Probability
3. **Risk Analysis** — ANALYZE: Quantitative; Qualitative; Combination. _Quantitative:_ Monte Carlo, others. _Qualitative:_ Risk Register, Risk Matrix.
4. **Risk Response Planning** — ACTION PLAN: Mitigation Plan; Action Officer; Resolve By. _Mitigation options:_ Transfer, Mitigate, Accept, Avoid, Contingency.
5. **Risk Monitoring & Control** — RE-ASSESS: Weekly; Monthly; Quarterly.
   (The process loops back from step 5 to step 1.)

### Risk Register / Issues Log & Mitigation Strategies

**Risk & Issue log columns:** # | Risk | Detail | Likelihood | Impact | Severity | Owner | Mitigation.

**Likelihood × Consequence severity matrix — described:**

- Consequence levels: **Insignificant** (risk easily mitigated by normal day-to-day process) → **Minor** (delays up to 10% of schedule / additional cost up to 10% of budget) → **Moderate** (delays up to 30% / cost up to 30%) → **Major** (delays up to 50% / additional cost up to 50% of budget) → **Catastrophic** (project abandoned).
- Likelihood levels: **Certain** (>90%), **Likely** (50–90%), **Moderate** (10–50%), **Unlikely** (3–10%), **Rare** (<3%).
- Cells grade from **Low → Moderate → High → Extreme** as likelihood and/or consequence increase (e.g., Certain + Moderate consequence = Extreme; Rare + Insignificant = Low).

**Risk Register worked example (Common project risks):** columns ID | Date raised | Risk description | Likelihood | Impact | Severity | Owner | Mitigating action. Examples:

- _Project purpose and need is not well-defined_ — Likelihood Medium, Impact High, Severity High, Owner Project Sponsor → Mitigation: complete a business case if not already provided; ensure purpose is well defined on Project Charter and PID.
- _Project design and deliverable definition is incomplete_ — Low/High/High, Owner Project Sponsor → Define the scope in detail via design workshops with input from SMEs.
- _Project schedule is not clearly defined or understood_ — Low/Medium/Medium, Owner Project Manager → Hold scheduling workshops with the project team so they understand the plan and reduce likelihood of missed tasks.

### Stakeholder Engagement

Add notes on how you will engage stakeholders, i.e.:

- **Stakeholder matrix** (e.g., interest vs influence)
- **Communication strategy**

**Stakeholder Mapping (interest × influence) matrix — described:** stakeholder mapping helps determine who stakeholders are and how much engagement/communication/consideration they need. Quadrants:

- **High Interest + Low Influence:** Inform completely; Monitor closely.
- **High Interest + High Influence:** Regularly engage; Keep satisfied.
- **Low Interest + Low Influence:** Essential information only; Minimal contact.
- **Low Interest + High Influence:** Monitor regularly; Anticipate needs.
  (Source credit on slide: SME Strategy.)
