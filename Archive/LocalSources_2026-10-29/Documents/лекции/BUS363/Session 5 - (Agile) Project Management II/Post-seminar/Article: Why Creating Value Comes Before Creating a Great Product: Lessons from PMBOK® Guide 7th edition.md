## The framing example

- Imagine you're made team leader to deliver an outcome — say, **build a new computer**. The challenge: align with your customer's vision, needs and expectations to deliver successfully, creating value for the customer, the organisation, and yourself. It's not easy.
- Whatever delivery approach you use, **projects are the key driver of value** in any organisation. The computer example carries enormous built-in **uncertainty**: you don't know all the constraints, and you can't build it as a "closed box" isolated from the larger ecosystem.

## Change is a given — the question is how we respond

- There's no single fixed approach that delivers every project; predictive, adaptive, etc. — none is a predefined solution for all challenges.
- This is the central theme of the **PMBOK® Guide 7th edition** (PMI). Project management is a **strategic discipline**; in a fast-shifting world, project leaders must navigate **complexity and ambiguity** while actively managing **risk**.
- **Delivering value must sit at the heart of every choice and process** — the core of the new edition.

## "Building the right product" vs "building the product right"

- From Vargas's experience at the **Brightline Initiative** (bridging how CXOs think about strategy vs its execution): you must think about long, hard-won **benefits / value**, not just "how many bricks or trucks of cement to procure."
- It's about **people** putting a strategic direction in place in global organisations — the challenges, the gaps, why so much **waste** appears in running businesses — and all these requirements change every minute.
- The goal is to make something that **delivers value to society and context** — going **beyond the time/cost/quality triangle**. Time, cost and quality are still relevant — they're just **not enough**.

## What changed: 6th → 7th edition

**Principles replace processes:**

- Earlier PMBOK editions refined the same **process-based** approach. The 7th introduces **principles** that apply regardless of the process/method used.
- It doesn't eliminate the 6th edition — you can still use all **49 processes** as your delivery method; they're fully compatible. The 7th is an **umbrella** over Scrum, Kanban, Disciplined Agile, the Spotify model, agile, or predictive/waterfall — principles overarch your decisions.
- Some key principles (paraphrased):
  1. **Stewardship** — caring for and taking care of others; doing the right thing. In the computer example: ensuring the new computer respects users' **privacy and security**.
  2. **Collaboration** — building a culture of accountability where team members respect each other, the process, and the outcomes. Making a computer is complex and needs humans collaborating with each other and with machines.
  3. **Stakeholders** — countering the idea that PMs sit "in the basement" while executives "on the top floor" toss work down and clients just want the product. It's your job to keep stakeholders **engaged and proactively supporting** the outcomes.

**Performance Domains replace Knowledge Areas:**

- Principles are put into practice through **performance domains** — things you act on to fulfil the principles, all executed **simultaneously**.
- The **eight performance domains:** Stakeholder, Team, Development Approach and Life Cycle, Planning, Project Work, Delivery, Measurement, Uncertainty.

## Tailoring, adaptability, and navigating complexity

- Key takeaway: understand the **connections across the whole ecosystem** inside and outside your organisation — a piece outside can directly affect your project. You're not operating in an isolated system.
- The primary "muscle" to build is **adaptability and resilience**: if you can adapt, it won't matter how the process looks in future — you'll be able to adapt yourself and your outcomes.
- Closing line: **be driven by principles, add value to achieve the right outcomes, and success will follow.**
