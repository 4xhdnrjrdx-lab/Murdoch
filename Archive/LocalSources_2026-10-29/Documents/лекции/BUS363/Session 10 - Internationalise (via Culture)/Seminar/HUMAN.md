## Part 1 — The filmmaker's motivation and perspective

- **Yann Arthus-Bertrand** positions himself as just one person among (then) ~7 billion.
- He has spent nearly **40 years** photographing the planet to understand the Earth and its people.
- His observation: despite humanity's progress, the world is still **"two-tier,"** undermined by inequality and ravaged by war.
- He voices frustration that humanity still can't live together harmoniously, and asks a child-like question: **why does humanity keep repeating the same mistakes** generation after generation?

## Part 2 — Shifting focus from scale to the human soul

- He deliberately chose **not** to look for answers in numbers or statistics, but in **humanity itself**.
- Having photographed the world on a **grand scale** before, he now finds a more powerful way to reflect the human soul through individual **eyes, faces, and words**.
- Guiding belief: the proverb **"the eyes are the mirror of the soul."**
- He describes someone looking directly into the camera and "bearing their soul" as the most compelling form of connection; every encounter is a step forward, every life story unique.

## Part 3 — Core philosophical questions driving the project

- Do all humans share the same desire for **love, freedom, and recognition**?
- In a world torn between **tradition and modernity**, are our fundamental needs the same everywhere?
- What does it mean to be **human** in the modern world? What is the **meaning of life**?
- Are human differences actually huge, or do we **share more values than we realise**?
- If shared values exist, **why is mutual understanding still so hard**?

## Part 4 — Production and scope

- He calls it a **"crazy challenge"** and a new kind of topic for his work.
- **Two years** of travel through **65 countries** (deck says 60 — verify).
- Filmed both the **natural world** and **places made by humans**.
- Interviewed **more than 2,000 people**, deliberately seeking out those **"we never talk about"** and people telling their stories for the first time.

## Part 5 — Artistic choices and thematic pillars

- Visual style: **detaching subjects from their environment** to focus entirely on faces and words.
- Designed as a **journey through vast landscapes**, where the world's beauty resonates through human voices.
- A major choice: **no commentary** — the voices are **pure and direct**.
- The film makes deliberate **critical/political choices**, placing key issues at its heart: **poverty, war, immigration, homophobia**.
- Alongside those heavy themes, participants also speak about universal experiences: the difficulties of growing up, **love, and happiness**.
- The **wealth and diversity** of these individual voices is the "heart of HUMAN."

## Key terms & references

- **Yann Arthus-Bertrand** — photographer and director (also known for _Home_, 2009).
- **"The eyes are the mirror of the soul"** — guiding cinematographic principle.
- **Two-tier world** — his description of global inequality.
- **Scope:** ~2 years, **65 countries** (deck: 60 — verify), **2,000+ interviews**.
- **Style:** subjects detached from environment; **no commentary** ("pure and direct" voices).
- **Themes:** love, freedom, recognition; tradition vs. modernity; poverty, war, immigration, homophobia; growing up, happiness.
