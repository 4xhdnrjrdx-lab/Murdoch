## Part 1 — The challenge of global leadership

- Globalisation constantly puts professionals into cross-cultural situations — e.g. an American leading a team in Nigeria, or a French person trying to sell in Japan.
- The **logistics** (doing business by phone, flying to meet people) are the **easy part**; the real difficulty is **style adaptation**.
- Effective leaders must work out how to **adapt their personal style** to the specific culture they're dealing with to get the results they need.
- Research shows cultures differ in how they:
  - **build trust**,
  - **make decisions**, and
  - define what counts as a **"good communicator."**

## Part 2 — The "Authentic Flexibility" framework

- The organisations that succeed internationally are those that carefully **analyse cultural differences** and stay **flexible** across them.
- They train leaders in what Meyer calls **Authentic Flexibility**, explained with a two-feet analogy:
  - **Left foot** = your one **natural, authentic** style.
  - **Right foot** = the effort to **learn and adapt** your style to the specific culture of your counterpart to get results.

## Part 3 — Strategy for international success

- **Global leadership** = the ability to use **both feet** — staying aware of your natural style while simultaneously adapting to the culture in front of you.
- An organisation is positioned for international success when its senior leaders have both: **self-awareness** of their own style, and the **specific knowledge** of what's required to adapt to the country they're working with.

## Key terms & references

- **Authentic Flexibility** — Erin Meyer's framework for leadership adaptation (be aware of your own style _and_ flex to the other culture).
- **Left foot** — your natural, authentic style.
- **Right foot** — the learned ability to adapt to a different culture.
- **Global leadership** — balancing authenticity with cultural adaptation.
- **Culture-varying processes named:** trust-building, decision-making, communication styles.
- **Erin Meyer** — author of _The Culture Map_; the deck's central cross-cultural reference.
