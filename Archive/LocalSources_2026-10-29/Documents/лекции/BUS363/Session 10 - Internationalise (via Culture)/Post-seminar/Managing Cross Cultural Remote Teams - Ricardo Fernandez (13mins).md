## Part 1 — The morning routine and the "digital commute"

- Opens with a demo of a typical glitchy remote call (static/connectivity), involving a participant named "Mark."
- His Madrid routine: wake **7:00–8:30am**, time with his kids, then a **<5-second "commute"** to his study.
- Opening his laptop, he's immediately met by **20–30 people** via video or names on screen.
- His role: managing a team of **30 people**, **10+ nationalities**, across **five offices and five time zones**, spending **2–6 hours/day** in remote interaction.

## Part 2 — His "nomad" background

- The core question of remote management: is the **"remote"** part (Skype, video, email) harder, or the **cultural interpretation** of everyday workplace events?
- His "nomad" life: born in **Spain** → moved to **Africa** before he was a month old → **Puerto Rico** (5 yrs) → **Netherlands** (5 yrs) → **USA** (LA, SF, NY; 10 yrs) → back to **Madrid**.
- He's not a formal academic on culture — he draws on hundreds of real-world experiences and **mistakes** managing multicultural teams.

## Part 3 — The millennial demand for flexibility

- Remote work is inevitable because the new generation of workers/managers **demands** it.
- Per a graph on millennial workplace preferences: millennials rank **training + flexible hours/remote work above everything** — above cash bonuses, private healthcare, extra vacation, and maternity/paternity benefits.
- For management it's a **"killer addition":** a huge employee benefit at **lower cost** to the company, fostering loyalty.
- Paradox: remote workers often work **far more hours** (often late at night) because they value the flexibility during the middle of the day.

## Part 4 — Case study: time interpretation in South Africa

- First week with a South African team: after a good call, a colleague said **"I'll call you just now."**
- Fernandez waited **15 minutes** before messaging to ask if they were still talking.
- The colleague sent an image explaining the SA meaning of **"just now"** — _not_ "immediately/in seconds," but possibly: later, hopefully today, tomorrow, or "maybe someday."
- Lesson: without physical proximity (no tapping someone on the shoulder), small **linguistic misunderstandings** become much harder to resolve.

## Part 5 — Case study: context and enthusiasm in India

- During a project launch in India he told a colleague **"You're killing it out there"** — meaning high praise.
- Next morning he got an email from that person asking **what they'd done wrong** and how to improve.
- Lesson: even in a shared language (English), a lack of **context** can flip the same sentence to the opposite meaning.

## Part 6 — Framework: The Culture Map

- After a stretch of frustration and poor results between his **US** and **London** teams, he bought **30 copies of Erin Meyer's _The Culture Map_** for the whole team.
- Bringing differences into the open revealed:
  - **Hierarchy:** Latin American members communicated very hierarchically.
  - **Interruption:** US and Dutch members found interrupting on calls totally normal — which was "driving other people absolutely nuts."
- The **self-awareness** from the framework became a key driver of the team's eventual success.

## Part 7 — Loneliness and the need for physical presence

- Despite the upside of flexibility, remote work can bring **deep loneliness**.
- His own "horrible" experiences: lonely in a home study or travelling; taking calls in hotel rooms, trains, planes, even public bathrooms; calls at **3, 4, 6am**.
- Suggested remedy: **co-working spaces** — for belonging, inspiration, and networking outside the home.
- His conclusion: technology enables "amazing relationships," but **physical presence is still needed to build true empathy**. Recommendation: remote teams should **meet in person at least a couple of times a year**.

## Part 8 — Final vision

- Closes with a vision of waking up with your family and taking your first call from a beautiful natural location — being physically away from co-workers isn't always a negative.

## Key terms & references

- **_The Culture Map_** — Erin Meyer (the framework he used to fix his team).
- **Millennials** — cited as the primary drivers of the remote-work trend.
- **"Just now"** — South African time expression (not "immediately").
- **"Killing it"** — US idiom for performing exceptionally well (misread in India).
- **Empathy** — the missing ingredient in purely digital relationships.
- **Co-working spaces** — remedy for remote-work loneliness.
- **Tools/places:** Skype; Madrid, South Africa, India, USA, Netherlands, London.
