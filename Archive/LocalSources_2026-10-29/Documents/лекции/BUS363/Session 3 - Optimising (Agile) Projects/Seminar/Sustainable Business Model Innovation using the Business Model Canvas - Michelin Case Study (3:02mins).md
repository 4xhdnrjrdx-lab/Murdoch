## Lecture framing (Session 3 slide)

An innovative sustainable business model used by Michelin tyres to deliver sustainability benefits and improve their business. Michelin faced strong competition from low-cost competitors among haulage companies. They believed they had superior technology but couldn't capture its value by competing on price. So Michelin decided to **give away their tyres for free** — a redesigned model giving customers tyres free but charging **per kilometre**, with **computer chips** installed in the tyres to measure performance such as distance and maintenance requirements.

## Part 1 — Competitive Challenges and the Traditional Model

- Michelin faced intense competition from **low-cost haulage-tyre competitors**. Despite superior technology, they struggled to capture its value while forced to compete mainly on price. Rising **climate-change regulations** were also starting to affect Michelin and its customers.
- The traditional model: **value proposition** = quality tyres; **revenue** = direct sale of tyres to haulage customers; **end-of-life** = the customer was responsible for disposal.

## Part 2 — The Redesigned Model: "Pay-per-Kilometre"

- Michelin redesigned its model by effectively giving tyres away free and shifting to a **service-based** approach, charging **per kilometre** rather than for the physical product.
- Enabling technology/services:
  - **Computer chips** in tyres to measure distance travelled and maintenance needs.
  - **Professional services** bundled in to help customers reduce fuel consumption and optimise vehicle performance.

## Part 3 — Economic Incentives and Value Extraction

- Charging per kilometre let Michelin extract maximum value from its superior technology. Because their tyres last longer than low-cost alternatives, they generate more revenue per tyre produced.
- The incentive shift:
  - **Reduced material usage:** incentivised to use fewer tyres, since maximising km-per-tyre raises margins.
  - **Lower supply-chain risk:** less reliance on raw materials for constant new production.
  - **Revenue maximisation** tied to tyre **longevity** rather than sales volume.

## Part 4 — Environmental Impact and Product Stewardship

- Substantial **carbon-emission** reductions for both Michelin (manufacturing fewer tyres) and customers (improved fuel efficiency).
- **Product stewardship:** Michelin now takes responsibility for recovering tyres at end-of-life and processes them for **recycling and re-entry** into manufacturing — a circular flow.

## Part 5 — Social and Strategic Benefits

- A new customer relationship built on **direct engagement** and deeper integration into the customer's operations.
- Community benefits: less landfill waste, reduced air pollution, alignment with the **UN Sustainable Development Goals**.
- Conclusion: taking a whole-business-model perspective creates superior value for the business, the customer, and the planet simultaneously.

## Key terms & references

- **Business Model Canvas:** framework used to analyse/innovate Michelin's approach.
- **Pay-per-kilometre:** service-based revenue model — pay for usage, not ownership (a "product-as-a-service" / servitisation shift).
- **Product stewardship:** taking responsibility for a product's environmental impact across its lifecycle, including disposal/recycling.
- **Computer chips (IoT):** performance monitoring (distance, maintenance) enabling the usage-based model.
- **Circular manufacturing:** recovering used tyres to re-introduce them into production.
- **UN SDGs:** supported via waste reduction and responsible production.
