## Ice Breaker — "Draw a FISH" exercise

An in-class activity. Students first draw a fish freely (part 1), then "draw a fish" again following spoken step-by-step instructions read out by the lecturer onto provided grid-paper (part 2). The "result" slide shows a fish drawn by following a numbered 1–10 sequence of strokes/coordinates on the grid, so everyone's output converges toward the same standardised fish.

### Standard FISH Game — debrief / purpose

- As discovered through the exercise, the game highlights the need for good **Standard Work, Teamwork and Communications**.
- It's a quick and simple way to introduce concepts like:
  - LEAN
  - Standard Work
  - 'Continuous Improvement' efforts; and how
  - without some basic 'standards of work', it limits your ability to start any process-improvement efforts.
- Once you have the basic 'standards of work', you can start:
  - implementing improvement efforts
  - setting standards; and
  - 'continuous improvement' cycles.

### INTERFACE — Net-Works

Net-Works is a social, environmental and commercial solution tackling the problem of discarded fishing nets, by **Interface** and **ZSL** (Zoological Society of London).

- The programme evolved from a need to find a better way of doing business. By working in close partnership, Interface and ZSL are creating a solid business solution with long-term positive impacts on marine and freshwater ecosystems, while also providing financial opportunities to some of the poorest people in the world.
- Video: https://youtu.be/DX6Uidpg3VM

### Repurposing waste from one industry to create new products in different industries — Interface timeline

Interface's evolution shown as three stages:

- **1994:** Smokestacks; "Take – Make – Waste"; petroleum-intensive products; disconnected supply chain.
- **NOW–2020:** Factories to Zero; recycled, closed-loop materials; low-carbon products (reducing CO₂); sustainable supply chain.
- **BEYOND 2020:** Factories as Forests; products from dispersed materials; products that sequester carbon; supply chain that benefits all life.

### INTERFACE: Integrated Sustainable Business Model (Net-Works® detail)

Net-Works® is an innovative, cross-sector initiative designed to tackle the growing environmental problem of discarded fishing nets in some of the world's poorest coastal communities.

- Interface partnered with the Zoological Society of London to buy discarded fishing nets from some of the poorest communities in the world.
- The fishermen from these communities recover the "ghost nets" that are polluting the oceans and indiscriminately killing sea life, therefore helping conserve and sustainably use the ocean.
- Interface purchases these nets, providing income to the community; the Zoological Society of London helps the community establish banking services and provides education on sustainable fishery management and alternative income sources like seaweed farming.
- The nets are recycled into new yarn for Interface's carpet tiles by their yarn manufacturer.
- This directly supports Interface's **Mission Zero** goal to source 100% recycled material for its carpet tile.
- It reduces supply-chain risks for raw material, as they now need less virgin nylon (a product of crude oil).
- Source: https://bluetribe.co/sustainable-business-model-innovation-interface-case-study/

### Repurposing waste — ScanCom / DuraOcean (new products in new industries)

Process ("How does it WORK?"):

1. Used ropes from the marine industry are collected and prevented from entering the oceans.
2. A partner transforms the marine plastic waste into a valuable recycled raw material.
3. The result is a new recycled plastic granulate ready to be used.
4. ScanCom receives the raw materials and starts the production process in the plastic factory.
5. In-house steps: MIXING (polymer resin + additives mixed together) → INJECTION (polymer resin + additives injected into the mould) → FINAL Q/A (final product is cooled, inspected, tested and packed).

- End product example: the DuraOcean chair (made from recycled ocean rope/net waste).
- Source: https://www.scancom.net/products/duraocean/

### Other new products

Examples of products made from recycled ocean/marine plastic waste shown: eyewear/glasses frames, drink bottles & cans, computer mouse, watches, belts, toothbrushes/razors (POPi — "Preserve Ocean Plastic Initiative"), yarn/twine, and clips.

### INTERFACE: Recycled PVB — from windscreens to carpet

- PVB: a new purpose for an old material.
- The EU is pushing for recycling of windscreens, but the laminate material (**PVB** — polyvinyl butyral) that prevents the windows from shattering had not been given a second life, until recently.
- Interface partnered with the supply chain to process the laminate and use it in the production of carpet tiles.

## 1. LEAN

- Involves creating constantly improving sets of activities that lead to the services and products that customers value.
- Improve the practices that add value and avoid those that don't.

### The 5 LEAN Principles (cycle)

**Value**

- A specific product or service meets the needs of the customer.
- Focusing on customer value helps provide a framework for what the company and employees do.

**Value Stream**

- All the actions that contribute to a finished product or service — from solving problems to engineering, design, manufacturing, and process development — make up the value stream.
- You keep refining and improving the value stream until you reach perfection.

**Flow**

- Once you get the value stream set up and it begins to operate in a harmonious manner, you get an operational movement called flow. It's like the business equivalent of a musician hitting a groove.

**Pull**

- Instead of making the decision for customers concerning what they want, you wait until they tell you.
- It's a pull process, rather than push. You can learn what they want in a number of ways, including formal requests, informal discussions, or even market research techniques.

**Perfection**

- When you keep improving the value stream, get flow, and respond to customer needs, you reach a state in which everything you do is valued by the customers. In other words, it doesn't get much better than this.

(The principles form a continuous loop: Value → Value Stream → Flow → Pull → Perfection → back to Value.)

### LEAN 'business model' problems

A classic lean business model can create problems, including the following:

- Too much focus on physical flows and not enough on quality, risk, or corporate responsibility.
- Too much input from customers can lead to missing out on strategic positioning for the company and anticipating where the market will eventually go.
- Lean business models don't give much attention to proper training, communications, and HR policies, along with other factors that are people-centric.
- Too heavy a focus on alleviating wasteful practices while ignoring other important factors.
- Too much internal focus and not enough on relationships with partners.

## 2. Quality

- Reduce waste and rework, saving money in the process, and improve results, making the company more effective.

**Quality nesting model (diagram):** three nested circles — **Quality Control** (innermost, smallest) sits inside **Quality Assurance**, which sits inside the overarching **Quality System** (outermost, largest).

### Quality Improvement Approaches

- There have been multiple versions of quality management in business theory over the years, such as **statistical process control** as taught by **W. Edwards Deming**; the **total quality management (TQM)** movement popular in the 1980s; or practices like **Six Sigma**.
- Originally intended for manufacturing, these practices have expanded into many operational aspects of companies.
- The idea is to reduce waste and rework, saving money in the process, and to improve results, making the company more effective.

### Garvin's 8 Dimensions of Quality

1. **Performance** — A performance characteristic describes a product's essential function.
2. **Features** — Features are a secondary aspect of performance.
3. **Reliability** — Reliability is the ability of a product or service to perform as expected over time.
4. **Conformance** — Conformance is the degree to which a product conforms to its specification.
5. **Durability** — Durability is the measurement of product life.
6. **Serviceability** — Serviceability is the ease at which a user can repair a faulty product or get it fixed.
7. **Aesthetics** — Aesthetics refers to the appearance of a product or service.
8. **Perceived Quality** — Perceived quality is the overall opinion of the customers towards the product.

## 3. Forecasting

- Forecasting is a technique for making predictions of the direction of future trends based on analysis of past and present data.
- Businesses use forecasting to determine how to allocate their budgets or plan for anticipated expenses for an upcoming period.
- Video: _What is Forecasting? Methods | Process | Benefits_ (5:03 mins) — https://youtu.be/M8Kiwv9gDJU

### Forecasting Methods

- Poor forecasting leads to being unprepared to meet market demand and/or wasting money/resources.
- More detail: https://clockify.me/forecasting-models

**Forecasting methods taxonomy (diagram):**

- **Qualitative forecasting methods:**
  - Delphi method
  - Market research
  - Panel consensus
  - Visionary forecast
  - Sales force composite
- **Quantitative forecasting methods:**
  - **Time-series models:**
    - Straight-line method
    - Moving average
    - Exponential smoothing
    - Trend projection
  - **Associative models:**
    - Simple linear regression
    - Multiple linear regression

### Forecasting using the 'Delphi Method'

- The Delphi method is a structured way to pool the opinions of many experts to reach a group solution. It was developed in 1969 by the **Rand Corporation** to facilitate technological forecasting. It has the benefit of overcoming the bias that results from some voices being more dominant than others — by virtue either of personality or eminence.
- The primary uses of the Delphi method are in forecasting and decision making. The Delphi method tends to produce robust predictions based on experience, by eliminating the wilder contributions.
- However, when the past is a poor guide to the future and there is a possibility of massive and discontinuous change, the Delphi method is not suitable.
- This makes it inappropriate for leading-edge projects.
- Video (13:16 mins) — https://youtu.be/48LlvwFGhC8

## 4. Consumer-Centric Approach

- Customer comes first.
- Focus operations and strategy to embrace customers and make them happy.
- A customer company aligns its processes to offer a seamless, consistent and positive experience.

**Customer Centricity model (Venn diagram, 5 overlapping elements):**

- Customer-focused leadership
- Feedback drives continuous improvement
- Empower the front line
- Designing the experience
- Understanding your customers
- Metrics that matter
- (All converging on the centre: **Customer Centricity**.)

### 7 Lessons for Customer-Centric Leadership — in Project Management

- "The customer is king; but unfortunately, they aren't always treated as such."
- Explains how customer-centric leadership can help your business, with real-life case studies from successful companies such as **Apple, Amazon and Virgin Airlines**.
- Video (6:56 mins) — https://youtu.be/gOknGZ0nn7s

### Product vs Consumer-Centric Approach

- Management teams are often fond of saying how their companies are customer-centric. Think about your own experience as a consumer and then consider how many companies actually do put the customer first.
- A customer-centric approach to business is actually incredibly efficient. Ultimately, it is customers and their perceptions of and attitudes toward a business that decide its fate.
- Focus operations and strategy to embrace customers and make them happy, and you're on the fast path toward business success.

**Product Centric vs Customer Centric companies (comparison table):**

| Metric                   | Product Centric                                   | Customer Centric                                         |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Strategy                 | Create the best product                           | Create the best solution for the customer                |
| People/Culture           | Rewards new product development                   | Customer Culture — searching for new needs to fill       |
| Processes                | New product Development                           | New Solution Development, Customer Experience Management |
| Rewards/Metrics          | New Culture — looking for new products to develop | Rewards people with deep insight into customers          |
| Organizational Structure | Product Divisions with P&L                        | Customer Segments with P&L                               |

### What is Customer Centricity?

- An organization is only truly customer-centric when the viewpoint of the customer plays a pivotal role in its decisions, processes and expected behaviours.
- Customer centricity is a personal choice and a company requirement, as organizations understand that every action influences customer experience positively or negatively.
- Video (2:19 mins) — https://youtu.be/3_EXavzcV50

## 5. Process Re-engineering

- Reworking your operational processes to become more efficient.
- "Less talking, more action."
- Remove wasteful processes, develop more effective ones.

**Business Process Re-engineering cycle (8-step diagram):**

1. Develop Vision and Objectives
2. Understanding Existing Process
3. Identify Process for Re-Design
4. Identify Change Levers
5. Implement the New Process
6. Make New Process Operational
7. Evaluate the Process
8. Ongoing Continuous Improvement

### Process re-engineering — detail

- At one time, business-process re-engineering was one of those periodic management fads. Companies were going to rework their operational processes to become more efficient.
- As many such fads go, it was often too much talk, too little action. And yet, the idea hasn't lost its validity. Business processes tend to develop over time. As conditions change, companies keep adapting and adding to them.
- By the end, you have a clumsy process in place that was designed by committee. But by truly re-engineering, companies can pinpoint the wasteful processes concerning how they do business and develop more effective procedures.
- While re-engineering your business processes, remember to get frontline employees involved. They're the ones who actually know how things happen, and they might even have input into how you could make them better.

### Sustainable Business Model Innovation using the Business Model Canvas — Michelin Case Study

- An innovative sustainable business model used by Michelin tyres to deliver sustainability benefits and improve their business.
- Michelin tyres was facing strong competition in their business with haulage companies from low-cost competitors.
- Michelin believed they had a superior technology but were not able to capture the value from it simply by competing on price.
- In order to compete, Michelin decided to **give away their tyres for free**.
- Michelin created a new product with a redesigned business model that involved giving the customer the tyres for free but charging their customers **per kilometre**.
- They installed computer chips in the tyres to measure performance such as distance and maintenance requirements.
- Video — https://youtu.be/22-6cfoAy_c
