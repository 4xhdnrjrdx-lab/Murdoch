## Context / setup

- Many businesses are going through **digital transformation** and adopting **Agile** (once mainly a software-development approach; McKinsey extended it toward "agile businesses").
- This leads to **design thinking**, which is **user-centric** — and that idea expands to **customer-centric**.
- Jennifer Bridges examines well-known, respected companies as case studies in customer experience and customer service, then extracts leadership lessons.

## Case studies (customer experience & service)

- **Apple — Steve Jobs:** start with the **customer experience**, not the technology. Jobs's approach: ask "what incredible benefits can we offer our customers?" first, then work _back_ to how the technology delivers that.
- **Amazon — Jeff Bezos:** work on what customers like and want. Amazon knows its customers want **low prices, big selection, and fast delivery**, so everything the company does focuses on those.
- **Virgin Airlines — Richard Branson:** the key to success is **superior customer service**. Branson **empowers employees** so they go above and beyond for customers — achieved by building a customer-centric environment.

## Customer-centric leadership

- A customer-centric environment **starts with the leader**: the leader generates the energy/focus that creates a good customer experience.

## The 7 lessons (tips)

1. **Be visible** — don't stay locked behind your desk; get out among employees, partners and customers, gather feedback/input, and feed it back into the system so people feel part of what's delivered.
2. **Express a passionate commitment to serve the customer** — it starts at the top; the leader sets the tone and the team follows, especially when given the tools and environment to succeed.
3. **Your employees/team are your greatest asset** — you're only as good as the people around you; hire top talent and train them, giving them the tools and techniques to be great.
4. **Hire for attitude** — define the right attitude for your company and hire for it. Virgin hires positive, "cup half full" people who bring positive energy.
5. **Empower your employees/team to make every experience great** — let them make decisions to keep the customer happy; even if a decision goes wrong, you quietly clean up behind the scenes (a form of **servant leadership**).
6. **Engage social media with a genuine voice** — don't post bland, impersonal press releases; engage the audience with sincerity and personality, and have customer-service support behind the posts to answer questions/input.
7. **Have fun** — hard work and results can coexist with fun; if the leader is having fun, it sets the tone for the team, customers can sense the engagement, and it makes a real difference. It all starts with the leader.

## Pro-tip / further

- Other ways to engage customers: set and communicate expectations while balancing customer needs against project control.
- Customer-centric leaders often adopt the **transformational leadership** style.
