## Part 1 — Definition

- **Forecasting** = a technique for predicting the direction of **future trends** by systematically analysing past and present data.
- Businesses use it to allocate resources and plan for anticipated expenses in upcoming periods.
- It works as both:
  - A **decision-making tool** — managing the impact of future uncertainty by examining historical data.
  - A **planning tool** — charting next moves and building budgets to cover potential uncertainties.

## Part 2 — Methods of Forecasting

Companies generally choose between two methods:

1. **Qualitative (judgmental) method:** subjective; relies on the personal judgment, knowledge and experience of experts/forecasters. Because it depends on individual perspective and interpretation, these forecasts are often biased. Example: predicting a football game's outcome from personal interest/motivation rather than objective data.
2. **Quantitative method:** a mathematical process, so results are consistent and objective. Moves away from opinions/feelings, instead using and interpreting large quantities of data and figures.

## Part 3 — The Four-Step Forecasting Process

1. **Develop the basis of forecasting:** investigate the company's current condition and its standing in the market.
2. **Estimate future operations:** project future industry conditions and how the company will handle them.
3. **Regulate the forecast:** compare previous forecasts with the actual historical impact; analyse the differences (deviations) and consider why they occurred.
4. **Review the process:** check every step for accuracy and refine/modify the forecasting model.

## Part 4 — Benefits of Forecasting

1. **Predicting the future:** gives management a general idea of what to expect and a sense of direction to function better in the market.
2. **Customer satisfaction:** predicting demand keeps products available and orders delivered on time.
3. **Staying up to date:** continuous forward-thinking helps anticipate and adapt to changing market trends.
4. **Learning from experience:** gathering and analysing past data helps remember what worked and what didn't.
5. **Securing financing:** banks often require data on future sales/profits before approving a loan.

## Key terms & references

- **Forecasting:** predicting future trends from past and present data.
- **Qualitative (judgmental) method:** subjective; expert opinion/experience; prone to bias.
- **Quantitative method:** objective; mathematical; uses large datasets.
- **Four-step process:** develop the basis → estimate future operations → regulate the forecast → review the process.
- **Five benefits:** prediction/direction, customer satisfaction, staying up to date, learning from experience, securing financing.
- **Other terms:** historical data; deviations (forecast vs actual).
