- Transaction Conversion Rate (TCR)

## Video transcript — Analytics of Paid Search (decision variables)

Hello and welcome. In this video we will talk about analytics of paid search. The key decision variables in paid search are **keywords, bids (or maximum cost per click), ad copy, and landing page**. Each of these individually and in combination has impact on paid search campaign performance.

The steps in this process are relatively simple: a user search on the keyword triggers an **ad impression**; an impression may trigger a **click** that in turn takes the user to a **landing page**; interaction with the landing page may result in a **conversion**. A set of metrics or KPIs associated with these stages could help analysts assess performance and diagnose potential problems with the campaign.

**Why the choice of a keyword is important:**
- The keyword is what users use to interact with the search engine. If the keyword is a common way for users to express a search intent, you'll observe a lot of searches; if not, you'll find few opportunities to serve your ad.
- Keyword selection affects the **level of competition** — a keyword attractive to many firms costs more to advertise on.
- The keyword may suggest **how close the user is to a purchase decision** — some keywords are common among shoppers still exploring, others among shoppers ready to buy. So some keywords convert to sales and some do not.

**How your willingness to pay for a click (bid) affects performance:**
- If competition exceeds the number of ad slots on the results page, some advertisers won't be shown — bidding too low, that advertiser might be you.
- A more aggressive bid may earn **top positions** (which attract more visual attention and clicks), affecting your traffic.
- The more you bid, the more you pay per click — direct implications for advertising cost.
- Less directly, position may correlate with **conversion probability** (e.g., people clicking lower-position ads might be further along in the search process and more likely to buy) — this should be tested empirically per business context, not generalized.
- Watch your **ad budget**: underspending may indicate bidding too low (missing opportunities); over-aggressive bidding may overpay per click and exhaust the daily budget on expensive leads.

**Ad copy:** This is what the prospect evaluates before deciding to visit your website. A relevant, appealing ad earns higher click-through. Ad copy performance also has cost implications (the search engine considers it when deciding placement and cost-per-click), and good ad design acts as a selection tool to attract higher-value customers.

**Landing page:** Where you take prospects after they click. The direct implication is the **conversion rate**. A good landing page should be well-designed, have a clear value proposition, and a clear call to action; confusing design may cause users to leave before taking a valuable action. User interaction with the landing page (alongside the ad copy and keyword) also affects the search engine's assessment of user-experience quality — and accordingly your costs and placements.

## Video transcript — Analytics of Paid Search: PPC Report (diagnosis)

Hello and welcome. In this video we'll talk about how to diagnose some common problems in paid search campaigns, looking at a report from an online store selling allergy-free jewelry. Daily budget per keyword is $500 and the max cost per click (bid) is $3.

Applying some basic diagnostic groups helps make sense of the numbers:

- **Gold Earrings** — never triggered an impression. "Gold earrings" is a popular product, so it's unlikely nobody searches it; the more likely explanation is the **bid is too low** and we're consistently outbid. If it's a good keyword to pursue, consider raising the bid as long as it makes economic sense.
- **Nickel Free Stud Earrings** — triggers showings but low volume → very few clicks, no conversions. It's probably *not* a competition issue, because our CPC is well below our max bid. This is likely just an **unpopular keyword** — keep it running longer to see if sales come, or alter the keyword to something more commonly used.
- **Earnings without Nickel** *(Earrings without Nickel)* — healthy traffic, good CTR and TCR, reasonable margins. The only concern is **hitting the daily budget limit** — if happy with the margins, consider increasing spend on this keyword for more sales.
- **Earrings Allergy Free** — most KPIs okay but a **lower-than-average CTR**. It could be a low page position, but that would imply high competition and a CPC closer to the max — which isn't the case. Most likely the **ad copy** isn't appealing → recommendation: revisit the ad copy.
- **Nickel Free Hoop Earrings** — the obvious issue is **not breaking even**: EPC is $1.46 while we pay $2.92 per visit. Either **lower the bid** or **improve the transaction conversion rate** to increase margins.
- **Allergy Free Jewelry** — doing great with strong margins, CTR, and conversion rate. It would be nice to see more volume (may be at its limit) — strategy: **search for similar keywords** and add them to the campaign.

While there are other considerations when analyzing paid search campaigns, these principles should help you get started.
