Now that we have introduced Search Engine Optimization or SEO, let's dive in deeper. The key objective of SEO is to be ranked higher on the search results page for a given set of keywords. To achieve that, SEO practitioners need to focus on three areas: **on-page SEO, off-page SEO, and technical SEO**.

## Definitions

- **On-Page SEO** is a practice of optimizing individual web pages including the content and HTML source code of a page.
- **Off-Page SEO** is any effort taken outside of a website including backlinks, online mentions, and social signals to improve its search engine rankings.
- **Technical SEO** is a practice of optimizing your website's technical elements to ensure that search engines can crawl, index, and render your web pages correctly.

Let's start by exploring the analytics of on-page SEO.

## Video transcript — Analytics of On-Page SEO

*(4.5-minute video.)*

Hello and welcome. In this video we will talk about Search Engine Optimization, or SEO. The key objective of SEO is to get prominence on search engines for a given set of keywords, or be ranked higher on the page. To achieve that, SEO practitioners need to focus on three areas: On-page SEO, Off-page SEO, and Technical SEO.

**On-page SEO** is a practice of optimizing individual web pages, including the content and HTML source code of a page. **Off-page SEO**, as the name implies, is any effort taken outside of the website — mainly by getting other sites linking back to the focal site, but it may also include online mentions and various social signals. **Technical SEO** focuses on technical elements of the website, assuring that search engines can crawl, index, and render web pages correctly.

Let's talk about Analytics of On-page SEO first. The key success factor for a page ranking on a search engine is its **relevance to the user's search intent**. Hence, the better the web page addresses the user's search objective, the more likely the search engine puts it at the top of the results. While intuitively this makes sense, from a practical standpoint it is not that easy to assess how the intent expressed by a user for a certain combination of query words would be seen by a search engine as matching the page content.

From the SEO analytics standpoint, a few tools and techniques might be helpful. The starting point would be to **search for the key phrase** on which you want to rank and see what type of content the search engine considers relevant for that phrase. If your page addresses a very different search intent, you may consider changing your target keyword. For example, a business school offering a marketing analytics program may want to rank on "masters in analytics." To check whether the search engine associates that keyword with the intended intent (application for the program), they could search the keyword and see what comes up. The top result might be more of a general overview of master's programs rather than the intent the school wants to capture, while the rest of the results align with the desired intent — so the keyword passes a quick intent-relevance check.

Your next step might be deeper **competitive analysis**. Consider the page at the top rank for your target keyword: what other keywords does that page also rank highly on? Chances are these are closely related to your target keyword and should be used in your content too. You may also explore how your top competitors' pages are organized — what keywords appear in the page title vs elsewhere, and the overall content structure. Finally, you can explore competitors' sites to learn what parts of their content get linked from other sites (link building is a focus of off-page SEO, but this gives ideas on what to include in your content).

You may also use **third-party tools** for an on-page SEO audit. For example, the On-Page SEO Checker by **SEMrush** can audit how well a page is optimized for a keyword — suggesting which page on the domain is the best candidate to rank for a keyword, flagging that critical elements (title, body, header) don't use the target keyword, and recommending more informative content plus semantically related keywords.

## Video transcript — Analytics of Off-Page and Technical SEO

*(4-minute video.)*

Hello and welcome. In this video we continue our discussion about analytics of search engine optimization, talking about **off-page and technical SEO**.

Let's start with off-page SEO. Search engines take into account many off-site factors when deciding how to rank a page; **inbound (back)links** are perhaps the most important of these. Analytics tools come in handy here too. For example, the inbound link analysis tool from **Moz** can tell you what other sites link to your page and how valuable those sites are. For each linking page, the tool gives its **page authority** (predicts ranking potential of the source page) and **domain authority** (predicts ranking potential of the domain providing the backlink).

Backlink analytics is also useful in **competitive analysis**. For example, we can identify our top direct competitor for a given keyword (in the example, MIT Sloan School of Business). Using Moz, we can identify the most valuable pages and domains that give backlinks to the MIT Sloan page but *not* to the Smith School page — these become potential targets for link-acquisition activities.

The third area, **technical SEO analytics**, audits issues related to the site's technical performance — how well it can be crawled, indexed, and rendered, and how it's organized in terms of website architecture. Important factors include:

- **Duplicate/highly similar content** — if not properly marked in the site code, duplicated content across pages sends the wrong signal and ranking may be penalized.
- **Structure and navigation** — a shallow site with clear navigation contributes to stronger ranking.
- **Broken links and poorly structured URLs** — both negatively affect ranking.

These issues are hard to find manually (especially for large sites), so tools like **Moz, Ahrefs, or Screaming Frog** are handy. (Example shown: a Moz Crawl report identifying technical issues.) Finally, **site speed** is an important aspect — slow-responding pages negatively affect ranking. (Example shown: Google's **PageSpeed Insights** report.)

To summarize, search engine optimization is an important element of digital marketing strategy, and a variety of analytic tools could provide support for SEO activities.
