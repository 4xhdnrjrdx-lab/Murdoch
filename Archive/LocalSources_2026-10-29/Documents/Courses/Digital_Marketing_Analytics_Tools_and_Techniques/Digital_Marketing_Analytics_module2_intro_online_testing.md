Let's start our exploration into Online Testing by looking at why firms may want to use online testing, one type of testing — **A/B Testing** — and what kinds of things can be tested.

## Definition

**A/B Testing** is a user experience research methodology that allows you to compare two versions of a single variable to determine which of the two variants is more effective.

## Video transcript — Introduction to Online Testing

*(4.5-minute video.)*

Hello and welcome. In this video we will talk about online testing. To start, let's establish why firms may want to use online testing. While a significant part of managerial decision-making is based on professional knowledge and intuition, the need for testing comes in situations where that might not be sufficient to make the optimal decision. This is particularly common in design and other creative fields.

As an example, consider the landing page for the Master's in Marketing Analytics Program at the University of Maryland. This page is used in promotional campaigns that aim to stimulate applications. It provides general information about the school, features student testimonials, and displays a **call-to-action button** inviting the visitor to engage further. One business objective is to learn about the visitor's interest in business education and collect contact information for subsequent marketing communications.

How successful the site is at engaging the visitor depends on numerous factors — from the ad copy that brings the user to the site, to various design elements of the page. The most obvious condition for the call to action to be effective is that the box at the top of the screen should attract visual attention; if visitors don't notice it, they won't click. Less straightforward is telling which **wording** of the call-to-action button is more effective at stimulating clicks — "next page" or "start here." Web designers and marketing communication professionals may have different views, and the only way to establish the truth is to put it to testing.

**So what is A/B Testing?** Simply put, A/B Testing is an approach to compare two versions of some design to figure out which version does better on a given performance dimension. In the call-to-action example, we'd want to learn which of the two wordings results in more clicks. In the most basic case, we consider just two versions of our creative. Say we have two versions of a paid search ad — ad A and ad B — to promote our program on Google. A priori, we don't know which works better. We test by **splitting the Google search traffic into two groups** — one sees ad A, the other ad B. After showing both versions to a sufficiently large group, we collect performance statistics to establish which is better — then we stop showing ad A and keep version B.

More generally, the idea of testing applies to various aspects of design. **What do we commonly test in digital marketing?**

- **Landing pages** — besides the critical call-to-action, you can explore different ways of wording your offer, different layouts and formats, or the color scheme / background image.
- **Email marketing** — the first thing to test is the **subject line** (if it's not appealing, targets never open the email).
- **E-commerce product pages** — how many product features to show, what product images to use, whether to include full reviews or just average star ratings. These are hard to answer intuitively, which raises the need for testing.
