To start, let's talk about Search Engine Marketing, specifically about some analytic approaches and tools used in Search Engine Optimization (SEO) and Paid Search.

## Definitions

- **Search Engine Marketing** is a type of online marketing that aims to establish a firm's visibility on search engines and bring prospective customers to the firm's website.
- **Paid Search** is a form of search engine marketing that involves the promotion of websites by increasing their visibility in search engine results pages through paid advertising.
- **Search Engine Optimization (SEO)** is the process of improving a firm's visibility on search engines by optimizing content and design elements of the website, inbound links, and various technical aspects of site performance.

## Video transcript — Introduction to Search Engine Optimization and Paid Search

*(3.5-minute video, presented by Michael Trusov.)*

Hello and welcome. In this video, we will talk about search engine marketing, and specifically about some analytic approaches to search engine optimization and paid search.

Let's start with some definitions. What is search engine marketing? Search engine marketing is a form of internet marketing that aims to establish a firm's visibility on search engines and to bring prospective customers to the firm's website. It is one of the primary online customer acquisition tools in today's digital marketplace.

Marketers use search engines to reach prospective customers mainly either through **paid search advertising** or **organic search results**. In paid search, firms attract prospects by placing ads to match these with the search terms used by consumers. In search engine optimization, they improve their visibility on search engines by optimizing content and design elements of the website, inbound links, and various technical aspects of the site.

Now let's look at some examples. As regular users of search engines, we are constantly exposed to both forms of search engine marketing. Assume you're contemplating the idea of getting a master's degree in analytics, so you may start your search on Google by typing in something like "masters in analytics." A second after you click the search button, Google will return a page which may look like this.

At the top you will find so-called **sponsored search results**, which are essentially advertisements placed by different universities who want to attract applicants to their programs. You can see ads from the University of Maryland, from Johns Hopkins, SBU, and William and Mary. You may have noticed that while I didn't provide the specific geographical location that I'm interested in, Google pulled up the schools in my proximity. This could either be the result of advertisers targeting local applicants, or inferences Google made about my personal preferences for the location. The sponsored search listings are clearly marked with ad labels.

Below, you see a section of so-called **organic results**, which are what the search engine thinks is the best match to my search intent. Providing high-quality, relevant results is of the highest priority to a search engine, as this is a key value they create for their users. You may also notice that the listing for the University of Maryland comes up here as well. In contrast to sponsored search, this type of targeting in organic results is fully under the search engine's control.

Now, if you turn to the firm's side: to reach out to prospects through **paid search**, marketers focus on keyword selection, bid optimization, landing page design, and ad creatives. **Organic search** hinges on building inbound links and optimizing the website's performance and content. Ultimately, these efforts are aimed at gaining search visibility so the firm's listings get noticed and clicked.
