# Business Communications – The Basics — Difficult Conversations

> **Source / origin note.** External course material — *not* Murdoch. From the edX course **DoaneX CMS-3161x — "Business Communications: The Basics"** (Module 2). Text reproduced verbatim for study reference.

---

While we all probably like to give praise or have fun conversations, in the business world it will be necessary to have difficult conversations with co-workers, supervisors, or subordinates. Here are some things to think about when having difficult conversations.

## Use "I" language

Many times we make statements using the "you" language, and that can come off as verbal finger-pointing to the receiver. Instead, we need to use "I" language that actually focuses on the speaker. Here are a couple of examples:

**"You" Statement:**
> "That was a dumb promise you made. We can never have the job done by the end of the month."

**"I" Statement:**
> "I'm worried about the promise you made. I don't see how we can get the job done by the end of the month."

## Other principles for difficult conversations

Next, we will want to focus on solving problems — we need to communicate with **problem-oriented messages**. These messages work at solving both individuals' needs.

We also need to be **honest** and not manipulate any situation. Once someone finds out that they have been manipulated or lied to, they will have a tough time trusting and believing that individual again. Even if the news is not good, being honest is the best option.

When we are having the difficult conversations, we need to show **concern, respect, and keep an open mind**.
