Directors tend to get results, stimulate action, accept challenges, and make decisions. Directors act with authority and stir things up; they are organized and they solve problems. However, directors are not good at analyzing the pros and cons, and they also do not typically use caution.

## How to communicate with a Director

When you are speaking with a director, be sure that you are clear and to the point, stick to the subject, and avoid rambling. Also, it is best to be logical when presenting the facts and establish a time frame.

**Things to avoid** when communicating with a director: talking too much, generalizing, and repeating yourself.

## Example

For example, if you're giving a presentation to your boss who's primarily a director — on the five recommendations you come up with for, let's say, improving customer experience on your company's website — the last thing you'd want to do is to say something like this:

> "All right, my first recommendation is to revamp our sales funnel in order to take a consultative approach to the sales process. Instead we should create two sales funnels, both distinct from each other; this will sort the customers based on their current familiarity with our products. Regular customers can go straight to the shopping cart while first-time customers will be redirected to a custom FAQ page relating to the products they're looking at. My second recommendation is to employ customer service surveys and have constant communication between our customer service department and our tech team…" and so on and so on.

We are going to stop right there, because at this point your boss's head is probably already exploding — and the speaker is only on the second recommendation (thank goodness they only had five, right?).

A better way to get this information to a director communicator is to cut right to the chase, like this:

> "Hey boss, I have five recommendations to improve customer experience on our website:
> 1. Take a consultative approach
> 2. Use customer surveys
> 3. Interview users
> 4. Implement responsive web design
> 5. Make it more social
>
> Please let me know which one you want to hear more about."

It is really quite amazing how much of a difference this simple act of changing how you phrase things makes. The boss that used to seem so impatient is suddenly responsive, and they start developing trust in you — because you're showing them that you can deliver the results the boss wants, and in a way that they want.
