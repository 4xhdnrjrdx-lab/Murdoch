Everyone is part of a communication network, no matter the type of job that they have. **Communication networks** are simply the pattern of contact created by the flow of messages among communicators through time and space. There are 2 types of networks: **formal** and **informal**.

## Formal networks

Formal networks are designed by management to dictate who should talk with whom to get the job done. In a small organization, this can be fairly simple, but as organizations get larger the formal network gets more intricate. Think of an organizational chart when you think about formal networks, and how the communication flows: **downward, upward, and horizontal**.

**Downward communication** is when communication moves from the top down to subordinates. Downward communication can serve many purposes, some of which are:
- job instructions
- job rationale
- feedback
- indoctrination

**Upward communication** is the exact opposite; this is when the subordinate communicates with their superiors. Upward communication can serve four types of messages:
- what the subordinates are doing
- discuss unsolved work problems
- suggestions for improvement
- how subordinates feel about their job and their coworkers

While most businesses say they want this to happen, not all businesses appreciate that communication. However, it has been known that some of the best ideas come from those working the front lines of a business each and every day.

**Horizontal communication** (which can also be called **lateral communication**) is typical of employees who are in the same department, or even possibly in different departments but on the same level of the org chart. Horizontal communication can serve five purposes:
- task coordination
- problem-solving
- sharing of information
- conflict resolution
- building rapport

## Informal networks

Not only does every organization have a formal network (created by management), but there are also **informal networks**. These networks are formed by interactions that are based on friendships, common personal or career interests, and proximity.

While some informal communication may be non-work related, informal communication can have valuable functions such as:
- confirming a formal message
- expanding on a formal message
- expediting an official message
- contradicting an official message
- circumventing formal channels
