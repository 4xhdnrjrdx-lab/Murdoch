Hello and good day. I'm Eric Mead. I'm going to share with you five ways to give praise.

Not too long ago, a global insurance company surveyed thousands of its employees through an employee satisfaction questionnaire in an effort to improve what the senior executives felt was a morale problem. One of the survey questions provided some important insight. The question asked was: "What can your manager do to make this a better place to work?" **89 percent of the employees answered: recognize me for a job well done.**

One of the most basic findings in psychology is that rewarding specific behavior increases the likelihood of the behavior being repeated. Praise serves as an important reward and motivation for better performance. Praise strengthens the relationship a manager has with his or her direct reports.

In another study, Dr. Gerald Graham, a professor of management at Wichita State University, surveyed 1,500 employees from a wide variety of organizations and industries and reported the following responses:

- 58 percent seldom, if ever, received praise from their manager.
- 76 percent seldom, if ever, received written thanks from their manager.
- 78 percent seldom, if ever, got a promotion based on performance.
- 81 percent seldom, if ever, received public praise.
- 92 percent seldom, if ever, participated in a meeting designed to build morale.

Okay, so many would agree that praise is lacking, which begs this question: why don't leaders praise more often? Here are some excuses I've seen. Despite good intentions, many managers feel they have so much to accomplish that praise falls to the bottom of their to-do list. Or leaders focus a lot of energy on eliminating barriers to achieve results and get caught in the trap of focusing solely on employee failures that don't meet expectations. In many businesses, the culture of praise is set from above; many managers report they never receive praise from their senior leaders. Or leaders have not developed the habit of letting people know how much they are appreciated.

Rather than believe the hype and live by excuses, what can you do to achieve a higher standard of effectiveness in offering genuine, appropriate praise?

**Number one: Be genuinely appreciative.** Every person on your team is doing part of your job. While it's easier to recognize the true home runs of performance, don't forget those who hit singles solidly and consistently every day. Don't take the little things people do for granted — recognize and appreciate those too.

**Number two: Deliver praise from your heart.** Your appreciation of their efforts must be evident in your facial expression, your tone of voice, and how you phrase your praise. Express your feelings with intent and purpose. Let them know that the job they perform well is important to you, the team, the department, and the organization.

**Number three: Deliver praise now.** Don't wait until the quarterly or annual performance review to mention something that an employee did months ago. By continually observing performance, you can offer timely expressions of acknowledgement and appreciation. Genuine praise helps people feel good about themselves and creates even stronger commitment to better performance.

**Number four: Make praise specific.** How many times have you heard someone say "nice work, Jim"? Now, how motivating is that? Not describing the exact behavior or skill along with your expression of appreciation is disingenuous. A better, more motivating approach is describing specific examples of what was done and what that means to you. Specific praise assures employees that you are truly paying close attention to what they do and how they do it.

**Number five: Praise people publicly.** Acknowledging people in public accomplishes two important things. First, employees feel even better as they are recognized in front of their peers. And second, providing public praise to an individual that is specific is a great way of reminding other employees what you want from them.

So remember to use these five praise reminders in your leadership practice. Praise is an effective tool which, if done right, increases employee engagement and promotes a positive work environment. Praise may only take a few seconds to deliver, yet the impact of consistent, genuine, appropriate praise can be immediate and long-term.

## Quick reference — the 5 ways to give praise

1. Be genuinely appreciative.
2. Deliver praise from your heart.
3. Deliver praise now.
4. Make praise specific.
5. Praise people publicly.
