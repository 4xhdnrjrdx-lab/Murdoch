# Business Communications – The Basics — Communication, Culture and the Work Environment

> **Source / origin note.** External course material — *not* Murdoch. From the edX course **DoaneX CMS-3161x — "Business Communications: The Basics"** (Module 1). Text reproduced verbatim for study reference.

---

To many, the word culture makes one think of people from different national backgrounds. While national cultures certainly do exist, and they play an important role in shaping the way people communicate, we also need to look at differences such as race/ethnicity, socioeconomic class, gender, and age. If we take all of these factors into account, we can define **culture** as a learned set of shared interpretations about beliefs, values, and norms that affect the behaviors of a relatively large group of people.

As was mentioned earlier, national background does not alone signify one's culture. While we need to take race and ethnicity into consideration, we also need to look at social class, age or generational differences (like one might see between baby boomers and Millennials). Not only that, but we must consider regional differences and disabilities as well.
