# Business Communications – The Basics — Listening (video transcript)

Listening is simple, right? We all know how to do it and we all do it well, correct? Well, we may not all be as great at it as we think we are. The issue is that, more often than not, when it comes to communication we pay more attention to the messages that we are *sending* than to the messages that we are *receiving*. One of the number one traits that organizations want in their employees is quality listening skills.

There are **four misconceptions about listening** that we are going to address.

**Misconception 1 — It is the sender's responsibility to communicate effectively.** While it is true the sender does need to communicate effectively, the receiver is also responsible. If the receiver is not listening, then even the best-planned message will be lost.

**Misconception 2 — Listening is passive.** The idea that a listener just quietly absorbs all information is not true. Sometimes, to listen, you may need to actually speak — to ask questions or clarify a statement — in order to make sure the information is understood. A good listener is actually an active communicator.

**Misconception 3 — Talking has more advantages.** While it might seem that the person who does all of the talking is in control of the conversation, those who are talking all of the time will likely miss out on important information.

**Misconception 4 — Listening is a natural ability.** While we can say we have been listening since we were very young, the fact is that most people overestimate their listening skills. Many organizations invest in developing their employees' listening skills.
