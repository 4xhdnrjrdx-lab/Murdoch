# Business Communications – The Basics — Communication Channels

> **Source / origin note.** External course material — *not* Murdoch. From the edX course **DoaneX CMS-3161x — "Business Communications: The Basics"** (Module 1). Text reproduced verbatim for study reference.

---

One thing to consider when preparing your message is deciding on the channel or method you are going to use to send your message. There are a few things to think about when choosing the channel. First is the **richness** of the channel — this refers to the amount of information that one would be able to receive. In a face-to-face setting, you would be able to evaluate body language, tone, and facial expression, while in an email you would not be able to evaluate any of that information; thus the face-to-face interaction would have more richness. One should also think about **speed**, or how quickly the exchange of messages occurs. You will also want to think about how much **control** you may have over managing the communication process.

Here is a graph detailing some common communication methods and how they rate with regard to the channel methods.

| Method | Richness | Speed* | Control over the message | Control over Attention | Typical Tone | Typical Level of Detail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Face-to-Face | High | Synchronous | Minimal | High | Personal | Moderate |
| Phone (incl. Phone Conference & Video Conference) | Moderate | Synchronous | Minimal | Moderate | Personal | Moderate |
| Voicemail | Moderate | Asynchronous | Moderate | Low | Moderate | Low |
| Email | Low | Asynchronous | High | Low | Impersonal | High |
| IM (Instant Message) | Low | Asynchronous | Moderate | Moderate | Moderate | Low |
| Text Message | Low | Asynchronous | High | Low | Impersonal | Low |
| Normal Message (i.e. handwritten or typed letter) | Low | Asynchronous | High | Low | Dependent on writer | High |

\* **Synchronous** — happens at the same time.
\* **Asynchronous** — does not happen at the same time.
