# Business Communications – The Basics — Barriers to Listening Effectively

> **Source / origin note.** External course material — *not* Murdoch. From the edX course **DoaneX CMS-3161x — "Business Communications: The Basics"** (Module 2). Text reproduced verbatim for study reference; decorative images omitted.

Despite the importance of understanding others, research suggests that misunderstandings are the rule rather than the exception. Conversational partners typically achieve no more than 25 to 50 percent accuracy in interpreting each other's remarks. Research shows that immediately after a 10-minute presentation, a normal listener can recall only 50 percent of the information presented. After 48 hours, the recall level drops to 25 percent.\*

## Environmental Barriers

These can be loud noise in a factory or a crowded restaurant, but this can also include hot or cold places, uncomfortable furniture, and this can also include phone calls, emails, text messages, etc. The best bet to avoid environmental barriers is to move to a different, quieter, more comfortable location and silence devices if possible.

## Physiological Barriers

Poor listening can be a result of one's poor hearing; there could also be the issue of processing difficulties such as auditory discrimination or memory. Another physiological barrier is our brain. The brain can usually process information at about 500 words per minute, but the average person speaks about 125 words per minute — this difference allows the brain extra time to wander.

## Psychological Barriers

These are the issues that interfere with people's willingness to listen, but also their mental capacity to listen effectively.

- **Preoccupation** — When you have too many other items to think about (whether they are work related or personal), those can take over your thoughts and cause you not to listen effectively.
- **Message Overload** — With so many different ways to receive messages (phones, laptops, etc.) we run into the problem of multicommunicating. Trying to switch between all of the different messages actually makes us less effective. It is better to concentrate on one task and complete it before moving to the next task.
- **Egocentrism** — This is the belief that your message or idea is better than those with whom you are communicating. While this is rarely the case, this person will lose out on valuable information because they essentially blocked the messages from others.
- **Ethnocentrism** — An individual's cultural ignorance or prejudices can create psychological noise that interferes with messages being received correctly.
- **Fear of appearing Ignorant** — Many people don't want to look like they are ignorant or don't know something, so they choose not to ask questions, when in fact asking questions will pay off in the long run.
