## Notes from the linked Mindtools quiz

### What the quiz measures

It's a short self-assessment built on **Daniel Goleman's five elements of emotional intelligence**:

1. **Self-awareness**
2. **Self-regulation**
3. **Motivation** (self-motivation)
4. **Empathy**
5. **Social skills**

You rate 15 statements on a five-point scale (Not at All / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Very Often), answering as you *actually* are rather than as you think you should be, then total your score for a banded result (roughly: "needs work" → "OK" → "emotionally intelligent / strong leadership potential").

### Which questions map to which pillar

- **Self-awareness** — questions 1, 8, 11
- **Self-regulation** — questions 2, 4, 7
- **Self-motivation** — questions 6, 10, 12
- **Empathy** — questions 3, 13, 15
- **Social skills** — questions 5, 9, 14

### Development tips per pillar (paraphrased)

- **Self-awareness** — Goleman describes self-aware people as aware of their moods *as they happen*. Build it with mindfulness (focusing on the present and on how you feel), journaling about emotional situations, a personal SWOT analysis, and asking others for feedback on your strengths and weaknesses.
- **Self-regulation** — It's about staying in control. Notice what triggers anger and why; use techniques like deep breathing; pause before responding to emails/requests so you don't say something you'll regret; manage anxiety and stress; and take accountability so your actions align with your values.
- **Self-motivation** — Emotions can distract you from finishing tasks. Build self-discipline, celebrate small wins, set and review longer-term goals (especially when personal goals connect to career ones), and rediscover your sense of purpose if motivation is low.
- **Empathy** — The ability to recognise others' emotions and understand their perspective (Goleman's "fundamental people skill"). Develop it by considering others' viewpoints, using active listening, not interrupting or steering to your own feelings, and reading body language.
- **Social skills** — Even non-"people persons" can improve. Assess your communication skills, build trust and rapport, don't avoid conflict or difficult situations, and build self-confidence by practising in progressively larger group settings.
