# BUS279 Sprint 2: Velocity Fit Launch Strategy
**Faiaz Dzhafarov (35553299) · Digital and Social Media Marketing**

This sprint works the Velocity Fit brief, the AI-driven personal training app, across the two workshop stages: the creative and influencer testing from Lesson 6, and the awareness, ad and analysis plan from Lesson 7. The total working budget the CEO has given us is $10,000, and it has to stretch across the content shoot, the ad spend and the tracking software.

The root problem, the "itch" I keep coming back to, is not awareness. People scroll past a thousand fitness ads a week. The real itch is that most people who want to get fit feel exposed and unsure: they do not know if they are doing it right, and they do not want to be judged while they figure it out. Velocity Fit's AI form-check is the answer to exactly that, but our own funnel data shows we are scaring people off at the very moment we ask them to use it. So my whole strategy is built to lower that fear, build trust before we ask for anything, and only test one thing at a time so we actually learn what works.

## PART A: Creative and Influencer Testing (Sprint 2a)

### Round 1: Hook and Visual, the Creative A/B Test

The job here is two "challenger" ads that change exactly one thing, so any difference in cost per acquisition can be traced to that one variable.

**The single variable: emotional driver (Dopamine vs Oxytocin).** Everything else stays fixed: same 15-second length, same app footage, same offer, same call to action. The only thing I change is the feeling the ad sells.

**Version A, the Lifestyle Dopamine cut.** Fast cuts, upbeat track, sweat, a personal best on screen, a body mid-rep, bright colour grade. The message is the dream: this is the energy you could have. Hook in the first second is a jump-cut from tired-at-a-desk to mid-workout.

| Frame | What is on screen |
|---|---|
| 1 (0-2s) | Pattern interrupt: someone slumped at a desk, then a hard cut to them mid-sprint |
| 2 (3-6s) | Fast montage of results, the app's progress numbers ticking up |
| 3 (7-11s) | The phone propped up, AI form-check drawing lines on the body, "your coach, in your pocket" |
| 4 (12-15s) | CTA: "Start free. Train smarter." |

**Version B, the Solution Oxytocin cut.** Calmer, slower, a single real person, the camera staying on the phone screen so you can actually see how the AI form-check works. The message is reassurance: this is the tool that quietly fixes what you are unsure about, and nobody is watching but you.

| Frame | What is on screen |
|---|---|
| 1 (0-2s) | Close, honest: "I stopped working out because I thought I was doing it wrong" |
| 2 (3-7s) | The app's AI checking a squat, gentle on-screen correction, no other people in frame |
| 3 (8-12s) | Same person, more confident, "it just tells me, privately" |
| 4 (13-15s) | CTA: "Start free. Train smarter." |

**Reading the result.** The data we were given says Version A pulled a 5.8% click-through, $0.85 cost per click, 4.2 second watch time, and an "excited" sentiment, while Version B pulled 2.1% click-through, $1.95 cost per click, 9.8 second watch time, and a "confusing, too much text" sentiment. On the surface A wins, it is cheaper and it stops the scroll. But A also has the higher bounce rate later, because the dream gets the click and then the reality of the app does not match the hype, so people leave.

My read is that this is not "A is right, B is wrong." A is a better top-of-funnel hook and B is a better trust builder, but B was executed badly, too much text, too slow to show the payoff. The smart move is not to pick one, it is to use A's energy to get the click and rebuild B so the trust message lands in under three seconds. That feeds straight into Round 2, where the influencers do the trust job that a paid ad struggles to do on its own.

### Round 2: The Influencer Audit and Selection

Budget covers two influencers, and the brief wants a genuine A/B: one aspirational, dopamine-led creator, and one relatable, oxytocin-led creator. The trap here is picking by follower count. A novice buys reach. What I care about is whether the audience actually listens, so I audited the shortlist on engagement quality, not size.

**The ones I rejected, and why.** Jaxson Fit has 450k followers but only 2.1% engagement, which is a classic engagement gap that usually means bought or passive followers, so his reach would not turn into clicks. Chloe V. (850k, 1.5%) and Urban Athlete (310k, 1.2%) are the same story, big numbers, dead audiences, pure vanity reach. Coach Kev (200k, 1.8%) has the same problem plus a drill-sergeant tone that spikes cortisol, which is the opposite of what our anxious user needs. Reach is not resonance, so all four are out.

**My two picks.**

**Aspiration / Dopamine: Tech-Tom, the Bio-Hacker (85k, 3.2%).** He is the aspirational pick because his whole brand is optimisation and cutting-edge tech, which maps directly onto Velocity Fit's AI angle. His engagement is real, not inflated, and his audience already believes in data-driven training, so they are pre-sold on the idea of an AI coach.

**Relatability / Oxytocin: Bec's Journey, Weight Loss (18k, 18.2%).** She is the relatable pick and honestly the strongest creator on the list. An 18.2% engagement rate on 18k followers means a small but fiercely loyal audience, real super-fans, not spectators. She has lost 30kg herself, so when she says the app helped, people who feel exactly the fear in our "itch" believe her. (Big Mike and Sarah-Jane were close runners-up on the same logic; I went with Bec because her transformation story carries the most trust for someone scared to start.)

**Creative brief for each (two lines).**
- *Tech-Tom:* present the AI Camera as the elite path, "the tool that finds the 5% of your form holding you back." Lean into data, before-and-after metrics, the science of correction.
- *Bec's Journey:* present the AI Camera as the relatable path, "the tool that means you never have to feel lost or watched again." Lean into honesty, privacy, "if I could start, you can."

**Platforms.** Both run on the platform where their audience already lives: Tech-Tom on YouTube and Instagram (longer, explainer-friendly), Bec's Journey on TikTok and Instagram Reels (short, emotional, shareable). This also gives us a clean platform read for Sprint 2b.

**Four-frame storyboards.**

*Tech-Tom*
| Frame | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Most apps just count reps. This one watches your form." Phone scanning a deadlift. |
| 2 | Split screen: bad rep flagged red, corrected rep flagged green, angles on screen. |
| 3 | Tom reading his own progress data, "this is the part I could never measure before." |
| 4 | "Link in bio. Train on data, not guesses." |

*Bec's Journey*
| Frame | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Bec, phone in hand, "I quit the gym because I felt like everyone was watching me." |
| 2 | At home, the AI quietly correcting her squat, no one else around. |
| 3 | "It just tells me, privately. No mirrors, no judgement." |
| 4 | "Start free, like I did. Link below." |

These are deliberately different scripts, not one script copied twice, because the whole point of an A/B is to learn which message converts.

### Round 3: Post-Mortem Storyboards (Position, Problem, Action, Effect)

Now I take the post-mortem results we were given for my two creators and storyboard the recovery or acceleration move for each. The test is whether my "action" actually fixes the specific "problem" the data showed.

**Tech-Tom, the "Analysis Paralysis" problem.**
- **Position:** strong reach and credibility, but his post got admiration and very few sign-ups. People watched, nodded, and did not act.
- **Problem:** he made it too scientific. He scratched the curiosity itch so thoroughly that there was no tension left, the viewer felt educated, not compelled. If we leave it, we keep paying for views that never convert.
- **Action:** pivot his next piece from "here is everything the AI measures" to one sharp, single outcome, "this one fix added 15kg to my squat in three weeks." Strip the jargon, add a hard call to action with a trackable link.
- **Expected effect:** lower the cost per acquisition by turning passive respect into a clear next step, moving him back toward a "scratch" the viewer can act on.

**Bec's Journey, the "Organic Engine" success.**
- **Position:** her post overperformed, an 18% engagement rate and real traction inside weight-loss community groups. This is working.
- **Problem:** the risk with a winner is under-investing in it. If we leave it as a one-off, the momentum dies and we miss the cheapest acquisition we have.
- **Action:** scale it. Put paid spend behind her best-performing post (spark ads / whitelisting), seed her code into the community groups where it already spread, and brief her for a short series, not a single post.
- **Expected effect:** push her from a viral spike to a repeatable, low-cost acquisition channel, our "organic engine," and use her content as the trust layer the paid ads in Sprint 2b point to.

The honest takeaway across both: the relatable micro-creator out-earned the high-tech one, which lines up with the Round 1 finding that trust converts better than hype once you are past the first click.

## PART B: Awareness, Ads and the Plan (Sprint 2b)

### Deliverable 1: Platform and Positioning

**Platform: TikTok.** Velocity Fit's core buyer is the under-35 person who wants to get fit but feels self-conscious, and that is exactly who lives on TikTok, where short, high-dopamine, fast-cut video is the native language and where fitness content already spreads through "people like me" rather than polished brands. The cost per thousand views is low, the creative is cheap to produce (UGC, not studio), and the algorithm rewards resonance over follower count, which suits a new brand with no audience yet. Instagram Reels is our secondary channel because the same vertical content cross-posts, and Google Search is the bottom-of-funnel catch for anyone who hears the name and looks us up.

**Positioning: the private AI coach that never judges you.** Most fitness apps shout about results and elite bodies, which actually raises the anxiety that stops people starting. We stand out by owning the opposite space: Velocity Fit is the tool that checks your form privately, corrects you gently, and lets nobody watch but you and the AI. On a platform full of intimidating "look at me" fitness content, a brand that says "no mirrors, no judgement, just quiet help" is a genuine pattern interrupt.

---

### Deliverable 2: Two Ad Concepts, A/B Test

**The single variable: the hook type.** Same platform, same length, same product footage, same call to action. The only thing that changes is how the first two seconds grab attention, a "How-to" hook versus a "FOMO" hook. If I changed the music, the actor and the headline all at once I would never know what moved the numbers.

**Ad A, the How-to hook.** Opens with "Three squat mistakes your gym never told you about," then shows the AI flagging each one. It pulls the viewer in with usefulness and curiosity. It speaks to the person who suspects they are doing it wrong and wants the fix.

| Frame | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Three squat mistakes you're probably making" (text + a flagged rep) |
| 2-3 | AI correcting each mistake, quick and clear |
| 4 | "Get your form checked free. Velocity Fit." |

**Ad B, the FOMO hook.** Opens with "Everyone at my gym started using this, so I caved," then shows the social proof and the app. It pulls the viewer in with belonging and the fear of being left behind.

| Frame | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Everyone started using this. I finally tried it." |
| 2-3 | A relatable person using the AI form-check, looking relieved |
| 4 | "Get your form checked free. Velocity Fit." |

We run both, measure the hook rate (three-second views over impressions) and the cost per acquisition, and keep the winner. My hypothesis is that the How-to hook wins on quality of clicks because it attracts people already feeling the form-anxiety itch, but the test, not my gut, decides.

### Deliverable 3: Brand Equity

The goal is not to be one more piece of background noise that people scroll past and forget. It is to add a "star" to the user's mental map so that next time they think about getting fit without being judged, our name is already filed under that feeling.

Every ad we run hammers the same three associations: privacy, smart AI help, and "people like me succeeded." That consistency is what builds a constellation of variables around Velocity Fit, the same way Apple owns "sleek and private" without having to say it. We are not trying to win the sale on the first view with an external "buy me now" shout, which is the loud, forgettable kind of equity. We are building internal equity, the quieter "I already know who they are and what they stand for," so that by the time someone is ready, choosing us feels obvious.

In plain terms, we are trying to get past the brain's filter, the reticular activating system that throws away noise and keeps signal. A one-off funny ad might get remembered while the brand gets forgotten, the vampire effect. So our creative always ties the hook back to the brand promise, no judgement, private AI coaching, so the memory that sticks is ours.

---

### Deliverable 4: The Plan, Getting Past the Friction Wall

Our own onboarding data shows two walls. The AI Camera Assessment is where it hurts most: only 45% finish it, and the feedback is "felt exposed, awkward." Then the payment gate converts just 12%, with "too early, high friction." The ads have to do work before the user ever opens the app, so these walls feel lower when they arrive.

**The AI Camera wall (the cortisol spike).** People freeze at the camera because it feels like being watched and judged, the exact fear in our itch. So the ad's job is to pre-load trust. Every creative, especially Bec's, says the quiet part out loud before download: "no one sees this but you, not even us in a way that's shared." By the time they hit the camera, the idea that it is private and non-judgemental is already planted, which lowers the cortisol at the moment of truth. I would also push the in-app flow to offer a "no-camera demo first" path so the nervous user can see the value before exposing themselves.

**The payment wall (too early).** Asking for money before the user has felt any value is what kills the 12%. The whole funnel is built around a free start, the call to action is always "start free," not "subscribe." We let them feel one genuine win first, one corrected lift, one "oh, that's better," because that win is the real scratch to the itch. Only after that do we introduce the paid tier, framed as "keep your AI coach," not "pay now." The ad sets up the free entry, the app delivers the value, and the payment ask comes when trust is highest, not lowest.

The bridge, in one line: the ad is the itch (you could train without feeling lost or watched), and the app is the scratch (here is your private coach, free to start).

### Deliverable 5: Process Audit, the MarTech Toolkit

The CEO wants to know which tools we actually use. The principle from Lesson 7 is not to get "tool-drunk," buying every piece of software and hoping it solves the problem. Software is an accelerator, not a strategy, and we are a seed-stage startup with a small team and a $10k mindset, so I am choosing the few tools that match our stage and saying no to the enterprise overkill.

| Tool | Use? | Why / why not |
|---|---|---|
| Salesforce (enterprise CRM) | No | Gold standard but heavy and needs a team to run. Overkill at our stage. |
| HubSpot (all-in-one CRM) | Yes | Our nervous system. Connects TikTok ads to email to CRM in one place, perfect for a small team. |
| Dragonfly AI (visual heatmap) | Yes | Cheap insurance on the first two seconds, it predicts where the eye goes, which is exactly our hook problem. |
| Hotjar (UX session recording) | Yes | Critical. It shows us video of users hitting the AI Camera friction wall so we can see why they freeze. |
| Sprout Social (listening + sentiment) | No | Useful but overlaps with cheaper options at our size. Revisit when we scale. |
| Hootsuite (scheduling + monitoring) | Yes | Simple, affordable way to schedule and watch trends across TikTok and Instagram. |
| Mailchimp (email automation) | No | HubSpot already covers our email, so this would be duplicate spend. |
| Brand24 (web mention listening) | No | Nice to have, not a priority while we have almost no brand mentions yet. |
| GA4 (web analytics) | Yes | Free and essential, it tells us where traffic comes from and where it drops. |
| Canva Pro / Adobe (creative editing) | Yes | We need fast, cheap ad edits to A/B test hooks, this is core to our creative loop. |
| Cometly (ad attribution) | Yes | Important, it tells us which TikTok ad actually drove the install despite the multi-day lag. |
| Klaviyo (retention SMS/email) | No | Strong tool but it overlaps HubSpot; not worth a second platform yet. |
| Zapier / Make (automation glue) | Yes | Cheap connective tissue so a TikTok lead auto-flows into HubSpot without manual work. |
| Amplitude (in-app product analytics) | Yes | Essential for our specific problem, it shows exactly where in the app users drop, like the camera step. |
| Jasper / Copy.ai (AI copywriting) | Yes | Helps generate ad-hook variants quickly for testing, a cheap accelerator for the creative loop. |
| SEMrush (competitor intelligence) | No | Helpful later, but not where our limited time and money go right now. |
| Typeform (interactive quiz) | Yes | A "find your fitness starting point" quiz is a low-friction capture that warms users before the scary camera step. |
| UGC production | Yes | Our cheapest, most authentic content, exactly the relatable style that converts for us. |
| Ad spend (the fuel) | Yes | Non-negotiable, this is the engine; most of the $10k lives here. |
| SEO / content | No (not yet) | Long-term play, too slow to matter in this launch phase. Park it. |

The pattern in my choices: I keep HubSpot as the brain, the cheap analytics and attribution that tell me the truth (GA4, Hotjar, Amplitude, Cometly), the creative accelerators (Canva, Jasper, UGC), and one low-friction capture (Typeform). I say no to anything that duplicates HubSpot or that only pays off at a scale we have not reached.

## How I would test and measure

I am treating none of this as a final answer, only as a set of bets to validate. Round 1's creative test reads on hook rate and cost per click. The influencer A/B reads on cost per acquisition, Bec's against Tech-Tom. The two ad hooks read on hook rate and quality of sign-ups, not vanity likes. The friction-wall fixes read on the two numbers that actually matter: the 45% AI-Camera completion and the 12% payment conversion. I would run each test on a clean single variable until the result is stable, keep the winner, and feed the budget toward whatever lowers our cost per acquisition. The market is the final judge, so the plan is built to learn fast and cheaply rather than to bet everything on my first idea.
