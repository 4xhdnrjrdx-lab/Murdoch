## Opening: the diagnosis

*[2-3 строки в самом верху листа, задают рамку всего питча]*

The dossier shows people already know Volt-Vantage and enjoy watching it. Likes are high, conversion is zero. So the leak is not awareness. The leak is fear and hassle: 60% of site drop-offs happen on the Charging & Storage page, and the comments say "I'd be terrified to ride that to the office". Every part of this pitch attacks those two frictions.

## 1. Website architecture & user journey (15 pts)

*[Нарисовать карту: прямоугольники со стрелками. Landing → Commute Calculator → Peace of Mind hub → Book a Test Ride → Purchase (подписать "The Scratch"). Сбоку пометить, куда идёт внимание на каждом шаге.]*

- Landing: hero is a professional in work clothes gliding past a traffic jam at 8am. Not a stunt. One CTA above the fold: "Book a 15-minute test ride".
- Commute Calculator: parking plus fuel per week against the bike. People have no idea what their commute costs until someone shows them a number, so this is our reference price.
- Peace of Mind hub replaces the current Charging & Storage page, which is where 60% of visitors quit. Battery certification, fire safety standard, warranty and insurance, weight and folding demo, a "charge at your desk" guide. The friction wall lives here, so this page sits one click away from everywhere on the site.
- Test ride booking is the conversion I actually want. It asks for an hour, not $4,000.

A/B testing plan: (1) hero image, commuter in a suit vs product shot; (2) CTA wording, "Book a test ride" vs "Build your commute"; (3) Peace of Mind layout, demo video first vs specs table first. One metric for all three: click-through to test ride bookings. Likes told this brand nothing so far, so I am done measuring them.

## 2. Creative storyboarding (15 pts)

*[6 кадров-скетчей в две строки, простыми человечками. Подписи под кадрами короткие.]*

1. 8:47am, an exec circles the CBD looking for parking. Twenty minutes, straight from the customer interviews. Cortisol face.
2. A colleague in a suit glides past the jam and arrives calm. Now the itch is visible.
3. That evening he searches "beat CBD parking" and our search ad is the first answer. *[это обязательный по рубрике сценарий онлайн-обнаружения]*
4. He lands on the calculator, sees the weekly number, clicks into Peace of Mind, watches a 20-second folding demo.
5. Saturday test ride, helmet on, grin visible.
6. Tuesday, 8:55am: bike folded next to the desk, coffee in hand. Same man, same street as frame 1, different chemistry.

## 3. Channel & influencer strategy (10 pts)

Current Instagram is producing dopamine for the wrong crowd. Keep the account but re-task it: commuter lifestyle content and retargeting, no more stunt reels. Add LinkedIn, because the Tuesday Morning professional scrolls it in work mode and trusts it with career-adjacent decisions; a peer posting "my commute changed" is social proof there, a jump over a staircase is not. Google Ads works as our intent sensor: demand capture on searches like "e-bike commute" and "CBD parking alternative", people who are already itching. TikTok stays out of the launch, its stunt-hungry audience is exactly the perception we are trying to leave behind.

Influencer types, not names: micro influencers with relatable reality. Finance and tech professionals, productivity creators, cycle-to-work people whose feed looks like our persona's life. Mega and celebrity stunt riders would rebuild the old image at ten times the cost. Before any deal, an audience audit: engagement gap, decay curve, no bought views, and a genuinely corporate follower base.

## 4. Strategic ad spend (10 pts)

*[Нарисовать воронку сверху вниз, проценты подписать напротив этапов.]*

- 35% Google Search. Intent sensors at the decision stage, the highest ROI dollar we have. This catches the exec from frame 3.
- 20% LinkedIn. Awareness and consideration inside the professional context.
- 20% Meta/IG retargeting. Desire stage: everyone who dropped off Charging & Storage sees the folding demo and the safety certificate instead of another stunt video.
- 15% micro influencer partnerships. Trust and social proof, mid funnel.
- 10% creative production and the A/B budget, which keeps the other 90% testable.

The logic in one line: weight sits where intent is, background noise gets nothing. Retargeting gets a big share because the leak is mid funnel, not at the top.

## 5. Target market deep dive (15 pts)

The Tuesday Morning persona: 32 to 50, corporate professional in the CBD, calendar full by 9am, drives in and resents it. Psychographics: careful with his body, conscious of status, wants to arrive composed; he will ride a bike as long as he does not look ridiculous on it. Behaviour: researches at night on a desktop, reads reviews, will not buy transport he has not touched. Geographics: the 5 to 15km ring around the centre, too far to walk, close enough that parking pain feels absurd. His pains come straight from the dossier: lithium fire fear, a heavy frame up the office stairs, "forgot e-bikes were an option", and rental scooters cluttering the pavement, which cheapens the whole category in his eyes.

Business metrics. The unit is premium, so CAC can run high and the sale still pays; what matters is that intent-led capture keeps CAC well below what stunt-video awareness was costing per actual buyer, which so far is infinite. CLV runs past the bike itself: service, accessories, battery replacement, and each convert is a door into a corporate fleet deal. Behavioural mining on the site (heatmaps on Peace of Mind, drop-off tracking) tells us whether the persona is behaving the way this page assumes.

## 6. The AIDA push & the leaky bucket (15 pts)

*[Нарисовать ведро с водой, дырка сбоку на уровне Desire, подписать "Friction Wall: Charging & Storage, 60%".]*

- Awareness: already there. The likes prove it. We stop buying more of it.
- Interest: exists, the dossier says "interested but isn't buying" in as many words.
- Desire: this is the hole. Safety fear plus storage anxiety, sitting at one known address, the Charging & Storage page, with a 60% drop-off to mark it.
- Action: never reached, so nothing to fix there until the hole is plugged.

The plug: the Peace of Mind hub answers both fears with proof; the test ride gives a small first commitment instead of asking for $4,000 cold (start low on the commitment spectrum); retargeting pulls the people who leaked out back to the exact answer they were missing. After purchase: onboarding and service reminders, so the bucket does not open a second hole at retention.

## 7. Competitive advantage, blue sky (10 pts)

The shift we are selling: Volt-Vantage stops being an extreme toy and becomes the professional standard. Ideas beyond the media budget:

- "Office Ready" program: partner with CBD buildings and employers for charging docks and secure storage in lobbies and parking levels. The moment offices have Volt-Vantage docks, storage anxiety dies, and every dock doubles as an ad in front of the exact right audience.
- Salary packaging and corporate fleet pilots: one HR agreement delivers dozens of on-persona customers at almost no acquisition cost.
- Ownership against clutter: position openly against rental scooters. Owned, insured, serviced, yours. The premium price stays, it is doing Veblen work, signalling which tier this product lives in.
- The long game is a strategic fortress made of the ecosystem: docks plus fleets plus a service network. A competitor can copy the bike in a season; copying relationships with fifty office buildings takes years.

## 8. General strategy, the glue (10 pts)

STP: segment urban commuters, target the Tuesday Morning professional, position as The Professional's Advantage, the calm and controlled way to arrive.

A quick 7Ps pass to show one machine, not eight ideas: Product (certified safe, foldable), Price (premium held, reframed as cost per week against parking), Place (test rides and office docks, the website as the hub), Promotion (the intent-led channel mix above), People (concierge-style staff, no bro-culture sales floor), Process (search, calculator, test ride, purchase, each step one click from the last), Physical Evidence (certificates on the page, docks in real lobbies, reviews from real commuters).

Why this is not guesswork: every dollar and every page in this plan attacks the two frictions the data actually shows, fear and storage. The demand already exists, executives hate their commute; the job is to take the wall down between the itch and the scratch, and that is exactly where all the weight went.
