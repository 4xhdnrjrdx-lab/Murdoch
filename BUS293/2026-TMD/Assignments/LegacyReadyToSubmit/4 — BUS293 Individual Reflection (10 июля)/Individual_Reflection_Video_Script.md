## PART A: My contribution & key findings  (~1.5 min, ~185 words)

Hi, I'm Faiaz. For our team report, my group looked at team dynamics at a company called Westpac. Westpac is one of Australia's big four banks.

My part was team trust. I also made the reference list and the appendices, and I wrote the conclusion after everyone finished.

I learned that trust is not one thing. It is built on three things. First, you see the person as honest. Second, you feel they really care about you. Third, you see they are good at their job. If one is missing, trust drops. Trust also grows slowly, so one broken promise can set you back.

What surprised me most was how trust sits under psychological safety. That means feeling safe to speak up. In a bank with strict rules, if people do not feel safe, they stay quiet about problems. Westpac learned this the hard way. In 2020 it paid a record fine of one point three billion dollars, for more than twenty-three million breaches of money-laundering law. One reason was that problems did not reach the managers. So my ideas were about safe ways to speak up, and fair, steady leadership.

## PART B: Reflection on our teamwork (Ch.8 Team Dynamics)  (~2 min, ~295 words)

Now let me look back at how our team really worked. Chapter eight gave me the words for it.

The first big thing was a change in team size. We started with four people. Then one person left the unit, so we were three. We had to share his work and organise again. But the chapter says something helpful: smaller teams often work together and trust each other better. That is what happened with us.

Roles were important too. Nobody was the official leader. But one teammate, Owethu, quietly became what the chapter calls the Organiser. She set up our shared document, the schedule and the forum posts, and she chased our deadlines. The chapter says people often take roles by themselves, and nobody gives them out. That is exactly what we saw.

In Tuckman's stages, losing a member pushed us back. We went from norming into a short storming, then back to norming when the roles were clear. From the Five C's, our Coordinating was strong, thanks to Owethu. But our Comforting and our face-to-face Communicating were weaker, because our team was in different time zones and we worked at different times.

This is where my own topic became real: swift trust, which means fast trust. We had no time to build trust the slow way. So we trusted clear roles and shared deadlines instead. Seeing my report topic happen in my own team was the best part for me.

To be honest, our weak point was team reflexivity. This is Schippers' idea that a team should stop and check how it works. We almost never did. So we lost time and got confused about who did what. My lesson is simple: in a remote team, clear roles do the job that sitting in the same room normally does.
