# БЛОК 1 — FAIAZ · 0:00–3:10 (417 слов, посчитано)

### [0:00–0:25] Hook + intro · [SLIDE 1]

> Four hundred cocoa beans. That is what goes into one bar of chocolate. Hi, I am Faiaz, and this
> is Uliana and Artem. Last semester I mapped the operations behind a small bean-to-bar chocolate
> brand in Perth. Today we want to show you how we would take that same small workshop and scale
> it into a second country.

_Пометка: держать плитку в руке. Это единственный реквизит, он держит внимание первые секунды._

### [0:25–1:00] Где бизнес сейчас · [SLIDE 2]

> Here is where the business stands right now. One workshop in Perth. We roast, grind and temper
> about a hundred and twenty bars a day, and we sell through a handful of local stockists and a
> market stall. That is roughly eighteen thousand seven hundred dollars a month, on running costs
> of six to ten thousand. It works. But it only works in one postcode. The recipe is proven, the
> reach is not.

### [1:00–1:35] Что такое масштабирование · [SLIDE 3]

> So what does scaling actually mean? Hallowell defines a scalable business model as one where
> revenue grows faster than the cost base. That distinction decides our whole plan. If we simply
> open a second workshop, our costs rise in step with our sales and nothing really changes. What
> we want is the opposite. The brand, the recipes and the online storefront should serve ten times
> the customers without ten times the cost.

### [1:35–2:22] Пять рычагов Stampfl · [SLIDE 4]

> Stampfl and his co-authors give us five design levers for that. Technology, the cost and revenue
> structure, adaptability to legal regimes, network effects, and user orientation. We tested our
> business against all five and two answers were uncomfortable. Technology is our weakest link,
> because right now we have no real e-commerce at all. And legal adaptability is the lever that
> decides everything, because chocolate crossing a border means food registration and
> certification. Network effects are thin for a physical product, so we lean on user orientation
> instead. On people who care where their food comes from and tell someone else.

### [2:22–3:10] Выбор рынка · [SLIDE 5]

> Then the question is where. We looked at three. Japan has the most sophisticated craft chocolate
> audience in the world, but also the most crowded shelf and the slowest relationship cycle.
> Singapore is easy to enter and small. We chose the United Arab Emirates, and not only because we
> happen to study here. The gifting economy is enormous, both corporate and personal. Premium
> imported food already has a shelf. Ramadan and Eid create two predictable demand peaks we can
> forecast against, which matters a lot when your product melts. And an Australian origin story
> works for us here rather than against us. Uliana will show you what has to change about the
> product itself.

# БЛОК 2 — ULIANA · 3:10–6:25 (427 слов, посчитано)

### [3:10–3:38] Рамка: пертская плитка — не продукт · [SLIDE 6]

> Thanks Faiaz. The mistake I want us to avoid is assuming the Perth bar is the product. It is
> not. Schein describes culture as an iceberg. The visible part, the packaging and the
> advertising, sits on top of values you cannot see. So we started below the waterline and worked
> up.

### [3:38–4:33] Культурное чтение рынка · [SLIDE 7]

> Take Hall first. The Arab world sits at the high-context end of communication and at the
> polychronic end of time. In practice that means our packaging should not shout a list of claims
> the way it does in Australia. It also means a distributor conversation that takes six weeks is
> not a distributor losing interest. Hofstede adds power distance. Deals move through senior
> people and status matters, which is exactly why the gift box, not the single bar, becomes our
> lead product. And Trompenaars is the one that changed our plan the most. This is a particularist
> market. Contracts follow relationships, not the other way around. So we budget for two buying
> trips before we budget for a single advert.

### [4:33–5:35] Редизайн продукта · [SLIDE 8]

> Now the redesign. Five changes. First, formulation. A standard tempered bar starts collapsing
> above thirty degrees, and Dubai spends five months above forty, so the summer range moves to a
> heat-stable temper on a sixty-five percent dark base, and we sell the milk range only in the
> cooler half of the year. Second, halal certification and a full Arabic and English label. That
> is not marketing, that is the price of entry. Third, flavour. We keep two signature Australian
> bars as the origin story and build three local ones. Date and sea salt, pistachio and tahini,
> and camel milk. Fourth, format. Our hero product becomes a six-bar gift box at fifty-five
> dollars, designed for corporate gifting. Fifth, calendar. Ramadan and Eid editions planned six
> months ahead, not six weeks.

### [5:35–6:25] Позиционирование и канал · [SLIDE 9]

> Positioning follows from that. In Perth we sell craft. Here we sell a considered gift with a
> traceable story. That speaks to authenticity and to the environment, values the unit covered in
> Session nine, without pretending they are read the same way in every country. The channel is
> deliberately staged. We start cross-border, direct to consumer, on a bilingual storefront with a
> temperature-controlled courier, because that lets us learn the customer without paying for a
> warehouse. Once we can show repeat orders, we move to one UAE distributor for hotels and
> corporate gifting. Only then do we go after retail shelves. That order is the whole point. Sell
> online, learn, then sign. Artem will take you through who we partner with and how we pay for it.

# БЛОК 3 — ARTEM · 6:25–9:40 (438 слов, посчитано)

### [6:25–7:07] Коллаборации · [SLIDE 10]

> Thanks Uliana. The unit gave us three tests for a partner. Shared target market and buyer
> persona, complementary brand identity, and similar goals. And not a competitor. A specialty
> coffee roaster in Dubai passes all three. Same customer, same care about origin, no overlap in
> what we sell. A supermarket private label fails the second test outright, because it would make
> our story invisible. So our first two agreements are a co-branded box with a coffee roaster, and
> an amenity placement in a five-star hotel.

### [7:07–8:00] Финансирование · [SLIDE 11]

> Money. We need two hundred and fifty thousand dollars over eighteen months, and the split is on
> the slide. Session six is our map for raising it. We bootstrap the first forty thousand from
> retained earnings, because that keeps control. We raise sixty through a Kickstarter pre-sale, all
> or nothing, the same model Flow Hive used out of Australia. We take a hundred thousand from an
> angel for roughly twelve percent, because an angel brings market contacts a bank loan does not.
> The rest comes back through the Australian export grant. That mix carries us across what the
> unit called the valley of death without handing over the company.

### [8:00–8:45] Дорожная карта и governance · [SLIDE 12]

> The plan runs in three gates. Gate one, months one to six. Certification, the bilingual store,
> first hundred online orders. Gate two, months seven to twelve. One distributor signed,
> twenty-five stockists, second tempering line. Gate three, months thirteen to eighteen. The UAE
> line is profitable and we are ready for Saudi Arabia. We do not move to the next gate until the
> previous one clears its numbers. That is PMBOK seventh edition thinking. Tailor the process to
> the work, and judge by value delivered, not by the calendar. Our top risk stays the cocoa price,
> so we hold two suppliers and forward-buy a season ahead.

### [8:45–9:40] Лидерство, этика, финал · [SLIDE 13]

> Two last things, and they are the ones that usually get skipped. Leadership. The strataplex
> model says the skills that run one workshop are not the skills that run two countries. The
> founder has to add the business and the strategic layer, or hire it, and we put that inside gate
> two. The honest risk is hubris. We read about Theranos, where nobody in the room could tell the
> founder no. Our answer is small but real. A monthly review with an outside mentor who has the
> right to stop a gate. And ethics. We keep the fair-trade sourcing and the recyclable packaging
> we started with, because scaling is exactly the moment when those quietly disappear. Same beans,
> same farmers, bigger order. That is our venture. One workshop in Perth, one country at a time.
> Thank you for watching.

## Что критично при записи (из рубрики)

- **Presentation Style оценивается индивидуально (7 баллов).** Оценка Faiaz не зависит от того,
  как отчитал Artem, и наоборот. Каждый отвечает за свою треть.
- Высший балл дают за **conversational style** и за ритм, который держит слушателя. Худший — за
  «sounds like the presenter is reading». Поэтому текст лучше знать, чем читать.
- **Correct grammar** прямо в дескрипторе. Лучше простое короткое предложение, которое произносишь
  уверенно, чем сложное, на котором спотыкаешься.
- Слайды не должны быть главным. Если камера показывает только слайды — это минус по Element 1 и
  Element 4 сразу.
