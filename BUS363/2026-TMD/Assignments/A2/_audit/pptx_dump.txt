===== SLIDE 2 =====
[TEXT] Background & Objectives
[TEXT] • Context: IT delivery firm engaged to build LMS App v2.0 — fixed price A$500k, 6 months, requirements signed off.
• Vision: a fast, mobile-first app — one place for timetable, grades, unit materials, notifications and submissions.
• Goals (SMART):
– Ship v2.0 in 6 months within A$500k budget
– ≥99.5% crash-free sessions; SSO with myMurdoch
– ≥80% active-student adoption within 3 months of launch
• Key deliverables: cross-platform app (iOS/Android); system integrations; Systems Manual; 3-month hypercare.
• Assumptions: requirements frozen at kickoff; Murdoch provides API access + test data; one client Product Owner available.
[NOTES] The brief gives me a fixed scope and price, so my job is to plan delivery that protects time and quality. Objectives are written SMART so success is measurable, and I've stated assumptions explicitly because they frame the limitations of the plan.

[TEXT] Constraints & baseline
[TEXT] • Fixed price A$500k & fixed scope → schedule and quality are the managed levers (triple constraint).

• Scope baseline = scope statement + WBS + schedule (100% rule: all work captured, nothing extra).
[NOTES] Because cost and scope are fixed, I manage the triple constraint by treating schedule and quality as the flexible levers, controlled through change control. The scope baseline is what every change request is measured against.

– Agile delivery core — 2-week sprints, product backlog, MoSCoW prioritisation
• Why hybrid: fixed-price / fixed-scope needs predictive control; the software build benefits from iterative feedback.

===== SLIDE 7 =====
[TEXT] Key Deliverables & Acceptance Criteria
[TABLE 7x3]
  | Phase | Deliverable | Acceptance criteria
  | Initiation | Project charter & plan | Board sign-off
  | Discovery & Design | UX/UI designs + solution architecture | Product Owner sign-off; architecture review passed
  | Development | App increments (per sprint) | Definition of Done; automated tests pass; demo accepted at sprint review
  | UAT | Tested release candidate | All severity-1/2 defects closed; UAT sign-off
  | Deployment | Production release + Systems Manual | Go-live sign-off; manual incl. component inventory, install/config, release notes, backup & DR plan
  | Close | Handover + closure report | Client sign-off; support handover accepted
[NOTES] Every phase produces a defined deliverable with explicit acceptance criteria — this is what makes the plan auditable and protects us commercially, because payment milestones tie to accepted deliverables.

===== SLIDE 12 =====
[TEXT] Risk & Issue Management
[PICTURE]
[TABLE 7x4]
  | # | Risk | Score | Response
  | R1 | Legacy integration harder than expected | 20 H | Mitigate — integration spike in Discovery; integration BA
  | R2 | Scope creep despite sign-off | 12 H | Mitigate — change control; locked baseline
  | R3 | Fixed-price cost overrun | 15 H | Mitigate — EAC/ETC tracking; 10% reserve; MoSCoW de-scope
  | R4 | Key resource unavailable | 9 M | Mitigate — cross-skilling; named backups
  | R5 | UAT delays / tester availability | 9 M | Mitigate — book UAT early; student pool
  | R6 | App-store approval delay | 6 M | Transfer/Accept — submit early; follow guidelines
[TEXT] • Issue log: ID · description · severity · owner · status · resolution date.   Responses: Mitigate / Avoid / Transfer / Accept / Escalate.
[NOTES] Risks are scored on likelihood × consequence. The three high risks — integration, cost overrun and scope creep — get the most attention. Integration is de-risked early with a proof-of-concept spike; cost is protected by EAC/ETC tracking, a reserve and the ability to de-scope 'could-haves' through MoSCoW.

[TEXT] Monitoring & Control
[TEXT] Schedule & scope
[TEXT] • Weekly RAG status board
• Fortnightly sprint burndown + reviews
• Milestone tracking against baseline
• Change control: every request impact-assessed vs baseline, Board-approved
[TEXT] Commercial control (EVM-style)
[TABLE 9x2]
  | Field | Value
  | Commercial basis | Fixed price
  | Budget at Complete (BAC) | A$500k
  | Actual cost to date | A$40k
  | Percent complete | 8%
  | Estimate to Complete (ETC) | A$460k
  | Estimate at Complete (EAC) | A$500k
  | Budget variance | A$0
  | Forecast finish | Month 6
[NOTES] Control runs on two tracks. Delivery is tracked with a weekly RAG board and sprint burndowns; commercially I use an earned-value-style table — budget, actuals, ETC, EAC and variance — so any drift shows up early. Change control protects the fixed-price baseline.
